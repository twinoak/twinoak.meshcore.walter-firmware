**The night the unit was not heard.** The first unit ran on its own battery through the night of
7 to 8 November 2026: **56 wakes since the power-on at 20:37, and meshmaster.dk heard it on about
every sixth one.** The unit showed *quiet* there, and two jobs waited from 09:25 UTC. That is this
release's first and largest fix; the five others are what the same board showed across those two days
— an offer over LTE that failed in two seconds, a journal that claimed a server nobody had spoken to,
a thirty-second stall that only a build of its own could trace, a radio that had lost its identity
and read as one awaiting its first install, and a download message that promised something the
Walter's own slot cannot do.

**Over LTE, and this time it can be.** A unit at `v0.1.2` or `v0.1.1` takes this release without a
cable: *Offer a release* on the unit's page with the Walter's product, then *Apply the candidate*.
That is what `v0.1.2`'s notes promised and what has never once worked, for the reason in the second
row above; both halves of the fix are in place now — **meshmaster.dk's task 177 is deployed** (its
release 61, 8 November 2026 16:17 UTC), which serves a download to a unit that sends no version
header, and this release sends one on every request. The image comes from **meshmaster.dk** and never from github.com, held to ISRG
Root X1, and the whole file is checked six ways against the bytes in the Walter's own flash before
anything is switched. One thing to know before pressing it: **the Walter's own download does not
resume**, so the ~785 kB has to finish inside one wake's twenty-minute exchange, as twelve 64 kB
ranges against a megabyte of allowance.
[`docs/flashing.md`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware/blob/main/docs/flashing.md)
has the walk, step by step, with what the console and the unit's page say at each one. **A unit still
at `v0.1.0` goes by cable**: not for the header, but because its second HTTPS connection of a wake
was the one that failed, and an offer is fifteen of them.

**One thing to expect on the first boot, once.** The record this firmware keeps in RTC memory across
a sleep grew four bytes, for the seconds a sleep asked its timer for. A record of another size is not
the same record, so **the first boot of `0.1.3` reads none**: that one wake says *the record did not
survive*, `wake`'s list is that wake alone, the battery band is `unknown` and the next interval is
60 min rather than the 15 a full pack allows. The wake after it is normal. That is the rule working;
a record that does not come back on the **second** wake would be a fault.

**The radio half is still
[`twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware` v0.1.2](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware/releases/tag/v0.1.2)**,
published 7 November and the current release of it. Link 1.2 is unchanged by this release: a radio at
`v0.1.1` or `v0.1.0` carries no `LOCAL_REQUEST` capability bit and its status, neighbours and owner
info stay *failed* exactly as they do today, and nothing here compares the link's minor.

**A correction, about the radio's key.** When the first unit's half-written radio was recovered over
its own USB-C on 6 November, it **did not come back with its identity**: its next `HELLO` carried a
**new key**, meshmaster.dk logged a changed companion, and its repeater had to be registered again.
The reading is the repeater firmware's own — the Adafruit core's `InternalFS.begin()` erases and
formats the file-system region when the volume will not mount, which is what a torn page from an
interrupted flash leaves, and the next lines then draw a new key without a word. The sentences that
said the key was kept were in this repository's README and two of its phase-8 task pages, written on
7 November, and all three now say what happened. **From repeater `v0.1.2` a radio in that state
refuses instead** — the link stays up, `IDENTITY` is answered `ERR_STATE`, and it waits for a person
to type `erase` and `reboot` — and this release is the one that tells that refusal apart from a radio
awaiting its first install. The region boundary still holds: neither the flasher nor the `.uf2`
writes the bytes the node's key lives in.

**What has been on a board, and what has not.** The first unit has run `v0.1.0`, `v0.1.1` and
`v0.1.2` at a desk: on 7 November it gave its radio a golden image and pushed a release in over the
cable; it then ran the night above on `v0.1.1`, on its battery; and on 8 November it took `v0.1.2` by
cable, the offer over LTE having failed, with its radio at repeater `v0.1.2` the same day. That is
where every fault above was read. **Nothing of this release has been on silicon**, no release has
yet walked a whole bench or a mast, and no unit has yet taken its **own** release over LTE — the
thing this one exists to make possible. What holds it is the host tests against the simulators and the measurements in the six
reports. The walk that turns that sentence over is
[`docs/bench.md`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware/blob/main/docs/bench.md),
and this is the release it is walked with.
