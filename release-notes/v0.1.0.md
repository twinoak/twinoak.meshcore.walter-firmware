**The first release of the Walter firmware** — the LTE box of a [TwinOak standard repeater
v2](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.standard-repeater-design-v2). The box that makes
sure the repeater on the mast cannot be bricked from afar and can be looked after without climbing
to it: it watches the radio and brings it back, replaces the radio's firmware over the debug port
and rolls it back if the new one is not healthy, updates itself A/B, and checks in with
meshmaster.dk over LTE-M once a wake.

**What has been on a board, and what has not.** A Walter has run a *development* build of this
firmware and checked in with meshmaster.dk on 23 October 2026 (`0.0.0-dev`, as *LTE test node*).
Nothing else has: no released image has been flashed to a module, no pin has been wiggled on a
real RAK, no radio has been reflashed over SWD and no unit has walked the bench. Everything in
phases 0 to 5 is tested on a host against simulators — a simulated nRF52 down to its flash
controller, a flash that loses power at any operation, a scripted repeater, a modem that fails in
every way a real one does, a scripted meshmaster.dk. The walk that turns that sentence over, one
step at a time, is
[`docs/bench.md`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware/blob/main/docs/bench.md),
and this is the release it is walked with.

**Putting it on a Walter:**
[`docs/flashing.md`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware/blob/main/docs/flashing.md)
— one page, from a module out of the box to a unit that checks in, and the three lines to look at
on the console when it does not.

**The LoRa box.** A unit is two boxes, and the other one runs
[`twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware),
which has **no release of its own yet** — flash it from a build of its `main` over the adapter's
USB-C for now (`docs/flashing.md`, *The RAK*). When its first release exists, the release it goes
with is named here; a Walter with this firmware can then offer it to the radio over the air
without a cable.

**The key.** Signed with release key `73efdd383a957596`, made on 20 October 2026, which is the one
key that also signs the repeater firmware's releases. A build of this firmware trusts that key and
no other, so **a Walter flashed by cable with this release can take every later release over
LTE** — that is the reason this one exists.

**Taking it over `0.0.0-dev`.** A factory flash keeps the unit's settings and the radio images it
has stored (below), so a board already provisioned stays provisioned; `trust` on the console says
`1 key` instead of `none`, which is the change that matters.
