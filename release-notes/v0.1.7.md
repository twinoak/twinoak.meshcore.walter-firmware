## What is new

- A new firmware on trial now gets its check-in through even when the wake before it ended badly, and tries again for the whole five minutes rather than once — so a slow attach no longer sends a good release back and bans it. When one is sent back, the unit says how many times it tried. Seen in the simulated tests only; not yet on a board.
- A login this unit floods goes out in the radio's default scope, with one try without it, once the radio has its 1.3 too. `rak scope` reads that scope at the console. Seen on the host against a simulated radio of both versions, not yet on a board.
- A unit's check-in now carries how its floods have fared — answered in its radio's scope, answered only without it, or not at all — counted since the unit was made and kept across a sleep, a restart and a power cut. `agent state` prints the same line at the console. Nothing of it appears until the radio has its 1.3 too. Seen in the host tests, not yet on a board.
- After a release, the pull request that carries its notes now says what GitHub really does with it: the check on it is held for approval — approve it and merge when it is green, or merge without waiting, in which case that held check goes red at the merge and means nothing. Nothing on a unit changes and there is nothing to put on a board.
- An update of the radio now fills its page in as it happens - flashing, booting, proving, each with the time - instead of saying nothing for the quarter of an hour a flash takes, and it keeps its claim on the job long enough: an update that has to put the old firmware back takes about forty minutes and used to be cut short and called failed. Seen in the host tests, not yet on a board.
- Pressing *Wake it up* now gets an answer. A unit that woke on its radio's line used to send nothing whenever the wake before it had ended in a failed call; it works out how long it was away from the radio's own clock instead, and a wake somebody asked for does not wait at all. Shown on the simulated bench, not yet on a board.

**Taking it from the release before, over LTE.** *Offer a release* on the unit's page on
meshmaster.dk with the Walter's product, then *Apply the candidate*; the unit fetches it on its
next check-in, writes it into its other slot, switches, and is brought back by the bootloader if
the trial fails. A unit that will not come up at all goes by cable, with the factory image and the
`esptool` line below.
