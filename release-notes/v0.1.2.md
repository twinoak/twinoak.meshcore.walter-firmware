**The weekend the first unit worked, and what it cost.** On 7 November 2026 `v0.1.1` did the three
things `v0.1.0` could not: it gave the first unit's radio its first image over SWD, took a whole
repeater release in over the cable in seconds rather than hours, and ran a whole check-in with
meshmaster.dk. Then the scheduled `config.read` of its own repeater **reset the Walter**, and four
smaller things came out of the same evening. This release is those — and it is the first release a
unit already in the field can take **over LTE**.

**Over LTE, for the first time.** A unit at `v0.1.1` takes this release without a cable: *Offer a
release* on the unit's page with the Walter's product, then *Apply the candidate*. The unit's own
image comes from **meshmaster.dk**, never from github.com — the fetch is `{server.url}{base}`, held
to ISRG Root X1, the root that loaded and matched on the first board on 23 October — and the whole
file is checked six ways against the bytes in the Walter's own flash before anything is switched.
One thing to know before pressing it: a radio image's download resumes across wakes and **the
Walter's own does not** — its slot is erased at each `beginWrite` — so the ~750 kB has to finish
inside one wake's twenty-minute exchange, as a dozen 64 kB ranges. If it does not, the next wake
starts the file again. [`docs/flashing.md`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware/blob/main/docs/flashing.md)
has the walk, step by step, with what the console and the unit's page say at each one. **A unit at
`v0.1.0` still goes by cable**, for the reason that page gives.

**Link 1.2 needs the radio at repeater `v0.1.2`.** The radio half is
[**`twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware` v0.1.2**](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.repeater-firmware/releases/tag/v0.1.2),
published 7 November and the current release of it, which answers `LOCAL_REQUEST` and also stops a
half-written radio from quietly becoming a new node. This Walter asks in 1.2 and **reads the
session's capability bit, not the radio's version**: a radio at `v0.1.1` or `v0.1.0` does not carry
the bit, and then a status, a neighbour list and the owner info of the unit's own repeater are
*failed* and its access list silent — exactly as they are today. Nothing else compares the link's
minor: a 1.2 Walter accepts, flashes, proves and rolls back a 1.0 repeater image as before.

**Two sentences of `v0.1.1`'s notes were wrong**, and the review of 7 November found them. They are
corrected here because that release's body is published and only a person can edit it:

- *the radio's first install flashes the stored `v0.1.0` candidate* — it cannot. A first install
  that fails puts its candidate's hash on the bad list **before** it enters *stuck*, and such an
  image is refused before a sector is erased and cannot be pushed back in. The repeater's own
  release is what goes in.
- *the console's CR LF conversion made a push take two and a half hours* — the conversion broke the
  push outright; the two and a half hours were `walterctl`'s end waiting out its whole 5 s reply
  timeout for every acknowledgement.

**What has been on a board, and what has not.** The first unit has run `v0.1.0` and `v0.1.1` on a
desk, and on 7 November it flashed its radio's first image, pushed a release in over the cable and
completed whole check-ins with the real meshmaster.dk. **No release has yet walked a whole day, a
whole bench or a mast**, and nothing of this release has been on silicon at all: what holds it is
the host tests against the simulators and the measurements in the six reports. The walk that turns
that sentence over is
[`docs/bench.md`](https://github.com/twinoak/twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware/blob/main/docs/bench.md),
and this is the release it is walked with.
