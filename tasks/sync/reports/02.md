## What upstream changed that this session had to answer

Eight of upstream's tasks and four releases (`v0.7.4`…`v0.7.7`), of which **only `v0.7.6` has a
changelog section**: the other three say *Nothing yet*. That section answers its four questions
with *two methods of `IUplink` moved, `IMesh` gained one with a default, no `MeshReply` constant
moved, the fixtures' manifest did not move* — and that is what was found. `kMeshReplyDataCap` is
184 and `kMeshPathCap` 64; `library.json`'s only change is the version, so ArduinoJson is still
**7.4.3** and `dependencies.lock` agrees (`deps` green, the only line of the lock that moved is
the manifest hash, because the component's own version did); `mm::kMaxKeptImageLen` is still
**19 368**, so `agent::kLargestKeptImage`'s two static asserts stand and the 19 456-byte scratch
still has 88 bytes over.

Four things needed answering.

1. **The host tests stopped at a `#error`, and the copy was not whole.** Upstream's loop suite
   reads four envelopes from `tests/fixtures/contracts-ahead/` (its §9.1: shapes meshmaster.dk has
   agreed in words and does not generate yet) and refuses to compile without
   `AHEAD_FIXTURES_DIR`. `tools/sync-agent-core.ps1` carried two fixture directories and not the
   third, so the define had nothing to point at. The script now carries it, the manifest holds
   its five files like every other file of the copy, and `host_test/CMakeLists.txt` gives the
   define wherever the copied suites are compiled. It is **not** this repository's own
   `host_test/fixtures/contracts-ahead/`, which holds the Walter's waiting files and is untouched;
   both names are explained where they could be confused.
   *On the way*, the re-copy showed that the manifest's **line order depended on the machine**:
   `Sort-Object` compares the way the culture does and the watch's Linux runner and this Windows
   machine disagreed about `advert.hex` and `advert-path.hex`, so a bump pushed by the watch and
   re-synced by a session differed in a hundred lines of order and nothing else. The script now
   sorts ordinally (case folded), which is the order the manifests already had.
2. **The firmware stopped at two signatures, and both are one line of plumbing.**
   `IUplink::claimJobs` gained `transactionsLeft`, `holding` and `holdingCount`, and
   `postJobResult` a `bool& jobsWaiting`. `agent::ModemUplink` passes all three claim members
   straight to `mm::writeClaimRequest` and writes **neither member itself** — a claim this
   firmware composed by hand would be a second dialect — and reads `jobsWaiting` out of the
   answer with `mm::resultReplySaysJobsWaiting` **before the reply is wiped**, which is the one
   place it can be read: the result post has never kept a byte of its answer. False for a result
   the gate withholds (nothing went out) and false whenever the call failed.
   The claim's request buffer was `char request[32]`, which `{"max":2,"holding":[]}` still fits
   and a list of held jobs does not; it is now `mm::kMaxClaimRequestLen` (256), **the longest body
   that writer can write**. A body that does not fit is refused whole by the writer, so a buffer
   sized by a guess would have gone out as an empty POST and been a 400 —
   `host_test/agent/test_modem_uplink.cpp` sends the longest claim the library can make (eight
   19-digit ids and a transaction count) and reads it back off the wire.
   The shim (`host_test/agent-uplink/shim/support/FakeUplink.h`) and the one other `IUplink` of
   this repository (`host_test/agent/test_heartbeat_body.cpp`) follow. The shim grew the
   original's new knobs and records — `failClaims`, `loseTheAnswerToClaims`,
   `sayMoreWaitsAfterAResult`, `claimRequests()`, `claimHoldings()`, `resultReplies()`,
   `leasedAndLost()` — each with the original's meaning beside it, and the scripted server behind
   it hands a lost lease back on `holding`, which is meshmaster.dk's own half (its task p4.47-254)
   and the half without which the library's new cases prove nothing. The two members' bytes are
   recorded from what the library handed in, as the original records them; that they are the bytes
   `ModemUplink` puts on the wire is held in `test_modem_uplink.cpp` against the waiting envelopes
   themselves.
3. **The way back is told by nobody here**, and that cost sixteen of upstream's cases
   (*Deviations*). `IMesh::tellTheWayBack()` has a default that sends nothing, so `LinkMesh`
   compiles untouched and behaves exactly as it did; `MeshReply::route` is `NotKnown` and a job's
   log reads *this mesh tells no way back* and *reply route unknown*. §6 of the link protocol has
   no request that sends a PATH packet and §6.3's reply record cannot say how a reply arrived

   first: `tasks/phase-18/FOLLOWUPS.md` carries it with the cost measured.
   The **shim had to override the method all the same**, and that is the find of this bump:
   upstream's repeater fake implements it, so an inherited call told a way back *on the repeater*,

   override points it at the link, which tells none.
4. **A second claim appeared in a wake that nobody asked for one in**, and two of this

   `host_test/agent/test_what_is_kept.cpp` went from two entries in `agent calls` to three. That

## What acts on this unit at once, with nothing here asking for it

`maxClaimsThisWake` stays unset, so this unit still makes **one claim a wake** (pile 18's task 02
is where the battery decides otherwise). Three things of `v0.7.6` act anyway.

### One more claim after a claim that did not come back whole (p1.20-70)

The server leases a claim's jobs in the moment it answers, so an answer that did not come back
whole leaves it holding leases this unit knows nothing of — the lost answer takes this unit's
claim now and then (`tasks/phase-17/FOLLOWUPS.md`, *A claim whose answer was lost*), and the
evening of 21 November 2026 at Bellevue is the case upstream wrote it for. Such a claim is now
made **once** more, at once, with the same `max` and the same `holding`.

Shown with the fake modem in `host_test/agent/test_a_claim_that_was_lost.cpp`, over the wake bench
and the scripted meshmaster.dk — a wake whose first claim is leased and whose answer never
arrives:

* **two claims, and no third**; both bodies byte for byte the same, `{"max":2,"holding":[]}`, with
  no `transactionsLeft` (the first claim of a wake states none, and the second is priced as the
  first is);
* the two leases handed back to the second claim on its empty `holding`, both jobs run, both
  results posted, nothing left leased on the server;
* **the wake was 9 200 ms of its 300 000 ms bound** (9 s awake, 7 requests), ended at step 10 by
  **going to sleep** — `Ending::kSlept`, not the guard — with the uplink's own budgets untouched:
  `budget` 0 (no call refused for want of room or of the wake's deadline), `held` 0 (the
  120-a-minute ceiling), `wakesRoom().no_room` 0;
* and the pair is **one** entry in the list a person reads, not two, because the second claim was
  the one that failed only when it failed too. A claim the server **refused** (503 with a
  Retry-After) is asked once and no more, which is the second case of that file.

One thing read in the code and not provoked: a call this firmware never **begins** — no room left
in the bound, the 120-a-minute ceiling, no token — leaves `reachable` false with `httpStatus` 0,
which is one of the three shapes `mm::answerWasLost` knows, so such a claim is asked a second time
and refused at the same gate. Nothing goes on the wire and the wake ends the same way; what reads
high is a counter. It is a follow-up and not a fault.

### `holding` in every claim, and what this firmware keeps that the library does not

`JobRunner::claim` fills `holding` from **its own offline buffer** — results it made and has not
had taken — and the wake posts those results at the step **before** the claim (`wakeResults`, then
`wakeJobs`), so the member is `[]` in almost every claim this unit makes and carries an id only
when a result's post failed in this very wake. A server of meshmaster.dk's task p4.47-254 hands
back, on `holding: []`, **only a lease whose answer it never managed to deliver**; a claim it did
answer leaves no such record, so nothing else is handed back. The three kinds of work this firmware
keeps across a sleep, which the library's buffer knows nothing of:

| what is kept | in `holding`? | what a p4.47-254 server hands back | what this firmware does with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| a job the result gate left to its lease — a fetch that ran out of the wake's budget (`ResultGate`, unit-protocol §5) | no: nothing was kept, the runner was told *taken* | nothing on this claim; the job returns when its **lease lapses**, as it did before the bump | continues the download — `runOffer` keys on `expectedSha256` and a slot that already holds that file fetches nothing, so no byte is paid for twice |
| a session the wake's bound cut (`twinoak.noisescope.session`) | no: its result is owed in the **journal**, not in the library's buffer | nothing on this claim; the lease lapses | `postOwedResult` posts the journalled result at the next boot, outside any job; a re-handed session is refused while one is journalled as in progress |
| an apply whose result waits for the next boot | no, for the same reason | nothing on this claim; the lease lapses | the same, and a second apply is refused with *an update is journalled as in progress (S-7)* before anything is flashed |

So **no job is run twice and no flash is started twice** — and the one case where the server
*does* hand a job back, a claim whose answer was lost, hands back a job this unit never began. The
library's own rail for a server that does **not** read `holding` is in the suite that runs here
(`AJobHandedOverAgainWhoseResultThisEndKeepsIsNotRunAndItsKeptResultIsPosted`, through
`ModemUplink`): a job handed over again whose result this end keeps is answered with that kept
result and is not run. `test_a_claim_that_was_lost.cpp`'s third case is the same thing on the wake
bench, with the claim's `holding:[39502]` read off the wire.

### The way back (p1.21-71)

Nothing: `IMesh`'s new member has a default that sends nothing, `LinkMesh` is untouched, and a
reply's route reads *not known* here. A Walter's replies are flooded by the repeater exactly as
they were, which is what `docs/bench.md` §28's third item says out loud so that it is not read as
a fault.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **Sixteen of the library's 136 runner cases are not run through the link shim**, in three
  groups, named one per line in `host_test/CMakeLists.txt` (`LINK_SHIM_SKIP_CASES`) with the
  reason above them. Two are `v0.7.3`'s late-reply pair, unchanged. The fourteen new ones:
  * **eleven for the way back.** They assert the ways back a mesh told, or that a reply came
    *flooded* rather than by a route this end cannot read. A Walter tells none and cannot read
    one, for the protocol reason given above; they run unchanged in upstream's own
    `agent_core_runner`, and the Walter's half of all eleven is one case of our own —
    `host_test/agent-link/test_link_mesh.cpp`'s
    `AMeshOverTheLinkTellsNoWayBackAndSaysSoInEveryJobsLog`: the default answers *not told*, the
    repeater is told none (`waysBackCount()` 0, no `wayback` frame), the job's log says so once,
    the reply's route is `NotKnown`, and `client_tx` is still the runner's transactions.
  * **three because the job log is full.** `mm::JobLog` is 1 024 bytes of whole entries, and this
    road writes more of them than upstream's — the sentence about a mesh that tells no way back,
    and *reply route unknown* on every reply — so in three of upstream's longest jobs the entry
    the case greps for has fallen off the front. The behaviour is the same on both roads; what
    differs is how much of a job's own evidence fits beside it. (`ARevokeIsConfirmedByABlankLogin…`
    and `ARevokeOnARepeaterWithAGuestPassword…` still pass upstream and still do the same thing
    here — the log they assert on is the part that was cut.)
- **The copy was re-made, which the task said not to do.** The task says not to re-copy because
  the branch already carries the copy; `tests/fixtures/contracts-ahead/` was the one thing it did
  not carry, and the host tests cannot compile without it. `sync-agent-core.ps1 -Ref v0.7.7` was
  therefore run again at the same commit: the diff is **five added files and five added manifest
  lines**, and no existing byte of either tree changed (the ordinal sort above is what keeps that
  true on this machine).
- **`tools/new-sync-task.ps1` had a third fault**, found by running it rather than reading it: with
  the numbering mended, `Measure-Object` answers a **Double** in PowerShell 5.1 and `'{0:d2}'` is
  a format for integers, so the first run threw *the format specifier was invalid* and wrote a
  file called `-agent-core-v9-9-9.md`. Fixed with the other two, and the rehearsal shows
  `03-agent-core-v9-9-9.md` with its row **after** 02's (then reverted).

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The shim records a claim's bytes from what the library handed in**, as upstream's fake does,
  rather than from the request the scripted server received. The lost-claim knob refuses the TLS
  before the request goes — the same road the observations' lost answer takes — so there is no
  received request to read in exactly the case the member matters most; and
  `test_modem_uplink.cpp` is where the wire is held to the library's writer, as it already is for
  the heartbeat.
- **The lost claim is modelled as *no answer at all*** (`Fault::Unreachable`, the TLS refusing
  after the request went), where upstream's fake answers *http 200 with no body*. Both are shapes
  `mm::answerWasLost` knows; this one is the shape a modem gives and the one the Bellevue evening
  left in the unit's own call list.
- **The scripted meshmaster.dk of the wake bench learned the server's half** — the lost lease, and
  the hand-back on `holding` — because a unit cannot be tested against this bump without a server
  that does what meshmaster.dk's task p4.47-254 does. It is the same knob, by the same name, as
  the library's own fake.
- **`withheld_claims` in that script now counts a claim that found nothing queued **and** handed
  nothing over**, which is what its name says; before, with no hand-back possible, the two
  readings were the same.
- **The ordinal sort in `sync-agent-core.ps1`** rather than leaving the order to the machine. It
  is the same promise the LF line ending in that file already makes, and it is why the re-copy's
  diff is five lines instead of a hundred and five.
- **No case of this repository was deleted or rewritten to suit the bump.** The two that changed
  (`test_what_is_kept.cpp`'s pair of claims) changed their numbers and gained a sentence about
  why; the rest is new.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session can build and run the host tests; it cannot flash a board, reach a network or talk to
meshmaster.dk. Everything above is tested against simulators only — a scripted meshmaster.dk
behind the firmware's own HTTP/1.1 and a simulated stock repeater behind a simulated RAK — and the
same three items are in `docs/bench.md` §28, in the order the rig is brought up.

- [ ] ★ **A claim whose answer was lost, and the work still done in that wake.** On the unit in
      service: wherever `agent calls` shows a `claim` entry saying *nothing answered*, that wake
      must still have run its jobs, and the exit node's page must show their results at that wake
      and not an hour later. Two `claim` entries at one wake number are the expected shape. Only
      a real network loses an answer for real; the simulator decides when to.
- [ ] **And nothing done twice**, over the same night: no job with two results on the exit node's
      page, and no download or flash of the radio begun twice. The reasoning is in the table
      above and the simulators agree with it; the server is the only place it can be seen.
- [ ] **Replies still come home flooded**, which is a reading and not a step: a job's log says
      *this mesh tells no way back* and each reply *route unknown*. It is here so that a person
      reading a job log after this bump does not take it for a fault.
