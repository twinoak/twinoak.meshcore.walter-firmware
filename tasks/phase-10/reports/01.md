# p10-01 — The wait sleeps too

A wait the server asked for is a number of seconds of **real** time. Until the exit-node firmware's
task p1.10-42, `mm::AgentLoop::restore()` re-based what was left of it against the new boot's
`millis()` and the sleep in between counted for nothing — so a 30 s hold set by one failed call was
put back whole at every wake and spent five seconds at a time across six wakes, **each of which
sent nothing**. That is the night of 8 November 2026: 56 wakes since 20:37 and meshmaster.dk hearing
from the unit on about every sixth. At the usual hour between wakes it is six hours of silence
after one failed call.

This task syncs 42 and tells the library how long the board was away:
`core::cycle::WakeReason::timeAwayMs()`, **the seconds the last sleep asked its timer for, less the
timer's own error** — and **0 for every wake that is not the timer's**, because a sleep cut short by
the radio's line, a cable or a reset is a sleep of no known length. The rule is *never more than
really passed*.

Two more things came with the sync. **p1.10-43** gave `mm::IMesh` a second door —
`answersLocallyFor(publicKeyHex)`, the local door asked for a *key* before a job has begun — so the
wake prices a 34-line `config.read` of the unit's own repeater at its **272 s** of reply waits
instead of at **952 s** of airtime gaps it never spends; `LinkMesh` overrides it with `beginJob`'s
own expression, and `tasks/phase-9/FOLLOWUPS.md`'s *The wake still prices a local job as air* is
closed by it. **p1.10-44** reads a bare `>` as the empty value rather than a raw line, which needs
no code here and is one bench line.

- [x] **A timer sleep takes its own length off the hold, never more than was slept; other wakes as
  before (tests).**

  **Where the sleep is written down.** `Sleep::sleepFor` now hands `WakeReason::endingInSleep(lasted_s,
  asked_s)` the seconds the timer is being asked for, as the last thing before the chip goes down,
  and the duty-cycle retained record carries them to the boot on the other side. It has to be
  retained storage: `interfaces/clock.h` is explicit that `esp_timer` restarts at zero after a deep
  sleep, so there is nothing else on this board that can say how long it was away. The record grew
  four bytes for it — see *Decisions*.

  **The rule, and the figure.** `WakeReason::timeAwayMs()`:

  | this wake | what it was away |
  |---|---|
  | `kTimer` | `asked_s × 1000 × (100 − kTimerErrorPercent) / 100`, in 64 bits and clamped |
  | everything else | **0** — `kAttention`, `kSpurious`, `kUsb`, `kPowerOn`, `kReset`, `kCrash`, `kWatchdog`, and a record that did not survive |

  `kTimerErrorPercent` is **10**, and the comment beside it is three documents read in the build
  image at v5.5.5 rather than a memory of them:
  - `$IDF_PATH/components/esp_hw_support/port/esp32s3/Kconfig.rtc` — `choice RTC_CLK_SRC`,
    `default RTC_CLK_SRC_INT_RC`, *"Internal 136 kHz RC oscillator"*; and `sdkconfig.defaults` sets
    no `CONFIG_RTC_CLK_SRC_*`, so this build takes that default (no 32 kHz crystal is fitted);
  - the same file: `CONFIG_RTC_CLK_CAL_CYCLES` defaults to **1024** for that source, so the clock
    is calibrated against the main crystal at **every boot** — what is left is not the oscillator's
    absolute tolerance but its drift between that calibration and the wake;
  - `$IDF_PATH/docs/en/api-reference/system/system_time.rst`, *RTC Timer Clock Sources* — *"the
    frequency stability of this clock source is affected by temperature fluctuations, so time may
    drift in both Deep-sleep and Light-sleep modes"*. **No tolerance is published for it.** The only
    figure on that page is the high-resolution timer's APB source, *"a frequency deviation of less
    than ±10 ppm"*, and that is a different clock which a sleep does not use.

  So the figure is a **chosen bound and not a read one**, and it is in *Decisions* with its
  reasoning. What the documents settle is the direction.

  **The tests.** `host_test/core/cycle/test_time_away.cpp` (new, eight cases, phase 3's duty-cycle

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**The remainder in deliverable 5's fourth case is 990 s and not 900 s.** The task asks for *"a
Retry-After of 1 800 s and a 900 s sleep: the next wake still holds, for the 900 s left"*. With any
non-zero timer error — which deliverable 2 asks for by name — the remainder is
`1 800 − 900 × 90 %` = **990 s**, not 900. The two sentences of the task cannot both be satisfied,
and the rule wins over the round number: the test asserts the arithmetic
(`owed − believedMs(900)`, with a 120 s allowance for the seconds the wake has itself spent) rather
than a figure that would only be right if the sleep were believed whole. This is the same choice
upstream's report 42 made about its own round numbers, for the same reason.

**A 900 s sleep after a 1 800 s `Retry-After` is driven by hand, because the wake cycle cannot
produce one.** `WakeCycle` lengthens every sleep to a hold the seam carried out
(`core/cycle/wake_cycle.h`), so through a wake the sleep is 1 800 s and the case the task describes
is unreachable. The test uses `duty().sleepFor(900)` — a person's `sleep 900` on the console — and
says so where it does it. The shape the task is really about (a sleep shorter than the wait leaves
the rest owed) is also covered through a whole wake by the line-wake case.

**The spec says nothing about any of this and does not disagree.** `spec/SYSTEM.md` §8 settles what
the unit says to meshmaster.dk and not how a backoff is kept across a sleep; `spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md`
§8's `SLEEP_NOTICE` carries the same seconds this task writes into the retained record, and the two
now come from one variable in `Sleep::sleepFor`, which is the only place either is decided. Nothing
under `spec/` was edited; `python spec/tools/specsync.py verify spec` is green in `tools/verify.ps1`.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **Ten per cent, and why a figure had to be chosen at all.** No document gives a tolerance for the
  ESP32-S3's internal RC slow clock — not ESP-IDF's pages, not its Kconfig, and the datasheet's
  tables are not published in a form a session can read (`docs.espressif.com`'s datasheet path
  answers 404; the Nordic/Arm habit of this repository does not have an Espressif equivalent). What
  *is* documented is that the clock is calibrated against the crystal at every boot and that
  temperature is what moves it afterwards, so the error to bound is the drift across one interval
  and not an oscillator's spread. Ten per cent is an order of magnitude above a plausible
  temperature coefficient for such an oscillator over a quarter of an hour to a day, and the cost of
  being too conservative is nil for the thing this is for: a 30 s to 120 s hold is spent whole by
  **810 s** of a 900 s sleep. The cost of being too generous would be calling a server back before
  the wait it asked for, which is the one thing the rule forbids. The constant is **not** marked for
  the bench (`docs/bench.md`'s table is for numbers a measurement will settle, and this one is a
  bound rather than a measurement); §19's night item is nevertheless where it would show up wrong.
- **The seconds the sleep asked for live in the duty-cycle retained record, and the record grew four
  bytes for them.** The alternatives were worse. NVS is where the agent's state already goes, but
  the sleep's length is only known *after* the agent has saved, so it would be a second NVS write at
  every sleep and wear is the one cost that code is written against. The journal is not written at
  every sleep on purpose. Squeezing the figure into the record's one spare byte would have meant
  quantising it (255 × 256 s is 18 h, less than the longest interval the policy can ask for) for a
  slot that costs `kRetainedRecordMax` = 128 bytes whatever it holds — the record went 96 → 100
  bytes and **no RTC memory was spent at all**. The price is paid once: a retained record of another
  size in the same slot is not the same record (`interfaces/retained.h`), so **the first boot of
  0.1.3 reads none** and the wake log, the band's hysteresis, the spurious count and the faults in a
  row start again, as after a power cut, with that one wake at the `unknown` band's hour. That is
  the rule working rather than failing — the old bytes meant something else — and it is a test
  (`test_time_away.cpp`'s *a firmware whose record is another shape*), a paragraph of
  `docs/cycle.md` and the first item of the bench's §19.
- **`endingInSleep` takes the seconds with no default.** One caller, and a default would let a
  future one sleep without saying how long — which is the failure this task is about, silently
  reintroduced. The same reasoning upstream's `estimateJobMs(job, local)` is written with.
- **Every wake that is not the timer's passes 0, including a wake on the radio's line after a sleep
  this firmware *did* write down.** The seconds are known; how many of them had passed when the line
  moved is not, and the task's own rule — *what this Walter knows was slept* — admits nothing else.
  It errs the safe way: a wait is owed a little longer, never less. A unit that wakes on the line
  inside a hold therefore behaves exactly as 0.1.2 did, which is also what makes the line-wake test
  a regression test for the measure reading a wake it should not.
- **The away time is read at `AgentBackend::begin()`, from the duty cycle it is already handed**,
  rather than passed in by `main/`. `Parts::duty` is there and the figure belongs to the wake
  reason; a `setTimeAway()` before `begin()` would be a second place for the same fact. Where no
  duty cycle is handed over — a test that has none — the figure is 0, which is the behaviour the
  call had before it was measured.
- **The wake bench now works out the wake *before* it builds the agent's half**
  (`host_test/core/cycle/wake_bench.h`). That is `main/main.cpp`'s order — `duty.begin()` at :492,
  `backend.begin()` at :614 — and this change is what made it matter: the backend restores the
  library's state over how long the board was away, and the only thing that knows that is the wake
  reason. The bench used to begin the duty cycle afterwards, in `begin()`, so every agent test was a
  Walter whose state was restored before anything knew why it was awake; no test had noticed.
  `WakeWalter::begin()` is idempotent about it now (`if (!duty->begun())`), the change is
  unconditional rather than agent-only so that there is one bench and not two, and the whole suite
  is green on it with nothing else moved.
- **`answersLocallyFor` answers the same for an upper-case key as for a lower-case one**, because
  `beginJob` does — both go through this end's one `parseKey`, whose hex digits are
  case-insensitive — and **agreeing with `beginJob` is the contract**, not matching the claim's
  spelling. A claim carries 64 lowercase hex, so nothing the server sends takes that path; the test
  walks it anyway, with a job begun for the shouted key, because the alternative (two predicates
  that disagree about one key) is exactly the bug the contract forbids. The first draft of that case
  asserted `false` and was wrong.
- **The 34-line local read's result is no longer asserted to carry *this mesh answers for this
  repeater without the air*.** That line is the library's first log entry for the job and a 34-line
  read's log overruns its kilobyte, so the entry is dropped (upstream's p1.10-38). The new case
  asserts the absence of *wake budget* and the counters instead — `client_tx` 0, the gate at 0
  transmissions and 0 ms of waiting, `local_jobs` 1 — which say the same thing and cannot be lost to
  a full log. The existing p9-05 case that *does* assert the line does it on a one-line `cli` job,
  where the log fits.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board, attach to a network, queue a job on meshmaster.dk or let a night
pass. Everything below is on the unit at Thomas's bench, and the same lines are in `docs/bench.md`
**§19, *A night that is not silent***, in the order the rig is brought up. The whole of this task is
about what happens over hours, so item 3 is the acceptance and nothing else can stand in for it.

1. ★ **The first boot of 0.1.3 loses the wake log, once.** Expected and deliberate (see
   *Decisions*): on that boot only, `wake` says *the record did not survive*, the last-ten list is
   that wake alone, the band is `unknown` and the next interval is **60 min** rather than the 15 a
   full pack would allow. The wake after it is normal. A record that does not come back on the
   *second* wake is a finding; this is not.
2. ★ **A failed call costs one wake, not six.** Make one call fail and no more — point `server.url`
   at a host that refuses for one `agent wake`, then put it back — and read `agent state` on the
   next wake: `hold 0 ms left; 30 s owed before the sleep, 810 s slept`, and the wake
   **heartbeats**. The library's *N s of a wait the server asked for is still owed from before the
   sleep* must **not** appear: at any interval this unit sleeps, a 30 s to 120 s hold is spent whole
   by one sleep. If it does appear, the two figures on the hold's line say why — *0 s slept* or *no
   sleep this end could measure* means the wake was not the timer's.
3. ★ **And then the night.** Unplug, leave it, read the morning: `wake` for the count of wakes and
   `agent state` for the heartbeat counter. **The two must be within a wake or two of each other.**
   31 heartbeats against 56 wakes was the symptom; this is the item that says it is gone, and the
   simulated figures cannot.
4. **A wake on the radio's line still owes the whole wait** — `agent state` saying *no sleep this end
   could measure*, and nothing sent until the wait has really run out. A line wake that *did* clear
   a hold would mean the measure is reading a wake it should not.
5. **A `Retry-After` the server really names, across a shorter sleep.** A 429 with a long
   `Retry-After`, then `sleep 900`: the next wake owes **the remainder** (*1 800 s owed before the
   sleep, 810 s slept*, with the library's *still owed* line present) and does not call before it.
6. ★ **The read that would have been refused, priced as local** — the 34-line `config.read` of the
   unit's own repeater, `ok` in seconds at `transactions` **0**, with no *wake budget* in its log.
   The simulated figure is 3.4 s of walking and 272 s of pricing. This is the item upstream's report
   p1.10-43 asks for by name, and the only way to see the price is to make the wake smaller than the
   old figure.
7. **A refusal that is a real one reads as local** — *needs about 272 s, answered without the air*.
   **952** appearing for a job of the unit's own repeater means the door is not answering for this
   radio: `agent id`'s `local door: open` line and the key beside it are where to look.
8. **A job for a repeater that is not the unit's own is still priced as air** — the second repeater
   of §17: **952 s** in a refusal, no clause, the `waiting 20 s` lines and a real transaction count
   when the wake is large enough. The item that catches a door answering for too much.
9. **The reboot guard, now that it can speak.** With the server away for two hours of wakes, the
   wake record and `status` should show the attention raised and the reason (*N server calls
   unanswered in M min, wifi up*) — **and the unit must not restart**: `selfReboots()` 0, the
   supervisor untouched. 42's own bench item, and the one consequence of the bump a reader could be
   surprised by.
10. **An empty `get` is a value** (44). A `config.read` whose key list includes `guest.password` on a
    repeater with **no** guest password: `"guest.password": ""` under `values`, and no
    `get guest.password: >` line under `raw`. One look, and it closes a gap on the server side.
