# p10-03 — A radio without its identity

The first radio lost its key on 6 November, and the reading is the repeater firmware's
`tasks/phase-3.9/reports/01.md`: the Adafruit core's `InternalFS.begin()` erases and formats the
file-system region when the volume will not mount, and upstream's next two lines then draw a new
key without a word. From repeater `v0.1.2` a radio in that state **refuses** instead — the link
stays up, `caps` is `0x21`, `IDENTITY` is answered `ERR_STATE`, an attention of reason 6 detail 1
is raised, and it waits for a person to type `erase` and `reboot`.

This firmware could not tell that refusal from a radio awaiting its first install: `readKey()`
treated every answer that was not a key alike. Now there are **three states and one place that
decides them** (`components/core/include/core/link/radio_identity.h`), and the three things that
read the radio's identity read it there — `agent id`, the heartbeat's counters, and the update's
health check. The pages that said the key was kept are corrected with a dated line.

`tools/verify.ps1` is green on this machine, every step, on the final tree.

## What was built

**One place (`components/core/include/core/link/radio_identity.h`, `link/radio_identity.cpp`).**
`IdentityState { kKnown, kNotMadeYet, kLost }`, `IdentityReading` (a key, `ERR_STATE`, the
session's `caps`, the attention), `identityState()`, `keyIsReal()` (§5.6's 32 bytes, which
`agent::Identity` used to test inline), `capsSayIdentityLost()` (`caps == 0x21` **exactly**, not
"those two bits are set"), `kCapsIdentityLost` and `kFaultIdentityLost = 1` — the detail code is
the repeater firmware's own and is cited as such.

**`agent::Identity`.** `readKey()` builds the reading and keeps the state and the evidence;
`forget()` clears both. `identity_reads` and `no_identity` count exactly what they counted
before, and `identity_lost` is new: of the answers that left this end with no key, the ones that
were a refusal.

**`agent id`.** For a refusal: *the radio refuses: its identity is lost*, that the heartbeat
carries a counter saying which of the two this is, the evidence (`IDENTITY answered ERR_STATE`,
and `caps 0x21 (CLI, ATTENTION)` when the session carries it), and what a person does — read
`docs/flashing.md` §3a **first**, because the old key cannot be recovered, then `rak cli erase`
and `rak cli reboot`, after which the radio is a **new node** whose key MeshMaster must be told.
Six `print`/`printf` calls, because a console line is cut at 200 bytes (task p9-04). The counters
line gains *N of them a refusal (ERR_STATE)*.

**The heartbeat, and where the counter is added.** `AgentBackend::run()` hands the state to the
uplink (`setRadioIdentityLost`) beside `setLinkUp` and `setDeadline`, from the identity it has
just read before the claim; `ModemUplink::postHeartbeat` copies the library's heartbeat, appends
the counter through `agent::addRadioIdentityLost` and writes that. The console's `agent heartbeat`
goes through the same function from `Identity::fillHeartbeat`, so the printed body and the posted
one carry the same key under the same condition. The one-line wake report
(`AgentBackend::sayRadio`) has three reasons where it had two.

**The health check.** Check 1a sits between check 1 and check 2 and is made in two places, both
through one private `nodeRefusesIdentity()` and the shared classifier:

- right after `HELLO` matches the descriptor, **only when §5.1's `caps` are the lost mode's own**.
  Why not unconditionally: the link client sends a session's pending `TIME_SET` before any other
  request (§5.4), so an `IDENTITY` asked there would have check 2 reading a `TIME_GET` where it
  means to read a `TIME_SET` — the refusal would be swallowed by `Client::serviceTime` and a
  candidate whose clock is broken would pass. Gating on `caps` keeps a healthy radio's proof
  request for request what it was (the suite's 1079 existing cases are unchanged);
- and when check 2 is about to fail (`failTime`), where a frame costs nothing because the run was
  going to end anyway. This is the path that mattered on 6 November's successor: a radio in the
  lost mode brings no clock up either, so `TIME_SET` and `TIME_GET` are both `ERR_UNSUPPORTED`
  and the run would have ended at *the radio would not take the clock* — a good image on the bad
  list and the golden one flashed into the same unmountable file system. It also means that a
  future lost mode with another `caps` word is still caught.

**The simulator.** `sim::ScriptedRadio::loseIdentity()` / `keepIdentity()` model the whole mode
and not one refusal: `caps 0x21`, `IDENTITY` → `ERR_STATE`, `TIME_SET`/`TIME_GET` →
`ERR_UNSUPPORTED` (that boot has no clock interface either), everything else the fixtures' own
answers. `Boot::kIdentityLost` in the update bench and in `chip_radio.h`, and a boot clears it as
it clears the other per-firmware knobs.

## What meshmaster.dk's page will show

Read in `../meshmaster.dk` at `3214596`, nothing checked out or written there:

- the envelope needs nothing: `AgentHeartbeat.Errors` is
  `IReadOnlyDictionary<string, long>?` and documented as *an open key space of counters*
  (`src/MeshMaster.Contracts/Envelopes.cs`);
- `AgentProtocol.cs:344` serialises whatever came and stores it on the agent's row verbatim
  (`agent.Errors`), refusing only a `radio`/`errors` JSON longer than `AgentLimits.Json` =
  **4 000 characters**; the seven counters are about 150;
- the exit node's page reads it back with `AgentViews.ReportedErrors` and prints the pairs on one
  row (`Pages/ExitNodes/Details.cshtml:323`, `data-testid="agent-errors"`):

  > **Counters** overheard 0, wifiReconnects 0, httpFailures 0, observationsDropped 0,
  > resultsDropped 0, reboots 1, **radioIdentityLost 1**

  and `Companion` stays *not identified*, as it does for a radio awaiting its install. The same
  dictionary goes out on `GET /api/.../agents` (`Api/AgentsApi.cs:123`). Nothing on the server
  needs changing, and nothing there interprets the key — which is why the unit's own `agent id`
  is where the evidence and the way out are written out.

**And yes, the library's own `errors` overwrites anything a board puts in the struct.**
`mm::AgentLoop::doHeartbeat` (`components/agent-core/src/AgentLoop.cpp:346-359`) builds the
heartbeat, then assigns `hb.errors` and `hb.errorCount` from a local array of its own six — so a
counter set in `buildHeartbeat`'s struct, or in anything the loop reads, would be dropped. The
copy is `agent-core`, upstream is the exit-node firmware's private repository and task 01 of this
pile syncs it at a commit, so it was not touched: `ModemUplink::postHeartbeat` — our own
`mm::IUplink`, the one seam the bytes actually pass through — is where the counter is added, on a
copy of the struct the caller owns.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The attention is an input to the classifier but nothing feeds it yet.** Deliverable 1 names
  *attention 6/1 or `caps 0x21`* as the evidence. `caps` is read off the session the answer came
  from, which costs nothing. The attention is decoded and kept by the supervisor
  (`core/supervisor/supervisor.h`, `attentionAt()`), two components away from the identity, and
  `STATUS`'s `attention` field carries a reason without a detail — so plumbing it would be a new
  path across `core/cycle` for a fact `ERR_STATE` already settles at the same moment.
  `IdentityReading::attention_identity_lost` is in the one place, is tested, and says *lost* on
  its own; the caller that fills it is left for whoever wants `rak status` to say it too. Noted
  because it is less than the deliverable's letter.
- **An apply job reports `stuck`.** The server's outcome vocabulary is four words
  (`FirmwareApplyOutcomes`; meshmaster.dk's `docs/dev/unit-protocol.md` §5) and a node fault is
  none of them: not `committed`, not `rolledBack` (no rollback was spent, deliberately), not
  `refused` (the flash happened). `stuck` is the one that means *a person's business now*. The
  journal's last line names it — the step word is `node-fault` and the note is the reason, and
  that page says *the step words are yours* and that the server stores the journal verbatim. A
  fifth outcome would be a server change and is out of scope; it is a follow-up below.
- **`release-notes/v0.1.1.md` left as published**, as the task asks: it is the record. Task 05 of
  this pile owns v0.1.3's notes.
- **The task's reading list also named the supervisor's `radioHealth()`**: read, unchanged. A
  radio in the lost mode answers `PING`, so the supervisor classifies it `kHealthy` and neither
  `linkKnownDown` nor `radioKnownDown` is lifted — which is right, and is why the lost mode had
  to be found through §5.6 and not through the ladder.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **A new terminal state, not `stuck` with a new reason.** `stuck` is documented as *the radio
   has no proved application … no further attempt by this machine*, and both halves of that are
   false here: the candidate is on the radio, it is on no bad list, and the right next step is to
   apply it again once a person has typed two words. `node-fault` says *the image is fine, the
   node is not* and leaves the machine's other arrows exactly as they were. `decode()`'s bound
   moved from `kRefused` to `kNodeFault`, so a journal written by an older firmware still reads.
2. **Nothing is promoted on a node fault.** The candidate did not pass the health check, so it
   does not become golden; the unit keeps the golden image it had to roll back to later.
3. **A session with `caps 0x21` and no key is *lost* even when `IDENTITY` was not answered.**
   That capability set is the lost mode's by construction and no repeater with a key advertises
   it. It covers an `IDENTITY` lost to a retry, and it is what makes check 1a cheap.
4. **A key answered wins over every other piece of evidence.** The state is what the radio *has*;
   a radio that handed over a key is known, whatever its `caps` said. Held as a property.
5. **`identity_reads` still counts answers, including an all-zero key**, as before this task: the
   counters a person has learned to read did not change meaning.
6. **The classifier lives in `core/link/`,** not in `components/agent/`: the update's health
   check is in `core/` and `core/` cannot name `agent/`. It is the protocol's answers being read,
   which is where it belongs.
7. **The simulator models the whole lost mode**, including `TIME_SET`/`TIME_GET` answering
   `ERR_UNSUPPORTED`. Without that the health-check test would have proved only half of what it
   claims — the clock check is what used to spend the rollback.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opened the unit's ports: the first unit is on Thomas's bench (this pile's README), and
a radio in the lost mode has to be *made* — one word written over `0xED000` over SWD, which is
the repeater repository's own bench line and needs its `"identity": "disposable"`. The two lines
are in `docs/bench.md`, in the order the rig is brought up:

1. **`docs/bench.md` §9, p10-03, *A radio that refuses its identity costs no rollback*.** With a
   golden image and a candidate: `rak update` reaches *proving* and then `rak update: the image is
   fine, the node is not: …`; `image bad` does not name the candidate, `image list` shows the same
   golden image, `rak update status` ends `node-fault`, and `rak hello` says the **candidate's**
   version because the flash was not undone. Then `rak cli erase`, `rak cli reboot`, and the same
   `rak update` again must reach *done*. Write down whether `rak hello` says `caps 0x21`: that is
   what makes check 1a cost no frame on a healthy radio.
2. **`docs/bench.md` §17, p10-03, *A radio that refuses, told apart from one awaiting its first
   install*.** `agent id`'s words and evidence, that it does **not** say *the radio has no
   identity yet*, `agent heartbeat` carrying `"radioIdentityLost":1` and no `companionPublicKey`,
   and then the thing the counter exists for: `radioIdentityLost 1` in the **Counters** row of
   the unit's exit-node page after a wake. Then the way out as a person, and the new key confirmed
   and the repeater registered again (`docs/flashing.md` §3a's *What then*).

Both need the repeater at `v0.1.2` or later, which the unit's radio is since 8 November.
