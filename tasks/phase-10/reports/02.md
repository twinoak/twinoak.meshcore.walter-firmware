# p10-02 — What a wake did

Two readings of the same console went wrong on 7 and 8 November 2026, and both times the words were
this firmware's.

**`wake` gave each wake's length and nothing else.** Three wakes of about 40 s and four of 6 s were
read first as wakes that had skipped the modem and then as a failed connection. `lte` said both
readings were wrong: seven attempts, no failures. The journal keeps only wakes with news, so a
quiet wake left nothing but a number of seconds and a reader had to guess what filled them.

**The journal said *the backend was reached* about wakes that sent nothing.** The flag was set for
every exchange that did not *fail*, and the agent's loop answers *nothing to do* both when it
talked and had nothing waiting and when it never opened its mouth — a hold, no uplink, nothing
configured, a halt on a 401. So the wakes of 8 November that sent nothing to meshmaster.dk claimed
they had reached it.

One cause under both: **`interfaces::Exchanged` has three values and they answer two questions at
once.** This task gives the seam the second half of the answer, makes the journal's one claim an
answered heartbeat and nothing else, and puts three letters beside each wake of `wake`'s list.

## Acceptance checklist

- [x] **The seam tells answered, sent nothing and failed apart; the journal says *reached* only for
  an answered heartbeat (tests).**

  **A reason beside `kNothing`, not a fourth `Exchanged`** — the choice deliverable 1 asks for, and
  the reasoning is in `interfaces/backend.h` beside the enum. One value more could not carry *why*,
  and the why is the point: a hold, no uplink, nothing configured and a 401 are four different
  things to do about it. And `Exchanged`'s three values are what the wake cycle's rails are written
  against — S-7's one update step, the budget, the sleep a wait lengthens, `agent wake`'s own
  return: every one of them turns on `kFailed` and nothing else — so widening the enum would have
  moved code that has nothing to do with this question.

  So `interfaces::Reached`, a field of `BackendAnswer` beside `did`, `carried`, `outside_ms` and
  `sleep_at_least_s`, which are the other things in that structure that say *what happened* rather
  than *what is wanted*:

  | | |
  |---|---|
  | `kUnsaid` = 0 | the implementation did not say, and **nothing may be claimed** |
  | `kAnswered` | a heartbeat went out and the backend answered it |
  | `kWait` | nothing was sent: a wait is still owed (`sleep_at_least_s` is what is left of it) |
  | `kNoUplink`, `kNotConfigured`, `kHalted` | nothing was sent, for the loop's own three other reasons |
  | `kElse` | nothing was sent, for a reason this seam has no word for |

  **kUnsaid is the zero and that is the rail.** `out` is zeroed by the caller, so an implementation
  that says nothing is never credited with having reached anybody — the journal's *reached* is a
  claim about a server, and a claim nobody made is false. A test holds it
  (`test_what_a_wake_did.cpp`, *kUnsaid is the zero, so a backend that says nothing is never
  credited*), and it is why the two values are not a `bool answered` and a reason that could
  disagree with it: one field cannot drift from itself.

  **One reading, in one place.** `interfaces::wasAnswered(how, answer)` is the whole of it —
  `how != kFailed && answer.reached == kAnswered` — and the journal's flag, the record line's
  clause, `wake`'s `S`, `cycle`'s line and every test come off that one function. The flag is
  renamed `kWakeFlagAnswered` for what it now means.

  **Every caller reads it the same way**, and two of them changed:
  - `WakeCycle::stepBackend` keeps `reached`, `owed_s` and `jobs_ran` on the pass, and **drops
    whatever a failed exchange left in `out`**: *the exchange failed* is the step's own outcome and
    the whole of the news, so a failure claims nothing either way.
  - `AgentBackend::wake()`'s bool was `how != kFailed || report_.why[0] == 0`, which is true for
    every reachable state of a begun loop — the one thing it is ever asked (the console prints *the
    loop did not run* on a false) is whether the loop ran, so it now reads the library's own answer
    to that, `report_.why[0] == 0`. The guard at the top of the function already answers the one
    case the old expression could have been false for.
  - the null backend answers `kNotConfigured`: a build running it must never read *the backend was
    reached* in its journal, because nothing ever was.

  **The agent's half, where the reason is named.** `mm::AgentLoop::wake` decides *whether* anything
  was sent and says so in one of five static sentences; what it cannot give is a code, because
  `why` is prose. So `AgentBackend::reachedThisWake()` reads *whether* off the library (`why[0] ==
  0`, never guessed at) and names the reason by walking the library's own chain in the library's own
  order, over the same state the library read: halted, then not configured, then the uplink, then
  the hold. **Every branch is walked through the real library** in
  `host_test/agent/test_what_a_wake_did.cpp`, including the precedence (a halt and a down uplink
  both true, and the answer is `kHalted`), so an upstream sync that reordered that chain fails this
  repository's tests rather than mislabelling a wake. See *Decisions* for why the chain is walked
  twice rather than the sentence compared.

  **The tests deliverable 4 asks for**, four cases through whole wakes on phase 3's bench, with the
  journal and both console commands read for each — `host_test/core/cycle/test_what_a_wake_did.cpp`
  (10 cases) and `host_test/agent/test_what_a_wake_did.cpp` (8 cases, through the real
  `mm::AgentLoop` and the scripted meshmaster.dk):

  | the wake | the seam | the journal | `wake` |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **held back by a wait** | `kNothing`, `kWait`, 30 s owed | an entry, **no** *reached*, *nothing sent: a wait still owed, 30 s* | — |
  | **answered, with a job** | `kAsked`, `kAnswered`, `jobs_ran` 1 (bench 2) | *the backend was reached*, no *nothing sent* | `MSJ` |
  | **a heartbeat that failed** | `kFailed`, `kUnsaid` | no *reached* and **no** *nothing sent* either | `M--` |
  | **no uplink at all** | step 6 failed, step 8 skipped, `kUnsaid` | — | `---` |
  | **never reached sleep** | — | — | `???` |

  and, on the agent's side: answered with a job and answered with nothing waiting (both
  `kAnswered` — *nothing to do* is not silence, which is the distinction the whole task is about), a
  503 (`kFailed`, `kUnsaid`), a wait carried across a sleep (`kWait`, with the seconds), an uplink
  held down (`kNoUplink`), a 401 and the wake after it (`kHalted`, twice, the second with the
  uplink down too), a unit with no `server.url` (`kNotConfigured`, and the server never heard
  from), and `agent wake`'s own line for an answered wake and a halted one.

- [x] **`wake` says per wake what it did; docs updated; `tools/verify.ps1` green.**

  **Three letters and a legend**, in the retained record beside each wake's reason and duration:

  ```
  wakes counted: 1204
    M the uplink came up, S the server answered, J a job ran; '-' not, '?' not recorded
     1. timer     MSJ - this wake
     2. timer     MS-; awake 41 s
     3. timer     M--; awake 6 s
     4. attention ???; it never reached sleep (a reset, a crash or the bound)
  ```

  `kDidModem` is written at step 6 when the uplink came up, `kDidAnswered` and `kDidJobs` at step 8
  — **each the moment it becomes true**, not at the end — so a wake ended by a reset, a crash or the
  bound still leaves what it had managed, which is the wake a reader most wants to know about.
  `kDidSaid` is the fourth bit and is why there are four: it is set at step 10, so `---` (*it ran to
  its end and did none of the three*) is told from `???` (*nobody wrote it down*), and a zero byte
  — an entry from 0.1.2, or a wake that never reached step 10 — reads as the second.

  **The record's size did not change for them.** `WakeEntry`'s `lasted_s` went from 32 bits to 16
  and the two bytes it gave up are `did` and a spare: a wake is bounded at five minutes and `stay`
  holds one open for twelve hours at the most, so 18 h is a ceiling no wake reaches (and
  `endingInSleep` clamps rather than letting it wrap). The retained record stays at 100 bytes, and
  it did not have to grow: those four bytes were a little-endian `u32` whose top two are 0 for
  every wake shorter than 18 h, so **an entry written by a build of 0.1.3 before this task reads
  back with the right length and `did` == 0** — which is *not recorded*, and honest. 0.1.2's
  96-byte record is a different size and is already refused, as p10-01 documented.

  Legible on a 100-column terminal: the legend is 85 characters, the longest list line is about 80,
  and both the new test and the existing one in `test_console_sleep.cpp` hold every line of `wake`
  to 100.

  assertions are all of the difference), `agent_link_tests` **28** and `agent_backend_tests` **19**,
  both unmoved. `ctest` is 8 of 8, and the second host configuration (`WALTER_AGENT=0`, the wake

  **The cost on the board**, both builds at `0.0.0-dev` in the same tree and the same ccache

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**The journal entry grew from 32 bytes to 37, and the decoder reads both.** The task does not say
where the reason should live and the payload had no spare bit: `flags` is eight flags, `asks` is a
set of six and `carried` an `Ask`. Growing it costs **nothing** — a journal slot is 64 bytes
whatever the payload holds (`core/update/journal.h`, `kPayloadMax` is 48) — but it would have cost
the entries already on the unit's flash, which `decode()` would have refused for being short. So
`kWakePayloadBytesV1 = 32` is what every build up to 0.1.2 wrote, `decode()` accepts it, and it
answers *it did not say* and 0 for the two fields it never held. A test holds both lengths, and
that a reason byte which is no `Reached` is refused rather than cast.

**The record line gained a clause the task did not ask for.** Deliverable 2 is the journal and
deliverable 3 is `wake`; the one compact line the port prints at *every* wake is neither, and it is
what a person watching a unit actually reads. It ends `, nothing sent: <reason>` when a wake sent
nothing and the exchange said why — and prints nothing for *answered* (the ordinary wake) or *it
did not say* (a step that never ran, which has nothing to explain). `docs/cycle.md` documents it
with the rest.

**`BackendAnswer` gained a second field, `jobs_ran`.** `J` is the third letter deliverable 3 asks
for and nothing of "a job ran" crossed the seam: `did` is the closed set's bits, `asks` is what was
wanted, and `Exchanged::kAsked` is true for a wake that only posted an observation batch. It is a
saturating count of the backend's own work, 0 for an implementation that has no jobs (this
repository's), and **nothing decides on it** — it is a letter for a person, which the comment beside
it says.

**The spec says nothing about any of this and does not disagree.** `spec/SYSTEM.md` §8 settles what
a unit says to meshmaster.dk, not what its own console says about a wake; `interfaces/backend.h` is
this firmware's own seam and names no protocol, and nothing in `Reached` is a field, a status or a
word of anybody's wire format. Nothing under `spec/` was edited and
`python spec/tools/specsync.py verify spec` is green in `tools/verify.ps1`.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **A wait owed on a timer wake is news; the other silences are not.** The decision deliverable 2
  asks for, with the arithmetic it asks for. Since p10-01 a sleep takes its own length off a wait
  and step 10 lengthens the sleep to cover one the seam carried out, so a wake that comes round *on
  its timer* and still owes means a sleep did not cover its hold — which is exactly the failure of
  the night of 8 November and must leave a trace. **What a night of them costs:** in practice one
  entry per failed server call (a hold under ninety per cent of the interval is spent whole by one
  sleep; a longer one costs a second wake and is then spent too), and at the very worst one entry a
  wake — **48 a night at the quarter-hour interval**, so a night of nothing but these would still
  take three weeks to wrap the 1024-entry log. Against that, the other four reasons are left out
  each for its own reason: *not configured* is **every wake of an unprovisioned unit for ever**,
  which is the log wrapped in ten days and the thing task p3-04's whole arithmetic exists to
  prevent; *no uplink* is already news through step 6's failure; *halted on a 401* raises the
  Walter's own attention, which the record carries and `status` says in a sentence; and a wake on
  the radio's line inside a hold is expected — p10-01 claims no part of such a sleep on purpose, so
  owing the wait is the rule working. A test holds all three halves of this: the entry for a timer
  wake, no entry for three *not configured* wakes in a row, and no entry for a line wake owing the
  same wait.
- **The library's chain is walked a second time rather than its sentence compared.** `report_.why`
  is a `const char*` to a literal inside the library's own translation unit: the only way to turn it
  into a code from outside is `strcmp` against the five sentences, which would break silently on a
  reworded sentence and is not a thing to hang the journal's words on. Walking the same state in the
  same order is a duplication of the *ordering* and nothing else — the facts themselves
  (`report_.halted`, `server->configured()`, `known.uplink.up`, `report_.sleepAtLeastS`) are each
  read from the one place that owns them — and it is the kind of duplication a test can hold, which
  is what `test_what_a_wake_did.cpp`'s six library-side cases do. Report p10-01 made the opposite
  call for `answersLocallyFor` (one expression, two callers) because there the two predicates were
  both ours; here one of them is upstream's and is prose.
- **`lasted_s` became sixteen bits instead of the record growing.** The alternatives were worse. A
  12-byte `WakeEntry` would put the record at 140 bytes, over `kRetainedRecordMax` (128), and
  raising that constant costs 256 bytes of RTC SLOW memory across all eight slots, of which
  `interfaces/retained.h` records there were 176 free when p3-05 began. A parallel `did[10]` array
  would split the ring the console reads as one thing. And sixteen bits is not a compromise: the
  bound on a wake is five minutes, the longest `stay` is twelve hours, and the clamp means the one
  way sixteen bits could lie — a wrap — cannot happen. That the old top bytes are provably 0 and so
  read back as *not recorded* is luck, but it is checked luck: the comment says why, and it is why
  this shape was chosen over a fifth and sixth byte of the record.
- **`kDidSaid` is set at step 10 and not at the first of the three.** A wake that got its uplink
  but was then reset must read `M??`… and it does not: it reads `M--`, because `kDidModem` implies
  `kDidSaid`. The alternative — a *said* bit only at step 10 — would have made such a wake read
  `???` and thrown away the one letter it had earned. So the rule is: any bit written implies the
  record was kept, and step 10 writes the bit for a wake that earned none. The case a reader has to
  be able to trust is `???` meaning *nothing was written down*, and that holds either way.
- **`wake`'s legend is printed every time, not only when a letter is unusual.** One line of 85
  characters above a list of up to ten, and the alternative is a letter a person has to look up in a
  document they do not have open at 3 a.m. The three readings this task exists because of were
  misreadings, not missing data.
- **`cycle`'s and `agent wake`'s lines name the reason in brackets** — *nothing to do (a wait still
  owed)* — rather than growing a line of their own. `agent wake` already prints the library's own
  sentence through `printReport`; the bracket is what the *wake cycle* was told, and the two
  together are the hand-over across the seam, which is the thing a bench session is reading those
  two commands to see.
- **`ScriptedBackend` writes `*out` for every answer but a failure**, where it used to write it only
  for `kAsked`. The seam's contract comment said the same thing and was already untrue — the agent
  has written `did`, `outside_ms` and `sleep_at_least_s` on a `kNothing` since p4-05 — so the
  comment is now what the code does: the asks are written for `kAsked`, and the fields that say
  *what happened* whenever the exchange did not fail. The bench's default answer leaves `reached`
  at `kUnsaid`, which keeps every test that does not script it honest.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board, attach to a network or queue a job on meshmaster.dk. Everything
below is on the unit at Thomas's bench and the same lines are in `docs/bench.md` **§20, *What a wake
did***, in the order the rig is brought up. **None of it needs a night of its own** — §19's walk
produces every case — and none of it changes what the unit does, only what it says it did, which is
why the simulated consoles can stand in for most of it and why the two starred items are the ones
that cannot.

1. ★ **`wake`'s letters, read against `lte` and the server's own page.** After a few wakes on the
   battery: the legend, then `MS-` for a healthy wake with nothing queued, `MSJ` for one that ran a
   job, `M--` for one whose heartbeat failed. The two readings that went wrong on 7 November were
   exactly *did the modem come up* and *did the server answer*, so this is the item that closes
   them. **No line of `wake` past 100 columns.**
2. ★ **A wake that never reached sleep reads `???`.** The wake the cable or the reset took has no
   letters and says *it never reached sleep*. A `???` beside a wake that *did* reach sleep would
   mean step 10 is not writing the bits down, and a `---` on the wake that was interrupted would
   mean they are being written too early.
3. ★ **The journal says *reached* only when the server answered.** After a wake that heartbeated:
   *the backend was reached*. After one that sent nothing: none of that, and *nothing sent: …* with
   the reason — `nothing sent: a wait still owed, N s` on a timer wake being §19's symptom made
   visible. And the one that catches the news rule going wrong: a unit before `server.url` is set
   says *nothing sent: not configured* in its record line at every wake and writes **no** journal
   entry for it.
4. **`agent wake` names which of the three it was**, in brackets after the seam's word. With a
   deliberately wrong `server.token`, two wakes in a row read *halted on a 401* and *the loop did not
   run* — and no part of either token appears in any line of it.
5. **The record line of a unit that sends nothing** ends `, nothing sent: <reason>`, and an answered
   wake's line has no such clause. The cheapest of these, and the line a person watching the port
   actually reads.
