# p19-01 — A WAIT is waited out

Since Walter 0.1.11 the scanner flash of every NoiseScope session on the unit at Bellevue has
failed at its first try — eight sessions of eight, *scan: swd wait-timeout … nothing written*, at
`0x036000` after 32 to 33 s four times and at three other places and moments. Phase 18's 05 caught
it and carried six of seven, at the cost of half a minute, a second scan and, once, a session
refused.

**What this task did.** The debug port asked a WAIT ten times back to back — a few milliseconds —
and gave up. It still does that first, and then it **waits the operation out**: the same request
again with the line idle between tries, for as long as the longest flash operation an application
can start may take. The figure is the Product Specification's with a margin, not mine. And the unit
now **says what it waited** — how long, where, and how many transfers — in the debug port's own
record, in the flasher's counters, in a line of the journal's story, at the console, in `rak update
status` and in the unit's report. The second try is untouched.

`tools\verify.ps1` **green in 562 s**; `core_tests` **1 550 cases and 842 692 assertions**, all
passing (1 538 + 12 new). The image is **867 115 bytes against main's 865 619** in the same tree,
**+1 496**; rail R22's tightest big allocation has 5 964 bytes to spare against main's 6 060, and
the heap the main task's stack comes out of has the same 15 644 bytes in both.

## The bound, and where it comes from

Read out of the nRF52840 Product Specification **v1.11** (`4413_417 v1.11`, 2024‑10‑01), §4.3.10.1
*Flash programming*, with pypdf's layout mode so the columns survive:

```
Symbol        Description                            Min.      Typ.      Max.   Units
nWRITE        Number of times a 32-bit word can be written before erase    2
nENDURANCE    Erase cycles per page                  10000
tWRITE        Time to write one 32-bit word                                41¹    µs
tERASEPAGE    Time to erase one page                                       85¹    ms
tERASEALL     Time to erase all flash                                      169¹   ms
  ¹ Applies when HFXO is used. Timing varies according to HFINT accuracy when HFINT is used.
```

**ERASEALL, 169 ms, is the longest flash operation an application can start** (`NVMC.ERASEALL`;
`ERASEPAGE` is 85 ms and a word write 41 µs). The footnote sends the reader to the internal
oscillator, and §5.4.4.1 *64 MHz internal oscillator (HFINT)* gives `fTOL_HFINT` "Frequency
tolerance" a **maximum of ±8 %** (typical ±1.5 %), which makes 169 ms at worst **184 ms**.

- **`core::swd::kWaitBoundMs = 500`** — 184 ms with a margin of **2.7**, which is room for one
  operation that began just before the access and the next one the application has queued behind
  it. The margin is mine and is said here and in the header.
- **`core::swd::kWaitPauses = kWaitBoundMs / kIdlePauseMs = 50`** — so the loop is bounded by a
  constant as well as by the clock. The clock is what decides (`delayMs` keeps its promise by
  overshooting) and the clock is what is reported, so the unit says what it really waited: the
  tests measure **508 ms** for a WAIT that never clears, 500 ms of pauses and 8 ms of the 51
  transfers themselves.
- **`core::swd::kWaitBudgetMs = 30000`** — what *all* the WAITs of one connection may cost
  together. Why it exists is under *Decisions* and its arithmetic is below.

The pause between two tries is `swd::kIdlePauseMs`, 10 ms, the one this firmware already uses
between SWD stretches: the idle task of the core runs, so the **task watchdog is fed**. A wait-out
therefore feeds the watchdog *more* often than the page read it interrupts, not less, and cannot be
what makes it fire (task p8-01's finding is the other way round).

## Deliverable 2: every caller, and by how much at most

Every user of the debug port is reached by this, not only the scan, and the answer to "how much at
most" is **one number for all of them**, which is what the budget is for: a connection may spend at
most `kWaitBudgetMs`, 30 s, waiting WAITs out, and **`connect()` is what starts a fresh budget**.
From the code, `connect()` is called in exactly two places — `nrf52.cpp:134` (`Nrf52::open()`) and
`console.cpp:1716` (`rak swd dpidr`) — so:

| Caller | Transfers that can now wait | At most, per call |
|---|---|---|
| `flasher::Flasher::flash` / `verify` / `flashTest` / `startAgain` | `openChip()` opens the port **once** (`Nrf52::open()` → one `connect()`), and everything else of the call is inside that one session: the SoftDevice read, the scan, the halt, the settings page, the pages, the read-back | **30 s**, once a flash |
| `update::RadioUpdate` (an apply, a session, `rak update`, `rak role …`) | the flashes it makes, each its own session | **30 s a flash** — a pass may make two (a first try that wrote nothing, then the whole flash), each inside its own window below |
| `supervisor::Supervisor` (the golden-image rung) | one flash | **30 s** |
| `nrf52::ProvisionDoor` (`rak provision`, S‑5a) | `reopen()` reconnects after every reset it makes (`provision_door.cpp:266`), and each reconnect is a **fresh** budget | 30 s per connection; a typed command with a person watching, and no bound of a wake is on it |
| the console's `rak swd id` / `mem` / `nrf …` / `flash-test` / `provision check` | one session each | **30 s**, and a person waiting |
| `swd::Dp::connect()` itself | the `SELECT` write (one, two after a FAULT). `DPIDR`, `CTRL/STAT` and `ABORT` never WAIT (ADIv5 B4.2.3, B4.2.4), so the power-up's hundred polls are untouched | inside the same 30 s |

**And nothing of a pass, an apply or a session is passed** (the arithmetic of
`tasks/phase-18/reports/05.md`, §9's table). Each flash window bounds its own flash, so the 30 s is
checked against each window separately:

| Window | Now | What it is set from | Room | With 30 s |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| `kFlashWroteNothingMs` | 180 s | the scan, the only long way out before the first write: 106 pages of the 424 kB image of 23 November at ~0.9 s a page ≈ **95 s** | 85 s | **125 s** — inside |
| `kFlashWindowMs` | 960 s | the unit's own journal: **891 s** (14 min 51 s, 14 Nov) | 69 s | **921 s** — inside |
| `kOnePassMs` | 1 385 s | arithmetic over the two above | — | **unchanged** |
| `kWholeRunMs` (an apply) | 2 770 s | two passes | — | **unchanged** |
| `kSessionBoundMs` | 4 045 s | two passes and the ceiling | — | **unchanged** |
| `kBackendBoundMs` | 4 105 s | the session and a minute | — | **unchanged** |
| `kRaisedBoundMax` | 70 min | the session with room beside it | — | **unchanged** |

**No bound was moved**, so no sentence of the Product Specification is owed for one, and the static
assertions of `core/cycle/wake_cycle.h` and rails R19 and R22 are green and unedited.

Two things make this arithmetic honest rather than hopeful:

1. **Every transfer that reaches the wait-out at all is one that would have ended the flash before
   this task.** Ten WAITs back to back was `kWaitTimeout`, and `kWaitTimeout` in the scan is the
   failed flash of 23 November. So a flash that goes through costs **exactly** what it cost: the
   back-to-back tries are unchanged and nothing new is spent on the ordinary path. The test *a
   flash that met nothing: the counters are nought* is that, and the 1 538 cases that were green
   before this task are all still green with every wait counter nought.
2. **Nothing outside a flash can stop one.** A flash is one blocking call inside one `step()` of
   the update machine, so `cycle::WakeGuard::tick()` cannot run during it and the guard's reset
   only arrives after the flash returns. That is why the per-transfer bound alone would not have
   been enough, and why there is a budget (see *Decisions*).

**After the halt** (the second half of deliverable 2) nothing but this firmware's own flash
operations can hold the bus: the application is stopped, and its erases and writes are polled on
`READY` (rows 8 and 13), which is a register and does not stall. The DRW accesses that make up
those operations *can* still be answered WAIT — `tWRITE` is 41 µs against a 432 µs transfer at
125 kHz, so in practice they never outlast even the ten back-to-back tries (the existing test *a
slow write stalls the bus* at 1 000 µs a word still ends inside them, unedited) — and where one
did, it is now waited out instead of ending the flash. The test *the same operation after the
halt* holds that, on the bootloader's settings page, which is the one page a flash touches only
after the core is stopped.

## Deliverable 3: what the unit says

```
  3. seq 46: flashing-candidate: the debug port waited a flash operation of the radio's out
     - scan: waited 91 ms at 0x026000, once
```

That is the journal's line (`journal`, `rak update status`, and the unit's report through its
`journal` section), printed from a `kFlashWaited` entry's thirty-two bits. At the console a flash's
own summary carries the long form:

```
  the debug port waited 91 ms out at 0x00026000, while reading the pages the image covers; 1 transfer waited
```

and a first try that gave up says `scan: waited 508 ms at 0x026000, once` — **how long it waited
before it gave up**, which is the one thing the eight failed sessions could not say. A flash that
waited for nothing writes **no** line and no entry: the test *an ordinary flash writes no such
line* holds that, and it is why the journal's ring is not paying for this.

Where the figures live:

- `swd::Dp::waits()` — `transfers`, `longest_ms`, `total_ms`, `longest_at`, `longest_at_known`,
  `gave_up`; cleared by `connect()`, so it is **one debug session's** (see *Decisions*);
- `flasher::Counters` — `waits`, `wait_longest_ms`, `wait_longest_at`,
  `wait_longest_at_known`, `wait_longest_step`, copied off the port as each step passes so that the
  **step** is the one that was running at the time (`Flasher::noteWaits`);
- `update::FlashWait` and `kFlashWaited` — the journal's entry: the step in bits [5:0] (the same
  place as `FlashFailure`'s), the milliseconds in [17:6], the page in [25:18] (zero meaning *no
  address was known* — a WAIT on a register rather than a memory access; page 0 is the master boot
  record and no flash of this firmware ever reads it), and how many transfers waited in [31:26],
  saturating at 63 with *or more*.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **The Product Specification puts those times in the `Max.` column, and this repository's older
   comments call them typical.** Read out of `nRF52840_PS_v1.11.pdf` in pypdf's layout mode, the
   §4.3.10.1 header has `Min.` at column 108, `Typ.` at 121 and `Max.` at 135; `10000`
   (nENDURANCE) sits at 108 and `41`, `85`, `169` and nWRITE's `2` all sit at **135**. The HTML
   rendition read in task p1-04 was taken to mean *typical, with no maximum given*, and five
   comments say so (`core/nrf52/nrf52.h`, `core/nrf52/provision_door.h`,
   `core/update/radio_update.h`, `host_test/sim/nrf52_chip.h`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §5). **No
   number changes either way** — the new bound is 2.7 × 184 ms, which is 2.7 × the larger reading —
   and I did not sweep those five sentences: a wording change across five files for no behaviour is
   scope this task did not ask for. It is a finding for `FOLLOWUPS.md`, the reviewer's to tag.
2. **A second constant, `kWaitBudgetMs`, beside the one the task asked for.** Deliverable 1 asks
   for "a named constant in milliseconds" and that is `kWaitBoundMs`. On its own it cannot support
   the acceptance criterion *no bound of a pass, an apply or a session is passed*: a flash is one
   blocking call inside one `step()`, the wake guard cannot tick during it, and a chip answering
   WAIT to everything would have made a sixteen-minute flash into hours — 217 000 transfers of the
   424 kB scan at half a second each. So the whole of one connection's waiting is bounded too, at
   30 s, which fits inside both flash windows with room to spare and makes "at most" one number for
   every caller. It is still a loop with a constant bound and still the task's shape.
3. **The record is `Dp::waits()` and not `Dp::Counters`.** Deliverable 3 says "the debug port's
   counters gain …". The counters are the port's whole life and are never cleared, and a `Flasher`
   — and so its `Dp` — is built once at boot and lives for every flash the unit ever makes, so a
   high-water mark kept there would report the *first* flash's longest wait for ever. The figures
   therefore live in a `Waits` record beside the counters, cleared by `connect()` — which is one
   debug session, which is one flash — and `Dp::Counters` keeps its meaning and its two existing
   WAIT counters unchanged (`wait_retries` now counts the paused tries as well, which is what it
   has always counted).
4. **The address comes from the access port, through one new setter.** The task allowed "one
   argument more: yours, said in the report". `Dp::noteAddress()` is called by
   `core/swd/mem_ap.cpp` before each `DRW` access and by nothing else; the debug port itself only
   knows registers. A WAIT on a DP or control-port register has no address, says so
   (`longest_at_known` false, page 0 in the entry) and the line leaves it out. No ESP-IDF include
   was added to `core/swd`, and the pause comes through the `interfaces::Clock` the port already
   reaches through its wire.
5. **The line is not in what a session or an apply posts to the server.** My first cut wrote it as a
   note of the job's own journal as well, and that broke `test_a_refused_session_reports.cpp`: the
   fixture `host_test/fixtures/contracts-ahead/NoiseScopeSessionResultRefused.json` is the record of
   what session 5 of 24 November actually sent, held byte for byte, and two more lines in it would
   be a falsified record and a changed contract with meshmaster.dk's task 260. The acceptance
   criterion holds 06's tests unedited, so the line stays exactly where deliverable 3 puts it: the
   journal, the console, `rak update status` and the unit's **report** — whose `journal` section is
   the console command, so the line does reach meshmaster.dk, in a report rather than in a job's
   note.
6. **`docs/bench.md`'s *What to bring back* cannot name the two constants.** Rail B reads that
   section for constant names and failed when the prose there named `kWaitBoundMs` and
   `kWaitBudgetMs` while the source does not mark them for the bench (they are the specification's
   with a margin, not a measurement). The sentence names the header instead.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **500 ms, from tERASEALL and not from tERASEPAGE.** The task says "the longest flash operation an
  application can start". An application can start `NVMC.ERASEALL`, and that is 169 ms where a page
  erase is 85 ms; §5.4.4.1's ±8 % makes it 184 ms. The margin of 2.7 is for one operation that
  began just before the access plus the next one queued behind it. A tighter figure would have been
  a guess about what the radio's file system does in a row; a looser one costs a failed first try
  longer before it gives up.
- **30 s for the budget**, because it is the largest round number inside *both* windows: 180 s −
  95 s = 85 s of room before the first write, and 960 s − 891 s = 69 s of room for a whole flash.
  It is also ~350 page erases of the radio's own, where the scan is expected to meet a handful.
- **The back-to-back tries stay in front of the wait-out.** A WAIT that clears in a transfer or two
  must not cost 10 ms, and this is what makes "a flash that goes through costs exactly what it
  cost" true rather than nearly true.
- **The pause is `swd::kIdlePauseMs`**, the 10 ms this firmware already pauses between SWD
  stretches, rather than a new constant: it is where the idle task gets its slot and the task
  watchdog is fed, and reusing it keeps one number for "how long this firmware lets go of the wire".
- **The entry's layout** (step, ms, page, count in 6/12/8/6 bits) with **page 0 meaning "no address
  was known"**, which costs nothing: page 0 is the master boot record and no flash of this firmware
  reads it. No layout bit is needed because the reason itself is new.
- **Two phrasings, and which goes where.** `flashWaitNote` is the task's own example (`scan: waited
  86 ms at 0x036000, once`, under 70 characters) and is what the journal's line prints — so the
  story reads as the task wrote it; `flashWaitLine` is the longer phrase for a flash's own summary
  at the console. Both are tested for length against the journal note's bound.
- **The step is credited by the flasher**, in `noteWaits(Step)` called as each step passes
  (`openChip` → `kConnect`, the SoftDevice read, `report()` for every page of the scan, the pages,
  the first page and the read-back, the halt, each settings write, and `finish()` → `kReset`), plus
  at `failed()` so that every failure credits its own step. A step the sweep misses reads `none` and
  the line simply says less; it cannot be wrong about the milliseconds.
