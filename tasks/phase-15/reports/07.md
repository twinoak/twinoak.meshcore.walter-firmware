# p15-07 — what a call that worked looks like

The unit has said twice what the modem did in an observations call that failed. It had nothing to
be read against: the config, the heartbeat, the claim and a job's result are answered at every wake
by the same server through the same code, and nothing was kept of them. So `agent calls` and the
report's `calls` section now print **two** blocks — the newest call that failed, out of the store,
and the latest call of this boot that the server **answered**, out of RAM:

```
  what the modem said in it (heartbeat), on socket 2: 1174 bytes sent, 3 receives answered "nothing buffered yet"
    it ended as: the connection closed before the body was complete
    +100 ms: a ring: the modem announced 878 bytes
    +500 ms: the other end closed
    +30150 ms (asked at +150 ms): a receive failed after 30000 ms: nothing answered, the library gave up on the command
  what the modem said in the latest call that was answered (claim), on socket 1: 304 bytes sent, 0 receives answered "nothing buffered yet"
    it ended as: ok
    +20 ms (asked at +0 ms): a receive returned 64 bytes
    +20 ms: a ring: the modem announced 512 bytes
    +40 ms (asked at +20 ms): a receive returned 64 bytes
    +40 ms: the other end closed
    +60 ms (asked at +40 ms): a receive returned 64 bytes
    ...
    +200 ms (asked at +180 ms): a receive returned 35 bytes
    the answer: http 200, framed by a length, 472 bytes of body read, and it said Connection: close
    the other end's close was handed over before the last byte was read
```

**Both blocks are a host test's**, printed by the firmware's own console code; no byte of either was
read off a unit. The review of 01 to 03 asked for that to be said wherever a block is printed, and
it is said in `docs/bench.md` §13 over both of its examples now.

## What it is made of

**Two facts more in every record, and one of them is five bits.** A receive that returned bytes now
carries **how long its command took**, so when it was asked is `at_ms - took_ms` and a read can be
placed against the rings in both blocks — the arithmetic the task's own Goal does by hand for wakes
254 and 289. It goes in the moment's sixth byte, which for a `kData` moment was unused, through the
same **plus-one bias** the `+CME ERROR` number has: a 0 there means *not written down*, which is
what every record kept by an earlier firmware has in it, and not *it came back in the same
millisecond*. The record's stored size, its key and its version byte are untouched (79 bytes, 158
characters of hex, version 1), which is what makes the last acceptance criterion free.

**And what the answer said of itself**, for a call something answered: the status, whether it was
framed by a length, by chunks or by the close, how many bytes of body were read, whether it said
`Connection: close`, and whether the other end's close was handed over before or after the last
byte. Those four are **outside the stored bytes** — plain members of `CallRecord` — because they are
what a call that *worked* is read by and a call that worked is not kept over a sleep at all. A
record read back out of the store therefore says nothing about an answer, which is also exactly
what it said before this task.

**`Connection: close` is read by the response parser**, and by nothing else. `core/net/http1.h`
gained one rule and one bool: the `close` connection option among however many the header lists
(RFC 7230 §6.1, case-insensitive, whitespace dropped). It changes nothing the parser does — this
firmware keeps no connection alive and sends `Connection: close` itself — and a value longer than
the parser's own 32-byte buffer for a header it reads reads as *it did not say close* rather than a
guess. Eleven values and their answers are a test.

**The second record is RAM and no value of the store.** `walter_agent/call_log.h`'s `answered()`
copies the live record out before the next call overwrites it, and `ModemUplink` calls it in the
`out.ok` branch that is the exact complement of `noteFailedCall`'s. Keeping it would be a write to
flash at every good wake; it does not need keeping, because a report is made in the middle of a
wake, straight after the calls that worked.

**Where the pointer to it lives is a measurement.** It began in `CallLog`, which is where it reads
naturally, and that took the object from 96 to 100 bytes of static storage, moved `_heap_start`
eight bytes and the tightest big allocation from main's 916 bytes of margin to **908** — below
main's, which the task does not allow. So `core::net::CallRecord` carries it: that object is taken
from the heap in `app_main` and the list is static storage the main task's stack has to be found
around afterwards (rail R22). The second commit of this branch is that move and the figures.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**`spec/` is untouched** (`specsync.py verify spec`: ok) and says nothing about this. S-12 is the one
line that bears on it and is the rail the privacy tests hold.

**The pointer to the answered record is a member of `core::net::CallRecord` and not of
`agent::CallLog`.** The task's deliverable 4 asks that the margin not be below main's, and in
`CallLog` it was: four bytes of `.bss` there became eight after alignment and took the tightest big
allocation from 916 to 908. `CallLog` is the object that asks for the copy and would be the natural
owner; the record is the object that is on the heap. Both the header and `main/main.cpp` say so where
a later reader will wonder.

**A download range's answered call is not kept.** The list keeps a *failed* download range because a
fetch stops at the range that did not come back, so the last range is the one that failed and says
something. For a fetch that worked the record would say only that the **last range** of a download
arrived, and it would displace the handful of calls a report is read for. So `noteAnsweredCall` is
the uplink's seven calls and `firmware_jobs.cpp` is unchanged. Said where the function is.

**A record written by this firmware for a call that worked, read back out of the store, would say
nothing of its answer.** That cannot happen today — only a failed call is stored — but it is a
property of the format worth naming: the four facts of deliverable 3 are deliberately outside the
stored bytes, which is what makes the version byte and every older record safe.

## Decisions taken without asking

**A receive's duration is one byte, capped at 254 ms, with the plus-one bias.** The record is 79
bytes against the store's 80 and a moment is six, so there was no room to widen one; the sixth byte
of a `kData` moment was unused. A successful `AT+SQNSRECV` is a UART round trip of tens of
milliseconds, and the one that takes thirty seconds is the one that **fails** — whose duration is a
whole `u16` in `value` and always was. 254 and not 255 because the bias has to leave 0 meaning *not
written down*; a figure on the cap prints *(asked at +N ms or earlier)* so nobody reads it as exact,
and a test scripts a 400 ms command to see it.

**The clause goes after the moment and not after the sentence**: `+30310 ms (asked at +310 ms): a
receive failed after 30000 ms: …`. The reason for a failure belongs at the end of its line, and the
two times belong together where the eye already is. It is the one change to a line task p15-02
wrote, and the four tests that compared those lines were updated with it.

**A call that worked is one that came back 2xx with its body whole** — `out.ok` of
`agent::ModemUplink::call`, which is the exact complement of what goes in the list of failures (a
2xx whose body was cut is in the list and is therefore not the answered one). One definition, one
place, and the two can never both be the same call.

**The answer's lines are printed only when a status came back.** `answerWas()` is called for every
call, and `answer_known_` is `response.status != 0` — so a call nothing answered prints no answer
lines even while its record is live in RAM. Without that a failed call read from RAM and the same
call read from the store would print differently.

**Where the close fell is worked out from the moments, not stored.** The close's index against the
last receive that returned bytes is the whole of it, and both are already in the record. Four
answers: never handed over before the call ended, handed over with no receive having returned a
byte, before the last byte, after it. **The first is the common case for a call that worked** and is
not a fault: recording stops the moment the call ends, and a body framed by a Content-Length ends on
its last byte, so the `+SQNSH` behind it often arrives after the record was closed. The failed call
is the other way about — its close is handed over long before it gives up — and that asymmetry is
itself one of the differences `docs/bench.md` asks to be read. The line says *was not handed over
before the call ended* so that nobody reads it as a missing close.

**The bench's socket learned one more thing and the scripted server nothing.** `fake_tls_socket.cpp`
reads its clock either side of the one command that returns bytes, as the board does, and passes the
duration. The server already answers `Connection: close` on every reply and has `chunked` and
`no_length` knobs, so the three framings needed no new script.

**`hasToken`'s parameter is named `header` and not what RFC 7230 calls it.** The no-allocation check
over `components/core/net/` forbids the names of heap containers as words, and `std::list` is one of
them. The comment beside it says why, so that nobody renames it back.

**`CallRecord::drop()` exists so that `CallLog::forget()` means both blocks.** The list's `forget()`
erases two keys; the answered record is in no key, and *everything forgotten* has to include it.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port. The line below is `docs/bench.md` §13's, under the two items tasks p15-02
and p15-05 left, and it needs no cable: both blocks reach a person through the report.

1. **A call that worked, beside the one that did not** (§13, the new item). On a **release** build,
   after half an hour of the unit's own wakes: *Fetch now* on the Diagnostics tab and read the
   report's `calls` section. Expected: the kept record of the newest failed call and, under it,
   `what the modem said in the latest call that was answered (…)` with the ring, each receive's two
   times, `the answer: http 200, framed by …` and the line saying where the close fell. **Write both
   blocks down together** — the second one is gone at the next sleep — and read the six differences
   the item lists, each with what it would mean. Two readings decide something on their own:
   - **when the unanswered receive was sent against the rings, in the failed block, against when a
     good call's reads are sent.** If a good call never sends a read in the same millisecond as a
     ring and the failed one does, the race is real; if it does it too, the race is not the cause.
     This is the reading the two records of 17 November could not settle, and why
     `tasks/phase-15/FOLLOWUPS.md` withdrew *ask only after a ring*.
   - **the two blocks being the same in every line but the one unanswered receive.** Then the fault
     is in that one command and the trace build's item 1 is all that is left to read.
   If the second block says *no call has been answered in this boot* in a report, nothing of that
   wake was answered at all, which is a finding of its own about the uplink and not about this task.

Everything else of this task is host tests and a build. What the thirty seconds **are** is unchanged
by it, is `tasks/phase-15/FOLLOWUPS.md`'s first item, and is still not known; what changed is that
the question can now be asked from a report.
