# p15-02 — what the modem said

The call that fails at nearly every wake now writes down its own receiving side, as it runs, in a
release image, and keeps it over a sleep. On the unit in service that reads:

```
agent calls: 8 of the last 8 that did not come back 2xx (401 and 429 included, ...)
  1. observations, wake 1214, at 1792022430: nothing answered, the modem stopped answering for this socket before the body was complete
  ...
  what the modem said in it (observations), on socket 6: 1158 bytes sent, 612 receives answered "nothing buffered yet"
    it ended as: the modem stopped answering for this socket before the body was complete
    +102 ms: a ring: the modem announced 878 bytes
    +514 ms: the other end closed
    +30150 ms: a receive failed after 30000 ms: nothing answered, the library gave up on the command
```

Four of the five things `docs/bench.md` §13's ★ line asks the trace build for are in those lines,
and the ring's length — the 876-against-878 reading of `tasks/phase-10/FOLLOWUPS.md` — is there
without a cable. **It cures nothing**, which is the task's own last paragraph.

## What it is made of

**One record, 79 bytes, in the form it is stored in.** `core/net/call_record.h`: a seven-byte head
(which call, how it ended, the socket id, the bytes sent, the count of *nothing buffered yet*, the
count of the moments there was no room for) and a dozen six-byte moments (when, what, a value, the
modem library's own state and its `+CME ERROR` number). 158 characters of hex, two under what one
value of the settings store takes. The *not yets* are counted and not listed because a stalled call
makes hundreds of them.

**The events are taken where they happen.** `components/modem/walter_tls.cpp`: `socketEvent` for
the ring (with `data_len`, the `<n>` of `+SQNSRING: <id>,<n>`, which this firmware had never read)
and the close; `receive` for the three kinds of ask, with the clock read either side of
`socketReceive` so that the thirty seconds is measured and not inferred. `open` writes the socket
id. `core/net/http_over_tls.cpp` writes the request going out, its bytes and how the call ended.
**Two tasks write the record** — the library's event task brings the ring and the close — so a
moment is appended by taking an index with one `fetch_add`, which gives each writer six bytes of
its own, and the two counts are atomic. No lock, as `core/net/socket_rotation.h` has none.

**Kept under a key of its own**, `agent_call_rx`, written by `CallLog::record` in the same moment
the list of failed calls moves — so the two values can never name different calls, and the record
beside entry 1 is always entry 1's. Phase 13's follow-up asked for the extra bytes to go in *a
second key, not a longer entry*, and they did: an entry of `agent_calls` is still nine bytes.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**`spec/` is untouched** (`specsync.py verify spec`: ok) and says nothing about this: what a unit
writes down about its own modem is this repository's business. S-12 is the one line that bears on
it, and it is the rail the two privacy tests hold.

**The record is not a member of the transport; it is taken from the heap at boot.** The task's
deliverable 5 says the static storage is paid for and the margin is not below main's. Held as a
member of `HttpOverTls`, the record was **116 bytes of `.bss`**, `_heap_start` moved 120 bytes and
the tightest big allocation fell from main's 988 to 868. There was nowhere to take 116 bytes from:
phase 13's follow-up says to make room in `agent::kKeptScratchBytes`, and task p14-01 had already
taken that to the bottom of its allocator size class (19 456, against a largest kept image of
19 328 — anything from 18 433 up takes the same block, so the next step down would drop adverts a
sleep is keeping). So the record is `new`-ed in `app_main`, as the library's runner and that same
scratch are, and the three objects that write or read it hold a pointer. That is 12 bytes of
`.bss` instead of 116 — and, taken **in** `app_main` rather than in a static initialiser, it is
not an allocation `tools/heap_at_start.py`'s recorded programme would have to know about.

**The task says "a dozen events and a count of the rest", and a moment is six bytes, which made
the whole record 79.** 158 characters of hex against the store's 160: the head had to come down to
seven bytes for the dozen to fit, which is why which-call and how-it-ended share a byte (as an
entry of `agent_calls` already does), the socket id shares one with the count of the moments there
was no room for, and the `+CME ERROR` number is kept **plus one** — `+CME ERROR: 0` is a real code
(equipment failure), so a 0 there has to mean *the modem answered no code at all*.

## Decisions taken without asking

**Where the twelve bytes came back from, and the eight on top.** `core::net::SocketRotation` kept
its two socket ids and its slot index in a word each: `int id_[2]` and `int slot_`, with the
`live_` bool and three bytes of padding after them — sixteen bytes for an id that is 1 to 6 (the
modem has six sockets; the library's `WALTER_MODEM_MAX_SOCKETS` is 6) and a slot that is 0, 1 or
−1. They are `std::uint8_t id_[2]`, `std::int8_t slot_` and the bool now: **twelve bytes**, with
the comment that says why beside them, and every accessor still answers `int` so no caller
changed. `useIds` takes an id outside a byte as *no id* rather than truncating it into one that
names another socket. That is twelve back against twelve spent, and the alignment of what follows
puts the result eight bytes **below** main's `_heap_start`. The counters of that object were left
alone: they are read by `lte` and by seven tests, and the twelve bytes were enough.

**One counter for both values of the store, not two.** `CallLogCounters::writes` counts the list
and the record together — they are written in the same moment and always will be — because a
counter of its own was two more bytes of static storage. `agent calls`'s own line reads *N written
to the store* and now covers both.

**The record is written the moment the call failed, not at the end of the wake.** `agent_calls`
itself is written once a wake, from a dirty flag. The record cannot be: there is one record in RAM
and the next call overwrites it, so keeping it until the end of the wake would need a second copy —
a hundred bytes of storage this board has not got, which is the whole of the paragraph above. So
`CallLog::record()` writes it there and then. The cost is one value written per failed call instead
of per wake; a wake whose calls all come back 2xx writes nothing here, which a test holds. A power
cut between that write and the list's own leaves a record whose entry is not in the list yet — the
record says which call it was itself, so it still reads.

**A call that never got its request out is still a record**, with no moment in it and *on no
socket*. Without that, the record kept beside the newest entry of `agent_calls` could be an
**older** call's whenever the newest failure was a connection that never opened, and a person
would read the two as one call. A test holds it (`a call that never got its request out is still
the record`).

**A download range's record is the last range of the fetch.** A fetch is many requests and there is
one record; a fetch stops at the range that did not come back, so the last range is the one that
failed. Said in `firmware_jobs.cpp` where that entry is recorded.

**Every capped figure says so.** A time past 65 535 ms after the request went out prints `+65535 ms
or more`, and so do the bytes sent and the counts — rather than a number that reads as exact. A
test holds all three.

**The bench's socket learned the modem's four answers.** The loop that polls the modem and reads
`+CME ERROR: 4` as *not yet* is the board's (`components/modem/walter_tls.cpp`), and
`host_test/sim/fake_tls_socket.h` is its **peer**, not its caller, so no host test can run it. The
fake therefore says the same four things to the same record — `not_yet_receives`, `receive_hangs_ms`
with the library's state and a CME number, `ring_after_ms`/`ring_bytes`, `close_after_ms` — and the
hang **overruns the deadline**, as the library's blocking call does, which is why a 30-second stall
under a 15-second piece deadline comes out as `kSocketFailed` and not as a timeout. Every existing
test of that file is untouched and green: all four knobs are off by default and `close_after_ms` of
0 is the behaviour every other test has.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port. The two lines below are `docs/bench.md` §13's, in the order the rig is
brought up, and both are **new in this task**:

1. **The record read beside the trace build's lines** (§13, inside the ★ item). With the trace
   build on a board that has a radio on its link: `trace modem on`, `lte up`, `agent obs`,
   `agent wake`, `trace modem off`, and then `agent calls`. Write both down together. The record
   answers four of the five readings; the trace answers the fifth (**which** AT command went
   unanswered, and what a `+SQNSRECV` declared against its ring). A disagreement between them — a
   ring in the trace the record has not got, or the other way about — is a finding about
   `socketEvent` in `components/modem/walter_tls.cpp`.
2. **The record on a unit with no cable** (§13, the new item under the ★ line). On a **release**
   build, after half an hour of the unit's own wakes: *Fetch now* on the Diagnostics tab and read
   the report's `calls` section — the block above, with a socket id, the bytes sent, the count of
   *nothing buffered yet*, and the moments. Then `reboot`, or pull the power, and fetch again: the
   same block to the character. *nothing kept yet* after a wake whose call failed means the write
   is not happening.

Everything else of this task is host tests and a build. What the record is **for** — the cause of
the lost answer — is `tasks/phase-15/FOLLOWUPS.md`'s first item and is not known.
