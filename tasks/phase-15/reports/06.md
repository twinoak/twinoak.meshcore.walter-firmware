# p15-06 — What a wake was doing when it was ended

In the night of 17 November 2026 two wakes of this unit were ended by the five-minute bound on a
wake — 01:29 and 02:05 UTC, each holding meshmaster.dk's hourly `status` job of its own repeater
(job 32763). Both sent their heartbeat, claimed the job, and were never heard again in that wake.
The next boot read the bound's restart as a watchdog, slept half an hour instead of a quarter, and
**said nothing at all about what those five minutes had been spent on**: the journal had *woke by
watchdog*, `wake`'s list of ten had *it never reached sleep*, and the log, the claimed job and the
letters of the wake were in RAM, which the reset took.

This task is the one line that says it. **24 bytes of RTC memory, written as the wake goes**
(`core/cycle/wake_progress.h`): the step of the ten, the call that was out or the claimed job that
was being run and how many requests of the radio it had made, whether the modem or the link was
being waited on, and the two seconds that make *for how long*. The boot after reads it at step 1,
journals one line, and prints it under that wake's entry in the list of ten and in the report's
`wakes` section; a fifth total beside the four of `agent_wakes` counts the wakes that did not
reach sleep, and goes out with every heartbeat.

**Nothing of the copied library changed.** The four hooks are all this board's own: the step is
`WakeCycle::begin(Step)`, the call is `ModemUplink::call()` — the one place every call to the
server passes — the link's wait is `LinkMesh::await()`, and the job and its requests are
`mm::JobRunner::openJob()` and `LinkMeshCounters::requests`, read by `AgentBackend::noteProgress()`
from **the pump**, the plain function the library runs at every idle wait, which this board now
wraps with one of its own.

## Where the 24 bytes came from

RTC SLOW memory is 8 192 bytes. **6 996 of them are the pinned modem component's own
`.rtc.data`** — `_socketCtxSetRTC`, `_pdpCtxSetRTC`, `blueCherryRTC` and two more, read off the
build's linker map — and the eight retained slots were 1 120, which left **36**. A ninth slot of
`kRetainedRecordMax` costs 140, so there was no room for one.

So `interfaces/retained.h` gives each slot its own room (`kRetainedSlotBytes`), which is the note
task p3-04 left in `platform/retained.cpp` and the thing three earlier tasks worked around by
putting two unrelated records in one slot. The layout is written so that **nothing a unit in
service holds is lost**:

| slot | room | |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 | 128 | unchanged, and so are their offsets and the span the checksum covers: those records read back |
| 7 `kWakeCycle` | **116** | `core::cycle::kRecordBytes` exactly. The last of the eight, so giving its twelve unused bytes back moves no other slot |
| 8 `kWakeProgress` | **24** | the new record |

The cost is **one wake of the telemetry ring's ten readings and its refusals**, at the first boot
of the release that carries this: the checksum covers the slot's whole room and that room is now
smaller, so the stored record is refused once. That is the rule working rather than failing, and
it is the price every change of a retained record's shape has paid (p10-01, p13-02, p14-06).
`docs/bench.md` §25 has the item that reads it on the board, and the one that reads what is *not*
lost — the boot log, the supervisor's day, the uplink's backoff, the TLS trust table and the wake
log.

RTC SLOW is now **8 180 of 8 192**: twelve bytes free. The next thing that has to cross a sleep
has no room, and `interfaces/retained.h` is where a reader of this finds that out.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The heap margin is 80 bytes below main's**, and the acceptance criterion asks that it not be.
  The figures, from `tools/heap_at_start.py` over both builds in this tree:

  | | main | this task |
  |---|---|---|
  | the verdict's spare in the heap the main task's stack came from (R22's own figure, asked for at least 8 192) | 12 376 | 12 320 |
  | the tightest big allocation's room in the heap it landed in (the figure the script says moves first) | 996 | 916 |
  | DIRAM | 304 607 | 304 687 |

  **R22 is green** and is green by 4 128 bytes. What the 80 bytes are is the deliverable itself:
  a reading of the wake before (24 bytes of the record, latched because the slot is overwritten a
  moment later), the job in hand's type and the second it began at, the clock the seconds are
  counted from, and the five pointers the hooks are reached through. There is no version of this
  task that adds no static storage — the record has to be read by the boot after, and the thing
  that reads it is a member of a static object.

  **What was done about it.** The first build of this task was 136 bytes below main's. 56 of them
  came back by keeping **no copy of this wake's record in RAM at all**: the slot is where the line
  lives, and every change is a load, a change and a store of 24 bytes of RTC memory
  (`WakeProgress` is 60 bytes instead of 104; the second commit of this branch is that change
  alone, with the figures). The remaining 80 would cost the job's name after a call made inside a
  job — the one thing the night of 17 November actually needed said — so they were not taken.
  **If the reviewer wants the margin held to main's**, the two candidates are `job_what_` and
  `job_since_s_` (20 bytes, and a `status` job that posts a result halfway through would then read
  as the job with its name rewritten by the next pump) and `LinkMeshCounters`' twenty-five 32-bit
  saturating counters (100 bytes, and another task's data).

- **No whole-wake test of a wake ended at step 6, because the firmware cannot produce one.**
  The criterion asks for *a scripted wake that hangs before the uplink is up*. Every step of 3 to
  7 is bounded well inside the bound on a wake (`kStateBoundMs` 15 s, `kUplinkBoundMs` 150 s,
  `kClockBoundMs` 10 s against `kWakeBoundMs` 300 s), so a wake cannot be *ended by the bound*
  inside one of them: only steps 2, 3, 8 and 9 may raise the bound and outlast it. What is tested
  instead is the two halves of the same claim — that `begin(Step)` writes the step as it goes (*a
  wake ended before its uplink was up names that step* writes step 6 through the same call the
  wake cycle makes and reads it back at the next boot), and that a wake ended with nothing finer
  than the step names the step.

- **A record with no step in it is no word.** `endedMidway()` is *written down, did not reach
  sleep, **and had reached a step***. A boot with a USB host on the port never runs the wake cycle
  — a person is here and the console is the wake — and a bench session whose cable comes out is
  ended by the bound by design (`core/cycle/wake_guard.h`, and the 23 October and 7 November
  readings in it). Those would otherwise write a journal line and move the fifth total at every
  bench session, saying *the wake before did not reach sleep, and nothing had begun*, which is not
  a fault and is already in the boot log. The task does not say this either way; it is the
  smallest decision that keeps the total meaning what the night of 17 November made it mean.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **One text field for the call's name and the job's type, and not a code.** The record holds 16
  bytes of name (`mm::kMaxJobTypeLen` exactly), and the two writers put in the words they already
  print: `agent::callKindName()` for a call and the claim's own `type` for a job. A code would
  have been smaller and would have put a table of the library's thirteen job types in `core/`,
  where it would drift at the next bump of a copied library.
- **The call's name is `agent calls`'s, articles and all**, so the phrase reads *the call out:
  `a result`* rather than *the `a result` call*. One vocabulary for one thing was worth the
  slightly odd clause; a second, bare set of names is a thing that can disagree with itself.
- **The phrase goes on a line of its own**, under the list entry and under the journal line.
  Every line this firmware prints fits a 100-column terminal and the two together do not.
- **`agent_wakes` is version 2 and version 1 is still read.** A unit that has run 0.1.8 has four
  totals under that key and they are months of counting; the alternative was to refuse the value
  and start all five at 0. The older shape is rewritten in the new one at the first save, whether
  or not a total moved.
- **The pump the library is given is `AgentBackend`'s, not the caller's.** It is the one hook the
  copied library offers inside a job (`IPlatform::delayMs` and the runner's own waits call it),
  and it is where `openJob()` can be read. A file-static owner, as `WalterPlatform`'s log sink
  already does it, and the backend's destructor takes it away.
- **`progress_job_` is the job id's low 32 bits.** What it is asked is *is this the same job*; the
  whole `long long` would cost four more bytes of static storage for nothing.
- **The journal line is written at step 1 of the first pass of the boot, once.** `cycle now` at
  the console is a second pass of the same boot and the fault is the same fault.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board, so none of this has been on silicon. `docs/bench.md` **§25** is
the walk, in the order the rig is brought up, and these are its items:

- **A wake held past its bound, and what the next boot says.** `stay off`, `agent wake`, and the
  **cable pulled while the exchange runs**; a minute of USB grace later the bound ends the wake by
  reset. Then: `wake`'s list says *it never reached sleep* with `it was in: …` under it and **no
  line past 100 columns**; `journal` says *the wake before did not reach sleep (watchdog)* and the
  same phrase; `agent heartbeat` says `"wakesMidway":1`. `no step written down` would be a
  failure — it would mean the step is not being written as the wake goes.
- **A bench session that ends tidily leaves no word**, and a boot that is simply reset by hand
  leaves none either: no `it was in:` line and no journal entry.
- **`wakesMidway` on the exit node's page**, after *Fetch now* — and the four of task p13-02 still
  where they were, which is the stored value being read in either shape.
- **A power cut leaves the word behind and keeps the total**: RTC memory goes, the settings store
  does not.
- **The telemetry ring starts again, once**, at the first boot of the release — and nothing else
  of RTC memory does.

And the thing this task exists for, which only the unit in service can show: **the next wake that
is ended by its bound.** `tasks/phase-15/FOLLOWUPS.md`'s item about the two wakes of 17 November
is still open, because why they hung is not known and this task does not find out — it is how the
next one is read.
