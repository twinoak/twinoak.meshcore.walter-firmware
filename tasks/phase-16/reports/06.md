# p16-06 — A wake outlasts what it took on

In the night of 17 November 2026 this unit took the hourly `status` of its own repeater at
01:29:53 UTC and was next heard at 01:34:56 as a new boot; the same at 02:05:24 and 02:10:28. The
job was leased four minutes, was taken at both wakes and answered at neither, each reset was read
as a watchdog and followed by half an hour of sleep, and nothing was measured for over two hours.

The arithmetic that allowed it was two numbers that did not know about each other: step 8 promised
the exchange `kBackendBoundMs` — the longest machine it may run inside itself and a minute — and
the bound on the wake it was in was `kWakeBoundMs`, five minutes. So an exchange in a wake of the
cycle's own was promised the better part of an hour and reset at five minutes.

**They are worked out in one place now** (`core/cycle/wake_room.h`), and the rule is four lines:

| | |
|---|---|
| the exchange's deadline | its own number, or the most a wake may ever be raised to if that is sooner. A **ceiling**: the library's wake budget comes off it, so a result a long job made is posted rather than kept |
| every call | what is left of the **sooner** of that deadline and the bound as it stands — and the transport is given the same figure, so a call is bounded by the wake it is in. Too little is no call, by the rule a call already had |
| a claim | the bound raised to the end of the **latest lease** among the jobs it took and the calls after them: a result for each job and the observations, each at the modem's command timeout. Never past `kRaisedBoundMax`. A wake that claims nothing keeps its five minutes |
| a lease the ceiling cannot cover | **cut**, to where the room for those calls begins, so that the copied library ends the job `timeout` by its own rule and the result is posted |

Nothing of the copied library changed, and no path was added to anything that erases a chip.

seventy seconds** — which is what makes the night's shape reachable at all: a four-minute job fits
inside five minutes only when everything else in the wake takes under one.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **A job the radio never answers ends `unreachable`, not `timeout`.** The task's goal says *a job
  the radio never answers must end as a `timeout` the server is told of*, and three acceptance
  criteria use the word. The copied library does not work that way: a `status` whose repeater
  answers nothing gets two login tries (`agent/LoginSequence.h`) and is over in about half a
  minute with `Outcome::Unreachable` — there is no path on which it keeps trying until its lease.
  `timeout` is the library's word for a job the **lease** ended, in exactly two places
  (`JobRunner::runJob`'s first line, and `JobRunner::spend`'s *the budget's next slot is past the
  lease*), and both are tested here: a job that cannot be finished inside the lease it was given,
  and a lease the ceiling cut. **The deliverable holds either way** — the server is told, and the
  wake goes to sleep — and the firmware's behaviour is the right one: a job that cannot be reached
  should not spend four minutes of a battery finding that out twice. `docs/cycle.md` says it under
  *What this does not do*.
- **No whole wake in which a job outruns the cut lease.** The criterion for a sixty-minute lease
  asks that *a job still running then is reported `timeout` before the bound*. The library
  **prices** every job against the wake's budget before it runs it and declines one that does not
  fit (`AgentLoop::wakeJobs`; the result then reads *about 3 526 s left of this wake and
  config.read needs about 4 352 s*), so a job that would outrun the cut lease is never begun — the
  cut lease is the second of two belts and the library's own pricing is the first. What is tested
  instead is the cut itself in a whole wake, the exact second it is cut to in
  `test_wake_room.cpp`, and the case where the cut lands at *now* — a wake with no room left hands
  a lease that has already run out, and the library reports `timeout` at no transaction. Worth
  knowing for a later task: the pricing uses the deadline, which is now a ceiling of nearly an
  hour, so it is the bound and not the price that governs a wake with little room left.
- **The task's own figures are a pile out of date, and the report keeps the newer ones.** The task
  says the exchange is given *thirty-nine minutes*; since task p16-04 `kBackendBoundMs` is the
  scanner session's bound and a minute, **59 min 40 s**, and `kRaisedBoundMax` is an hour. Nothing
  of the task's argument changes: the gap between the exchange's promise and the bound on the wake
  is larger than it was when the task was written.
- **The bench's pump did not tick the bound on the wake, and now does.** Until this task
  `host_test/agent/agent_half.h`'s pump only polled the link, so no wake on that bench could be
  ended by its bound from inside an exchange — which is the one thing the night of 17 November did.
  A test of a long job could not tell a wake that reached sleep from one the bound would have
  reset. The pump now does what `main.cpp`'s does (`agentPump` → `Console::pump` →
  `DutyCycle::tick`). **Every test that existed before this change is still green with it**, which
  is itself the finding: no wake on this bench was overrunning its bound unnoticed.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The exchange's deadline is a ceiling, not the bound.** The alternative — give the exchange what
  the bound leaves — would have made the library's wake budget the bound's remainder, and a job
  that ran for ten minutes would then have had its result **kept** rather than posted
  (`agent/UplinkMeter.h`, `timeLeft()`), which is the failure task p14-04 exists to stop. So the
  deadline is the most this wake could ever be raised to, and the bound is what actually ends a
  call. One figure, two uses, worked out in `core/cycle/wake_room.h` and nowhere else.
- **A claim with no lease raises nothing, and the rail is what ends a wake its job outlasts.** A
  lease of 0 is what the library reads when the server sent none; there is no end to cover and no
  honest lease to invent, so such a wake keeps its five minutes and is reset if the job outlasts
  them — exactly as before this task. meshmaster.dk always leases
  (`docs/dev/unit-protocol.md` §3), and the one test that needs a job with no lease is the rail's.
- **A claim made before this firmware knew what day it was raises nothing either.** A lease is Unix
  seconds and a cut has to be made against a clock; with no clock there is nothing to compare. Step
  7 sets the clock before step 8, so this is the first wake of a unit that has never attached.
- **The lease is cut for every job of the claim, not only the latest.** The ceiling is the wake's
  and not one job's, so every lease is clamped to the same second. A lease that already ends
  earlier is untouched.
- **Four bytes of the journal's wake entry, and no byte of RTC memory.** The record says *the bound
  was raised to N s for 2 jobs* and *1 call(s) were not begun*; the entry's payload went from 37 to
  41 bytes, which costs nothing (a journal slot is 64 bytes whatever the payload holds) and reads
  back on an older entry as *nothing was said*. RTC memory has **twelve bytes free** since task
  p15-06 and the ten-wake list has no room for a field at all, so the wake that did it is what says
  it, and the boot after says only what that list already said.
- **`N s` is the bound itself and not how far past the claim it reaches.** The bound is counted from
  the boot and the wake record already carries it, so the phrase needs no field of its own — which
  is what let the two bytes of the seam become a question instead of a member.
- **The two bytes are asked of the seam (`Backend::roomTookOn()`), not carried in
  `BackendAnswer`.** That structure is a member of two objects that live for the whole boot, so two
  bytes in it are eight bytes of the heap the agent's kept scratch is carved out of. A virtual with
  a default, as `beat()` already is; the null backend answers *nothing was taken on*.
- **The guard's pointer and the uplink's three bytes were put where the padding already was.**
  `ModemUplink` derives from `mm::IUplink`, so its vptr shifts every offset by four: a pointer
  declared before `deadline_ms_` pushes that `std::uint64_t` past an eight-byte boundary and costs
  eight bytes of `.bss`, while the same pointer declared behind the other pointers lands in the
  four bytes the eight-byte alignment of `payloads_` already wasted. The three bytes of the record
  sit behind `radio_identity_lost_`. **`.bss` is main's to the byte**, and the two comments in the
  header say why the order matters so that the next task does not undo it.
- **A call with no room is news for the journal; a bound that was raised is not.** An ordinary claim
  of a leased job raises the bound at every wake that has a job, and seven hundred identical lines
  are not a page a person can read (task p3-04's rule). A call the wake had no room to begin is the
  one thing that says a wake ran out of room rather than out of work, and it costs at most one
  entry a wake.
- **`budgetIs()` on the scripted server.** The airtime budget is the server's to hand out and the
  heartbeat's reply already carries it; a test that needs a job of a few transactions to take the
  better part of an hour has no other way to say so. The unit's own 2-a-minute, 20 s gap is still
  the default, and every case here but one uses it.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board or attach to a network, so none of this has been on silicon — and
the thing this task exists for can only be shown by a unit in service: **the next wake that takes a
job on and keeps it.**

`docs/bench.md` **§17** has the one item, and it is the one item of that section that must *not* go
through `agent wake`, because that command raises the bound for the whole exchange and the bench
would never meet this:

- **A job the radio never answers, in a wake of the cycle's own.** A `status` waiting on the bench
  server, the radio's link pulled, `stay` off, nothing on the USB port, and a wake on the timer.
  What to hold: the job's page shows a **result** and not a lease left to lapse; `wake` after the
  next boot says the unit woke **by the timer** and not by a watchdog; `journal`'s wake entry
  carries `the bound was raised to N s for 1 job(s)` with N about the lease the server gave; and
  the sleep after it is the ordinary interval and not the half-hour a reset buys. A `watchdog` in
  the list, or thirty minutes of sleep, is the failure of 17 November happening again.

And two things to read on the unit in service once this is out:

- **`wake`'s bound line**, which until now printed five minutes whatever the bound really was. On a
  wake with a job it should read the raised figure, with `it was raised from the ordinary 300 s`
  under it.
- **whether any wake says *1 call(s) were not begun*.** That is the wake ending itself rather than
  being reset, and it is the line that says the arithmetic is being used.

`tasks/phase-15/FOLLOWUPS.md`'s item about *why* the two wakes of 17 November hung is still open:
this task is the arithmetic that makes such a wake survive, not the answer to what it was in.
