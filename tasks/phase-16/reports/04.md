# p16-04 — A session is a job

Task p16-03 made a session something a person starts at the console. A unit on a mast has no
console. `twinoak.noisescope.session` is the **sixth firmware job type**: the same machine, begun
by the server, with the three things a mast needs and a cable does not — the scanner's lines on
their way out as each step ends, the lease held across two flashes of a quarter of an hour, and a
result that outlives a restart.

Nothing of the session was reimplemented. The program comes out of the payload and is judged by
task p16-03's own `judge()`/`whyProgram()`, `start()` runs the whole check and opens the session in
the journal, and the job drives `step()` in slices inside the wake exactly as an apply drives its
machine. What is new is in `components/agent/`: `runSession`, one post of a scanner's lines
(`ModemUplink::postLines`, a call kind of its own), and the owed result.

## What was built

**The sixth type** (`components/agent/include/walter_agent/firmware_jobs.h`,
`firmware_jobs.cpp`): `kSessionType`, `runSession`, `refuseSession`, `postSomeLines`,
`SessionLines`, `postOwedSessionResult`, `readSessionJournal`, and three free functions so that a
test can hold them to the contract's own values — `writeSessionResult`, `readSessionPayload` and
`writeJournalLines` (which an apply's journal now goes through too, unchanged in shape).

**One call of our own** (`ModemUplink::postLines`, `CallKind::kLines`): `POST
/agent/v1/jobs/{id}/lines/{from}` through the same `call()` as the library's seven — the same token
path, the same wake deadline, the same 120-a-minute ceiling, the same `Call` record, the same line
in the failed-call list and the same name written down while the call is out (*scanner lines*). The
copied library is not changed.

**The owed result.** `JobEntry` gained a product (`2`, a session) and one of its four spare bytes
now carries **how many steps the payload had**, so that a result posted after a restart still has a
row for every step. `postOwedResult` branches on the product; `owedResultDue()` answers true for a
session the moment the session is not running, because there is no trial to wait for.

**`kBackendBoundMs` is the session's bound and a minute** — see *Deviations*.

**The words.** `docs/update.md`'s *A session as a job*, `docs/cycle.md` (the budget table's
backend row and the `Backend` page), `docs/console.md` (`agent jobs`' fourth line and the `owed`
line, and *scanner lines* among `agent calls`' kinds), `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §13's table,
`docs/flashing.md`'s figure, `docs/bench.md` §17's two items and its constants table, and one line
in `release-notes/unreleased/`.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **`kBackendBoundMs` went from 39 min 40 s to 59 min 40 s** (`core/cycle/wake_cycle.h`). Its own
  `static_assert` says *the exchange has to outlast the longest machine it may run inside it*, and
  since this task the longest is a session (`kSessionBoundMs`, 58 min 40 s) and not an apply. The
  number is not only the guard's: the library is given what is left of this deadline as its wake's
  budget, and **a result made after that budget has run out is kept for the next wake** — which on
  a job leased an hour from the claim is a job meshmaster.dk fails while the radio is perfectly
  well, and is the one thing task p14-04 exists to stop. A real session is two flashes of a quarter
  of an hour, so at the old number the result of every session on a board would have been late.
  It is still inside `kRaisedBoundMax` (an hour, which task p16-03 set), the guard's own cap, and a
  raised bound is still a bound. `docs/cycle.md`, `docs/flashing.md` and `docs/bench.md` say the new
  figure.
- **`three and 21` are the measurement lines, and a step hands over more than those.** The
  acceptance criterion reads *a session of two steps whose lines come to 3 and 21*. What a step
  hands over is every line the scanner sent while it ran, which is the command's own `ack` and its
  `done` as well — and the first step's also carries the greeting's three `ack`s and its `info`
  line, which is task p16-03's deliverable 2. So the test scripts 3 and 21 measurement lines and
  holds the result to **10 and 23**, with the arithmetic read off the posts themselves rather than
  hard-coded. `{from}` 0 and then 10, which is *the lines handed to posts so far*.
- **A six-step program cannot have six forty-character commands.** The contract bounds the payload
  at 384 bytes as sent, and six steps of forty characters are 422. The test for *a full journal*
  therefore uses the longest commands six steps can carry (thirty characters), and the result is
  far inside `kDataBytes`. A session's journal is bounded by its own stages — fourteen lines for
  six steps — and never comes near `kJournalMaxEntries`.
- **`journal` is refused with no word.** Task p16-03 added a ninth refusal for *the journal would
  not take the entry that opens a session*; the contract has eight words and that is not one of
  them. Rather than send the server a word its enumeration has no row for, the outcome is `refused`
  with **no `refusal` member** and the reason in the journal's `check` line — which is exactly what
  an apply does with a precondition the server has no word for (`writeApplyResult`,
  `tasks/phase-10/reports/03.md`).
- **A post of a scanner's lines is sent as `application/json`,** although the body is NDJSON. The
  one content type `core/net/client.h` is given for a POST comes from `ModemUplink::call()`, which
  every other call shares, and the contract says nothing about the header. Changing it for one call
  would be a guess about a server that is not written yet; the item is one for the comparison the
  follow-up already asks for when meshmaster.dk's task 236 lands.
- **A line longer than one body goes in a post of its own.** The contract bounds a body at 8 192
  bytes cut at a line's end. A line that is longer than that on its own cannot be cut at all;
  rather than split it (which would put half a line in front of the server) or drop it (which would
  lose a measurement silently), it is posted alone in the one body that may pass the bound. Nothing
  a scanner sends is that long — §9 bounds a line at 7 600 bytes — so it is a defensive branch, and
  the comment beside it says so.
- **The journal lines of an owed result all carry the session's opening epoch.** A journal entry is
  a sequence number, a kind and a payload (`core/update/journal.h`): the journal stamps no time of
  its own, so after a restart the only honest epoch this end has for each line is the one the
  session was opened at, which the entries carry. The lines of a session posted *as it ran* carry
  the moment each was written.
- **`lastEnded()` gained a product.** All three kinds of job entry share one journal kind, and a
  session's is the newest after one has run — so without the filter a unit that had run a session
  would stop telling `twinoak.fw.state` about its radio's last update. The default is the radio's,
  which is what its one caller asked for anyway.
- **The scripted NoiseScope moved** from `host_test/core/update/session_bench.h` to
  `host_test/sim/scripted_scanner.h`, unchanged but for one hook (`on_command`, so that a test can
  make the server go away in the middle of a step). The update bench includes it and task p16-03's
  tests are untouched.
- **The result of the no-uplink session is carried by the library's offline buffer**, not by the
  journal's owed entry. Both exist and both end at the server: the owed entry is for a **restart**
  (the result cannot be made at all, so it is made from the journal at a later wake), and a result
  that *was* made but could not be posted is the library's own to keep and flush, which is what
  `resultsKept` has always done. The test shows the second; the restart test shows the first.
- No disagreement was found between `spec/` and the task. `spec/SYSTEM.md` §9 still lists
  *NoiseScope as a job* under *later*: the phase's `FOLLOWUPS.md` already carries that item for
  Thomas, and it is the hardware repository's to change.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The session machine is the one the wake cycle has**, handed to the jobs through
  `Parts::session` from the same `main/` line, so that "an open session is ended, never begun
  again" and "one a wake" are true of a typed command and a job together. Rail R20 already named
  `components/agent/firmware_jobs.cpp` as the second door and holds it there.
- **A session is `update the radio`** in the closed set of asks, because that is what two passes of
  the radio's update machine are; S-7's one-update-step rule then counts it, which is the task's
  deliverable 3. There is no new ask: the seam's set is closed on purpose.
- **The program is a local of the job's call**, as the console's is: the machine keeps a pointer to
  the caller's program for the length of the run and six commands as `.bss` would be six commands
  the main task's stack has to be found around (rail R22). The same reasoning puts the sink — and
  with it every count the result reads — on that frame.
- **The lease's 1 800 is asked for on the machine's state and not on the session's stage**: before
  every `step()` whose inner machine is at `kFlashing` or `kRollingBack`, which is exactly the
  apply's own rule and covers both flashes and the rollback a scanner that never came up makes.
- **A session's three outcomes are carried in the entry's `outcome` byte** as `kCommitted`,
  `kRefused` and `kStuck`. The byte is read back by nothing but this object's log line — the
  result's word is written from the journal's session mark — and a fourth value in an enumeration
  the server owns would have been worse.
- **`stuck` is posted although the session stays open.** The journal still says open and the next
  wake tries the switch back again (task p16-03's rail 5), but the job is *one attempt* and the
  server is waiting: `stuck` with the journal it has is the honest answer, and the entry is settled
  so nothing posts twice.
- **`{from}` counts the lines handed to posts, answered or not.** The contract says *how many lines
  of this job the unit has handed to a post before this one*, and the server's own rule — a line it
  already holds is left alone, a gap is counted as lost — makes that the number that keeps the two
  ends in step without this end having to remember what was lost.
- **The `check` line's note is the three things the check reads**: the battery as this wake read
  it, *link up*, *clock set* — which is the contract example's own wording and is true of every
  session that got as far as being journalled.
- **`endWord()` and not an overload of `name()`.** A `name(core::update::StepEnd)` in `agent::`
  would be found by argument-dependent lookup beside `core::update::name` and neither would win;
  the sixth ending (`notRun`, a step the session ended before) is this end's anyway.
- **The fixtures are held to the task file.** Five shapes that do not exist on the server yet
  cannot be held to meshmaster.dk's bytes, and a transcription held to nothing is what
  `contracts-ahead/README.md` warns about — so the test reads
  `tasks/phase-16/04-a-session-is-a-job.md` and compares the five files with the five lines of its
  *The contract*, in order.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port, so **nothing below has been on a board**. Two items are in
`docs/bench.md` §17, beside the offer items of task p16-02:

- ⚠ **A session from a bench server.** A `twinoak.noisescope.session` of two steps queued and
  `agent wake`: about forty minutes with the radio off the mesh. What to bring back: **the `room`
  line verbatim** and **the wall-clock time of each of the two flashes** from the job's page (the
  simulated figures are nothing like a board's, and `kFlashWindowMs` is still a guess with one
  measurement behind it), the posts and their `{from}` read off `agent calls`, and — by eye — that
  `dump` and `journal` carry no byte of a scanner's line.
- ⚠ **The cable pulled while the radio is a scanner, with the result owed.** The next wake must
  put the repeater back by itself *and then post the job's result* — `restored`, `notRun` for every
  step. It costs a bench radio about twenty minutes of no repeater, and it is the whole of
  deliverable 9 on silicon.

Three things no test here can reach:

- **Whether meshmaster.dk takes these calls as this end makes them.** The server's half is its
  pile 4.42, task 236; until it is deployed the payload above has to be queued by hand and the
  `lines` route answered by something. The five fixtures are this end's reading of a contract
  agreed in words, and `tasks/phase-16/FOLLOWUPS.md` carries the item that compares them with the
  server's own files byte for byte and moves them into the copied tree.
- **What a real NoiseScope sends in a minute.** Every figure about the room — how often a step ends
  `full`, whether 8 192 bytes is the right room, how many posts a `scope` costs over LTE-M — comes
  from the scripted scanner here. The `room` line and the `lines` counts are in every result so
  that those numbers come from units rather than from this file.
- **A post over LTE-M of eight kilobytes.** The bodies here go through the fake TLS socket. What a
  real modem does with one — and whether the 15-second piece deadline of a real call is enough for
  it — is the first bench item's to show; a post that fails is counted as unanswered and costs the
  session nothing, which is the one thing that is designed rather than measured.
