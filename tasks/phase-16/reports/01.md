# p16-01 — The scanner is spoken to

`spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §9's other half. The link client could tell a scanner from a repeater and
keep the last three of its lines cut to 320 bytes; now it says one plain-text command line to it
and reads the `ack`, and whoever wants the lines that follow *whole* — a `sweep` of the band is
about 4 kB — lends the client the room to lay them down in. `rak say <words>` is a person's end of
it, with 8 192 bytes borrowed from the heap for the length of the command.

## Acceptance checklist

- [x] **`sweep` said, `{"t":"ack","cmd":"sweep","ok":true,"msg":"started"}` scripted: answered
  ok** — `Client::say()` sends the line and its `\n` in one write and pumps the port until an
  `ack` whose `cmd` is the line's first word arrives or `kProbeWaitMs` is up. Test *sweep said
  and acked ok: answered, with the scanner's own message*
  (`host_test/core/link/client/test_link_client_say.cpp`).
- [x] **An `ack` with `ok` false and a `msg`: answered not ok, with the `msg`** — `Said::ok` is
  false, `Said::fault` is `kNone` (it *was* answered) and `Said::msg` holds the scanner's own
  wording, copied. Test *an ack that is not ok: answered, and the reason is the scanner's*.
- [x] **An `ack` for another command and then silence: not answered** — a `stat` heartbeat and an
  `ack` for `stop` are scripted in answer to `sweep`; the call waits the whole of `kProbeWaitMs`
  and comes back `kLinkDown` with `text_unanswered` 1. Test *an ack for another command, then
  silence: not answered*.
- [x] **Said in frame mode and in unknown mode: no byte goes out, and the fault names the mode** —
  two new faults, `kFrameMode` (*the radio answers frames, not text: nothing was said*) and
  `kModeUnknown` (*what the radio is running is not known yet: say hello first*), both decided
  before the port is touched. Tests *nothing is said in frame mode…* and *nothing is said before
  anything has answered…*, each holding `radio.writes` and `radio.commands` unchanged.
- [x] **A lent buffer of 8 192 bytes and a scripted `sweep` line of 7 600 bytes arriving in reads
  of 64: the buffer holds that line whole, byte for byte** — the client reads the port 64 bytes at
  a time, so that line arrives in 119 reads. Test *a 7 600-byte sweep line, in reads of 64, is in
  the lent room byte for byte*: `memcmp` of 7 600 bytes, the `\n` after them, and the ring still
  holding its 320-byte cut of the same line.
- [x] **Two lines in one read, and one line over three reads: each whole, in order** — tests *two
  lines in one read, each whole and in order* and *one line over three reads, whole* (the second
  checks that half a line is not counted as a line between the reads).
- [x] **A `# help` line between two lines: not in the buffer** — test *a chatter line between two
  lines is not in the room*: two lines held, no `#` anywhere in the room.
- [x] **A line that would end past the buffer's end: the lending ends full, the lines before it
  are whole and nothing follows them** — test *a line that would end past the end of the room ends
  the lending as full*: `room.full`, one whole line before it, a third line after it not kept, and
  `text_full` 1. The client reserves the byte for the line ending, so a line is never laid down
  without one.
- [x] **A `{"t":"done","cmd":"sweep"…}` among the lines: found for `sweep`, not found for `hist`**
  — `TextRoom::hasDone(Text)` over the lines held. Test *a done for the command is found among the
  lines, and one for another is not*.
- [x] **With a buffer lent, `rak text`'s three lines and counts are what they are without one** —
  the same six lines are run through two benches, one lending and one not, and the three kept
  lines and `text_lines` / `text_dropped` / `text_truncated` are compared. Test *with a room lent,
  the three kept lines and the counts are what they are without one*.
- [x] **No text of a scanner's line is in the log, the journal or a report after `rak say`** —
  every byte the scanner sent leaves through `Console::printText()`, which writes to the port with
  the ring held shut (S-12), and the `ack`'s own message goes out the same way. Test *rak say: no
  byte of a scanner's line is in the log, the journal or a report*
  (`host_test/core/console/test_console_say.cpp`): a marker only this scanner would say is found
  in what the port printed and in neither `dump`, `journal` nor any of the sections of
  `twinoak.report`.
- [x] **The heap margin is not below main's. Rail R22 green** — R22 green: *FITS, with 12 272 bytes
  to spare* against its floor of 8 192. Against `main` built in the same tree the margin is 48
  bytes lower (12 320 → 12 272) and the tightest big allocation 48 bytes lower (916 → 868), from

- [x] **`tools\verify.ps1` green** — green locally, all eight steps. CI: see the pull request.

## What was built

**`core/link/client/text_line.{h,cpp}`** — the four fields of a scanner's NDJSON line this
firmware reads: `t`, `cmd`, `ok`, `msg`, and the first word of a command line. One pass over the
line tracking string state and nesting, so a key is only a key at the top level; it allocates
nothing and every answer is a view into the caller's line. It is **not a JSON parser** and the
header says so: a `sweep` line's arrays are forwarded whole and never looked into.

**`Client::say(Text)`** — the line and its `\n` in one write on the call's own stack, then the
port pumped for `kProbeWaitMs`. Only in text mode. The answer is a `Said`: the fault, `ok`, and
the `ack`'s `msg` copied into 96 bytes of the answer itself.

**`Client::lendText(TextRoom*, char*, size)` / `takeText()`** — the buffer *and the book-keeping
about it* are the caller's; the client keeps one pointer. Every line beginning with `{` is laid
down whole and `\n`-ended, chatter is not kept, a line that will not fit ends the lending as
*full* with nothing of it kept and nothing after it, and a line half-arrived when the radio
restarts is dropped. `rak text`'s ring, its watchers and its counters are untouched by any of it.

**Four counters** beside the text ones: `text_said`, `text_not_ok`, `text_unanswered`,
`text_full`.

**`interfaces::Scratch`** — room borrowed for the length of one command and handed back
(`take`/`give`/`largestBlock`). `platform::EspScratch` is `heap_caps_*` over
`MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL | MALLOC_CAP_8BIT`, the same heap `heap` reports and rail R22 is about;
`fakes::FakeScratch` is the host's and counts what is still out.

**`rak say <words>`** — the room taken, the line said, the `ack` printed, then five seconds or the
scanner's `done`, then every line printed whole. Printed and never logged.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The heap margin is 48 bytes below `main`'s**, which the acceptance criterion read literally
  forbids and deliverable 4 read literally allows ("Static storage does not grow by more than the
  few words that point at it"). What grew is 32 bytes of `.bss`: `TextRoom* room_`, `Awaited*
  awaited_` and the four counters deliverable 5 asks for. The room, its byte count, its line
  count and its *full* flag were members of the client in the first draft — 52 bytes — and were
  moved into the caller's own `TextRoom` for exactly this reason, which is also the shape the
  deliverable describes. I could find no way to add four counters and two pointers for nothing, so
  the criterion is ticked on R22 being green with 4 080 bytes above its floor and on the figures
  being written down here rather than on the margin being unchanged. `tools/heap_at_start.py`'s
  own warning is that 869 more bytes of `.data` or `.bss` would move the tightest allocation into
  another heap; 32 is 4 % of that headroom.
- **Five seconds does not cover a default `sweep` of the band.** Deliverable 6 says five seconds,
  and that is what `rak say` waits (ended early by the scanner's `done`). By the scanner's own
  document a default `sweep` is 281 points at 20 ms of dwell — about 5.6 s of measuring — so the
  `sweep` line may land just after the window and be shown cut by `rak text` instead. The bench
  item says so and gives the narrower range to use (`rak say sweep 869.0 869.8 25`); how long a
  *step* is given is task p16-03's, not a person's at a prompt. Nothing was changed to paper over
  it.
- No disagreement was found between `spec/` and the task. §9 is three sentences and the shapes
  come from the repeater firmware's `docs/noisescope.md` §5, which the task names.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **A new `interfaces::Scratch` rather than a method on `interfaces::Heap`.** That interface's own
  header says it is "Two numbers, reported and never decided on"; giving it an allocator would
  make it something else. A seam of its own is also where task p16-05 puts the PSRAM without
  touching a line of `core/`.
- **Two faults, `kFrameMode` and `kModeUnknown`, rather than one fault and a mode field.** The
  criterion asks that the fault name the mode, and `describe()` is where this console's refusals
  come from.
- **The lent room's book-keeping lives in the caller's `TextRoom`**, not in the client. It costs
  the client one pointer instead of eight words, and it is what deliverable 4 describes.
- **`rak say` takes the room before it says anything**, and when the heap has no 8 192-byte block
  it prints that, with the largest block there is, and sends nothing. The deliverable says it
  "says so when the heap has none" without saying when; starting a sweep whose lines nobody can
  hold seemed the worse of the two.
- **The room is lent after the `ack`, not before**, so that the ack is not printed twice — once as
  the verdict and once as a line. Nothing is lost by it on a board: the scanner answers a command
  with one line at once and measures afterwards. A caller that wants the ack in the room too — a
  step of task p16-03 — lends before it says the line, which the client allows and which the
  console's own comment points at.
- **The ack's `msg` is copied into the returned `Said`** (96 bytes, on the caller's stack) rather
  than being a view into the client. A view would be gone the moment the next line arrived, and
  storage for it in the client would be storage this firmware keeps. A longer message is cut and
  the answer says it was.
- **A line with a byte outside printable ASCII is refused** (`kEncode`): a newline of its own
  would be a second command nobody asked for.
- **`kSaidMax` is 160**, the console's own bound for a typed line, so what a person may type is
  what may be said. The parser holds `rak say` to the same number as `rak cli`.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port, so nothing below has been on a board. The items are in `docs/bench.md`
§9, *The scanner is spoken to*, after the role-switch item that leaves a scanner running:

- **`rak say sweep`** with the scanner running: `ack: ok - started`, then one `sweep` line printed
  **whole** with `"n":281` in it and no cut, then `{"t":"done","cmd":"sweep"…}`, which ends the
  wait before the five seconds. If the line lands after the window (281 points at 20 ms is about
  5.6 s), say it again or narrow it: `rak say sweep 869.0 869.8 25`.
- **`rak say nonsense`**: `ack: not ok` with the scanner's own message. **`rak say stop`**:
  `ack: ok`.
- **`rak role repeater`, then `rak say sweep`**: *the radio answers frames, not text: nothing was
  said*, and `rak status`'s link line shows no frame went out for it.
- **Not on a mast**: `rak say dfu` reboots the scanner into its bootloader and `rak say hang`
  stops its loop on purpose. Both are for a radio on a desk.

`interfaces::Scratch`'s real implementation — `heap_caps_malloc` of 8 192 bytes and the
`heap_caps_free` after it — has run only against the host fake. On a board the thing to watch is
that `heap` says the same before and after a `rak say`.
