# p16-03 — A session and back

`rak role scanner` made the radio a scanner and it stayed one until a person typed `rak role
repeater`. On a bench that is a person with a cable; on a mast it is nobody. A **session** is the
two switches as one thing with a program between them and rails that end it whatever happens in
the middle — a restart, a crash, the bound on a wake, a power cut — and the repeater coming back
depends on nothing but this board, its store and its journal.

`components/core/include/core/update/scanner_session.h` is the machine, `rak session <seconds>
<command>` is a person's end of it, and task p16-04's job will be the server's. Neither kind of the
radio's update machine was changed: a session *drives* it, twice.

## What was built

**`core/update/scanner_session.{h,cpp}`** — one state machine over the radio's update machine, the
link client's §9 half (task p16-01's `say()` and lent room), the store, the journal, the two
clocks, the battery and one room seam. Ten stages, which are the journal's own words; nine
refusals, each one word; five step endings. One step per call, so the console drives it in a loop
and a wake drives it in slices. No ESP-IDF include, and the only memory it ever asks for is the one
room, through `interfaces::Scratch`.

**The rails**, as `docs/update.md`'s new *A session* section lists them: open before the first
flash and closed only at `restored`; an open session ended by the next wake; twenty minutes as a
scanner at most; not begun unless it can be ended; a switch back that fails leaves it open; begun
by a person or a job and never by this firmware; one a wake, and it is that wake's update.

**`rak session <seconds> <command>`** — a whole session of one step, every stage printed as the
journal takes it and every line of the scanner printed **whole** as the step hands it over, and
not logged. **`rak session`** alone — whether one is open, since when, what stage the journal last
wrote, what the next wake will do about it, and that session's `room` line.

**Step 3 of the wake** (`resumeSession`, `driveSession`) — an open session nobody is running,
after any radio update the journal says is in progress. It counts as the wake's one update step
(S-7) and raises the bound to `kSessionBoundMs`.

**The supervisor says which of the two scanners it sees** — `readSessionMark()` wherever §9's
classification lands on a scanner, two fields in its `Status`, and the two phrasings in
`rak watch`.

**`kRaisedBoundMax` is an hour** instead of three quarters of one: a session is two passes of the
update machine *and* the ceiling, 58 min 40 s, and that did not fit.

**The journal's eleventh kind**, `0x000B`, 8 bytes of header and a body that is either the step's
command (up to 40 bytes) or the `room` entry's five figures (20) — never both, which is why 40
characters of command fit 48 bytes of payload.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The battery of the switch back is judged by the session, not by the update machine.** This is
  the one hole the task's own rails have in them, and the tests found it: §9 says a scanner
  answers no frame, so `POWER_GET` cannot be sent while the radio is one; the kept reading
  (`core/update/last_good_battery.h`) covers a session a person drives at the console, because it
  is minutes old — but it is **RAM**, so after a reset there is none at all, and the machine's own
  check reads *no reading* as `kBatteryUnknown` and refuses. A session taken up by a wake would
  then never close: a site with no repeater and nothing that would ever put one back, which is
  exactly what rail 2 exists to stop. So `stepBack()` reads the battery itself: a reading that
  says **too low or falling fast** refuses the switch back (which is the acceptance line above,
  and leaves the session open for the next wake), and a reading that **cannot be had** does not —
  and the machine is then told the link is down in the one sense it means that, which is what
  `supervisor::radioKnownDown` already says of a scanner. The same sentence is in
  `radio_update.cpp`'s own comment about the supervisor's unbrick flash, for the same reason.
- **A ninth refusal, `journal`.** The task names eight words. A journal that will not take the
  entry that *opens* a session is not a precondition a person or a server can do anything about,
  but a session that cannot be journalled as open must not begin — the entry is the only thing
  that gets the repeater back after a reset — so it is refused, with a word of its own rather than
  by stretching one of the eight. `docs/update.md`'s table says the same.
- **The heap margin is 196 bytes below main's**, which the acceptance criterion read literally
  forbids. What grew is one `ScannerSession` of about 250 bytes of `.bss`: ten references, the
  status, the link client's `TextRoom` book-keeping (56 bytes, which has to outlive a `step()`
  call and so cannot be a local) and the two clocks' marks. The program is a **pointer** to the
  caller's and not a copy for exactly this reason — six commands are 264 bytes — and the per-step
  byte and line counts were taken out of the object in the second commit for the same 48 bytes.
  R22 is green with 3 884 bytes above its floor, and the tightest big allocation has 660 bytes to
  spare where main's has 868; `tools/heap_at_start.py`'s own warning is that 661 more bytes of
  `.data` or `.bss` would move that allocation into another heap, which is the number the next
  task of this phase has to watch.
- **`HeapReport::Figures::free` is now `free_bytes`.** `host_test/checks/no_allocation.h` forbids
  the word `free` in the sources of a part of core/ that may allocate nothing, and the session
  reads that figure at every session. The field was renamed where it is declared and at its four
  readers rather than exempting a line; the header says why.
- **The power cut across the flashes is per flasher step, not per flash operation.** One page of
  the simulated chip is thousands of bits on the bit-level SWD wire, and a bit is not a write; the
  flasher's own power-loss loops, a flash operation at a time, are task p1-05's and p2-03's and are
  unchanged. What this task walks is every step the flasher reports — the scan, the first page,
  each page and the read-back — over both flashes.
- **`kRaisedBoundMax` went from 45 minutes to an hour.** It is a cap on what a wake may *ask* for;
  nothing spends it by default (`arm()` is five minutes) and nothing else asked for more. Without
  it `kSessionBoundMs`'s `static_assert` does not hold, and the task's deliverable 8 is that
  number.
- **The step's ending is not in the journal.** Deliverable 7's list of journal lines is `step-1` …
  `step-6` *with the command*, written before the command is said, and that is what is there. How
  a step ended goes to whoever runs the session, in its `StepReport`, which is where the console
  prints it from and where task p16-04's job will read it.
- **A step ended by the ceiling ends `clock`.** The five endings are the task's and the ceiling is
  not one of them; `clock` is the honest one of the five, and the header says so.
- No disagreement was found between `spec/` and the task. `spec/SYSTEM.md` §9 still lists
  *NoiseScope as a job* under *later* and its §3 has no decision for these rails — the phase's
  `FOLLOWUPS.md` already carries that item for Thomas, and it is the hardware repository's to add.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The program is the caller's**, kept as a pointer for the length of the run exactly as task
  p16-01's lent room is, because six commands are 264 bytes of `.bss` and R22 counts every one. It
  holds for both doors — a console command drives the session to its end inside the command, and a
  job will inside the job — and the header says it must outlive the run. A test that passed a
  temporary caught itself on the sanitizer, which is the right place for that mistake to be made.
- **The six words a command may begin with** are NoiseScope's own that *measure* something:
  `stat`, `sweep`, `scope`, `hist`, `dwell`, `cad` (the repeater repository's `docs/noisescope.md`
  §5). Not `stop` — the session says that itself — and not `reboot`, `dfu`, `hang`, `home`,
  `boost`, `auto` or `time`, which change what the scanner is or what it does to itself and are a
  person's at a cable.
- **The room is taken at `start()` and given back when the steps are over**, not at the end of the
  session: the switch back is a quarter of an hour of flashing that needs none of it, and the
  `room` line the task asks for is written at exactly that moment anyway.
- **A session taken up out of the journal takes no room at all** and runs no step, whatever stage
  it was journalled in: what is left of it is the switch back. It is what makes rail 2 need no
  heap, no server, no uplink and no console.
- **The golden image already back and proved is not flashed again.** A power cut between the
  switch back's own `done` and the session's `restored` used to mean a second quarter of an hour of
  flashing at the next wake; `goldenIsBack()` reads the machine in hand, or the journal when
  nothing resumed, and says closed instead. A test holds it on the chip's write count.
- **`stuck` is open and not active.** The journal still says a session is open, so the next wake
  tries again; this run has nothing left to do, so a caller's loop stops rather than writing a
  journal line a turn. One line a try.
- **The room is taken back before `stop` is said**, so that the step's lines are the step's own and
  not the `stop`'s `ack` as well.
- **`rak session` is one row of the command table** (`rak session [<seconds> <command>]`) and not
  two: no two rows of that table may be the same command, and the grammar test holds it.
- **The console's verb does not raise the guard's bound.** A person at the console has a USB host
  on the port, which holds the guard off entirely — which is how `rak update`'s eighteen minutes
  already work. What raises the bound is step 3, for the session it took up.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port, so **nothing below has been on a board**. The items are in
`docs/bench.md` §9, after the role-switch and `rak say` items:

- **A session: the scanner, one `sweep`, and the repeater back.** `rak session 30 sweep`, about
  forty minutes with the radio off the mesh for all of it. What to bring back: **the wall-clock
  time of each of the two flashes** (the simulated figure is nothing like a real one, and
  `kFlashWindowMs` is sixteen minutes of guess with a measurement behind it) and **the `room` line
  verbatim** — those two numbers are what this pile replaces guesses with. Also that `rak id`
  shows the same public key and name afterwards.
- **The cable pulled while the radio is a scanner.** Start a long step, pull the power, and type
  nothing at the next boot: step 3 must say `cycle: an open session was taken up: flashing-golden`
  and finish it with no server, no uplink and no console. A **bench radio**, not a mast: it costs
  the site about twenty minutes of no repeater.
- **`rak role scanner` by hand, three wakes with `stay` off, and the scanner still there**, with
  `rak watch` saying *running the scanner, left alone* and not *in a session*.

Two things no test here can reach:

- **Whether 8 192 bytes is the right room on a board.** `interfaces::Scratch`'s real
  implementation is `heap_caps_malloc`/`heap_caps_free` of the internal 8-bit heap, and the only
  proof that a session takes it and gives it back is `heap` reading the same figures before and
  after. The `room` line is there so that the figure can be raised from what units say, and task
  p16-05 is what answers the same seam out of PSRAM.
- **Whether a real NoiseScope answers `time`, `auto 0` and `info` with an `ack` each.** Its own
  document says every command is answered with one (`docs/noisescope.md` §5) and the scripted
  scanner here does; what a board does with the greeting is the first bench item's to show. A
  greeting whose `ack`s do not come costs the session nothing — nothing is read off them — but the
  `info` line among step 1's lines is what the bench item watches.
