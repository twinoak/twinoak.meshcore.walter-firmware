# p13-02 — What is kept for the asking

Task 01 sends what the unit knows. This task makes it know three things it forgot, each one a
question 13 November could not be answered from.

| the question that day | what the unit had | what it has now |
|---|---|---|
| *httpFailures 512 — which calls, with what, when?* | one counter with no start date, which leaves 401 and 429 out, and one `LOG_W` line a deep sleep forgot | **the last eight calls that did not come back 2xx**, each with its call, its status or how far the uplink got, the wake's number and the epoch — in the settings store, so a sleep and a power cut both leave it where they found it. `agent calls`, and the report's `calls` section |
| *a wake request reached the radio and nothing went out — why?* | `wake`'s list said `---` and stopped; only a **timer** wake inside a wait left a trace, in the journal | **every entry of the list says why**, in the journal's own words, with the seconds when a wait was owed — including the wake a person asked for |
| *did the line even move?* | nothing crossed to the server at all | **four counters in the heartbeat**: `wakesTimer`, `wakesRadio`, `wakesSpurious`, `wakesSilent` |

## How each of the three works

**The failed calls** ([`walter_agent/call_log.h`](../../../components/agent/include/walter_agent/call_log.h)).
`ModemUplink::call()` is the one place all seven of `mm::IUplink`'s calls pass, so that is where a
failure is written down — with the kind passed *in* rather than worked out from the path, because
a list that names the wrong call is worse than no list. The eighth kind, a **download range**, is
not one of the seven and is recorded where the offer's fetch returns
(`firmware_jobs.cpp`); `core::update::FetchResult` gained an `outcome` so that a range that never
got a status can say how far it got, as the other seven can. The ring is held in the nine bytes an
entry is **stored** as, and a record is a `memmove` and nine bytes over the front.

**Why a wake sent nothing** (`core/cycle/wake_reason.h`, `wake_cycle.cpp`). `interfaces::Reached`
already had the words — the journal and the record line have printed them since task p10-02 — and
the wake record did not carry them. It does now, two bytes an entry: the reason, and the seconds
when the reason is a wait. And **step 6 answers for the wakes step 8 never runs in**: the step
table makes step 8 need step 6, so a wake the uplink's backoff skipped reached no backend at all
and left *it did not say* exactly where a person was looking for a reason. One new value,
`Reached::kUplinkBackoff`, for the one of those that is not simply *no uplink*.

**The four counters** ([`core/cycle/wake_counters.h`](../../../components/core/include/core/cycle/wake_counters.h)).
In `core/` and not in `components/agent/`, although only a heartbeat reads them: the wake cycle is
what knows why a wake happened and whether it sent anything, and it cannot name `agent/`. What
`agent/` takes is the four numbers, through `addWakeCounters()` — **one function**, called by the
console's `agent heartbeat` and by `ModemUplink::postHeartbeat`, so the printed body and the posted
one cannot disagree about the keys or the order. The same arrangement task p10-03 used for
`radioIdentityLost`.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The criterion's one wake is two.** *"A scripted wake: the heartbeat is answered 503 with
  `Retry-After`, the claim gets no answer"* cannot happen in one wake: the copied library ends a
  wake whose heartbeat did not come back before the claim step is reached — "there is no accepted
  interval, no `jobsWaiting` to believe and nothing to be gained by putting the rest of the wake on
  a link that just failed" (`AgentLoop::wake`, and `components/agent-core/` is a copy this
  repository does not edit). The test scripts the two failures as the two wakes they have to be and
  holds everything the criterion asks of the list — both entries, newest first, each with its call,
  what came back and **its own wake number**, which is what the number is there for. The scripted
  server gained a per-route failure and a per-route silence for it
  (`host_test/agent/meshmaster_script.h`, `host_test/sim/scripted_server.h`).
- **A 2xx that did not arrive whole is in the list.** The criterion says *did not come back 2xx*;
  the list is written on `!call.ok`, which also catches a 2xx whose body passed the reply bound or
  stopped early. That is a call that failed, a `200` in this list with nothing else wrong is itself
  the finding, and leaving it out would have been a second definition of *failed* beside the one
  the uplink already has.
- **A call that never left the board is not in the list.** Not configured, a URL that did not fit,
  the 120-a-minute ceiling, too little of the wake's deadline left: nothing came back because
  nothing went out, and those four are `ModemUplink`'s own counters and `agent probe`'s. Named in
  the header where the line is drawn, and left as a follow-up, because a unit whose wakes all end
  in *budget* shows an empty list.
- **`wakesSilent` is drawn at *nothing was sent*, not at *nothing came back*.** A wake whose
  heartbeat went out and did not come back is `Reached::kUnsaid` — *the exchange failed* — and it
  is **not** counted silent: the unit did try, and the server knows it did not hear a heartbeat
  anyway. The same line the record's `nothing sent: ` clause has been drawn at since p10-02, so
  there is one vocabulary and not two. Said in the header, in `docs/cycle.md` and in the test.
- **The uplink's backoff gets a word in `interfaces::Reached` that no backend ever answers.**
  `kUplinkBackoff` is written by the wake cycle for the seam, not by the seam. It is in that enum
  rather than beside it so that the journal, the wake record and `wake`'s list have one vocabulary
  for *why nothing was sent*; the header says so where the value is.
- **The parser's refusal buffer grew from 96 to 112 bytes.** `agent calls` made the `agent`
  refusal's list of subcommands longer than the buffer, and the message was cut off **before the
  word it was refusing** — the one part of it a person needs. A `Parsed` is a local of `runLine()`
  and of the parser's tests and of nothing else, so the sixteen bytes are stack and not `.bss`.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **Two keys of the settings store, not RTC memory and not the journal.** RTC memory has no slot
  left (`tasks/phase-3/reports/05.md`) *and* a power cut takes it — and *how many times has this
  unit woken on its radio's line* is exactly the question a person asks after the power came back.
  The journal was the other candidate and was refused: an entry per failed call would be 512 of
  them on the unit of 13 November, which wraps a 1 024-entry log that exists so that an **update**
  is never guessed at. So: `agent_calls` and `agent_wakes`, in the shape `agent_state` has used
  since p4-05, **written only when they move**.
- **Nine bytes an entry, eight entries, one value.** A value of the store is 160 characters and
  hex is two a byte, so the whole list has 79 bytes: a version byte and eight entries of the kind
  and the outcome packed in one byte, the status, the wake and the epoch. The wake number is 16
  bits and **clamped, never wrapped**: a number that wrapped to a small one would read as recent.
- **The ring is kept in the form it is stored in.** 72 bytes of `.bss` rather than 96 for eight
  `FailedCall`s, and the counters beside it are 16 bits rather than 32 — which is what took rail
  R22's margin from 76 bytes back to 116. `at()` answers by value out of the packed bytes. That is
  not a micro-optimisation for its own sake: the margin that decides whether this image can create
  its main task is counted in tens of bytes (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §14), and the follow-up says
  what has to happen before the next task spends any of it.
- **The wake record grew rather than being squeezed.** `did`'s free bits could have held the
  reason and no byte of the entry could have held the seconds — and the seconds are half the
  answer: *a wait was owed* without *how long* does not tell a person whether the next wake will
  send either. 100 bytes became 120, inside the 128 a slot holds, and the first boot of the release
  reads no record. That is the rule working, not failing; `docs/cycle.md` and `docs/bench.md` §22
  both say it will happen once.
- **`calls` sits between `agent` and `uplink`** in the report's section order — where task 01's own
  payload block put it while it was still answered in `unknown`.
- **The four counters are written at every heartbeat**, not only when something is wrong, unlike
  `radioIdentityLost`. What woke a unit is not a fault, and a counter that appeared only sometimes
  would read on the exit node's page as one that had stopped moving.
- **All four or none.** `addWakeCounters` writes the four or leaves the count alone: three of them
  in a body would read as a counter that had stopped.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port. Everything below needs the rig, and the same lines are in
[`docs/bench.md`](../../../docs/bench.md) §22, in the order the rig is brought up.

- **A failed call on a real network, and the list a boot later.** The simulator's failures are
  scripted ones. The entries worth reading are the ones no simulator writes — a handshake that
  failed on the modem, a socket the modem stopped answering for, a timeout at the wake's budget —
  and the words `agent calls` prints for each are `interfaces/http.h`'s sentences, read off a board
  for the first time. Then a sleep and a power cut, and the list still there.
- **The 13 November wake itself.** A wake request sent to a unit on a mast while a wait is owed:
  `wake`'s entry for it must read `; nothing sent: a wait still owed, <n> s`. **That line is what
  the first open follow-up of this phase is to be decided from**, and until it is read on a board
  the decision rests on code read on 13 November and not on a unit.
- **The wake log starting again, once.** The first boot of the release that carries this reads no
  wake record, because the record grew. It must happen exactly once, and `wake` says so in words.
- **The four counters on meshmaster.dk's page.** They go out in `errors`, which the exit node's
  page shows as its *Counters* row; nothing of that has been seen. A `wakesRadio` that stays 0
  while wake requests are being sent is the 13 November fault seen from a desk, and that is the
  whole point of the four.
- **What it costs the flash, on NVS and not on a map.** A wake now writes two values rather than
  one. The wear of that over a year is arithmetic here and a measurement there.
