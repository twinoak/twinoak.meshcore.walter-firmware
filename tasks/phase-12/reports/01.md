## The two questions

### 1. Fourteen requests for twelve ranges

**When does one offer ask for a range twice?** When an attempt's bytes do not reach the slot.
`core/net/download.cpp` advances its cursor only by what the sink actually took —
`offset += delivered`, after `RangeSink` has written each piece through to flash — so the next
request is always `bytes=<where flash really stands>-…`. Two ways that leaves a byte to be asked
for again, and the server has already written and counted it either way:

- **Nothing was taken at all.** `RangeSink::decide()` refuses the response before a byte reaches
  the sink when it is not a 206 or a 200, when a 206 carries no `Content-Range` this firmware can
  read, when the `Content-Range` is not the range that was asked for, or when the `Content-Length`
  is not the range's length (`download.cpp`, `decide()`). `delivered` is then 0, `offset` does not
  move, and the **whole range is requested again** — while the server, which answered and began
  writing a body, counts what it wrote.
- **Part was taken.** A drop or a deadline mid-range leaves `delivered` short of
  `range_sink.limit()`; `got_all` is false (`download.cpp`, *Did this attempt deliver what it asked
  for?*), the attempt counter is not reset, and the next request starts after the bytes that did
  land. The bytes the server had already put on the wire but that never arrived as a piece are
  paid for and asked for again.

Both are the download working as designed: `kRangeTries` is 3, with `kRetryPauseMs` between
attempts, and a range that fails three times ends the download for that wake. 877 kB for a 780 528-
byte file in 14 requests is twelve ranges that landed plus two attempts whose bytes did not — about
96 kB — and the pages do not say which of the two kinds they were.

**Does anything say so?** **At a cable, yes; to the server, no, and it cannot.**
`net::DownloadResult` counts `ranges`, `retries` and `restarts`, and `core/update/fetch.cpp` prints
all three in its summary line, `fetch: %lu bytes in %lu range(s), %lu retr…, %lu restart(s), %lu
ms` — which on 10 November would have read *12 range(s), 2 retries*, and went to a port nobody was
at. Above that line the counts stop: `upd::FetchResult`
(`core/include/core/update/fetch.h`) has no field for any of them, so
`FirmwareJobs::runOffer` (`components/agent/firmware_jobs.cpp`) has nothing to report even in
principle, and its result carries `bytes` — what reached flash, 780 528 — with no mention of what
was fetched to get there.

`resumed: false` is **not** that missing word and is not wrong. It is
`r.resumed_from != 0 || held_before != 0` (`firmware_jobs.cpp`), and both halves are about an
*earlier wake*: `held_before` is read from the image store's transfer record and is skipped
entirely for the Walter's own product (`if (!walter)`), and `resumed_from` is `target.written()`,
which for the Walter's own slot is 0 by construction because `SelfUpdate::beginWrite` erases it.
So `resumed: false` says truthfully "no previous wake had left bytes of this file in flash", and
nothing about the two repeats inside this one.

**Defect?** Not the repeat — that is a retry doing its job. The gap is that the unit's own account
of a download cannot agree with the data plan's bill, which is a thing a person pays for. Not fixed
here (no code in this task): `tasks/phase-12/FOLLOWUPS.md`, first item.

### 2. Three journal lines with one second

**`at` is when the result was written down, not when the step happened.** The apply's journal in a
result is built by `FirmwareJobs::postOwedResult` (`components/agent/firmware_jobs.cpp`): after the
restart it walks the journal backwards from the owed entry, collects the self-update entries into
`story[]`, and then — in one loop, in one pass of one wake — calls `line(step, …)` for each.
`FirmwareJobs::line()` sets `l.at_epoch = nowEpoch()`, which is the wall clock *at that call*, and
`writeApplyResult` renders each `at_epoch` as the `at` string. The three calls are consecutive
statements; 15:20:13Z on all three is that loop.

The steps themselves carry no clock. `upd::SelfPayload` (`core/include/core/update/self_update.h`)
is a state, a slot, a reason, flags, a size, a hash and two versions — no timestamp — and the
journal `Entry` around it carries a `seq`, not a time (`core/include/core/update/journal.h`). The
journal is written across a reboot by a board that may have no time of day at all, so a sequence is
what it can honestly keep; `nowEpoch()` returns 0 when `wall_.now()` fails, and an apply posted by a
unit that never got the network's time would stamp every line `0`.

So: **the order of the three lines is real, the second on them is the moment of posting**, and
*switching → on-trial → confirmed* in one second is the unit writing the story down after it was
already over, not a trial that lasted a second. The trial's own length is bounded elsewhere — 300 s
to reach the main loop, HELLO the radio and reach the backend — and the apply's wall-clock span is
the job's own: made 14:53:18, ended 15:20:17.

**Defect?** No, but it is a reading that invites a wrong conclusion, so both `docs/flashing.md` and
`docs/bench.md` §18 now say what `at` is where the lines are described. Worth knowing: if the
journal ever does want real step times, the room has to be made in `SelfPayload`, not in the
reporting.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The bench item is rewritten, not ticked, and not split in two.** The task says to tick nothing
  the evidence does not carry, and the evidence carries no console reading at all — so the item
  stays open with the field's half written into it as given facts and the cable's half as what is
  still owed. A second item would have lost the fact that they are one walk.
- **`README`'s *Everything else is still simulators* stands.** It is a claim about everything other
  than what a board has shown, and a server-side result does not make it false. What was false was
  the sentence before it, and that one is replaced.
- **The three expired offers are explained in `docs/flashing.md` and not diagnosed.** The pages do
  not say why the unit was not checking in on that morning, and this unit reports a quarter-hour
  interval, so the document says both — that an offer has to be made again if the unit was away,
  and that a short interval is not a guarantee of catching one — without guessing at a cause.
- **`docs/console.md` names `status` as the way to see the banner again.** `Console::status()`
  prints `core::banner()` as its first line and goes through the console's own writer into the
  ring, unlike `main`'s `printf`; `reboot` is the other way, and report p11-04 named only that one.
