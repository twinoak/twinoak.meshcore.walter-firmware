# p17-02 — The PSRAM, again

Pile 16's task 05 wrote the room of a session out of the module's PSRAM, tested it, booted it in
QEMU where there is none, and then **reverted it** in its second commit: switching `CONFIG_SPIRAM`
on costs 5 904 bytes of static DRAM and the start had 644 to spare. Task p17-01 found 11 608 by
sizing the manifest's bounds to the releases there can be.

**The bytes were enough, and the work is merged.** `git revert` of `b5a1c94` applied with no
conflict on top of p16-06 and p17-01; the model says **FITS**; the tightest big allocation before
`app_main` has **6 348** bytes to spare against `main`'s 12 252 and the 644 this pile started from;
the main task's stack comes out of the same 32 kB heap it comes out of on `main`, at the same
address, with the same 15 636 bytes to spare; all 27 rails hold, R22, R23 and R24 among them; the
release's factory image boots in QEMU with no PSRAM at all, with its full 16 384-byte stack; and
`tools\verify.ps1` is green in 430 s.

So a step of a session is **64 kB of PSRAM where there is PSRAM that answers its pattern**, and
task p16-03's 8 192 bytes of the internal heap where there is not. And the words that task could
not write, because they would have described code that was not there, are written.

  **the chip's hardware is brought up at boot and the memory is not** (`CONFIG_SPIRAM_BOOT_HW_INIT=y`
  with `CONFIG_SPIRAM_BOOT_INIT=n`, ESP-IDF's own supported arrangement — the MSPI timing registers

  fifth state of the seam** (`none`, `asleep`, `up`, `failed`, so that `heap` can tell *nothing has
  asked* from *it was asked and none answered*), **the pattern is written over the big room only**,

  nought, which is the small room, and a test holds it). All four are now described in

  following p16-05's deliverable 10, which used the mark to mean *needs a board*. `docs/bench.md`'s

  and the §9 item carries the mark its section already carries for every item in it

  `tools/heap_at_start.py`, and the figure to put beside it is *the tightest is 6 348 bytes*.
- **§1 The PSRAM, proved by a Walter with no radio at all.** `heap`, then `heap room`, then `heap`.
  The first must say *not brought up in this boot* — an ordinary boot must not touch it; `heap room`
  must print `room: 65536 psram of 2097152; …` and `65536 bytes, external, written and read back;
  given back now`; the second `heap` must say `psram: 2097152 bytes, 2097152 free`. The item has a
  table of what each wrong answer means, the fall-back to task p16-03's line among them.
- **§1 A wake with no session is as long as it was.** `stay off`, three wakes, each the same length
  as before this release, no line about the PSRAM anywhere, and `heap` still *not brought up*. A
  wake that is measurably longer is memory being mapped where nothing asked for it, and the battery
  pays for it every fifteen minutes.
- **§9 A minute of `scope` in 64 kB of PSRAM.** `rak session 60 scope 863 870 250 0 1000` on a
  **bench radio**: sixty frames whole, ended `clock`, about 30 kB, and the `room:` line saying
  `psram`. `full` after about thirteen frames on a board whose `heap room` said 2 MB means the room
  was refused between the two, and the `room:` line is the first figure from a real scanner about
  whether 64 kB is the right size.

And two things no test and no bench can settle, carried as pile 16's follow-ups:

- **Whether 64 kB is the right room.** Every figure about it still comes from the scripted scanner.
  The `room` line is in every session's journal and in every session job's posts, so the next figure
  comes from units.
- **Whether a board whose PSRAM does not answer is common enough to matter.** The fall-back is
  tested three ways here and has never run on a board either; the `room` line is what will say how
  often it is the small room.
