# p9-04 — What the bench could not see

Five small things the first unit showed on 7 November. None of them broke anything and each one
cost a person time to read, so each one now says what it means — and two of them turned out to be
less true than the task file assumed, which is in *Deviations* below.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The task's own sentence *`lte up` before `rak update` lets it run* was not true, and was made
  true.** `Console::lteUp` printed the network's time and gave it to nothing: the only thing that
  ever filled `core::cycle::NetworkClock` was the wake cycle's step 6 (and the agent's server
  time, through `WalterPlatform`). So a boot a person drove by hand had no clock of its own
  however long the uplink had been up, the link had no time to give the radio
  (`spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §5.4), and the update's clock check was skipped in every such boot.
  Saying *`lte up` first* without changing anything would have been a second wrong message. See
  *Decisions*.
- **`docs/flashing.md` does not say *seven minutes or so* under *A unit at 0.1.0*** — the task
  file quotes it there, and the sentence is in that section's step 3 (*Then the radio's first
  install*). That is where it was corrected. `docs/bench.md` §795's *about seven minutes of SWD
  traffic* is a different and **correct** sentence: it is the flash alone, and the simulated
  figure beside it (413 s) agrees.
- **Nothing of `spec/` disagrees with the task.** The clock check's rule is §5.4's and is
  unchanged; what changed is which boots can make it.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The clock check: both halves of the choice the task offered, and not the second one alone.**
  The task offered *say it truthfully, and that `lte up` lets it run* **or** *have the update ask
  the uplink for the time*. The second would give `RadioUpdate` an uplink it has no business
  holding — it reads `interfaces::WallClock` and that is the seam. So: the message says what is
  true, **and** `lte up` hands the time it read to `NetworkClock::give()`. It is the same
  `interfaces::NetworkTime`, through the same window (`uplink::judgeTime`, which refuses a
  modem's 1970 and anything outside the build's dates), so nothing new is believed and the
  header's rule still holds: one place sets this object's time, and it is the network. A time
  taken this way is still forgotten at the next wake, because the monotonic counter restarts.
  `lte up` says so in its own line.
- **The wake's bound goes back when the command returns, rather than ending when the cable goes.**
  The task said to choose one deterministically. The bound is a property of the wake, counted from
  the boot; the cable is a property of the person, and the guard already has one rule for it (the
  grace). Tying the raised bound to the cable would have put a second clock and a second state
  into an object whose whole value is that it has one. With the bound put back, the existing rule
  does the work: a bench session is past `kWakeBoundMs` long before the cable comes out, so the
  reset lands one grace after it. The console names the figure so that a person is not left
  guessing — `endsAfterCableMs()`, which is the same arithmetic `tick()` does.
- **The `begun` list is ten deep and holds six bytes of each key.** Ten, so that it lines up with
  the library's *last ten*; six bytes, because twelve hex characters is how the library's own
  `sessions` line shortens a key (`%.12s...`) and it is enough to tell two apart by eye. Seventy
  bytes, in an object that is already a static — DRAM on this board is tight enough that the
  agent's runner is heap-allocated at boot in `main/`.
- **The job id is not in that list**, because `mm::IMesh::beginJob` is not given one. The two
  lists are both newest-first and the same depth, which is as close as this end can get without
  changing agent-core.
- **Two truncated console lines fixed while in them.** `printf` in the console and `sayf` in the
  backend both format into 200 bytes, and two lines had been over it for some time — so they were
  cut mid-word **and lost their newline**, and whatever came next ran on from them. `agent jobs`'s
  `firmware` line (one `sayf`, cut at *0 pow*) is three lines now; `agent id`'s `job types` line
  (up to 320 characters of list, cut at *twinoak.*) is printed in pieces through `print()`. Both
  are the line immediately before something this task added, which is how they were found; both
  are held by an assertion on the newline.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens the unit's ports this pile (`tasks/phase-9/README.md`), and three of the four
deliverables are about what a person reads on a bench. The lines are at the end of
`docs/bench.md` §18, in the order the rig is brought up:

- **★ The cable ends the wake an `agent wake` raised.** After a bench session of several
  `agent wake`s, pull the cable: the drop from about 41 mA to **8 mA** within a minute or so (the
  USB grace), one reset whose next boot says `software` and *the wake overran its bound*, and then
  the ordinary wake that ends in deep sleep. The last two lines of each `agent wake` name the
  figure before the cable is pulled. A quarter of an hour at 41 mA is this fault back.
- **The local door, on the console.** `agent id`'s `local door:` line and `agent jobs`'s two
  blocks, after a wake that ran a job for the unit's own repeater **and** one for a neighbour
  (§18's second item): the neighbour's must read *the mesh; NOT this radio's key …* with both keys
  shortened.
- **The clock check runs when the uplink has been up.** `rak update` with no `lte up` first prints
  the new *skipped* line; with `lte up` first the line is gone, `lte up` says *this Walter's clock
  is set from it*, and `rak watch`'s clock line says *was set*.
- **The journal names the image of a run's first entry.** `rak update status` after any run: the
  oldest entry, `check`, carries the candidate's `sha256` and not `0000000000000000…`.
- **The first install's wall-clock time** is still §9's item, now with a figure to hold it
  against: 413 s of SWD for the flash of a 417,284-byte image in 102 pages at 125 kHz, up to 20 s
  of boot window and 180 s of proving — **about eleven minutes** from the code, where the bench saw
  about a quarter of an hour (19:31–19:49 on 7 November, with a person's time in it). What §9 asks
  for is the flash alone, timed.

One thing is a reading and not a measurement, and is said here rather than left implied: the
**cause** of the lost key is the repeater firmware's, found by its pile 3.9 task 01
(`InternalFS.begin()` formatting a volume a torn page had made unmountable) and out of scope here.
`docs/flashing.md`'s new section links that guide for the cause and keeps only what is this
Walter's: how to see the new key, and what a person does next.
