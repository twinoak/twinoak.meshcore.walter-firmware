# p9-03 — A silent radio is not a scanner

p8-04 needed one fact — *this end has no key, so it cannot tell a job for its own repeater from a
stranger's* — and wrote it into a flag that already meant another: `WakeContext::radio_silent`,
whose comment says *the supervisor has classified the link as down*. From 7 November that flag was
`!radio_up || known.wake.radio == kSilent`, and `radio_up` is *there is a frame session*. A slot
running NoiseScope has no frame session. A slot awaiting its first install has none either. The
supervisor says, in the note on `Health::kAwaitingFirstInstall` itself, that neither of those is
*the link is known down* — and that flag was the only thing an apply's two refusals and a
self-switch's trial had to go on.

**The two facts are apart now, and the second one has a name of its own in two places:**

| | Fact | Where it lives | Who reads it |
|---|---|---|---|
| the key | *no key to tell a local job by* | the identity read before every wake (`Identity::open()`, `LinkMesh::setOwnKey`), reported in one line by `sayRadio()` | the **claim**: `supports()` answers no mesh type without the key, so the heartbeat offers none and the server routes none (p8-04, unchanged) |
| the classification | *the link is known down* | `core::supervisor::linkKnownDown(Health)` — **`kSilent` alone** | the radio update's `HELLO` refusal and its battery-unknown refusal (`radio_update.cpp`, about lines 458 and 477) |
| the classification | *the radio will not answer a frame `HELLO`* | `core::supervisor::radioKnownDown(Health)` — the three classified silences, **not `kUnknown`** | a self-switch's trial condition 2 (`self_update.cpp`, about lines 218 and 464–467) |

Both functions are `constexpr` and sit directly under the `Health` enumeration they are about, so
the rule is written once and the two callers — `WakeCycle` (which had the two expressions inline)
and `FirmwareJobs` — read it rather than each other. `FirmwareJobs` asks the `Supervisor` it was
already given (`Parts::supervisor`, there since p4-05 for `twinoak.fw.state`'s incidents), so
`WakeContext` now carries **nothing at all** about the radio's health and cannot be given the wrong
thing again; `agent_backend.cpp` says so where the line used to be.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **A scanner slot's self-switch is refused on the battery, not reached at the radio condition,
  and that is a property of the two machines rather than of this fix.** The task asks what a
  self-switch's confirmation says of the radio for each of the four states. For a scanner there is
  no confirmation to read, and the reason is worth writing down:
  `SelfUpdate::switchNow` → `preconditions(true)` asks for a battery reading **before** anything
  else; the gauge is the INA3221 on the *radio's* I2C and is read with a §5.8 frame; and §9's rule
  in `client.cpp`'s `pump` (`if (here().mode != Mode::kFrames) onTextByte(byte)`) means a client
  that has ever seen a frame in this Walter's life reads no text until something resets the RAK.
  So `kScanner` and a battery reading **cannot be had in one Walter life**: the only way to the
  classification is a Walter whose link has never carried a frame, and that Walter has no reading
  either. The test says so and asserts the refusal that does happen (`refusal: battery`). This
  also means `radioKnownDown(kScanner)` is belt and braces rather than a path — a scanner counts
  as *up* in `switchNow` anyway — and it is kept because the wake cycle had it and because the set
  is *the classified silences*, not *the ones that happen to matter today*.
- **A scanner's or an awaiting slot's apply is refused on the link, and the battery refusal behind
  it is not separately observable.** `RadioUpdate::stepCheck` asks the link (line 458) before the
  battery (line 477), so for every state where the link refusal fires the battery is never
  reached. Both refusals read the **same** flag, the table test holds `Health` → flag over all
  five values, and `host_test/core/update/test_radio_update.cpp` (about lines 359 and 375) already
  holds flag → behaviour for the battery. The `kSilent` row of the end-to-end test is where both
  are seen lifted together.
- **The review's third bullet is right about the effect and wrong about who it bit.** It says the
  flag *marks the radio's condition not applicable in a self-switch confirmation*, and names a
  scanner. A scanner never reached that line: `switchNow` computes
  `radio_up = hello.ok() || mode() == kText`, so a scanner is *up* and `kFlagRadioKnownDown` was
  never set for one. What did reach it is **any** wake whose `HELLO` had gone unanswered with
  nothing classified — the console's `agent wake` on a radio that died after the last power
  reading, which is the 7 November shape — and a Walter image was then put on a trial with its
  radio condition waived by nobody's decision. That is the case the new test holds, and it is the
  one assertion of the self-switch half that fails before the fix.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`radioKnownDown` includes `kAwaitingFirstInstall`; the wake cycle's own expression did not.**
  `wake_cycle.cpp` had `kSilent || kScanner`. Keeping exactly that would have made a Walter
  self-switch on a freshly provisioned unit refuse `linkDown` for ever — a slot with a bootloader
  and no application cannot answer a frame `HELLO` until it is flashed, so the trial's second
  condition could never be met and the switch is either refused or a guaranteed rollback. Today's
  code lets it through (by the broken flag), so refusing it would have been a new regression on
  p5-03's own path. The set is therefore *the three silences the supervisor has classified*, and
  the wake cycle gains `kAwaitingFirstInstall` with it. `linkKnownDown` is untouched at `kSilent`
  alone, which is what `supervisor.h`'s own note demands.
- **`FirmwareJobs` reads the supervisor instead of being told by the wake.** The alternative was
  to keep a field in `WakeContext` and fill it from `known.wake.radio`. That would have left the
  console's `agent wake` — which runs no step 2 and carries `kUnknown` in its record — behaving
  differently from a timer wake on the same unit, which is exactly what p8-04 set out to fix; and
  it would have left a field that can be filled with the wrong thing. The supervisor's `health()`
  is a const read of the classification step 2 made in this same wake, the pointer was already
  there, and a build without one classifies nothing and lifts nothing.
- **`kUnknown` lifts neither refusal, which is the pre-p8-04 behaviour and is the conservative
  one.** A console `agent wake` on a genuinely dead radio now refuses an apply with *the link is
  down and nobody has said why* where a timer wake that has classified it `silent` does not. The
  difference is a decision of the supervisor's, which is the whole point; a first install says
  *known down* for itself (`radio_update.cpp`, about line 306) and so does the supervisor's own
  rung, so neither unbrick path is affected.
- **The test drives the supervisor by hand rather than running whole wakes**, two `step()`s and no
  third (`stepIdle` asks, `stepClassify` decides, and the step after that would be the ladder's
  first rung). A whole wake would do it, but a wake whose radio is silent has no battery reading
  either, so its band is `no-reading` and `no-reading` refuses an update at
  `context_.may.update` — before the machine's own preconditions are ever reached. The exchanges
  are made by hand at `plenty` for that reason, and the wake's record carries `kHealthy`
  throughout, on purpose: a record that agreed with the supervisor would prove nothing about which
  of the two is read.
- **The new test file's name says the subject, not the task**
  (`test_radio_health_and_the_refusals.cpp`), as `test_radio_before_a_job.cpp` does.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot open the Walter's port or the radio's, and this phase runs at a desk
(`tasks/phase-9/README.md`: *Not on the board*). Two lines are in `docs/bench.md` §18, after the
band items:

1. **A scanner slot refuses an apply, and nothing is flashed under it.** ⚠ needs
   `"radio": "disposable"`. `rak role scanner`, then a `twinoak.fw.apply` of a stored candidate and
   `agent wake`: `refused`, `refusal: link`, the `check` line *the link is down and nobody has said
   why*, `image list` unchanged, `image bad` still empty, no new incident in `rak watch`.
   `rak role repeater` and the same apply commits. If the apply flashes a radio running NoiseScope,
   the refusal has been lifted by something that is not the supervisor and it is this regression
   again.
2. **A dead radio nobody has classified refuses an apply; one the supervisor has given up on does
   not.** Jumper out, a fresh boot (`rak watch`: `radio health: unknown`), `lte up`, `agent wake`
   with an apply queued → `refused`, `refusal: link`. Then `rak ladder` to its *STOPPED*
   (`radio health: silent`) and `agent wake` again → the machine runs and ends `stuck`, the
   battery-unknown refusal lifted with the link's. The only difference between the two wakes is a
   decision of the supervisor's.

Also not walked, and not reachable on a bench either: the scanner's *self-switch* row of the table
above. See *Deviations* — a scanner slot and a battery reading cannot exist in one Walter life, so
the refusal is the battery's and the trial's radio condition is never reached. Nothing to walk
there; what a person would see is `refusal: battery` on a Walter apply offered while the radio runs
NoiseScope.
