else: the link had no request that yields a status, a neighbour list, the owner info or an access

## What meshmaster.dk can rely on

Read at `twinoak/meshmaster.dk` **`0eba4c6`** (`origin/master`, read with `git show`; nothing
written there), `src/MeshMaster.Core/Transport/LocalDoor.cs` and
`tasks/phase-4.27/176-a-unit-reads-its-own-status.md`. **Its side has already landed**, and task
176's question — *if the Walter's 06 report names a better signal that the heartbeat already
carries, use it and say why* — has one answer: **there is none, and the two version strings it
chose are the right ones.**

1. **The two fields are the heartbeat's `agentVersion` and `companionFirmware`, and both are what
   it thinks they are.**
   - `agentVersion` is **this firmware's own version**: `agent::Identity` is constructed with
     `core::version()` (`main/main.cpp:531`), which is `TWINOAK_VERSION` from `PROJECT_VER`, and
     `Envelopes.cpp` writes it as `agentVersion`. `transport` is the literal `"native"`. So task
     07 publishing `v0.1.2` is what turns `WalterAsksLocalRequest` on, and nothing else does.
   - `companionFirmware` is **§5.1 `HELLO`'s `fw_version`**, copied into the session and from there
     into the heartbeat (`identity.cpp:179` and `:202`, and the header says so in as many words).
     That is the **repeater firmware's own release version** — `0.1.1+…` today, `0.1.2+…` once its
     pile 3.9's release reaches the radio — and **not** the MeshCore tag, which rides in
     `upstream` (`repeater-v1.17.1+d9296435`) and reaches the server as `companionUpstream`. So
     `RadioAnswersLocalRequest`'s two bounds are both right: `>= 0.1.2` matches the repeater
     firmware's line, and `< MeshCoresOwnLine` (1.0) excludes a radio that reports a stock
     MeshCore version, which has no `LOCAL_REQUEST` and answers `ERR_UNSUPPORTED` (§3).
   - `companionFirmware` is **absent** from the heartbeat until the key is known, and
     `FirmwareVersion.TryParse(null)` is `null`, which counts as older. That is the right way
     round: a unit that has not read its radio yet is offered nothing it cannot do.
2. **A 0.1.2 Walter with a 0.1.1 radio answers *Failed*, as today.** The firmware does not read
   the radio's version at all — it reads the **capability bit** of the session — and a repeater at
   0.1.1 does not carry it, so a `status` or a `neighbours` job of the unit's own repeater is
   `failed` with *the packet could not be built*, an access-list read is silence and a
   `config.read` keeps its other keys. Held by
   *With11TheSameThreeJobsAreExactlyWhatTheyWereBefore12* and by the unchanged thirteen-type case.
   The server's own sentence for that state, `NoStatusFromItsOwnUnit` — *Its own unit answers no
   status over the box link until its Walter and radio are at 0.1.2; its radio is older* — is the
   right thing for a page to say, and `OlderHalf` names the half correctly for every combination.
3. **What it may *not* rely on: the two signals are not the same signal.** The server decides from
   two **version strings**; the firmware decides from the session's **capability bit**. They agree
   for every radio that exists — the bit arrives with the repeater's 0.1.2 and with nothing else —
   but they are different facts, and the heartbeat carries no capability bit for the server to read
   (`caps` is not one of the heartbeat's fields; `companionFirmware`, `companionBoard`,
   `companionUpstream` and the `radio` block are). The one disagreement it allows: a radio that
   reports 0.1.2 or later and does **not** advertise `LOCAL_REQUEST` would be routed a `status`
   this firmware then answers `failed`. Its symptom on the bench is exactly that pair — `agent id`
   saying `from LOCAL_REQUEST: none` while the unit's page offers the job — and `rak hello`'s
   capability line is where to look. Adding the bit to the heartbeat would need a field on both
   sides for a case no radio is in, so it is named here rather than built.
4. **Nothing else of what the unit sends moved.** The heartbeat is byte for byte what it was: no
   field was added, none removed. The one thing that changed on the wire *to the radio* is
   `HELLO`'s `proto_minor`, 1 → 2, and `LOCAL_REQUEST` frames for a unit whose radio carries the
   bit. The descriptor's `link_minor` and the release manifest's `link.minor` move to 2 with it,
   and neither is compared against a radio image's (only the major is) — so a 0.1.2 Walter still
   accepts, flashes and talks to a 0.1.0 repeater image, which is what the unit on the bench will
   be for as long as it takes to offer its radio the update.
5. **The transactions of a local job are still 0**, as p9-05 left them, and a `status` or
   `neighbours` read through the door is now one more job priced as air by the server's planned
   load. An over-estimate against a budget is the safe direction; the open follow-up from p9-05
   (the wake's own pricing of a local job) is unchanged and is upstream's.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**The release manifest's `link.minor` had to move too, and the test that held it was a literal.**
`tools/release/build_release.py`'s `LINK = {"major": 1, "minor": 1}` is the manifest's `link`
object, and `spec/FIRMWARE-MANIFEST.md` §5 check 5 holds the descriptor inside the image against

third of the three places `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` calls *one version, not three copies of it*.

release notes as a literal; it now builds the expected text from `build_release.LINK`, so the next
minor moves one constant and not two. The task did not mention either file, and §5's own check is
what found them — which is the check working.

**One header had two copies of `fromHex`/`toHex` in one namespace, and this task was the first to
include both.** `host_test/fixtures/link_fixture.h` and `host_test/fixtures/manifest_files.h` each
defined `fixtures::fromHex` and `fixtures::toHex(const std::uint8_t*, std::size_t)`, identical but

wake bench reading the 1.2 fixtures includes both through `sim/local_door.h`, and the second

**`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §13 said a `status` for the unit's own repeater comes back
`unsupported`.** It comes back `failed` — `JobRunner::request` reports a `SendResult::Failed` as
`Outcome::Failed` with *the packet could not be built*, which is what every test of the old
behaviour asserts. Corrected in the same paragraph that now explains the 1.2 shape.

**`spec/` and the task agree everywhere else.** §5.9's four types, its `ERR_RANGE` for a request
the handler answers nothing to, its `ERR_MALFORMED` over 32 bytes and its *nothing goes on the air*
are implemented as written, and the fourteen fixture cases are read as they are.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`MeshCore`'s `RepeaterStats` is decoded in `core/link/codec/`, under a heading that says it is
  not a payload of this link.** `rak request 01` has to print the fields by name — a person on the
  bench reading `ac0f0000…` learns nothing — and the console is `core/`, which cannot name the
  agent library where `parseRepeaterStats` lives. So `messages.{h,cpp}` gains `RepeaterStats` and
  `decodeRepeaterStats` in a section headed *§5.9's `data`, read for a person alone*, with the
  layout cited to `spec/tools/linkref.py`'s `repeater_stats()` and the repeater firmware's pin. The
  test holds `kRepeaterStatsMinLen` equal to the library's own figure and the decoded fields to the
  fixture's, so the two readers of those bytes cannot disagree quietly. **Nothing of the firmware
  decides anything from it**: a `LOCAL_REQUEST`'s answer goes to the library untouched, as §5.9
  says.
- **The capability bit decides, not the minor.** §5.9 ends *a RAK without it answers
  `ERR_UNSUPPORTED`*, and §4's third column makes `LOCAL_REQUEST` an optional type like `CLI` or
  `POWER` — so `capabilityOf` carries it and the client refuses the call without the bit, as it
  does for every other optional type. `kLocalRequestMinor` exists for the record and is read by
  nothing but a test. This also means the firmware needs no version comparison of the radio at all,
  which is the difference between its signal and meshmaster.dk's (above).
- **`answersRequestsLocally()` is one expression with two readers.** The door asks it at every
  request send and `agent id` prints from it, the way p9-05 made `local()` and `answersLocally()`
  one question. A second copy of *does this session carry `0x80`* in the probe is how a console
  line comes to say something the code does not do.
- **An `ERR_RANGE` counts `local_silent` and not `local_unserved`.** The two counters answer
  different questions — *the door chose to say nothing* and *the link has no request for this* —
  and §5.9's `ERR_RANGE` is the first. So `local_unserved` stays exactly what it was: the three
  types a radio below 1.2 cannot be asked, plus discovery and trace. `agent id`'s wording (*N
  answered with silence, N with no request to make*) reads correctly either way.
- **The simulated handler answers three of the four from the fixtures and pages the fourth
  itself.** `host_test/sim/local_door.h` takes the status, the owner info and the access list
  verbatim out of `link-v1.2.json` — they do not depend on `data`, so a test reading them back is
  reading the specification — and builds the neighbour page itself, in `linkref.py`'s own layout,
  because the fixture's single two-row answer would let a Walter that asked for one page and
  stopped pass. Eleven rows against the library's nine-to-a-page is the smallest table that makes
  the second request happen.
- **The scripted radio's *fallback* answers are still 1.0's file.** `findCase()` now looks in 1.0's
  cases and then in 1.2's — so a test can name a 1.2 answer with `answerWith()` — but
  `responseFor()`'s fallback loop still walks 1.0's, which is what every existing test depends on
  and what the radio on the bench actually speaks. A radio given no `local_door` and asked a
  `LOCAL_REQUEST` is the `SimRak`'s `ERR_UNSUPPORTED`, which is §5.9's own answer for a role with
  no handler.
- **The three bench items went into §18 and not into §3** (*The link*), although two of them are
  pure link commands. All three need **both** v0.1.2s on the unit, and the third needs the server;
  splitting them would put the prerequisite in two places. The first of the three says the
  prerequisite in its first sentence.
- **The default radio of every bench is still a 1.1 one.** `ScriptedRadio::caps` is unchanged, so
  every test written before this task runs against a radio with no `LOCAL_REQUEST` — which is both
  the smallest change and the state the first unit is really in. A test that wants 1.2 says so
  (`makeIt12()`, `radioAt12()`).
