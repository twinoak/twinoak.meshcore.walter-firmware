# p4-05 — The agent behind the seam

The seam is filled. `components/agent/agent_backend.h` is `interfaces::Backend`, and its
`exchange()` is **one call to `mm::AgentLoop::wake(intent, budget)`** with the loop's 76-byte
`WakeState` restored from an NVS blob before the wake and saved after — so the backoff, the
accepted interval and the heartbeat's counters survive the deep sleep every wake ends in. The
`WakeReport` is mapped back into the seam's three words, and the three things it carries that are
the wake cycle's business are handed over, not acted on here: the sleep (an accepted `nextCheckInS`
honoured **only when it is longer** than the band's own interval — the battery wins), the server's
time to the `NetworkClock`, and the seam's `asks` produced from the **extension's** firmware jobs,
so S-7 and the journal see a `twinoak.fw.apply` exactly as they see a console-driven update.

`components/agent/firmware_jobs.h` is `mm::IJobExtension`: the four `twinoak.*` types
(`unit-protocol.md` §5), each mapped onto phase 2's image store and update machines and run **for
the unit's own repeater through the local door** — no `client_tx`, no packet on the air. And
`WALTER_AGENT=0` still builds and runs: the phase-3 unit over the null backend, the wake cycle's
own tests and the rails' two months, a second host configuration CI runs beside the first.

Done in one session. The things a reviewer should read first, all under *Deviations*: **the backend
step raises the wake's bound only when a firmware job actually ran a machine** (not on every
exchange); **the four `twinoak.*` job types are advertised even before the repeater has a key**;
and **the library's `JobRunner` and the kept-image scratch are taken from the heap at boot**, not
static `.bss`, because the board has no PSRAM and the internal DRAM is too small to hold them
statically beside the console and the update verdict.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

### The backend step raises the wake's bound only when a firmware job ran a machine

The task says the exchange "is bounded as an update is and may hold the wake open to that bound."
The first cut raised the guard's bound to `kBackendBoundMs` (= `kUpdateBoundMs`, twenty minutes) on
**every** exchange. That inflated every heartbeat-only wake to a twenty-minute bound and broke
`test_wake_radio`'s assertion that a silent-radio wake that climbs the ladder has exactly
`kLadderBoundMs`. The fix: the exchange is still *given* an update's deadline (so a firmware job
has room), but the firmware job's own machine time comes back as `BackendAnswer::outside_ms` and is
outside the ordinary budget, exactly as a climb's and an update's are; the bound is raised to cover
that wall time **only when such a machine actually ran** (`outside_ms > 0`). A wake that merely
heartbeated keeps the ordinary bound, or the ladder's if the radio was silent. `wake_cycle.cpp`
`stepBackend`, with the reasoning in `wake_cycle.h`'s `kBackendBoundMs` comment.

### The four `twinoak.*` job types are advertised even before the repeater has a key

p4-04's report noted that the *mesh* job type list is not reduced by the local door. The
extension's four `twinoak.*` types go one step further: `test_agent_backend.cpp`'s *a unit before
its repeater* shows them in the heartbeat **even when the radio has no key and the mesh list is
empty**. They concern the unit and its own store rather than a neighbour, the server routes them to
it only once it is linked, and the alternative — withholding them until a key exists — would make a
newly-registered unit look as though it could never take a firmware job. This is a small widening
of p4-04's "the list is what the server routes by"; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §13 carries both halves.

### The library's `JobRunner` and the kept-image scratch are heap, not static `.bss`

`docs/library.md` §6 asks that `mm::JobRunner` (~64 kB) be a namespace-scope or static object, to
keep it off the ~8 kB task stack. Building the firmware with the agent on overflowed the ESP32-S3's
internal DRAM segment by ~47 kB: the runner's 64 kB and the kept-image scratch's 20 kB would not
fit in the static `.bss` beside the update verdict (~35 kB) and the console (~31 kB), and PSRAM is
deliberately off (`sdkconfig.defaults`: an N16R2 has 2 MB but 8 MB octal variants exist and a
firmware configured for one aborts on the other). So `main/app_main` takes both **from the heap
once at boot** — never a stack local, never freed, so the whole-run lifetime the library asks for
holds — reached through a namespace-scope pointer. That honours §6's intent (not the stack) and
leaves ~38.9 kB of DIRAM headroom. `main/agent_anchor.cpp`, which held the library in the image
through `--gc-sections` while nothing called it (p4-01 to p4-04), is gone: `app_main` is a real
caller now.

### Firmware jobs for the own repeater carry no `client_tx`, and the Walter's apply result waits a boot

Two consequences of the architecture worth stating plainly. A firmware job acts on the unit's *own*
store and chip, so it sends nothing on the mesh and `client_tx` does not move for it (the month
asserts this). And a `twinoak.fw.apply` of the Walter itself cannot report its own outcome in the
wake that started it: the board reboots into the image being tried, and the result — `committed` or
`rolledBack` — is posted by the **next** boot's wake, after the trial's third condition (one
heartbeat `ok`) is reached. A wake that ends mid-download is the mirror image: it posts no result
and lets the lease expire, so the server never sees a half-answer.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **A per-wake download byte budget** (`kWakeDownloadBytes`, 1 MB default). `unit-protocol.md` §5
  says an offer may stop and resume, but the library refuses to *start* a job whose time estimate
  exceeds the wake, so a pure time budget could not produce a partial on the bench or in a test. A
  byte-per-wake cap is the real limit a metered LTE plan imposes anyway; `AgentBackend` exposes
  `setDownloadBytesPerWake()` for the test, and the constant is a bench placeholder.
- **`RegionStore` over a window of the `rakfw` partition after the image store**, rather than a new
  partition or an NVS blob. The image store already owns that partition and knows its own size
  (`kStoreBytes`); the window after it is free, sector-granular, and reached through the same
  `interfaces::FlashRegion` — so a kept image survives a sleep with no partition-table change. It
  is in the store test's allow-list for naming `interfaces::FlashRegion`, with a note that it is a
  third region and not the image partition.
- **The rollback scenario uses a distinct image** (`store-b`) and sets the old version to the
  committed golden's, so only the candidate mismatches its own descriptor while the golden proves
  itself on the way back — otherwise a reused candidate collides on the bad-list and the hash and
  the apply gets stuck rather than rolling back.
- **A bounded flat-JSON reader of our own** (`agent::json_scan`) for reading the firmware payloads,
  rather than pulling the library's ArduinoJson into `components/agent/`'s own code: the payloads
  are flat and small, and a bounded reader with no allocation keeps the component's no-ESP-IDF,
  no-alloc shape.

## Specification gaps / findings for meshmaster.dk

- **An offer that stops on budget posts no result and lets the lease expire.** `unit-protocol.md`
  §5 describes resume and progress but not the no-result case explicitly; this firmware treats a
  mid-download budget stop as "nothing to report yet", which the lease's own expiry handles. A line
  in §5 saying so would remove the ambiguity.
- **The Walter's own apply result arrives a wake late**, after the trial. The §5 table reads as
  though the result is posted in the apply's own job; for a self-update it cannot be. meshmaster.dk
  already has `Settle` for the equivalent on its side, so this is a note, not a conflict.

## New libraries (with the one-line justification)

**None.** The firmware payloads are read with `agent::json_scan`, a bounded flat-JSON reader of our
own with no allocation and no ESP-IDF — the component's shape forbids pulling the library's
ArduinoJson into `components/agent/`'s own code, and the payloads are flat and small. The copied
`agent-core` library and its one dependency (ArduinoJson 7.4.3) were already in the tree from
p4-01.
