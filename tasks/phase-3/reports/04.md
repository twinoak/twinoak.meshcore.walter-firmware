# p3-04 — The duty cycle

The Walter sleeps with its seven pads held in the safe state, wakes for two reasons — its timer
and a start bit from the radio — and lets the battery choose how long: a quarter of an hour on a
full pack, a day on an empty one, and below a floor no modem at all. A wake that does not reach
sleep is ended by a reset. A person with a cable is the third reason, and **it is not a wake
source on this chip**: a boot that finds a USB host stays awake.

**What is proven here and what waits for the bench.** Everything in `core/power/` and
`core/cycle/` is the first kind — logic, on the host, against fake pins that check the pin rules,
a fake sleeper that checks them again at the moment of sleeping, the scripted radio and a fake
modem. `components/platform/sleeper.cpp` is the second kind: 60 lines of ESP-IDF calls that
compile, link and have not run. And **every threshold and every interval is a placeholder**: no
meter has been in series with this unit and no pack's discharge curve has been measured, which is
`spec/SYSTEM.md` §10 item 5 and now `docs/bench.md` §14.

## USB cannot wake this board

The task said this was "a question to answer, not to assume". The answer is **no**, read in
ESP-IDF v5.5.5 in the image this repository builds in:

- `esp_sleep_enable_usb_wakeup()` is `#if SOC_PM_SUPPORT_USB_WAKEUP` and returns
  `ESP_ERR_NOT_SUPPORTED` otherwise (`components/esp_hw_support/sleep_modes.c:2322`);
- `components/soc/esp32s3/include/soc/soc_caps.h` **does not define `SOC_PM_SUPPORT_USB_WAKEUP`**
  (it defines `SOC_PM_SUPPORT_EXT0_WAKEUP`, `SOC_PM_SUPPORT_EXT1_WAKEUP` and
  `SOC_PM_SUPPORT_RTC_PERIPH_PD`, and nothing about USB);
- and even where it exists it is light sleep's: `esp_sleep.h`'s cause enumerator is
  `ESP_SLEEP_WAKEUP_USB, //!< Wakeup caused by USB HS (light sleep only)`, and
  `docs/en/api-reference/system/sleep_modes.rst` lists the USB wake example under
  `SOC_PM_SUPPORT_USB_WAKEUP` only.

So the rule the task names is the rule: **a person presses reset, and a boot that finds a USB host
stays awake** — while the host is there and for `kUsbGraceMs` (a minute) after the cable goes.
Said in `docs/console.md` (*Sleep, and what wakes it*), in `docs/bench.md` §14, at every boot that
finds one, and by `wake` and `status`.

How "a USB host is there" is answered: `usb_serial_jtag_is_connected()`. The console is the
ESP32-S3's own USB Serial/JTAG peripheral (`CONFIG_ESP_CONSOLE_USB_SERIAL_JTAG`), and the
component's `CMakeLists.txt` links the connection monitor in under exactly that condition, so the
tick hook that watches for SOF packets is already running and already paid for. Its own header
answers the right question: "USJ is considered connected so long as it is receiving SOF packets
from the host, even if there is no serial communications occurring … Having the USB port connected
to a power bank will never be considered as connected." A power bank is not a person.

One wrinkle, and it is in the code: `usb_serial_jtag_conn_status_init()` sets the status to
*connected* and then lets the tick hook disprove it — "Always assume USB Serial/JTAG is connected
until we are sure it is not connected … Consider it is disconnected only if SOF intr bit is not
raised within (ALLOWED_NO_SOF_TICKS + 1) tick periods", with `USJ_DISCONNECT_CONFIRM_PERIOD_MS`
being 3. So a read in the first few milliseconds of a boot always says yes.
`EspSleeper::usbHost()` waits until 20 ms after the boot before it believes the answer.

## The board's own sleep, not the library's

**This firmware sleeps the board itself and does not call `WalterModem::sleep()`.** Task p3-01's
report recommended it and this task read the library again at 1.5.0 to be sure
(`managed_components/dptechnics__walter-modem/src/WalterModem.cpp:4413`):

```
  } else {
    _sleepPrepare();
    vTaskDelay(pdMS_TO_TICKS(1000));
    esp_deep_sleep(sleep_time_s * 1000000);
  }
```

`esp_deep_sleep(us)` arms the timer and nothing else. So the library's sleep would:

- **arm no other source** — neither the radio's line nor anything else could bring the Walter
  back, and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §10 wants the line;
- **tell the radio nothing** — no `SLEEP_NOTICE`, so the RAK goes on expecting us and goes on
  sending events (§8);
- **hold no pad** — the seven pins would go to their reset state across the transition, which is
  rule 5's whole subject;
- **write nothing down** — no journal entry, no retained record, so the next wake would not know
  what the last one did.

What it does do that we now do not: `_sleepPrepare()` copies the library's own PDP, socket, CoAP,
MQTT and BlueCherry state into RTC memory, so that `begin()`'s `_sleepWakeup()` branch can restore
it. We take the modem down before sleeping (`UplinkPolicy::takeDown()`), so there is nothing worth
restoring; `begin()` will still take its `_sleepWakeup()` branch at the next boot, because the wake
cause is not `UNDEFINED`, and **that is what task p3-01's defect 1 and `WalterUplink::present()`
already handle** — it means `begin()` may answer false and the policy goes on with `reset()` and
`checkComm()`. Nothing new was needed here.

## The order around sleep entry and exit

This is the part a scope is pointed at (`docs/bench.md` §14), so it is written out. Every claim
below is cited in the code beside the call.

**Entry** (`core/cycle/sleep.cpp`, then `core/board/radio_pins.cpp`, then
`components/platform/sleeper.cpp`):

1. the journal, when the sleep is worth a line;
2. **`SLEEP_NOTICE`, as the last request** (§8) — last, because every request after it would tell
   the radio we are back;
3. the modem down;
4. `RadioPins::holdForSleep(arm_radio_line)`: `safe()` — nRESET input, SWDCLK and SWDIO inputs,
   KILL not driving, **TX pulled up** while the radio is believed powered, so its receiver does
   not see a break for hours — then the pads held;
5. the wake sources armed, and `esp_deep_sleep_start()`.

**The wake pad is the one exception to rule 5, and the reason is a real one.**
`ext1_wakeup_prepare()` (ESP-IDF v5.5.5, `components/esp_hw_support/sleep_modes.c:2078`) routes
the pad to the RTC function, selects the LP GPIO function, enables its input and — when the RTC
peripheral domain is to be powered down, which is the default — **enables the RTCIO hold itself**.
A hold we had put on first would block those writes: `sleep_modes.rst`, *External Wakeup (ext1)*,
is explicit that `rtc_gpio_hold_dis` comes before "any pin re-configuration". So
`holdForSleep(true)` gives GPIO8 the chip's weak pull-up and leaves that one pad out of our holds;
the chip holds it, in the same state, as part of arming the source. The pull-up is
`rtc_gpio_pullup_en()` and not `gpio_pullup_en()`, because the pull-up in force during the sleep is
the **RTC** pad's — `ext1_wakeup_prepare()` has routed the pad there — and a line without one would
float, which is exactly what a radio that is off, resetting or being flashed would then do to us.
`rtc_gpio_pulldown_dis()` beside it, because a pull-down on a line whose wake level is *low* is a
wake every time.

**ext1 and not ext0.** Both reach GPIO8 (the ESP32-S3's RTC IOs are GPIO0–21:
`SOC_RTCIO_PIN_COUNT 22`, and `esp_sleep.h` says "ESP32-S3: 0-21" of ext0, which is the task's
"GPIO8 is an RTC pin — confirm it", confirmed). ext0 "will work only if RTC peripherals are kept on
during sleep" (`esp_sleep.h`); ext1 "supports wakeup even when the RTC peripheral is powered down"
and uses the pad's hold to keep the pull-up (`sleep_modes.rst`). Keeping a whole power domain alive
for one input pin is current this unit does not have to spend, and the sleep current is what this
task exists for. `ESP_EXT1_WAKEUP_ANY_LOW`, because the radio's TX idles high and a start bit is
the falling edge of it. The non-deprecated call, `esp_sleep_enable_ext1_wakeup_io`, is the one used.

**Exit** (`main/main.cpp`, in this order and commented there):

1. constructing `RadioPins` is `safe()`: the pads' *registers* now hold the state they were held
   in, and nothing on a wire has moved because the pads are still latched;
2. `releaseHolds()`: `safe()` again, then the latches off — the pad is released into what it was
   held at, so it sees no change. **This is what keeps nRESET from glitching across a wake**, and
   it is ESP-IDF's own advice for `gpio_hold_dis`;
3. the sleeper's `begin()`: the wake cause read, and `rtc_gpio_deinit(GPIO8)` — "before using these
   pads as digital GPIOs, users need to reconfigure them by calling the `rtc_gpio_deinit` function"
   (`sleep_modes.rst`, ext1's warning). The UART needs that pad back;
4. `safe()` once more, so the pad that has just come back from the RTC mux is configured by the one
   object that owns these seven pins.

Nothing in ESP-IDF's start-up clears a per-pin RTCIO hold — the only thing that does is
`rtc_gpio_force_hold_dis_all()`, which is called from `gpio.c` and from nothing in the boot path —
so the holds really do survive the wake and really are software's to release. That is why step 2
exists and is where it is.

## The simulated month, over the whole bench

`host_test/core/cycle/test_duty_cycle.cpp`, the phase's own exit criterion ("a simulated month of
wakes — with a modem that drops, a radio that goes quiet and a battery that sinks — in which
nothing runs twice, nothing runs unbounded, and every rail holds"). Each wake is a **boot**: the
Walter is destroyed and built again over one world — the clock, the retained map, the journal's
flash, the scripted radio, the fake modem — which is what a wake from deep sleep is.

```
a simulated month: 130 wakes over 30 days, 130 sleeps, 92 modem wakes,
11 attention wakes, 3 spurious, 14 with no battery reading, 6 journal entries;
bands: plenty 27, normal 46, saving 21, critical 22, no-reading 14; ending at 3000 mV
```

- **130 wakes, 130 sleeps**: every wake ended in exactly one sleep and none was refused. Nothing
  ran twice.
- **nothing ran unbounded**: every wake's own duration is checked against the bound on a wake.
  The Walter's clock in the bench is its *own* — `BootClock` is the world's minus the moment this
  boot began, because ESP-IDF's `esp_timer` restarts at zero at every wake from deep sleep
  (`esp_timer.rst`: "Upon wakeup from deep sleep, the initialization timer restarts from zero") —
  so "how long was this wake" is a real question and not "a month".
- the radio went quiet for a day (14 wakes with no reading, all of them `no-reading` and none of
  them `plenty`); the modem stopped answering from the twentieth wake and the uplink's own backoff
  took over; the line twitched for nothing three times and raised a real attention eleven.
- **6 journal entries in a month**, which is the point of not writing an unremarkable sleep down.

Beside it: a week with the radio silent (168 wakes at the medium interval, `no-reading` throughout,
never `plenty`, no update allowed, the modem allowed), a run of six crash wakes in which the
interval doubles each time and one good wake ends the run, `wake.interval` over the real settings
store, the band and `saving`'s counter crossing a sleep and not a power cut.

## What RTC memory costs

Worth its own section, because it is now the binding constraint and the next task will meet it.

`platform/retained.cpp` gives **every** retained slot `kRetainedRecordMax` (128) bytes plus a
magic, a size and a CRC — 140 bytes a slot, whatever the record in it actually holds. Before this
task six slots were in use and **RTC SLOW had 316 of its 8 192 bytes free**; the rest is the modem
library's own `RTC_DATA_ATTR` arrays (the PDP contexts, the sockets, CoAP, MQTT, BlueCherry), none
of which can be switched off at 1.5.0 and none of which this firmware uses — phase 0's task 01
established that each library switch breaks the build.

So this task takes **one** slot and not two: `kDutyCycle` carries the wake log *and* the band *and*
`saving`'s counter in one 92-byte record, written through one `store()` by
`core::cycle::WakeReason`, and `core::power::IntervalPolicy` keeps nothing at all — it takes the
previous band as an *input*, which is what the task's own wording asks for ("a pure function of
(battery millivolts, …, the previous band, the settings)"). **176 bytes are free now**, which is
one slot short of another slot.

What a later task should do if it needs retained storage: not add a slot, but either join an
existing record or make `platform/retained.cpp` size each slot to what its record needs (it knows
the size at every call; the layout version in its magic word is there for exactly this). Neither is
this task's, and neither is urgent until something wants a seventh slot.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **`docs/bench.md`'s new section is §14, not the "13. Sleep" the task names.** §13 is the
   console's, from task p3-03 — which in turn says the same thing about §12, and p3-02 about §11.
   The section says so in its own first lines.
2. **An unremarkable sleep is not journalled.** The task says "Journal *sleeping until &lt;time&gt;,
   because &lt;band&gt;*", flatly. Taken flatly it breaks the journal: it is 1024 entries in one
   64 KB partition and it exists so that an *update* is never guessed at ("it wraps without ever
   losing the entries of the update in progress", `core/update/journal.h`), and a unit checking in
   every quarter of an hour writes 96 entries a day — the whole log would wrap in ten days and push
   every update and every incident out of it, and a sector would be erased every sixteen hours for
   the life of the unit. So: the *per-wake* record of what a sleep did is in RTC memory, where
   `wake` reads the last ten from and where a per-wake trace belongs, and the journal gets a line
   when there is **news** — the band changed, the interval was lengthened by a fault, the radio's
   line was disarmed or armed again, the wake was a crash or a watchdog, a person typed
   `sleep now`, or the sleep was refused. The simulated month above writes 6 entries in 30 days.
   A host test holds both halves: three sleeps in the same band write one entry, and each kind of
   news writes one.
3. **`core/power/IntervalPolicy` keeps no state across a sleep, and `core/cycle/DutyCycle` exists.**
   The task names four deliverables and all four are there as separate, separately tested units
   (`Sleeper`, `WakeReason`, `IntervalPolicy`, `Sleep`, plus the bound on a wake). `DutyCycle` is a
   fifth file that composes them and holds nothing of its own — it is what `main/` constructs and
   what the console is handed, because the alternative was nine more arguments on a console
   constructor that already has twenty. It does **not** run a wake: what a wake does, in what
   order, is task p3-05's and is not in this branch.
4. **The interface is `components/interfaces/`, not `components/hal/`** — as every task since
   phase 0 has said: ESP-IDF owns a component called `hal` (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §1).
5. **The USB state is said in a line, not in the prompt.** The task says "the console says so at
   the prompt". The prompt is `> ` and stays `> `: `docs/provisioning.md` marks every typed line
   with it and a host test replays that document line for line, `tools/walterctl.py` reads this
   port, and a prompt that changed shape when a cable was plugged in would be a prompt that is
   `usb> ` in every session a person ever has. So the fact is said where a person will actually
   read it — a line of its own at the boot that found a host, `wake`'s *awake* line, `status`, and
   whenever `stay` changes. A host test checks that `usb> ` appears nowhere.
6. **The spurious-wake window is "until the next timer wake", not "an hour".** The task says both:
   "after a few in an hour the line is disarmed … until the next timer wake". A Walter has no clock
   it can trust across a deep sleep (`interfaces/clock.h`; it is why task p3-01's backoff is counted
   in wakes), so the window that *can* be measured is the one the task's own re-arm condition
   names. With the limit at three and the shortest interval a quarter of an hour, that is at most
   three spurious wakes a quarter of an hour — each costing §8's eleven-second window and nothing
   else — and in `saving` or `critical` it is three a day, which is more conservative and not less.
   Said in `core/cycle/wake_reason.h` beside the constant.
7. **`saving`'s "modem only every Nth wake" starts with a use.** The counter is "wakes since the
   modem was last used", and **0 means it has not been used since this band was entered** — so the
   wake that enters `saving` uses the modem (the band change is itself worth reporting) and then
   every fourth. Leaving the band forgets the counter, so a pack that recovers and sinks again gets
   a check-in on the wake it re-enters and not four wakes later.
8. **Charging steadily lifts `normal` to `plenty` and lifts nothing else.** The task's table reads
   as a plain "above the upper threshold, **or** charging steadily"; a cell below the *lower*
   threshold was recently nearly empty, and one sunny quarter of an hour is not a reason to spend an
   attach on it. The band rises on its own as soon as the voltage does. Said out loud in
   `interval_policy.cpp`, in `docs/power.md` and in a test (`fromOne(3500, charging)` is `saving`).
9. **Three things the task did not mention, found by reading, and acted on.** Each is in the code
   with its citation and is the next section.

## Three things reading the documents changed

**1. A deep sleep during a self-update trial would roll the trial back.** This is the one finding
that changes behaviour rather than a number. `CONFIG_BOOTLOADER_APP_ROLLBACK_ENABLE` is on
(`sdkconfig.defaults`, S-6) and `CONFIG_BOOTLOADER_SKIP_VALIDATE_IN_DEEP_SLEEP` is **off** — its
own Kconfig default is `n` and this build does not set it — so after a wake from deep sleep the
second-stage bootloader runs in full, and `select_partition_number()` turns every `PENDING_VERIFY`
slot into `ABORTED` before it picks one
(`$IDF_PATH/components/bootloader_support/src/bootloader_utility.c:396-405` at v5.5.5). A wake from
deep sleep *is* a reset. So a Walter that slept during its own trial would wake in the old image
with the new one condemned, having proved nothing.

`core/cycle/sleep.h` therefore **refuses to sleep while `SelfUpdate::onTrial()`**, with a named
refusal, a journal entry and a console line that says what to do (`self confirm` or `self reject`,
or let the trial's own three conditions run). `host_test/core/cycle/test_sleep_refusals.cpp` shows
it over the self-update bench, whose fake application slots keep exactly the bootloader's rules.
This is also a note for task p3-05: a wake that finds itself on trial has to finish the trial
before it may sleep, and `Sleep::wouldRefuse()` is how it asks.

**2. The full bootloader runs at every wake.** The other side of the same reading: every wake pays
for the bootloader's SHA-256 over the running image before `app_main` is reached. That is energy
per wake that nothing in these piles can measure, and it is on `docs/bench.md` §14's list (the
energy of one whole wake). `CONFIG_BOOTLOADER_SKIP_VALIDATE_IN_DEEP_SLEEP` would remove it and is
**deliberately not set**: it also removes the rollback check that finding 1 is about, and it skips
the signature check on an image coming out of sleep, which S-6 is not willing to pay for a
millisecond of boot.

**3. `gpio_hold_en()` on one of our seven pins is `rtc_gpio_hold_en()`.** `gpio.c:744` routes a
hold on any RTC-capable pad to the RTCIO hold, and all seven of ours are (GPIO1–8, inside 0–21).
That is what makes rule 5 work across deep sleep at all — the digital `gpio_deep_sleep_hold_en()`
is for the *other* pads, which on this board is the modem's own reset pin, held by the library's
`begin()`. Our `holdThroughSleep(…, false)` at the wake calls `gpio_deep_sleep_hold_dis()`, which
clears that global; it is re-enabled by `holdThroughSleep(…, true)` before the next sleep, so the
modem's pad ends up held either way. Read, checked, and left alone.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The bound on a wake is built, tested and left *disarmed*.** `main/` does not call
  `duty.guard().arm()`. Nothing in this firmware reaches sleep by itself — the wake cycle is task
  p3-05 — so a bound that reset the Walter after five minutes would be a boot loop and not a safety
  rail. `arm()` is the one line p3-05 adds; `wake` says whether it is armed; a host test shows it
  ending a wake when it is. The task's claim ("without a USB host, the Walter is **never** awake
  indefinitely") is therefore true of the mechanism and will be true of the firmware the moment
  something sleeps by itself. Said again in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §10.
- **A reset and not a sleep when a wake overruns.** A wake that has overrun is a wake in an unknown
  state — a half-finished download, a flasher mid-page, a modem that answered half a command — and
  a boot is the one way back to a state this firmware understands. The journal is what carries the
  unfinished work across it, which is exactly why the reset is safe (task p2-03's design).
- **`health::Restart::kWakeOverrun`, so the next wake can tell our bound from a person's
  `reboot`.** Both are software resets; only one is a fault, and only one lengthens the next sleep.
- **The band `no-reading` is its own band and never `plenty`**, as the task says. What it allows:
  the modem (a Walter whose radio has died is exactly the Walter that should report it), no update,
  no flash, and the power cycle left to the supervisor, which has a battery floor of its own over
  the last good reading. Nothing that spends charge on the strength of a figure nobody has.
- **The attention comes from the supervisor and not from the link client's queue.** The supervisor
  is the one place in this firmware that decodes an `EV_ATTENTION`, and §8's "acknowledged by the
  next valid request" is its business too; it was written with `takeAttention()` for "whoever runs
  the wake". So `Supervisor::listen()` was added — three lines, polls and records, climbs nothing
  — and `core/cycle/attention_source.h` is the adapter, so the supervisor still knows nothing of
  `core/cycle`. A build with no supervisor hands the wake reason a `nullptr` and a line wake is
  then spurious by definition, which is the honest answer: nothing could have read the event.
- **`DutyCycle::readPower()` sends `HELLO` when there is no session.** `POWER` is a capability and
  the link client refuses a call whose capability nothing has advertised without putting a byte on
  the wire. The first step of a wake opens a session anyway (the supervisor's heartbeat); this is
  what makes `power` work when a person types it on its own.
- **The console's `sleep now` reads the battery first** when this wake has not. "Go to sleep for
  the interval the battery chose" has to ask the battery; sleeping in the band of "no reading"
  because nobody typed `power` first would be a surprise. `power` leaves a decision behind and the
  sleep reuses it.
- **`SLEEP_NOTICE` not going is not a reason to stay awake.** A link that is down, a radio that
  does not advertise `ATTENTION`, a scanner on the other end: the notice is a courtesy to the
  radio's own event-sending, and staying awake is what empties the battery they share. The refusal
  is recorded in the result, printed, and carried in the journal entry's flags.
- **A sleep with neither a time nor a source is refused**, in `Sleep` and again in
  `EspSleeper::sleep()`. "It is also possible to enter sleep modes with no wakeup sources
  configured. In this case, the chip will be in sleep modes indefinitely until external reset is
  applied" (`sleep_modes.rst`) is not a state this firmware may enter by accident.
- **`stay` is bounded at twelve hours.** A bench day, and not a week: a person who forgets
  `stay off` loses the rest of the day and not the unit.
- **`sleep <seconds>` is bounded at a week**, which is `wake.interval`'s own ceiling said in
  seconds.
- **The wake pad gets a pull-up and not a driven level.** The radio's TX is what drives that line;
  a few tens of microamps through 470 Ω is what rule 1 already allows TX itself, and rule 3's
  "every other pin is a plain input with its pull-up off" still holds because `safe()` — which
  every kill calls first — drops it.
- **A crash with a laptop attached is still a crash.** The USB relabelling turns any wake into
  "a person is here" *except* a crash or a watchdog: those keep their reason and keep counting,
  because a panic is not less of a panic for having been watched.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session can put a meter in series with a battery, point a scope at nRESET, or watch a pack
discharge for a month. `docs/bench.md` **§14, *Sleep***, is the walk, in the order the rig is
brought up; these are its items.

1. **The Walter's current asleep, modem off.** The datasheet's deep-sleep figure is the target;
   anything a few times higher is a pin being driven or a peripheral left on, and `pins` before the
   sleep is the first place to look.
2. **A scope on nRESET and on KILL across sleep entry and wake: no pulse.** Two channels, triggered
   on nRESET's falling edge; there should be no edge at all. Then the proof a scope cannot give:
   `rak status` after ten sleeps, with the radio's **uptime still counting** from the same boot.
3. **The radio's line wakes it.** From the repeater's own console, the command that raises
   attention reason 4: the Walter wakes inside §8's window and `wake` says *attention: a person
   asked*, with the detail and which repeat it heard.
4. **A chattering line does not keep it awake.** Unplug the RAK's UART while the Walter sleeps: it
   does not wake, or wakes at most three times and then reports the line disarmed — and a timer
   wake arms it again, with both entries in `journal`.
5. **USB does not wake it, and a boot with a cable stays awake.** Plug the cable into a sleeping
   Walter: nothing happens. Press reset: the boot says a host is here.
6. **The energy of one whole wake with an attach**, and of one that only talks to the radio. The
   pair that turns the policy's placeholders into a table, and the one measurement §14 asks for
   three times over.
7. **The pack's voltage against its state of charge.** The curve the three thresholds are read off.
   Nothing in these piles could even approximate it.
8. **The bands against a bench supply**, walked down and up, with the hysteresis holding.
9. **A day of it** on the battery and the panel with `wake.interval auto`, read off `wake`, `power`
   and `journal`.

Also not verified here and not this task's: eDRX and PSM tuning (a bench pile, as the task says),
GNSS, and what a wake *does* — that is task p3-05.
