# p3-03 — settings and the console

A person with a USB cable can make a Walter theirs — the APN, the SIM's PIN, the server, a token,
a name — and can see everything it knows without seeing anything secret. And the console, which
had grown a command at a time since phase 0, is now one thing: one grammar, one table of commands
with a line of help each, one table of settings, one document held to all three by a host test,
and one 4 kB ring of what the port said that `dump` prints.

**What is proven here and what waits for the bench.** Everything below is the first kind — logic
in `core/`, tested on the host against fakes — except five things that are the second: NVS itself
(`nvs_get_str`, `nvs_set_str`, the commit), the asterisk echo on a real terminal, `status` in an
80-column window, the SWD clock on a scope, and a wrong PIN against a real card. They are
`docs/bench.md` §13.

- [x] **One parser and one command table; no command is parsed anywhere else.** How it was
  checked, in `host_test/core/console/test_console_grammar.cpp`, as a test over the tree and not
  a sentence in a header (after `test_trust_rail.cpp`):

    `components/` and `main/`, drops the comment lines, and counts;
  - **no file but the parser compares a command word**: every code line of `components/` and

- [x] **The report lists what moved from build constants to settings and which protective
  thresholds deliberately did not.** Below, *What moved and what did not*.
- [x] **The secret test covers every command that prints anything.**
  `host_test/core/console/test_console_secrets.cpp` has one line per row of the command table and

### The eleven keys

`lte.apn`, `lte.pin` (secret), `lte.psm`, `server.url`, `server.token` (secret), `fetch.url`,
`wake.interval`, `wake.radio`, `swd.khz`, `log.level`, `unit.name` — exactly the task's table,
with the ranges and fallbacks it names and one deviation (`swd.khz`, below). Every key's range,
pattern, refusal wording and `get` rendering has a test in `test_keys.cpp`; every key is stored,
read back, kept across a simulated reboot and read through its typed getter in `test_settings.cpp`.

### Where a setting actually goes

`set` answers `ok <key>` and then **one line saying what the change reaches**, and that line and
the table's `effect` column and `docs/console.md`'s right-hand column are the same text. The
console is the one place it happens (`Console::settingChanged`), because the console is what holds
the uplink policy, the trial's server seam and the SWD clock:

- `lte.apn` → `UplinkPolicy::apnChanged` (new, two lines): used when the packet context is defined,
  the next time the uplink comes up. Not now: a modem reset would lose a card that had just been
  unlocked.
- `lte.pin` → `UplinkPolicy::pinChanged` (task p3-01 left it there for this): **at once**, and a
  refusal recorded against the old PIN is forgotten. Typing the *same* PIN again moves nothing —
  which is the point, because three refusals block a card. Tested end to end against a fake modem
  whose card wants `1234`: a wrong PIN is sent once and refused, a second `lte up` does not send
  it, a different PIN clears the refusal, and the card then opens on the second unlock.
- `lte.psm` → `UplinkPolicy::powerSavingChanged` (new): asked for at the next bring-up.
- `server.url` → `UplinkCheck::setServer` (new on the seam, implemented by both
  `UnconfiguredUplink` and `TlsHandshakeCheck`): the host a self-update's trial must reach. The
  URL is taken apart by `core/net/url.h`, so a URL the client would refuse leaves **no host**,
  which is "not configured" and the condition does not apply — not a host the trial would try.
- `swd.khz` → `swd::setHalfPeriodInForceUs` (new): a single value in `core/swd/wire.cpp` that
  every `Wire` reads as it is built (the constructor's default is `halfPeriodInForceUs()`,
  evaluated at every call) and that `Flasher::begin` re-reads at the top of every plan. One
  setting therefore reaches the console's three wires, the update machine's flasher and the
  supervisor's without being threaded through three constructors that have no business knowing
  the word "settings".
- `log.level` → at once: the console's **unsolicited** lines (the boot's own, a resumed update's
  resume line, the settings summary) are gated on it; a reply, a refusal, the prompt and a
  supervisor's STOPPED line never are.
- `server.token`, `fetch.url`, `wake.interval`, `wake.radio`, `unit.name` → read where they are
  used; the reply prints the table's own `effect` line.

`erase confirm` runs the same application again, so the fallbacks are in force at once.

## What moved and what did not

**Moved from a build constant to a setting** (the reports of phases 1 and 2 and `policy.h` were
searched for "until settings exist" and "a build constant today, a setting then"):

| Was | Is now | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `uplink::kApn` | `lte.apn` | the constant stays as the **unprovisioned** fallback (empty: the network chooses) |
| `uplink::kSimPin` | `lte.pin` | the constant stays empty and is never a default (S-13) |
| `uplink::kPowerSaving` | `lte.psm` | the table's fallback `off` is held to the constant by a `static_assert`, so a unit with no setting behaves exactly as p3-01 left it |
| `update::kSelfUpdateServerHost` / `kSelfUpdateServerPort` | `server.url` | empty is still "not configured", which is what keeps a factory image from rejecting its own first update (`core/update/uplink_check.h`) |
| `swd::kDefaultHalfPeriodUs` | `swd.khz` | the constant is the fallback, held to the table's `125` by a `static_assert` |

**Deliberately still constants.** Thresholds that protect the hardware: a typo at a console must
not be able to remove a safety margin. Said in `core/settings/keys.h`'s header, in
`docs/console.md` (*What protects the hardware is not a setting*) and here:

- the battery floors for a power cycle and for a flash (`core/update/radio_update.h`,
  `core/supervisor/supervisor.h`);
- the supervisor's daily caps, its quiet period, its boot-loop window and its rung order;
- the proving period after a flash; the Walter's own trial window and its three conditions;
- a wake's budget on the modem, every step's deadline, `kModemCallCeilingMs`, and the backoff's
  arithmetic; the PSM timers asked for when `lte.psm` is on (what the network grants is its own
  business, and a bench measurement decides the ask);
- the reset spacing, the kill bounds and the settle time (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §2);
- the HTTPS client's deadlines, range size and budgets; the TLS roots and the release keys, which
  are generated into the image — **a console cannot change what a unit trusts**;
- the RAT (`kRat`): LTE-M only. The modem can do NB-IoT and a setting could ask for it; nothing
  in this firmware does, and inventing the key would be a key with no reader.

`swd.khz` is the one hardware-facing number that *is* a setting, because a long cable on a bench
is a reason to slow the debug clock down, and `core/swd/wire.h` bounds it on both sides whatever
is typed.

## Commands unified, and what changed for a person

`help` was fifty `print()` calls and the list of commands existed three times — there, in the
parser, and in `docs/console.md`. Now there is one table. What a person notices:

1. **`help` is grouped** (the unit / the radio / images, trust and this firmware's own slots / the
   uplink / the journal) and each line is wrapped to 96 columns instead of running off the screen.
2. **`rak flash-test` is now `rak flash-test confirm`.** It is the one command that writes to a
   radio's flash outside the update machine. Without the word it refuses and says the whole line
   to type. `docs/bench.md` §5 was updated in the same commit.
3. **`rak kill <seconds>` over 5 s wants the word**: `rak kill 30 confirm`. A reset, a short power
   cycle and every rung of the supervisor's ladder are inside five seconds; longer is a person
   deciding to take a repeater off the air. `docs/bench.md` §2 was updated in the same commit.
4. **`status` is one screen** and no longer dumps the seven pins — `pins` is the command that does
   that. It now carries the descriptor (role, board, band, link), the unit's name, the radio's
   session, health and clock, the battery as the radio last reported it, the images in one line,
   this firmware's own slot and trial, the uplink's last attempt, the backend, and the next wake.
   Twelve or thirteen lines, each under 100 characters, held by a test.
5. **`fetch` may be typed with no base URL** and then uses `fetch.url`.
6. `erase confirm` is the **settings**; `image erase <which>` is the **store**. Two things called
   erase is a wart, and the alternative — renaming one of them away from the word the agent
   firmware's console uses — would be worse. Both say which they are in their refusals and in
   `docs/console.md`.

No command was removed and no command's name changed otherwise.

## `dump`, and what is never in it

Every line the console prints goes through `Console::print` or `Console::printf`, and **those are
the only two ways anything of the console reaches `interfaces::TextOut`** — so the ring is filled
by construction rather than by somebody remembering to log. What is not in it:

- **anything the radio sent as text** (S-12: "Nothing the mesh says is logged as text"): a
  scanner's NDJSON line, a CLI reply, an advert's name. They all go through `Console::printText`,
  which writes the bytes straight to the port inside a `LogRing::Quiet`. Tested: a scanner line
  is printed by `rak text` and is not in `dump`.
- **a secret**, because no printed line has one.
- the frames of a binary transfer, which never go through `print`.
- a `dump` of the ring itself, which is written straight to the port.

When the ring has wrapped, the part of the oldest line that survived is dropped rather than
printed as a line it never was, and `dump` says how many bytes have been thrown away.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **`swd.khz`'s fallback is 125, not the 100 the task's table names.** The nRF52840 Product
   Specification gives f_SWDCLK a minimum of 0.125 MHz ("Debug and trace > Electrical
   specification > Control access port"), which is what `core/swd/wire.h`'s default half-period of
   4 µs already was and why phase 1's task 03 chose it over the 100 kHz *its* task named. A
   fallback of 100 kHz would put every unit out of the box below the chip's own specification. The
   **range keeps the task's lower end** (25..1000), because a long cable on a bench is a reason to
   go slower than the chip promises to answer at, and the key's help line says so.
2. **A key is unset only by `erase confirm`; there is no `unset <key>`.** The task's table gives
   `lte.apn` "0..63 characters" and `unit.name` "0..32", which would make an empty value a way to
   unset one. The parser refuses a `set` with no value instead (as the agent firmware's does), so
   the minimum length of every text key is 1. A per-key `unset` is a command the task does not
   list, and the one thing a half-provisioned unit must not do is look provisioned.
3. **`docs/bench.md`'s new section is §13, not the §12 the task names.** §12 is HTTPS, taken by
   task p3-02 — which in turn says the same thing about §11. The section says so in its own first
   lines, as p3-02's does.
4. **The settings' own bench section is larger than the task's two lines.** The task asks for the
   provisioning session, a power cycle, `get`, and `status` fitting a window; §13 has those four
   and six more, because `set swd.khz` with a scope is where the real SWD ceiling is found and
   `set lte.pin` against a real card is the one rail in this firmware that protects something
   irreversible.
5. **`Settings::put`, not `Settings::set`.** The method a `set <key> <value>` calls is called
   `put`, as the agent firmware's `SettingsStore::put` is: `set` as a whole word is what
   `host_test/checks/no_allocation.h` reads as a `std::set` and refuses.

Nothing in `spec/` disagrees with the task here. S-12's rails are the secrets and the mesh's text
(both tested above); S-13's "no default password is ever on the air" is why neither secret has a
fallback.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`log.level` governs the lines nobody asked for, and nothing else.** This firmware has no
  logging framework and no `ESP_LOG` call anywhere; inventing one for a key would have been a
  seam with nothing behind it. What it governs is real and tested: the boot's own lines, the
  settings summary, a resumed update's resume line. `debug` adds nothing yet and
  `docs/console.md` says so.
- **The log ring lives inside the console**, not behind an interface. It is 4 kB of `char` and a
  member, like the console's 12 kB of flasher pages; putting it behind an interface would have
  added a seam whose only implementation would be the same array, and threading it through main/
  would have let a future line bypass it.
- **The PIN's generation is a counter in NVS** (`lte_pin_gen`), beside the keys and not among
  them: nobody sets it and `get` does not print it. A counter and not a hash, because
  `core/uplink/policy.h` is explicit that it must never hold a function of the PIN. `erase
  confirm` **bumps** it rather than clearing it, so that a new PIN cannot inherit the refusal of
  the one just forgotten.
- **A stored value this firmware will not have is ignored key by key**, not rewritten and not
  fatal: that key reads as unset and falls back, the boot says how many were ignored and names the
  first, and the bytes are left alone until a `set` replaces them. A unit never provisioned and a
  unit whose settings are rubbish then behave the same way, which is the state this firmware has
  to survive anyway.
- **`erase confirm` erases the eleven names of the table one by one**, and the `KeyValue`
  interface has no `eraseAll` at all — so "the settings and only the settings" is the shape of the
  code rather than a promise about an NVS namespace. (NVS's own `nvs_flash_erase` is called in
  exactly one place: `NvsKeyValue::begin`, when NVS itself says the partition cannot be read,
  which is a new unit's first boot. It is said out loud in a boot line.)
- **`erase confirm` says `error:` *and* what it did** when the store refuses one key: the rest are
  forgotten, and what is in force is the fallbacks, because a half-forgotten unit must not look
  provisioned.
- **The confirm word is `confirm` everywhere** — `erase confirm`, `rak flash-test confirm`,
  `rak kill 30 confirm` — so that a person who has typed it once knows it everywhere.
- **`rak update --first-install` and `rak role` did not get the word.** They are the update
  machine, which journals every step and rolls back; `--first-install` is already a word a person
  has to type. The one command outside that machine is `rak flash-test`.
- **The SWD clock in force is a module-level value in `core/swd/`.** The alternative was three
  more constructor parameters or a settings pointer in `Flasher`, `RadioUpdate` and `Supervisor`.
  It is one `std::uint32_t`, written by the console and by nothing else, and `wire.h` says so.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session has no board, no terminal, no scope and no SIM. These are `docs/bench.md` §13,
*Provisioning and the console*, in the order the rig is brought up:

1. **The session of `docs/provisioning.md`, typed at a real console.** The replies are held by a
   host test; what a bench adds is the two things a test cannot see — that the value of
   `set lte.pin` and `set server.token` **echoes as asterisks on a real terminal**, and that the
   session reads as a session when a person is typing it.
2. **A power cycle, then `get`: the same answers**, and `settings: N of 11 keys set` the same
   number. Then `idf.py app-flash` (not a merged image) and `get` once more: the `nvs` partition
   is above the application slots, so an application reflash keeps them.
3. **`status` fits a terminal window.** Twelve or thirteen lines, each under 100 characters, on an
   80-column terminal with no wrapping that hides one. Read it once as a stranger would.
4. **`help` fits too, and `dump` says what the boot said.**
5. **A secret is nowhere — and is in the flash.** With both set, read `get`, `status`, `lte`,
   `journal` and `dump`: neither value. Then `esptool.py read_flash 0x610000 0x10000 nvs.bin` and
   `strings nvs.bin`, and **find both of them**. That is what *NVS is not encrypted* means and why
   the boot says so in a line of its own.
6. **`erase confirm`, and what survives it**: the images, the bad list, the journal and the day's
   counts.
7. **`set swd.khz` with a scope on SWDCLK**: 2 µs at 250 kHz, 20 µs at 25 kHz, and 1 µs at 1000 —
   **this is where the real SWD ceiling is found**. Write down the fastest setting at which
   `rak swd id` is reliable over ten runs and put it in §4 beside the other SWD numbers.
8. **`set lte.pin` with the card's real PIN and with a wrong one.** A wrong one: `sim-locked`,
   `lte` says it was refused, and **a second `lte up` does not send it again**. Then the right
   one: the line says the refusal is forgotten and the attach succeeds. Use a SIM you can afford
   to lose, or one whose PUK you have.
9. **`set server.url` and a self-update's third condition**: `self status` names the handshake it
   must complete; after `erase confirm` the same line says the condition does not apply.
10. **`set log.level error`, then reboot**: the boot says only the invitation and the prompt, and
    every command still answers.

Also not verified here, and not this task's: NVS's own behaviour under a power cut mid-commit.
`NvsKeyValue::write` commits before it returns, so a `set` that answered `ok` is on the flash; NVS
is written to survive the cut and this firmware trusts it to, as the agent firmware does.
