# p3-06 — The rails and the bench

Four piles were written by many sessions, none of which saw a board. This task did the three things
a person needs before they sit down with the first one.

**Every promise this firmware makes is now a test or a grep that fails the build, in one place.**
[`tools/check_rails.py`](../../../tools/check_rails.py) has **one entry per rail** of the task's
table, each naming the decision it guards; it runs first in `tools/verify.sh`'s host-tests step,
before anything is compiled, and the four greps that used to live inside `verify.sh` (pin
ownership, the dual adapter's selector, `core/` and the modem library, the TLS rail) are entries in
it now. [`host_test/rails/`](../../../host_test/rails/) is the other half: the assertions that are
about **every test in the suite** rather than about one.

**`docs/bench.md` is one ordered walk** from an empty desk to a day on a mast, in the order the
hardware's own bring-up puts it — which moved the kill line from §2 to §6, after the first flash,
and shifted every section after it by one. It ends with *What to bring back*: **107 named
constants** in 17 files that no measurement has settled, with their current values, and
`check_rails.py` fails the build when that table and the source disagree.

**`docs/field.md` is new** — what a unit on a mast does, for the person who owns the mast — and
`README.md` now reads for someone arriving cold, including the sentence that matters most: *no
board has run this firmware*.

Two rails did not hold and were fixed here. They are in the table below and again under
*Deviations*.

## The fourteen rails

| R1 | nRESET is never output-high | **fixed here.** It held in every test, but it could be *silenced*: a test that set the fake pins' `strict = false` could then drive the pin high and the suite would not notice. Now every `FakePins` counts what it saw into a process-wide tally and, at its destruction, fails the test for any violation **no test declared** with the new `allow(rule)` — whatever `strict` said. Two files declare a rule on purpose and now say so. The grep half gained three checks: that the nRESET pin reaches the driver only through `RadioPins::ResetLine`, that `ResetLine`'s two methods name no push-pull mode and write no high level, and that the ESP-IDF output modes are configured only in `platform/pins.cpp`'s one switch. `host_test/rails/test_rail_pins.cpp` prints the run's tally and holds the undeclared count to zero; in a run of the rails' own test cases alone that is 279 sets of fake pins and 236 091 641 transitions checked, and the assertion the suite is held to is **0 undeclared violations** at every point of the run, because the enforcement is in each fake's destructor and not in this one test |
| R2 | everything radio-facing is an input before KILL goes high; two resets ≥ 1 s apart | **fixed here**, by the same mechanism, and the same tally. ## The failure-injection sweep

`host_test/rails/test_rail_sweep.cpp`. Four suites each have a power-loss loop of their own — the
image store's, the journal's, the flasher's, the radio update's — and each proves its own half.
What none of them proved is that the **handover** between them survives the same treatment. So the
sweep builds one world with all three of a unit's flash devices on it (the `rakfw` partition, the
`journal` partition, and the simulated nRF52's own flash), puts the release on a scripted HTTPS
server behind the firmware's own HTTP over a fake TLS socket, and runs **a whole update from
download to commit** with the power lost at one flash operation, then lets the next wake recover.

A clean run is **13 operations on the image store's flash, 12 on the journal's, and 3 erases and
1 038 word writes on the radio's**.

**39 injection points**: every one of the 13 and every one of the 12, and 14 of the radio's 1 041 —
every erase, the first two and the last two operations, and one word write in 97. The radio's
writes are sampled and not exhaustive, deliberately and with the reason in the file: each injection
costs a whole update through the bit-level SWD simulation, 1 041 of them would be minutes of the
suite's time, and task p1-05's own loop already interrupts **every** operation of a flash
exhaustively — what this sweep adds is the handover, not the flash.

Every one of the 39 ended in one of the three states the task names, and all three were seen:
**33 ended with the new image running**, **6 with the old one**, and of the 39, **31 were taken up
again from the journal**, **6 resumed the download** from the progress record rather than starting
over, and **2 were cut before anything had been journalled**. At every one: the chip holds one
whole image (never half an application), the store's golden image still verifies against its own
hash, no forbidden register was touched, and nothing was erased or written outside the region.

## The bench table

*What to bring back* gathers **107 named constants in 17 files** that no measurement has settled,

1. the **file's** own header says every number in it is a placeholder —

2. a **section** header, or the paragraph of comment under one, says so — `// -- the numbers, each

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **Two rails did not hold, and the task says that is a finding and not something to paper over.**
  1. **R1 and R2 could be silenced.** The fake pins' rules fired at every transition, but `strict
     = false` turned them into a counter nobody read. Two tests use it deliberately (to show a
     rule firing) and nothing distinguished them from a third test that broke a rail quietly. Now
     an undeclared violation fails the test at the fake's destructor, `allow(rule)` is how a test
     declares one, and the two files that need it say so. Nothing in the firmware changed; this is
     a hole in the *test* of a rail, which is where a rail of this kind lives.
  2. **R4 was not asserted by four tests.** "The simulated chip's write log, asserted at the end
     of **every** test that flashes" was true of the flasher's bench and the update's bench and of
     nothing else. The chip now refuses to be flashed by a test that has not said which region may
     be touched, and that change failed four tests in `test_slot_dual.cpp`,
     `test_console_flasher.cpp` and `test_nrf52_forbidden.cpp` on the first run. All four were
     flashing where they were meant to; the rail was simply not being asserted. Each now declares
     its window, and the declaration says in words what the test is about.
- **The bench walk's section numbers changed, and the earlier reports' references to them did
  not.** Moving the kill line to §6 shifted §§7–16. Fourteen references in code and current
  documents were updated; the nineteen task reports were **not**, because a report is the record
  of what a reviewer read at the time and rewriting one would make the record lie. `docs/bench.md`'s
  preamble says the numbering is the walk's and that the task files' own numbers are not, and this
  report's mapping table is the translation.
- **R9's one line in the task's table is narrower than the rail.** "Nothing the mesh or a scanner
  said is logged as text" cannot mean *never shown*: `rak text` exists to show a person the
  scanner's lines, exactly as `lte id` exists to print the subscription's numbers, and
  `ARCHITECTURE.md` §3 designed it that way. The rail as implemented and as tested is **never
  written down**: not in the log ring, the journal, the retained records, or any summary. The test
  excuses `rak text` and then checks that `dump` does not carry what it printed.
- **R6 cannot be an entry that checks the thing itself.** The rail is about bytes in a built image
  and symbols in a linker map, and `check_rails.py` runs before the build. Its entry therefore
  holds `verify.sh` to doing it — by name, so that deleting the check fails the rail — and does the
  one source-level part that is possible. Said in the rails table above and in the script's own

- **The sweep samples the radio's word writes.** The task says "every flash operation of a whole

## Decisions taken without asking

- **One script for the rails, and the greps move into it.** The task asks for `tools/check_rails.py`
  "run by `tools/verify.ps1`; one entry per rail". Leaving the four existing greps in `verify.sh`
  would have meant two places, so they moved verbatim in what they refuse. `verify.sh` lost 5 740
  characters of inline shell and gained one line.
- **An entry for a rail held elsewhere still checks something.** For R6, R8, R9, R10, R11 and R13 —
  rails whose real holder is a test or a built artefact — the entry checks that the holder is still
  there and still contains the markers that say it does its job. A rail that could be lost by
  deleting a file is not a rail.
- **`allow(rule)` rather than a list of blessed files.** A test that wants to see a pin rule fire
  says so in the test, beside the `strict = false` that it already needed. The alternative — a grep
  for files allowed to set `strict = false` — puts the knowledge in the wrong place.
- **The chip's region declaration is mandatory, not advisory.** A test that flashes and declares
  nothing fails. This is a behaviour change to a simulator four tests depended on; the alternative
  (defaulting to the `rak4630-single` profile's region) would have hidden the gap instead of
  showing it.
- **`kChipWriteStride = 97`** in the sweep, with the first two and last two operations and every
  erase always taken. A prime, so that the sample does not line up with any page or run boundary.
- **The bench table's completeness is a build check, not a report paragraph.** The criterion asks
  for a grep; a grep in a report is true on the day it is written. Entry **B** is the same grep,
  every commit.
- **Two headers extracted so that two tests can walk one thing**:
  `host_test/fixtures/command_lines.h` (every command of the console's table with something to
  type after it — task p3-03's secrets test and rail R8 both need it, and the table must not be
  transcribed twice) and `host_test/core/supervisor/supervisor_month.h` (phase 2 task 05's
  property run as a function of a seed and a number of days, so R11 can give it a longer horizon).
  Neither changes what the existing tests assert.
- **`host_test/rails/` relies on doctest's file ordering only for the numbers it prints.** doctest
  orders test cases by file path, so `rails/` runs after `core/` and the tallies printed are the
  whole suite's. Every *assertion* is order-independent: the undeclared counts are zero at every
  point of the run, and the per-test enforcement is in the fakes' destructors.
- **The three bench steps that matter most** are §5, §9 and §15. The task named them ("the first
  flash; the deliberate bad image; the scope on nRESET across sleep"); the walk marks them and the

## What will be wrong first

The honest list the task asks for: what in this firmware is most likely to be wrong when the first
board arrives, hardest first, with why. Every item is a thing a host test cannot reach.

1. **The SWD wire's timing through the bulkhead filters.** 470 Ω and 2.2 nF on each line is a time
   constant near 1 µs, and this firmware clocks the port itself at 125 kHz with the target's bit
   read at the end of the low half — a sample point chosen *because* of the filters and never seen
   on a scope. The simulator is bit-exact against Arm's document and says nothing about an RC. If
   anything here is wrong, `rak swd connect` fails or, worse, succeeds intermittently, and every
   flash above it is unreliable. `docs/bench.md` §4's second item is the measurement, and it is
   the first thing to do after the console answers.
2. **The Adafruit bootloader's settings page.** The flasher writes `bank_0 = BANK_INVALID_APP`
   before the first erase and a valid record with `bank_0_crc = 0` after the read-back, from
   reading two bootloaders' sources. `spec/SYSTEM.md` §10 item 3 is open precisely here. If the
   bootloader a RAK4630 actually ships with accepts a different record, a perfectly flashed radio
   lands in its UF2 bootloader and looks bricked — and the first person to see it will think the
   flasher is wrong when the record is. §5's fifth item, and `INFO_UF2.TXT` is where its version
   is.
3. **How long a real repeater takes from a reset to its first `HELLO`.** `kBootWindowMs` is twenty
   seconds, guessed. Too short and every good candidate rolls back — a unit that refuses every
   update looks broken and is in fact being careful. Too long and a wake spends the difference.
   §9's first item measures it, and it is the number most likely to need changing on the first day.
4. **The deep-sleep current, and whether a pad is left driven.** Every claim about the holds across
   deep sleep rests on reading `sleep_modes.rst` and `gpio.h`; the one pad deliberately left out of
   our holds (the wake pin, which ESP-IDF holds itself as part of arming ext1) is the kind of
   detail that is right in the document and wrong on the board. A few hundred microamps too many
   and the interval table is a fiction. §15's first two items.
5. **The attach time, and through it every budget in the wake.** `kRegistrationDeadlineMs` is two
   minutes and `kWakeBudgetMs` two and a half, both from QuickSpot's example rather than from a
   Danish LTE-M cell. If a cold attach is four minutes, a wake cannot afford one; if it is twenty
   seconds, every number in `core/cycle/wake_cycle.h` is too generous and the battery pays for it.
   §12's second item is three cold attaches.
6. **The modem's TLS actually validating.** The rail is held three ways in the source and not once
   against a server. §13's third item — a host whose chain ends at a root this firmware does not
   carry must be **refused** — is the only thing that can tell "the profile validates" from "the
   profile was configured and ignored". A 200 there is the most serious possible finding in this
   repository, and nothing before the bench can rule it out.
7. **ESP-IDF's otadata behaving as it was read to.** *pending-verify* becoming *aborted* at a
   reset is the whole mechanism of "a trial is one boot". The fake keeps the bootloader's rules as
   `bootloader_utility.c` was read to describe them; if the real bootloader differs, a trial that
   should roll back is confirmed instead — which is the one failure this design has no second net
   under. §10's fourth item.
8. **The power-up glitch, and whether the supervisor's grace is long enough.** The ESP32-S3
   glitches GPIO1–14 low for about 60 µs at power-up and that reaches nRESET, so a Walter power
   cycle reboots the radio. The supervisor knows and waits `kWalterPowerUpGraceMs` (twenty
   seconds). If a real RAK takes longer to come back through the filters, every power cycle of the
   unit opens an incident that is not one. §11's fourth item.
9. **The pack's discharge curve.** Not wrong so much as unknown: three thresholds read off the
   shape of a LiPo curve in general. A unit that enters `saving` too early is a unit that checks
   in four times a day for no reason; too late and it browns the repeater out. §15's seventh item
   is the one measurement nothing in these piles could even approximate.
10. **The console in an 80-column window.** The least serious and the most certain to be slightly
    wrong: a host test counts the characters of `status`, and a real terminal with a real prompt
    and a real line ending will wrap something. §14's third item, and it costs one commit.
