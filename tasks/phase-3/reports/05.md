# p3-05 — The wake cycle

Everything built in phases 0 to 3 became one firmware: a wake that runs the same ten steps in the
same order, each with a precondition that is **data**, a bound in seconds, and an outcome that is
written down — and then sleeps. A unit with no cable on its port runs one pass and goes away; a
unit with one stays awake and the console runs beside it, and `cycle now` and `cycle dry` are how a
person sees what a mast does by itself.

And it ends at a seam. `interfaces/backend.h` is one method, a plain structure of what this unit
knows, and a closed set of six requests **this firmware already knows how to carry out by
itself**. The only implementation in this repository answers *nothing to do*, every wake, for
ever (`core/cycle/null_backend.h`). Nothing in it names MeshMaster, a URL path, a JSON field or a
job, and the mesh client's calls are not used at all — a simulated month of 2 400 wakes holds the
radio to that.

**What is proven here and what waits for the bench.** Everything of `core/cycle/` is the first
kind: logic, on the host, against a bench of simulators — a scripted radio that goes silent, runs
NoiseScope or is being flashed; a fake modem with and without coverage; a battery that sinks
through a fortnight and recovers; a scripted backend that asks for everything in the closed set
and for things it may not have. Nothing of this task is of the second kind: **it added no line of
`components/platform/` and no line of `components/modem/`; `main/main.cpp` grew by 67 lines, of
which all but one are construction and comment.** What waits for
the bench is the one thing no simulation can give — a day on a battery and a panel, with a radio
on the other end of the seven wires (`docs/bench.md` §15).

## The ten steps

This is `kSteps` in `core/cycle/wake_cycle.cpp`, which is the only place the preconditions are
written down. `needs` is read as "every one of these must have **succeeded**"; a step whose
dependency did not is *skipped* and the record names the step it was waiting for.

| | Step | `needs` | `bound_ms` | `least_ms` |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | `why` | — | 11 300 | 0 |
| 2 | `radio` | — | 40 000 | 1 000 |
| 3 | `resume` | `radio` | 1 200 000 | 1 000 |
| 4 | `state` | `radio` | 15 000 | 1 000 |
| 5 | `band` | — | 1 000 | 0 |
| 6 | `uplink` | `band` | 150 000 | 20 000 |
| 7 | `clock` | `state`, `uplink` | 10 000 | 1 000 |
| 8 | `backend` | `uplink` | 60 000 | 30 000 |
| 9 | `update` | `band` | 1 200 000 | 1 000 |
| 10 | `sleep` | — | 15 000 | **0** |

Four rows carry the design, and a test asserts each of them so that a change has to come through
this table:

- **step 10 needs nothing and its least is zero.** Every wake ends in sleep; a wake that cannot
  sleep is a battery the repeater needed.
- **step 5 needs nothing.** "No reading at all" is its own band, because a Walter whose radio has
  died is exactly the one that has to reach its uplink and say so.
- **step 2 succeeds whatever it finds.** A scanner, a silent radio and a radio mid-update are
  named states of the wake, not failures of the step that found them out.
- **no dependency points forwards**, which would be a wake that could never run.

The record's letters — `y` done, `-` nothing to do, `s` skipped, `t` no time, `r` refused,
`x` failed, `.` not reached — are `docs/cycle.md`'s table, and a host test holds the document to
the enumeration both ways.

## How the closed set was chosen

Six members, and the rule that produced them: **an ask is in the set if and only if this firmware
already has a tested procedure for it.** Not "if a server might want it" — that is how a seam
grows a protocol — but "if the code to do it is here, with its own refusals and its own journal".

| Ask | The procedure it names | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| fetch a release | `core/update/fetch.h`: the manifest, its signature, checks 1–3, the image into the candidate slot in resumable ranges, checks 4–6 over the flash | task p3-02, and the console's `fetch` already drives it |
| update the radio | `core/update/radio_update.h`: flash, boot, prove, commit or roll back | task p2-03, and `rak update` already drives it |
| update the Walter | `core/update/self_update.h`: the other slot made the next to boot, one boot to prove itself | task p2-04, and `self switch` already drives it |
| change a setting | `core/settings/settings.h` through the table of keys | task p3-03, and `set` already drives it |
| climb the ladder | `core/supervisor/supervisor.h`'s five rungs with their caps | task p2-05, and `rak ladder` already drives it |
| nothing | — | the answer a unit that reports to nobody gets, which is this firmware's own default |

So the set is exactly **the console's own acting commands, minus the ones that are a person's**:
`rak flash-test`, `rak kill`, `rak reset`, `rak cli`, `erase confirm`, `self confirm`, `self
reject`, `rak time set`, `reboot` and `sleep` are all things somebody with the board in hand does,
each with its own reason — a chip's flash outside the update machine, a repeater taken off the
air, a clock forced backwards, a trial ended by hand. None of them is a thing a server should be
able to do over the air, and leaving them out costs nothing: a person at a console can still do
all of them.

Two shapes were deliberately **not** chosen:

- **A command string.** `exchange()` could have taken text and run it through the console's
  parser, which would have been twenty lines and would have handed whoever implements the seam
  every command there is, `erase confirm` included. The closed set exists so that an implementer
  *cannot* make this firmware do something it has no tested procedure for.
- **One ask per exchange.** The answer carries a *set*, because S-7's test is "a backend that asks
  for both updates in one wake gets one" — which is only a test if both can be asked for. The wake
  cycle is what decides the order and what fits.

## What an agent will need that this does not offer

The honest list, because it is what phase 4 has to add rather than discover:

- **Nothing of the mesh.** No job, no login, no CLI line over the air, no discovery, no trace, no
  observation pull. The link client has all of it from phase 1 and the wake cycle calls none of
  it; a test runs a month of wakes and holds the radio to it. That is `spec/proposals/01` and
  `03`'s work and it is not here.
- **No job identity, no lease, no progress, no result envelope.** An ask is carried out or refused
  inside the wake it arrived in, and what happened is in the next exchange's `known`. A server
  that needs to correlate a request with a result carries its own id outside this interface —
  which is exactly the shape `spec/proposals/02` describes (`twinoak.fw.offer` / `.apply` /
  `.state`) and exactly what should not be guessed at here.
- **No byte-level progress during a download.** A fetch that stops on its budget leaves the bytes
  and the store's progress record and resumes at the next wake; nobody is told how far it got
  until the next exchange tells them how many bytes are in flash.
- **No `nextCheckInS` and no `maxTransactionsThisWake`** (`proposals/01` B and C). The interval is
  the battery's and this firmware does not negotiate it. What it does offer is
  `known.wake.next_interval_s`, which an implementation may send as `nextCheckInS` — the number is
  there, the negotiation is not.
- **No asks across a sleep.** A refusal survives the sleep and is reported; the ask does not. A
  backend that still wants something asks again at the next wake. Said out loud because it is a
  design decision and not an omission: a firmware that remembered an ask across a reset could be
  made to do something nobody asked for twice.

## The simulated month

`host_test/core/cycle/test_wake_month.cpp`, one deterministic seed, a pack that is full for two
days, sinks to the floor over a fortnight and recovers; a radio silent in 6 % of wakes and running
NoiseScope in 3 %; a modem that is not there in 8 % and never registers in 12 %; a backend that
takes the readings in 20 %, asks for the radio's update in 3 % and for the ladder in 2 %; and §8's
attention on 10 % of wakes.

"A month" and "a few thousand wakes" are not the same number — a month of quarter-hourly wakes is
2 880 and a month of daily ones is 30 — so this walks the wakes, puts the pack's own month over
them, and lets the simulated days fall out:

| | |
|---|---:|
| wakes | **2 400** over 132.2 simulated days (**18.2 a day**) |
| modem attempts | **2 211** — the rest were skipped by the band or the backoff |
| exchanges with the backend | **1 762** |
| seconds awake | **55 576 in all, 421 a day** |
| the longest wake | **293.3 s of its 360 s bound** (a wake that climbed the ladder *and* lost its modem: steps `yy-xyxss-y`) |
| updates asked for | 59 |
| **flashes** | **0** — every one refused by the update machine's own preconditions |
| climbs of the ladder | 69 |
| journal entries | **810** — see below |
| readings dropped / taken | 87 / 339 |

**810 journal entries in 2 400 wakes** wants a word, because deviation 2 is about exactly this
number. This month is deliberately hostile: a fifth of its wakes lose the modem, a tenth lose the
radio and one in twenty cannot read the battery, so a step fails in a third of them and a failed
step is news. Six entries a day at that rate is 170 days of history in a 1 024-entry ring, and an
update *in progress* is protected from the wrap by `Journal::protect()` whatever else is being
written. A healthy unit writes **nothing**: `test_wake_record.cpp` shows three quiet wakes in the
same band writing no entry at all, which is the steady state the deviation is about.

Every one of the 2 400 wakes ended in sleep; none exceeded its bound; no step of any wake was left
*not reached*; the supervisor's three caps held at every wake; no pin rule was broken and the pins
were safe at the moment of every sleep; and **no request in the `MESH_CLIENT` or `OBSERVATIONS`
range was ever put on the wire**.

A second month, with a modem that never registers: **16 modem attempts in 200 wakes** — the
growing backoff of `core/uplink/failure.h`, honoured by the wake cycle.

The 421 seconds a day is the number `docs/bench.md` §15's last item exists to confront with a
meter. It is a *simulated* figure: it is the sum of what the fake modem, the scripted radio and
the policy's own deadlines say a wake costs in time, and it says nothing at all about current.

## What RTC memory costs, again

Task p3-04 left a note and this task spent it. `platform/retained.cpp` gives **every** retained
slot `kRetainedRecordMax` (128) bytes plus a magic, a size and a CRC — 140 bytes a slot, whatever
the record holds. There were 176 bytes free; this task's one new slot (`kWakeCycle`) took 140, and
**36 are free now**. There is no eighth slot.

So the note has to be repeated, sharper: **the next task that wants retained storage cannot have a
slot.** Its two ways out, in order of preference:

1. **Size each slot to its record.** `platform/retained.cpp` knows the size at every call and the
   layout version in its magic word is there for exactly this. The seven records in use are
   between 16 and 124 bytes and sum to about 540, so the fixed layout is wasting roughly 440
   bytes — more than three slots. The work is a capacity per slot (declared in
   `interfaces/retained.h`, static_asserted by each owner) and a byte pool with offsets in
   `platform/retained.cpp`. It was **not** done here: it touches the platform file that cannot be
   tested on the host and six owners' assertions, for a task that needed one slot and had one.
2. **Join an existing record**, which is what this task did with the refusals: they are not a
   record of their own, they live in the telemetry ring's, and the header says why.

The binding constraint is not this firmware's own records — it is the modem library's
`RTC_DATA_ATTR` arrays (the PDP contexts, the sockets, CoAP, MQTT, BlueCherry), about 7 300 bytes
of RTC SLOW that nothing in this firmware uses and that cannot be switched off at `walter-modem`
1.5.0 (phase 0's task 01 established that each library switch breaks the build). Reclaiming those
is a different and much larger piece of work — a patched or vendored library — and is not a thing
to start for 440 bytes.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **The interface is `components/interfaces/backend.h`, not `components/hal/Backend`** — as every
   task since phase 0 has said: ESP-IDF owns a component called `hal` (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §1,
   task p0-01's deviations).
2. **The wake's record is in the log at every wake and in the journal only when the wake has
   news.** The task says "one compact line per wake in the log **and the journal**", flatly. Taken
   flatly it breaks the journal, for exactly the reason task p3-04 gave about sleeps: 1 024
   entries in one 64 KB partition that exists so that *an update is never guessed at*, and a unit
   checking in every quarter of an hour writes 96 records a day — the whole log would wrap in ten
   days, push every update and every incident out of it, and erase a sector every sixteen hours
   for the life of the unit. The task's own sentence is the argument: it asks `journal` to be "a
   page a person can read" after a week on a mast, and 672 identical lines are not a page. So the
   line goes to the console's log (and the 4 KB ring `dump` prints) at every wake, and to the
   journal when there is news — a step failed, was refused or ran out of time; the band changed;
   the ladder was climbed; this firmware was on trial; an exchange happened; an update step or a
   fetch happened; the wake was a crash or a watchdog. The simulated month writes 128 entries in
   1 200 wakes. A host test holds both halves.
3. **Setting the radio's clock is not news.** It follows from 2 and is worth its own line, because
   it surprised this session: §5.4 has the Walter set the clock "after every `HELLO` whose
   `boot_id` it has not seen", and a Walter out of deep sleep has seen **none** — its RAM is gone.
   So step 7 sets the clock at *every* wake, by the specification, and if that were news the
   journal would wrap as in 2. It is a flag on an entry that exists for another reason.
4. **`docs/bench.md`'s new section is §15, not the "14. A day" the task names.** §14 is the
   sleep's, from task p3-04 — which says the same thing about §13, and p3-03 about §12, and p3-02
   about §11. The section says so in its own first lines.
5. **The wake's budget is two and a half minutes, not the uplink policy's four.** `core/uplink/
   policy.h`'s `kWakeBudgetMs` is what the modem may have when nothing else wants the wake; a wake
   has nine other steps and a five-minute rail. The uplink step may still spend the *whole* of the
   wake's budget — which is task p3-01's own rule, unchanged — and a wake whose modem really does
   take all of it then has *no time* for the backend, recorded as `t` rather than hidden.
6. **A climb of the ladder and an update are outside the budget, and raise the bound on the
   wake.** The task sanctions this for an update in so many words ("a flash may take several
   wakes' worth of time and is allowed to hold the wake open up to its own bound"). It had to be
   extended to a climb, and the reason is in the next section.
7. **A wake that is restarting does not also sleep.** The task's step 10 is unconditional. But a
   self-update's switch and a trial's rejection *restart the board* — on the board the call does
   not return, and on the host it does — so step 10 records `a restart was asked for: this wake
   ends in a boot` and calls no sleeper. `Ending::kRestarting` is one of the five endings and the
   simulated month asserts that nothing in it ends that way.
8. **`cycle dry` evaluates and does not rehearse.** The task says "prints what a pass would do
   without doing it". A dry run therefore puts no frame on the link, touches no modem, writes no
   journal entry and takes no decision the next real sleep would use: it reads the state as it
   stands, evaluates each precondition as a forecast and says so. A test checks all five of those
   nots.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **A climb may hold the wake open, and why it had to.** The supervisor's ladder waits 30, 60 and
  120 seconds between its rungs, and **the state of a climb does not survive a sleep**: only the
  counts, the window and a stop are in retained storage and the journal (task p2-05's design).
  So a climb that were cut off by the wake's budget would start again at rung 1 at the next wake,
  for ever, inside its caps — the worst of both. It climbs to its end inside its wake, up to a
  six-minute bound, and the time is outside the budget so that the task's own requirement for a
  silent radio still holds: "the uplink still used to say so".
- **A classification is not a climb.** §9 has four kinds of silence and three of them are
  explanations the supervisor does nothing about. The bound is raised as soon as the supervisor
  has anything in hand (a bound is a maximum, and a classification does not reach it), but
  `climbed` — which is what the record and the journal say — is read off the incident's cause.
  A test shows a radio being flashed by somebody else *not* counting as a climb.
- **One update step a wake, and the Walter's own goes first.** `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §7 has the
  Walter lead a link-version change, and its own update ends in a restart anyway — so the radio's
  happens at the next wake, if this one confirmed, which is the task's own test. The one that does
  not go is refused with a named reason, kept across the sleep and told at the next exchange.
- **The trial is the update step of a wake that finds itself on trial.** Task p3-04's finding was
  that a deep sleep during a trial is a rollback of the image being tried, and that
  `Sleep::wouldRefuse()` is how a wake asks. So step 3 notes the trial and raises the bound, the
  trial's three conditions are met by the wake's own steps (the main loop, the radio, the server),
  and step 9 drives it to an end before step 10 may sleep. A wake that cannot finish a trial ends
  in a restart or in the bound — **never in a sleep**, which a test asserts by counting sleeps.
- **The settings an answer asks for are applied in step 8 and not step 9.** They cost nothing,
  touch no pin, no modem and no flash, and they are part of the answer rather than a thing to be
  done with it. Step 9 is what the task reserves for "at most one update step".
- **A secret is never set over the seam, and a threshold is not a setting at all.** The first is a
  flag on the key's row (`core/settings/keys.h`); the second needs no code, because the battery
  floors, the supervisor's caps, the proving period, the trial window and a wake's budget are
  build constants and not keys — so asking for one lands in "no such setting", which is the
  refusal the task asks for. Both are tested, and the test checks the cap afterwards.
- **The telemetry ring is in retained storage, ten readings deep, and the journal was refused with
  the numbers.** Four readings to a 48-byte journal payload is 24 entries a day at a quarter of an
  hour, which wraps the log in six weeks and erases a sector every two and a half days — in the
  partition whose whole purpose is the one thing that must not be lost. A reading is ten bytes
  (the epoch, the millivolts, the current, four flags and the temperature **to the degree**) and
  ten of them fit what is left of one 128-byte record. The ring is *expected* to overflow, which
  is what the task asks for: oldest dropped first, and a dropped count that tells a backend what
  it missed.
- **The refusals share the telemetry ring's record**, because a slot costs 140 bytes of RTC memory
  whatever it holds and there was one slot left. The header says so. A unit in `critical` does not
  use its modem at all, so "the next exchange" can be days away and a refusal that did not survive
  the sleep would be one nobody ever hears about.
- **The unit's identity is asked once a boot, and only on a wake that has an uplink.** `IDENTITY`
  is one exchange on the link (§5.6) and the only reason to ask is to tell somebody; a wake with
  no uplink asks nothing.
- **`known.walter.version` is `core::version()` and the descriptor is read from the image's own
  96 bytes.** Two fields rather than one, because they can disagree — a build whose version
  definition and whose descriptor were made at different times is a build worth noticing.
- **The structure that crosses the seam carries no secret and nothing that identifies a
  subscription.** It is `BackendKnowledge` read as bytes and searched for both secrets and the
  fake modem's IMEI, ICCID and IMSI, in a test. S-12's rail, one layer further out.
- **A `const char*` of static text is the only `why` a step keeps.** No buffers, no formatting in
  a step: the record line is formatted once, at the end, into the caller's buffer.
- **The two simulators were made truer, and both changes are findings in their own right.** The
  scripted radio now keeps §5.4's clock — set over the link, reported by `STATUS` and `TIME_GET`,
  **lost at every restart**, because the first-batch boards have no RTC — and the fake modem is
  told where the Walter's own monotonic clock began, because the deadlines it is handed are on
  that clock and a bench that walked more than one wake was handing it deadlines in its own past.
  The second was a real bug in the bench (every modem call in a second wake timed out at once)
  and it would have hidden every multi-wake uplink behaviour this task exists to test.
