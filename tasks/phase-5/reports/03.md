# p5-03 — `rak provision`

The command `spec/SYSTEM.md` S-5a describes, on this firmware's own layers: a person reads a
factory radio's `DEVICEID` with `rak swd id` (or is told the radio is `locked`), types it back
with the word, and the Walter erases the chip through its CTRL-AP, writes the base from its
store, sets the UICR words, reads every word back and resets the radio once — through task
p5-02's door, which nothing else can reach. Every refusal comes before the first write; every
failure after it leaves a radio that the line `rak provision check` says to type finishes. The
slot then *awaits its first install*: the supervisor leaves the silent radio alone until
`rak update --first-install` commits, which takes the candidate and makes it golden.

What came of it, in one line each:

- `core/provision/provision.{h,cpp}` — the sequence, seven steps, each a name in the result:
  check, attach, variant, erase, write, verify, reset. `check()` for the console's `rak provision
  check` (nothing written), `run()` with a grant for the typed line; a `StepListener` so the
  console prints each step as it ends. The door's one caller (rail R3b).
- `core/provision/variant.{h,cpp}` — the table: INFO.VARIANT read as text, its build code's
  first character A–D legacy, F improved, anything else refused; the sources in the header.
- `core/supervisor/` — *awaiting its first install*: a fifth kind of silence that is not a
  fault, kept in the retained record and in every journal entry's flags, marked by the sequence
  before the erase, cleared only by a committed `--first-install` newer than the mark.
- `core/console/` — `rak provision check` and `rak provision <DEVICEID>|locked|locked:<key16>
  confirm`, the grant made from the typed word in `console.cpp` and nowhere else; `rak watch` and
  `status` say when a slot awaits its first install.
- `host_test/sim/` — the chip's power-loss countdown counts the CTRL-AP erase and UICR writes;
  the SWD target can fall silent after N transfers; `losePower()` is public.
- `host_test/core/provision/` — 40 test cases over a bench that has everything `main/`
  constructs (the console with the sequence, the supervisor, the update machine, the wake cycle)
  on task p2-05's world with the chip in each factory state.
- `docs/provisioning.md` *A factory radio* (a session replayed by a host test), `docs/console.md`
  *The radio's base*, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §1, §4, §8, §12, `bench.md` §4a, `supervisor.md`,
  `update.md`, `field.md` corrected, `README.md` and `CLAUDE.md`'s never lines.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **IN-141 and Nordic's compatibility matrix could not be read; the table cites what could.**
   Every URL for IN-141 and for the nRF52840 compatibility matrix answers 404 or 403 from this
   machine (as task p5-02 found for IN-141). The table's sources, each read for `variant.h`: the
   Product Specification's *Access port protection* ("Dxx and earlier" hardware-only, "Fxx and
   later" hardware-and-software, which makes the kind a function of the build code's first
   character), its FICR chapter ("last two letters of Package Variant and first two characters of
   Build Code, encoded in ASCII"), Nordic's APPROTECT note ("revision 3, build codes Fx0"), two
   DevZone threads (a Nordic engineer reading `QIAAD0` as revision 2 with D the hardware revision
   code; the four characters as variant, hardware version, production configuration) and the MDK
   header on this machine (8.30.2: `AAAA`, `AAAB`, `AABA`, `AABB`, `AACA`, `BAAA`, `CAAA`). So the
   table keys on the build code's first character alone and does not insist on an `AA` prefix as
   the bench tool's `protectKind()` does — `BAAA` and `CAAA` are Nordic's own values with another
   package variant — and it refuses a word that is not two upper-case letters, a letter and a
   letter or digit before it looks anything up.
2. **The sequence's headers live under `include/core/provision/`**, as every header of `core/`
   does (p5-02's deviation 2), and rail R3b — the grep and the test — now allows that directory
   beside `components/core/provision/`. Found by the first `verify.ps1` run, fixed in the script
   and in `test_nrf52_forbidden.cpp`, and the rail's own output names both.
3. **"The slot lock not free" is an update's run in hand or the supervisor's climb.** With one
   slot the `SlotLock` nests a second take of the same slot (that is what lets the link client
   work inside the sequence's hold), so the lock itself is only ever *busy* for another slot —
   refused too, and unreachable on a single-radio unit. What holds the radio in practice is the
   update machine with a run in hand and the supervisor with a climb in hand; both are refused at
   step 1 and both are tested. The ARCHITECTURE paragraph says how the supervisor learns of a
   provisioning: the mark goes down before the erase, and the core runs in one task, so nothing
   steps the supervisor or an update while the command runs.
4. **HELLO is asked in step 1, before the battery, not in step 2.** The battery is read over the
   link (`POWER_GET` needs a session, which HELLO opens), so the link is asked once, first, and
   the answer — and IDENTITY's key, when something answered — is what step 2 names a closed radio
   by. The task's "Closed: ask the link for HELLO" is kept in substance; the cost is one HELLO per
   command, 4 s of the link's three tries and the text probe on a factory radio.
5. **"The same command, typed again" is the line `rak provision check` says.** After a power cut
   the right word can change: an improved part with something in flash and `UICR.APPROTECT` not
   yet `0x5A` reads *closed* at the power-on, so the `DEVICEID` that opened it is refused and
   `locked` is the word; a legacy part locked by its firmware reads open after its UICR was
   erased, so `locked` is refused and the `DEVICEID` is the word. The power-cut tests type what
   `check` says, a separate case shows both changes, and `docs/provisioning.md` says so.
6. **The session is replayed by its own test, from a block marked `console-provision`.** The
   existing replay (`test_provisioning_doc.cpp`) runs every ```` ```console ```` block of
   `docs/provisioning.md` on a rig without a simulated chip, where `rak provision` could only be
   refused. The new block is fenced differently so that the old replay skips it, and
   `test_provision_console.cpp` replays it on the provisioning bench the same way, with the same
   `...` rule; a missing block fails that test with the transcript the console would give.
7. **The mark goes down before the erase, not after a successful provision.** A Walter that
   restarts between the erase and the end would otherwise find a silent radio and climb — a power
   cycle on an improved part would close the port again, and rung 4 would try the golden image
   back. The mark is set the moment the sequence is about to erase (after every refusal that
   leaves the radio untouched), so a refused command marks nothing and a cut-short one leaves the
   radio alone until it is run again.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The variant table is strict and the simulator's model is not** (p5-02's deviation 5 holds):
  `E` and anything after `F` is refused here even though the silicon's "Fxx and later" would
  make a `G` improved; a part this table has never seen gets a person's attention, and a test
  shows the table extended.
- **Marking the slot lifts a stop in force** (the counts stay). A stop is about the radio that is
  being erased; the radio after the command is a new radio, and a supervisor stopped on *the chip
  is protected* would otherwise sit through the quiet period on a radio that is fine now.
- **The console asks the supervisor to notice a first install the moment `rak update` ends**
  (`noticeFirstInstall()`, in `printUpdateOutcome()`), so that `rak watch` after the command says
  the mark is gone; `begin()` and every `step()` read the journal for it too, which is what
  clears it after a first install the wake cycle resumed, or one committed just before a restart.
- **`rak provision check` reports every finding of step 1, not the first.** The store, the
  battery, the kill line, an update or a climb in hand, the link and the radio are all looked at
  and printed; what the command *would* refuse is one more line. `check` is how a person learns
  what to push and what to type, and a radio is read whether or not the base is there.
- **A port that answers after the reset and then stops is `kLookFailed`**, like no port at all:
  the base was verified before the reset either way, and the console says so. Found by the
  transfer-cut test, which cut inside the look's three reads and found the run "ok" with an
  empty `NRFFW[0]`.
- **Runs through the door are at most 256 words** — the memory access port's 1 KB auto-increment
  block — flash and UICR never mixed in one run; the verify reads the same runs back and names the
  first differing address, the base's word and the radio's.
- **The simulator** counts the CTRL-AP erase and each UICR word in the power-loss countdown (the
  erase cannot be half done in a simulator that erases a whole array; it is erased and then the
  power goes); the SWD target's `silent_after_transfers` and `on_silenced` are what cut the power
  at a read; `losePower()` is public for that; the update bench's world is wired to the chip's
  pin reset in the provisioning bench, as p5-02 said the task that needed it would do.
- **`kBootloaderSettleMs = 1500`** after the pin reset, the bench tool's figure, a placeholder in
  `bench.md`'s table.
- **The console's `Provision` is a member, ~11 KB of static storage**, for the reason the flasher
  is one; the battery reached it as a new last argument of the console's constructor
  (`&battery` from `main/`, nullptr — *unknown*, accepted — in every rig that does not pass one).
- **The timing, from the simulator's clock at 125 kHz** (`test_provision.cpp`, *the timing*): the
  48 KB fixture base, 12 320 words, wrote at **447 µs a word** and verified at **866 µs a word**
  (a read is two transfers). For a real ~180 KB base (46 080 words) that is about **21 s to
  write, 40 s to verify**, the erase under a second (tERASEALL 169 ms typical), 1.6 s for the
  reset and the bootloader, 4 s for HELLO's three tries on a factory radio: **about 65 s of debug
  port**, plus what the board cannot show the host — the base's 98 passes (49 to write, 49 to
  verify) over ~450 KB of HEX out of the Walter's own flash, which is `image verify base`'s
  milliseconds times 98. The expectation for the bench is **a minute and a half**; the bench
  tool said "under 1 minute" with its image in RAM and no verify of the UICR by a second pass.
