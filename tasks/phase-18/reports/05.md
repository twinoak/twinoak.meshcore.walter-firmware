# p18-05 — A flash that wrote nothing

The second NoiseScope session on the unit at Bellevue (23 November, meshmaster.dk job 41878)
measured nothing. Its journal: *flashing-scanner* at 11:37:41Z, **`rolling-back: the flasher did
not finish`** twelve seconds later, the whole golden image flashed back in 6 min 26 s, *restored*
at 11:47:25Z — nine minutes off the mesh, no line measured, every step `notRun`, and `rak update`
saying of the scanner image that it *failed its health check (the flasher did not finish) and is on
the bad list*. By the times of every flash that unit has made, **that flash had changed nothing on
the chip**.

**What this task did.** A failed flash now says what failed — the flasher's step, the status of the
layer under it, the address and whether the chip was changed — in the journal's entry, at the
console, in a report and in the note that goes to the server with the job. A flash that ended
having written **nothing** is made once more, after a pause and after the radio's `HELLO` where the
first try had halted it; one second try and never a third, on every kind of flash. Twice, and the
run ends `refused` with the radio exactly as it was: no flash of any kind, no outage, nothing on the
bad list. And the bad list is held to the sentence the specification puts it under: an image goes on
it for its own fault or its proof's, never for a debug port that gave up.

`tools\verify.ps1` **green in 505 s**; `core_tests` **1 522 cases and 825 477 assertions**, all
passing. The image is **864 479 bytes against main's 859 463** in the same tree, **+5 016**; rail
R22's tightest big allocation has 6 116 bytes to spare against main's 6 156, and the heap the main
task's stack comes out of has the same 15 636 bytes in both.

Read against the nRF52840 Product Specification v1.11 and Arm's ADIv5 (IHI 0031G), in the order a
flash meets them. **No bound was changed**: the task's rule is *change a bound only with the
specification quoted; mend nothing by guess*, and nothing I read says any of these is wrong.

**The last two columns, and a disagreement with the task.** The task's framing is that each flash
now "begins on a chip that a pin reset started and whose application is running; before 01 the chip
stayed in debug interface mode from one flash to the next". The second half of that is **not what
the code did**: before task p18-01 `finish()` issued `SYSRESETREQ` and *then* called `close()`,
which withdraws the power-up request — and "once the debugger stops requesting power via
CxxxPWRUPREQ, the device is back in normal mode" (PS *Debug Interface mode*). So a flash has always
left the chip in normal mode with its application running, and rows 1 and 3 have always met a chip
outside debug interface mode. Two things **are** new since p18-01, and they are what the column
would have been for:

- a **pin reset** resets the *Debug* components, all but the SWJ-DP, and the retained registers
  (PS 5.3.6.8's *Reset behavior* table), where a soft reset resets CPU, Peripherals and GPIO and
  nothing else. So since p18-01 every flash's connect meets a DAP whose debug logic was reset since
  the last flash — `C_DEBUGEN` clear, `DEMCR` clear — where before it met one left as the previous
  debug session had configured it. That can only make rows 1–3 easier, not harder;
- on a **build code Fxx** module whose firmware does not write `APPROTECT.DISABLE`, a pin reset
  closes the access port ("remains disabled until" a pin, power or brownout reset, PS *Access port
  protection*), where a soft reset did not. So row 5 can now read *protected* on a module where it
  never did — p18-01's own deviation 1.3, and it is reported at row 5 as `kProtected`.

**Which I would suspect, and why.** In order:

1. **Row 11, the scan's reads.** Twelve seconds of flash is about 26 kB of reading, which is the
   scan's first six or seven pages — the arithmetic of `FOLLOWUPS.md` lands exactly here. A flash
   read stalls the bus while the NVMC is busy, the only bound is ten WAITs a transfer, and the
   repeater firmware writes its own flash (its file system) — which the application has just been
   started by a pin reset to do. **This is what the new simulated knob models**, and the step it
   produces is `scan` with `swd wait-timeout`, which is the one word that would settle it on the
   unit.
2. **Row 8, the 300 ms before the first flash read.** 3.5 × one erase's typical time is not 3.5 ×
   a *sequence* of erases: a file system doing its housekeeping at boot holds READY low for much
   longer, and the Product Specification gives no maximum for tERASEPAGE at all ("varies according
   to HFINT accuracy"). The step would be `connect` with `nvmc-timeout`.
3. **Row 10, the SoftDevice read.** The same stall as row 11, one read earlier; it would read
   `softdevice` with `swd wait-timeout`.
4. **Row 3, the power-up's hundred polls** — least likely: the debug power domain is not the
   application's, and 100 ms is generous. Worth naming only because it is the one bound on this
   path measured in time rather than in retries.

The journal now distinguishes all four by name, which is the point: the next session on the unit
says which of them it is, or says nothing because the first try went through.

## The specification, for deliverable 5

`spec/SYSTEM.md` S-5 (~L95–99) says *"The Walter keeps a known-good image of the RAK's firmware in
its own flash and puts it back when a new one fails its health check"* — the bad list is not in that
sentence at all. It is in §6's drawing (~L218–224), and **under the health check's *no***:

```
              flash candidate over SWD (application region only)
                                    │
                    reset ─► HELLO within the window, version and role as flashed,
                             link steady, clock set, the radio initialised
                              │ yes                          │ no
                              ▼                              ▼
                 candidate becomes golden        flash golden back; the candidate's hash
                                                 is remembered and never tried again
```

*The candidate's hash is remembered and never tried again* hangs off the arrow out of the health
check, and off nothing else. So an image that never reached the chip, or whose flash the debug port
gave up on, is outside that sentence: it never ran and it never failed a health check. Nothing in
S-5 or in the drawing says a flash that failed puts a hash on the bad list, and nothing forbids
flashing the golden image back without putting one there — which is what a failure after the first
write does now. **S-5 as written forbids none of this**, so there was nothing to stop and say.

Where the line falls, in code: `flasher::blamesTheImage()` is true for exactly two steps, `kImage`
and `kDigest`. `kImage`'s every way out is a statement about the file — empty, not page-aligned, not
inside the region, more pages than the chip has, bytes the store would not read — and `kDigest` is
the image's proof: the SHA-256 of what came back off the chip is not the one the record gave.
`kReadBack`'s own mismatch is **not** the image's: the bytes went in and the flash did not hold
them, which is the chip's. Everything else — the slot, the lines, the debug port, the identity, the
SoftDevice, a page that would not take, a core that would not halt — is the unit's.

## The bounds (deliverable 9)

The second try and its pause are inside the time a pass, a session and a wake are given, and the
arithmetic is in `docs/update.md` and in `core/cycle/wake_guard.h`:

| | Before | Now | Why |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `kFlashWroteNothingMs` | — | **3 min** | what a flash that wrote nothing costs before it gives up. Every way out before the first write is a bounded wait of milliseconds (table rows 1–10) **or the scan**, which is the only long one: 2 048 transfers a page at about 0.9 s, so the 424 kB image of 23 November is 106 pages and a little over a minute and a half. Three minutes is that with room for a bigger image and for the idle pauses |
| `kFlashAgainPauseMs` | — | **5 s** | the pause between the two tries. Not a reset and not a reconnect: on 23 November the next flash met the same chip two seconds later and went through, so what it is for is an erase the application had in flight finishing — 85 ms typical |
| `kSecondTryMs` | — | **3 min 25 s** | the two above and one boot window, for the radio the first try may have halted and started again |
| `kOnePassMs` | 19 min 40 s | **23 min 05 s** | arithmetic: the flash window, the second try, the boot window, p18-04's second window, the proving period |
| `kWholeRunMs` (an apply's bound) | 39 min 20 s | **46 min 10 s** | two passes |
| `kSessionBoundMs` | 60 min 35 s | **67 min 25 s** | two passes, the ceiling, the scanner's watchdog |
| `kBackendBoundMs` | 61 min 35 s | **68 min 25 s** | the session and a minute |
| **`kRaisedBoundMax`** | 65 min | **70 min** | **raised, and this is the saying so**: the exchange's bound did not fit sixty-five, and the static assertions of `core/cycle/wake_cycle.h` are what hold it to the arithmetic. A raised bound is still a bound and still not a spend — `arm()` is five minutes, and only a machine that says what it needs raises it |

A flash that gets as far as writing is never tried again, so the second try is **added to** the
flash window rather than doubling it: the worst case of a pass is a first try that got nowhere
(`kFlashWroteNothingMs`), the pause, the `HELLO` window, and then a whole flash.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **The task's premise about debug interface mode is not what the code did**, and the table above
   says so with the Product Specification's sentence: `finish()` has withdrawn the power-up at the
   end of every flash since task p1-05, so a flash has never left the chip in debug interface mode.
   What p18-01 really changed for the path before the halt is that a **pin reset** resets the debug
   components where a soft reset does not, and that on an Fxx module whose firmware does not write
   `APPROTECT.DISABLE` the pulse closes the access port. Neither is a reason to change a bound.
2. **The simulated chip has one knob that is not "before the halt"**, `kPageWillNotTake`, and
   deliberately: the other half of deliverable 5 — that a flash which **did** change the chip is
   still followed by the golden image and still not blamed on the image — needs a failure a
   console-driven `rak update` can run end to end, with the golden image's own flash behind it
   clean. Every knob is scoped to a number of debug sessions, so one flash fails and the next does
   not. The nine that *are* before the halt are what deliverable 7 asked for.
3. **A first install whose flash wrote nothing twice now ends `refused`, where it ended `stuck`.**
   A first install has no golden image to return to, so `fail()` used to make it `stuck` with *no
   image to return to*. With nothing written the radio is exactly as the run found it, and `refused`
   is the honest word; `stuck` was true as well, but it said *the radio has no proved application; a
   person's business now* about a run that had touched nothing. Two tests of task p5-03 asserted the
   old word and are edited (below). The golden image's **own** flash on a rollback keeps `stuck`, as
   the task asks.
4. **`status().flash_tries` is the flash in hand's, not the run's.** A run may make two flashes (the
   candidate's and the golden image's), and each gets its own two tries: the counter is reset at
   every transition **into** `flashing` or `rolling-back`, which is what makes "one second try a
   flash" rather than "one a run". The journal is where a run's whole story is, and
   `countWith(story, kFlashOnceMore)` is what the tests read for "was any flash made twice".
5. **A line of the story is a `StepResult` with `changed` true and `before == after`.** The second
   try's journal entry is written in the state the machine is already in (as task p18-04's look
   already did), and a caller that posts a job's lines to a server has to hear of it. So
   `enter()`'s `changed` is no longer "the state moved": `printUpdateStep` prints such a line
   without an arrow, and `firmware_jobs` writes it as a journal line with the step word of the
   state it is in. The alternative — a second flag on `StepResult` — would have needed every caller
   to learn it.
6. **`SessionStep` gained two fields** (`said`, `detail`) for the one line of the *update*

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The second try lives in `RadioUpdate::stepFlashing`, not in the flasher.** The task asks for one
  second try on *every* flash this firmware makes, and the flasher would be the one place that
  covers them all — but the journal has to say so **before** the second try and the flasher has no
  journal. `Flasher::flash()` has exactly one caller in this firmware, `stepFlashing`, and the
  candidate's flash, the scanner's, the golden image's, a first install's and the supervisor's
  (which drives this machine with `Kind::kRoleRepeater`) all go through it — so one place still
  covers every flash, and it is the place that can write a line.
- **The HELLO before the second try is bounded by `kBootWindowMs` and then given up on.** A flash
  needs no link, and a radio that will not speak is the thing a flash is for; waiting longer would
  only hold the lines. The note says which happened.
- **`wrote_nothing` is read off the counters** (`erases == 0 && words_written == 0`), not off a
  step's name, so a path added later cannot forget to set it. The cost is that a successful flash of
  an image the chip already holds also reports *wrote nothing*, which is true and is asserted.
- **Six hex digits for the address in the server's note** (`0x026000`): the chip is a megabyte, so a
  seventh says nothing, and the note has seventy characters.
- **`flashFailureLine` says *what was read back did not match*** where the nRF52 layer's status is
  `kOk`. A `kFailed` with `kOk` under it is the flasher's own comparison — a page, the whole image or
  the settings record read back and not matching — and printing *ok* there would be a lie about the
  one thing a person reading the line wants.
- **The second try's line goes to the server; p18-04's look's lines still do not.** Only
  `kFlashOnceMore` is passed through the session and written by the apply. Widening that to the
  look's three reasons is a change to what a job's page carries and is not this task's.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port and no board is on this machine. `docs/bench.md` §2 carries the new item, in
the order the rig is brought up:

- **A flash made to fail before its halt** (the new item, before the *flash a repeater image* one).
  With SWD wired and a candidate in the store, `rak update` with the **SWD jumper pulled for the
  first few seconds** — during the `swd:` lines and the first *reading the pages the image covers*
  lines, before the core is halted — then put back. What to bring back: that the console prints
  `update: flashing-candidate: the flash did not begin: once more after a pause`, that the run then
  ends `rak update: done:`, that the radio's **own console keeps answering throughout** (the core was
  never halted), and **the step and the status `rak update status` names** — on the wire they will be
  the debug port's, and which one tells a jumper pulled from what the unit met on 23 November. Then
  the same with the jumper left out: `error: rak update: the flash did not begin, twice (…)`, nothing
  flashed back, the candidate **not** on the bad list in `image list`, the radio still serving, and
  `rak update` again running. And ★ the other half: if the jumper comes out after *erasing, writing
  and verifying the pages*, the chip **was** changed and the golden image goes back with
  `rak update: restored: the candidate's flash failed (…) and is NOT on the bad list` — note which of
  the two happened.
- **`kFlashAgainPauseMs` and `kFlashWroteNothingMs`** have rows in *What to bring back* (now 122
  constants): how long the chip really needs between the two tries, and how long a flash that wrote
  nothing really costs — which on the wire is the scan.
- **The next session on the unit** is what the task's *What only a board can show* asks for, and it
  is Thomas's from meshmaster.dk: either it runs at its first try and 23 November was a moment, or
  its journal says *the flash did not begin (the step, the status): once more* and goes on — and that

  and a newer image has another hash, which this task does not change) and a session with

- **The 46 min 10 s and 70 min above are arithmetic, not measurements.** The bench item for a
  rollback timed end to end now reads against 46 min 10 s, and the figure it brings back is what says
