# p18-06 — A refused session reports

NoiseScope session 5 on the unit at Bellevue (24 November, meshmaster.dk job 43526, Walter
0.1.12+e018a62) ended after eighty-eight seconds: *flashing-scanner* at 13:13:34Z, the first try's
failure at 13:14:07Z, **refused** at 13:15:01Z — the ending task p18-05 had built, with the radio
never off the mesh. Then nothing. The result said `"outcome":"refused"` with no `refusal`, the door
answered *A refused session names which precondition stopped it* and kept nothing of it, and an hour
later the job read ***the exit node took this job and never reported a result***, which is not what
happened.

**What this task did.** Every refusal a unit can make now reaches the server in a word the contract
has — `flash` for an apply and a session alike, `journal` where none was sent — each failed try of a
flash has its own journal line and the ending has its own sentence, no line this firmware writes
into a journal can be cut, a session's result says what it was from wherever it is posted, and a
result the server's door gives its verdict on is settled rather than owed for ever. The script of
the server holds the door's rule about a refusal, so a result with no word fails a test here rather
than a session on a unit.

`tools\verify.ps1` **green in 488 s**; `core_tests` **1 538 cases and 841 687 assertions**, all
passing (run by hand in the image for the counts). The image is **865 744 bytes against main's
864 608** in the same tree, **+1 136**; rail R22's tightest big allocation has 6 060 bytes to spare
against main's 6 116, and the heap the main task's stack comes out of has the same 15 644 bytes
with the same 15 636-byte block in both.

## Deliverable 6: what the firmware did, and what it does now

**What the door answers**, read in meshmaster.dk at 13b192e (`Transport/AgentProtocol.cs`,
`ResultAsync`, and `Web/Api/RegisterApi.cs`, `AnswerAsync`, which maps its outcomes to HTTP):

| answer | when | status |
|---|---|---|
| `Invalid` | the envelope's own fields; **the contract of the type** — a unit report's (`UnitReportContract`) and a session's (`NoiseScopeSessionContract`): the record's shape, an outcome or a step ending nobody can read, a `steps` list that does not match the program, a journal over its entry bound, **a refusal that is not in the list, and a `refused` session with none** | **400** |
| `NotFound` | no such job; or this exit node never attempted it (`AttemptedAsync`) | **404** |
| `Conflict` | the `clientId` was used for another job | **409** |
| `Done` | taken — **including for a job that has ended**: `late = !(Leased && mine)` is recorded on the row and answered as `late: true`, and the result is kept | **200** |

So a job that has ended takes the result anyway; the three 4xx are the door's verdict on the body,
and the same bytes would be answered the same way at every wake for ever.

**What the firmware did with one.** `ModemUplink::postJobResult` answered `false`, which is the
library's *not taken*: `JobRunner::post` pushes the body into its offline buffer, posts it again at
the next flush of that wake, and — because that buffer is kept across the sleep — again at every
wake after it. The firmware's own journal entry was already `kEnded`, so nothing of this was
written down anywhere: `agent jobs` said nothing, the journal said nothing, and the only record was
a counter of failed calls. On 24 November the result was posted once and the wake ended, so nothing
was seen; a session whose result is **owed** (a restart between the ending and the post) would have
been posted and refused at every wake for as long as the unit ran.

**What it does now.** A status that is the door's verdict on the body — 400, 404 or 409, named in
one place (`ModemUplink::resultIsTheServersVerdict`) — is counted, remembered for the wake, and
answered `true` to the library, so that it neither keeps the body nor posts it again; and the
`ResultGate` is told. `FirmwareJobs::resultNotTaken` then **settles the job's entry**: a
`kNotTaken` entry with the status, written after whatever the job's last entry was, so that
`resultOwed()` — which reads the newest agent entry — finds it and no wake posts again. `agent
jobs` prints a line when the count is not nought. A 401, a 403, a 429, a 5xx and no answer at all
are owed exactly as they were.

*a 401, a 429 and a 5xx on a result leave it owed, as they were* and *a session's result the server
answers 503 stays owed, and the next wake posts it again* are the two tests that hold the other
half.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **The fixture's clock and address cannot be the bench's**, so the test substitutes those two
   and holds everything else byte for byte. The five `at` stamps would mean pinning how long each
   call of a wake takes in simulated time; the address `0x036000` is where that chip's SoftDevice
   puts the application, and every generated image of this suite loads at `0x026000` with the
   simulated chip's SoftDevice saying so, so the note cannot read anything else without a new
   fixture image. Everything the contract is about — the members, their order, their values, the
   five step words and the five notes — is held.
2. **Six refusals of an apply, and nine of its call sites, carry no word, and the test names them
   one by one** rather than asserting a word for every one. Deliverable 2 asks for a word of *the
   contract's list* for every refused ending; `FirmwareRefusals` after meshmaster.dk's task 260 has
   eight words and none of them means *the slot holds nothing to flash*, *the image does not
   verify*, *it is not this board's* or *the payload is not one this firmware can read* — and that
   task's own deliverable 2 keeps ***the unit gave no reason*** as a case the server prints, so
   inventing a ninth word here would be a word the contract has not, which the same sentence of this
   task forbids. So the test holds a **partition**: a refusal either has a word of the list or is one
   of the six reasons / nine sentences named in the test, and one that is in neither fails it. A
   **session's** half — which is what 24 November was about — is exhaustive with nothing excepted.
3. **The two journal lines made of numbers are bounded by what the numbers can be**, not by what a
   `%lu` can hold. The room line with five figures of `4294967295` is 86 characters, and the trial's
   uplink line with two of them is 83; the room line's own bound was written for this chip (*every
   figure of either is under a megabyte on this chip bar the PSRAM's own*,
   `core/update/scanner_session.h`), and a trial's window is minutes with one heartbeat a wake in
   it. The test uses six digits a heap figure, eight megabytes of PSRAM and five digits of the
   trial's two counters, and says so where it does. The alternative — clamping the figures where
   they are printed — is code for a line no unit can write.
4. **One journal note can still be cut, and it says so**: a panic line comes off a coredump and is
   up to `core::health::kPanicMax` (95) characters of a person's text, not a sentence of ours. The
   result's own `panic` member carries it whole with `panicCut` beside it, so the journal line ends
   in `...` where there was more rather than ending mid-word.
5. **`noMemory` is already in the server's list.** The task's *What went wrong* says *the same door
   meets `noMemory`, a word it has never had*, and meshmaster.dk's task 260 says *a session it
   refuses for want of memory says `noMemory`, a word the list does not have*. At 13b192e
   `NoiseScopeRefusals.All` is `[Battery, Link, Clock, InProgress, NoGolden, NoScanner, Program,
   NoMemory]` — it has it. Nothing follows for the code: deliverable 1 asks for `noMemory` *as it
   is*, and it is unchanged.
6. **A result the door refuses is answered `true` to the library**, which is the one honest reading
   of an API that has no third answer — the same reading task p4-05 made for a result the gate
   withholds, and for the same reason: `false` means *keep it and send it again*, and the library
   keeps a body across the sleep. The journal's `kNotTaken` entry is where *the server did not take
   it* is actually written down, and `ModemUplink::resultWasRefused` is how this firmware's own two
   owed-result paths tell a result that went from one that did not (they count neither
   `owed_posted` nor a `kPosted` entry for it).
7. **`JobEntryState` gained a fifth value and `JobEntry` two bytes of its spare four.**
   `kNotTaken = 4` with the status as a `std::uint16_t` at offset 46. An entry written by an older
   firmware reads `http_status` 0, and `decode` takes the new state; a `kNotTaken` entry read by an

8. **`agent jobs` prints its new line only when the count is not nought.** Five lines of counters

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The verdict is the three statuses the door actually gives**, named in one place and nothing
  wider. A rule of *every 4xx but 401, 403, 408 and 429* would settle a 422 or a 413 the door does
  not give today and cannot be read against its source; a 404 is in because the door gives it for a
  job this exit node may not see, which no wake of this unit will change.
- **`resultNotTaken` settles whatever entry the job has**, found by walking back for its id, and
  settles nothing at all for a job with no entry (a state job, a report, a mesh job) — those have
  nothing owed, so the line and the counter are the whole of it.
- **The refusal is remembered for the wake in the uplink and not in the journal.** Four ids, which
  is more than the jobs of a wake; past that the oldest is forgotten and a body is posted once more,
  which costs a call and tells the server nothing new. What carries the verdict over a sleep is the
  journal entry.
- **The step's word, not a shorter phrase, for the rolling-back line.** Shortening
  `describe(ProveStep)` would have made the console's own lines poorer for the sake of a journal
  note; a word is what a journal line wants and a phrase is what a person at a port wants.
- **The session's refusal word is read off `status().refusal`**, which the machine sets when it
  enters `Stage::kRefused`, rather than being worked out by the caller from the update machine's
  reason. The machine is where a session's refusals live, and `rak session` gets the word for
  nothing.
- **A session's result never carries the word `none`.** `core::update::word(kNone)` answers *none*,
  which is a word the contract has not; `refusalWord()` answers nothing at all for it.
- **The test reads two source files at run time** (`radio_update.cpp` and `firmware_jobs.cpp`) to
  count the refusals, as p16-04's own test reads its task file for the contract's lines. A table of
  names in the test is what turns a name into a value, and a name the table does not know fails the
  test with a sentence saying so.
