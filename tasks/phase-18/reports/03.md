# p18-03 — a step is listened to

The fifth step of the first session a unit ever ran — 21 November, Bellevue, meshmaster.dk job
39506, `hist 869.418 869.818 25` — sent sixteen lines numbered `seq` 245 to 260 and a `done`
numbered 261. The server holds 245 to 251 and 261. Nine measurements are nowhere, and the step's
result said *done, 9 lines*.

Both halves of the cause were on this side of the UART, and both are gone:

- **the moment.** A step is now entered by one call of the machine — the journal entry, the stage
  `step`, the command as the entry's body — and its command is said by the **next** call. In the
  moment between the two the scanner is silent and the step's seconds have not begun, and that is
  where the job writes its *step N* line and posts it and sees to its lease. From the command until
  the step has ended the job makes no call to the server at all: not a progress post, not a lease,
  not a line. `ScannerSession::listening()` is the one definition of that interval, the three
  methods of the job that can reach the server during a session ask it before they call anything,
  and rail **R25** holds them to asking.
- **the ring.** `kRxBuffer` was 1 024 bytes with the note that *a task away for a tick loses
  nothing*. A task away for a call to the server loses nine lines of a `hist`. It is now 4 096,
  by arithmetic from the baud rate and the longest the reader is away, with the numbers and two
  `static_assert`s in `core/link/client/port.h` — and the driver is installed with the event queue
  that says when a ring filled or a FIFO ran over, so a loss that happens anyway is counted and
  said.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The count is of the moments, not of the bytes.** The task asks for *bytes that are lost all
  the same are counted and said* and for a step to say so *with the count*. Neither end can say how
  many bytes went: ESP-IDF's `uart_event_t::size` is documented as *UART data size for UART_DATA
  event* and is nothing for `UART_BUFFER_FULL` or `UART_FIFO_OVF` (read in the pinned image at
  `components/esp_driver_uart/include/driver/uart.h`), the ISR drops the stash it could not push
  and resets the hardware FIFO without counting (`src/uart.c`, around L1304 and L1371), and the
  hardware counts nothing. So what is counted — everywhere, on the board and in the simulator
  alike — is **how many times** the port lost what had arrived, and every line that prints it says
  so in those words (*the port lost bytes 3 time(s)*). What the unit at Bellevue was missing was
  not a number; it was any word at all that something had been lost. The simulator could have
  counted bytes and deliberately does not expose them as the firmware's figure (it keeps
  `lost_bytes` for a test's own use), because a test that asserted on a number the board cannot
  produce would be proving a fake capability.
- **The journal entry of a lost step carries no command.** A journal slot is 48 bytes: eight of
  header and forty of command, exactly, with nothing left over. The entry that ends a lost step
  carries the two bytes of the count instead, with the step's `StepEnd` in the flags' bits 4..6
  where the entry's header has always said a `StepEnd` would be. The command is in the entry that
  **opened** the step, which is the line before it in the journal, in `rak session`'s print and in
  the result's `journal` array — so nothing is actually unsaid.
- **`kRxBuffer` is now `core::link::client::kRxRingBytes`.** The task names `kRxBuffer` in
  `components/platform/uart.cpp` §1. The number and the arithmetic live in `core/` instead,
  because what sizes the ring is the session loop's idle and not the port, and because the
  simulated port must use the same figure and cannot include a platform header. `uart.cpp` keeps a
  `kRxBuffer` that is `static_cast<int>(core::link::client::kRxRingBytes)` and names no number of
  its own.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The simulated port's wire and ring are one switch, off by default, turned on where a scanner
  is scripted.** Deliverable 5 asks that the port behind `sim::ScriptedRadio` hold as many bytes as
  the driver's and lose the rest, and that a scanner's lines arrive by the clock and not all at
  once. The two are one thing: a ring without a wire that takes time loses bytes no port loses — a
  whole 7 600-byte line in a single instant — so bounding the ring while bytes still appear
  instantly made `test_link_client_say`'s *the longest line this scanner can send* fail, and
  letting bytes through at the baud rate for **every** radio made 19 cases of
  `host_test/core/link/client/` fail, because every test of the codec, the exchange and the events
  is written on the fiction that what was queued at a moment is readable at that moment — and is
  right to be: a frame is 246 bytes and arrives inside one tick of the client's polling. The
  acceptance criterion says the link's tests pass unedited. So `wire_rate` is off by default and
  `bench::ScriptedScanner::install()` turns it on: a scanner is the only thing on this link that
  says more than a frame at a time, which is where the fiction has to end and the only place it
  does.
- **`kReaderWorkMs` is 50 ms, and it is a guess.** The reader's absence is the loop's idle, which
  is known (`kListenIdleMs`, 100 ms), plus what the loop does between two reads, which since this
  task is one call of the machine and nothing else — no server call falls there any more. Fifty
  milliseconds is a FreeRTOS tick and the slack of a task that is not the only one on the chip. It
  is deliberately **not** marked as a bench constant (it would then need a row in `docs/bench.md`'s
  *What to bring back*, and it is not a threshold a person sets): the bench item below says what to
  do if the ring turns out to be short, and `kReaderWorkMs` is the number to raise.
- **`kLeaseAfterStepMs` is three minutes.** What a step's lease must cover beyond the step itself:
  the posting of its lines (one post of 8 192 bytes for the small room, eight for the big one) and
  the calls that follow. A `static_assert` holds the longest step plus this margin inside one
  ordinary extension, which is why the five-minute timer never has to fire inside a step.
- **The guard asks the machine, not a cached flag.** A step's lines are handed to the job from
  inside `step()`, so a flag the loop set from the previous answer would still say *in a step* at
  the one moment the lines are meant to go out. `quietInAStep()` reads
  `parts_.session->listening()` every time, and R25 holds it to that.
- **A refused call is counted, not silently skipped.** `calls_in_step` cannot rise if the loop's
  shape is right. It exists because on 21 November the loop that lost the nine lines was doing
  nothing wrong at the time, and a rule that only one place holds is a rule one edit can lose.

   `0 step(s) lost bytes at the port` and `rak text` `the port lost bytes 0 time(s)`.

   range the server holds. It means 4 096 bytes is not enough for what that scanner sends at that
   moment, and `kReaderWorkMs` is the number to raise.

What a host test cannot say, and these two can: whether the ESP-IDF driver's event queue actually

unit's `heap:` line where this report expects it — the first `heap` of a session on the board is
worth writing down beside the `room:` line.
