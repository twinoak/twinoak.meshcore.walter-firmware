# p18-04 — An image is started either way

Since task p18-01 every flash of the radio ends in a pulse on nRESET, and that task's report calls
the reset line its largest untested assumption. The unit at Bellevue says the assumption does not
hold there: on 21 November the supervisor wrote *cured by rung 2, a reset pulse* and the radio's own
hourly `uptimeS` points at one boot days earlier, with `RESETREAS` showing the watchdog's reset and
then the re-flash's and nothing in between. The pulse restarted nothing.

**What this task did.** Three things, each decided from what the chip says and not from a record:

1. **`UICR.PSELRESET[0]` and `[1]` are read at every flash** — and written nowhere, ever. Where
   they say the module has no reset pin, no pulse is made: the image is started through the debug
   port, which is the exit every flash had before p18-01.
2. **A pulse gets no confirmation, so an empty boot window is worth one look.** The debug port is
   opened once more and `DHCSR` read: a core still halted where the flash left it is a pulse that
   reached nothing, and the image is started through the port with the window beginning again; a
   core that is *running* is a pulse that worked and an image that does not speak, and nothing is
   sent to it. One look a flash, never two.
3. **A soft reset does not clear a watchdog a scanner started**, so a proof that follows one waits
   that watchdog out before it begins — at the boot after its reset, or when its longest time has
   passed without one. The proof itself is unchanged: a restart inside the proving period is still
   a failure.

And two things a person reads: the unit keeps what a pulse has been seen to do beside the reset
spacing in RTC memory, and the supervisor's second rung is called a cure only when a new boot
answered its pulse.

`tools\verify.ps1` **green in 544 s**; `core_tests` **1 493 cases and 818 623 assertions**, all
passing; the image is **859 600 bytes against main's 854 672** in the same tree, **+4 928**.

## What the Product Specification says

Read from the extracted text of Nordic's own **nRF52840 Product Specification v1.11**
(`4413_417 v1.11 / 2024-10-01`), the copy task p18-01 fetched and left in `out/docs/nrf52840_ps.txt`
(git-ignored).

**4.5.1.62 PSELRESET[0]**, address offset 0x200, and **4.5.1.63 PSELRESET[1]**, 0x204 — "Mapping of
the nRESET function (see POWER chapter for details)", with the same note on both:

> **Note:** All PSELRESET registers have to contain the same value for a pin mapping to be valid. If
> values are not the same, there will be no nRESET function exposed on a GPIO. As a result, the
> device will always start independently of the levels present on any of the GPIOs.

and the same three fields on both, reset value 0xFFFFFFFF:

| Bits | Field | Values |
|---|---|---|
| [4:0] | `PIN` | "GPIO pin number onto which nRESET is exposed" |
| 5 | `PORT` | "Port number onto which nRESET is exposed" |
| 31 | `CONNECT` | "Disconnected 1 Disconnect, Connected 0 Connect" |

So `enabled` is the whole rule in one line: the two words are equal and bit 31 is 0. An erased UICR
(0xFFFFFFFF in both) is *Disconnected*, which is a module with no nRESET on any pin; a provisioned
RAK4630 holds 18 in both, which is nRESET on P0.18.

**5.3.6.2 Pin reset** — "A pin reset is generated when the physical reset pin on the device is
asserted. Pin reset is configured via both registers PSELRESET[n]." That is the sentence that makes
these two registers the question to ask before pulsing a line, and **5.3.6.8**'s *Reset behavior*
table (quoted in full in p18-01's report) is the one that makes a soft reset the thing that leaves a
watchdog counting: the WDT column is marked for a watchdog, a pin, a brownout and a power-on reset,
and blank for *Soft reset*.

The scanner's own number is the repeater firmware's `src/roles/scanner/config.h` at commit
`eaa19a7`: `WDT_TIMEOUT_S 60`, with the comment "the nRF52840's WDT, which cannot be stopped and
which survives the soft reset into the bootloader with the same timeout".

## The bounds

The second window and the watchdog's wait are both real time a pass may spend, so they are in the
arithmetic rather than in somebody else's margin:

| | Before | Now |
|---|---:|---:|
| `kStartAgainWindowMs` (new) | — | `kBootWindowMs`, 20 s |
| `kScannerWatchdogMs` (new) | — | 60 000 ms, the repeater firmware's `WDT_TIMEOUT_S` |
| `kScannerWatchdogWaitMs` (new) | — | 75 000 ms, that with a quarter of it as margin |
| `kOnePassMs` | 19 min 20 s | **19 min 40 s** (flash + boot window + the second window + proving) |
| `kWholeRunMs` | 38 min 40 s | **39 min 20 s** |
| `kSessionBoundMs` | 58 min 40 s | **60 min 35 s** (`kWholeRunMs` + the ceiling + the watchdog's wait) |
| `kBackendBoundMs` | 59 min 40 s | **61 min 35 s** |
| `kRaisedBoundMax` | 60 min | **65 min** |

The watchdog's wait is counted from the moment the image was started, not from the `HELLO`, so the
boot window is inside it: what a pass really spends on it is at most 55 s. It is charged once to a
session and to nothing else — the scanner's own pass feeds that watchdog from its loop — which is
why it is in `kSessionBoundMs` and not in `kOnePassMs`.

The measured session is well inside: 302 s against the 3 635 s the bound allows, on a bench whose
flash is seconds rather than a quarter of an hour.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **"One outage" is one outage of the session and two interruptions of the repeater, and it cannot
   be fewer.** The task's acceptance line asks for a session on the Bellevue unit that ends
   `restored` "with one outage". What the test measures and asserts: the repeater leaves the air
   **once** at the scanner's flash and is serving when the session ends — the session is one outage,
   and it leaves no second one for a later wake to mend, which is what 21 November's `stuck` did.
   Inside it the radio goes dark **three** times where a unit with a working line goes dark twice:
   the two flashes, and the scanner's watchdog resetting the golden image a few seconds into the
   wait. That third one is unavoidable on such a unit: "after it is started, the watchdog's
   configuration registers … will be blocked for further configuration" (PS 6.36.3), and the one
   reset that clears it is the pin reset this unit's line cannot make. The alternative would be to
   forgive a restart inside the proving period, which the task forbids and p18-01 exists to stop.
   The test asserts `outages == 2` on the repeater's own count and says why in a comment; both
   numbers are in its `MESSAGE`.
2. **The resumed boot is given the look, and the golden image's resumed proof is given the wait.** A
   Walter that died between the pulse and the look comes back not knowing what started the image.
   It makes one look if its window ends empty — the look sends nothing to a core that is running, so
   the cost of being wrong is two register reads — and the **golden image's** proof (a rollback or a
   switch back) waits the watchdog out once, 75 s. The candidate's own path does not: a restart
   there costs a rollback, which is what a rollback is for, while a restart in the golden image's
   proof is `stuck` and the end of a session. A resumed `rak role repeater` on a healthy unit
   therefore waits 75 s it does not need; it is a resumed run, it is bounded, and it is the one
   place this firmware cannot ask the chip what happened before it.
3. **A start through the debug port does not count for the reset spacing, and the report says why.**
   `ARCHITECTURE` §2 rule 2 exists for `BENCH-BREADBOARD.md` §6 caveat 4: two resets **of the pin**
   less than about half a second apart are the Adafruit bootloader's double-tap. A soft reset never
   touches nRESET, so it can be neither tap, and recording it as a pulse would only make the next
   real pulse wait a second for nothing. `resetPulse()` records; `Nrf52::systemReset()` does not.
   The case named in *The rails* shows a whole flash of a module with no reset pin leaving
   `lastResetEndMs()` where a pulse had left it.
4. **A UICR read that fails does not fail the flash.** `openChip()` reads the two words and ignores
   the result: `known` stays false and the flash goes on, which on a port that cannot read two words
   will fail at the next thing it is asked anyway. A flash with an unknown answer gets the pulse,
   which is task p18-01's flash unchanged.
5. **Five files edited beside the new ones**, none of them in what it asserts about an outcome:
   - `host_test/core/cycle/test_wake_room.cpp` — *an hour-long lease is cut* becomes *a two-hour
     lease is cut*, and `deadlineFor(kBackendBoundMs)` is now the number asked for rather than the
     ceiling less a minute: with 65 minutes a wake a minute old can hold the whole exchange, where
     before it was twenty seconds short;
   - `host_test/agent-backend/test_a_wake_outlasts_what_it_took_on.cpp` — the same, for the gtest
     half: `AnHourLongLeaseIsCutInsideTheCeiling` is `ALeaseLongerThanTheCeilingIsCutInsideIt`, with
     a two-hour lease;
   - `host_test/core/update/test_scanner_session.cpp` — the arithmetic of `kSessionBoundMs` and
     `kOnePassMs`, asserted against their new definitions;
   - `host_test/core/nrf52/test_nrf52_forbidden.cpp` — R3's sweep, as above;
   - `docs/provisioning.md` — the replayed `rak swd id` session gains the two `UICR.PSELRESET`
     lines (`test_provision_console.cpp` replays that block line for line).
6. **Three benches changed**, and one of them is a behaviour of the simulators worth saying out
   loud: **a halted core answers nothing.** The flasher halts the core before it writes, and where
   the reset at the end reaches nothing the core is still halted — so the scripted radio must go
   dark, or it would go on answering as the firmware the flash has just replaced, and a flash of a
   scanner image onto a unit with a dead line would be "proved" by the repeater that is still
   talking. `UpdateWorld::settle()` and `ChipRadio::settle()` now silence the radio while
   `chip.coreHalted()`. The supervisor's bench gained
   `reset_line_reaches_the_radio` and the update bench `scanner_starts_watchdog`.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **`kRaisedBoundMax` is raised from sixty to sixty-five minutes.** It was twenty seconds above the
   session it was set from, which is no headroom at all for a task that gives a pass anything, and
   the static assertion `kBackendBoundMs <= kRaisedBoundMax` is what would have stopped the build.
   Sixty-five is the new session (60 min 35 s), the exchange's minute, and three and a half minutes
   beside it. It is an allowance and not a spend: `arm()` is still five minutes, only a machine that
   says what it needs raises it, and a wake that overruns the raised bound is still reset with its
   work in the journal. `docs/cycle.md` and `docs/update.md` carry the new numbers.
2. **The look is in the update machine and the start in the flasher.** `Flasher::startAgain()` is
   the port, the two UICR words, `DHCSR` and — only on a halted core — `AIRCR`; which boot window it
   belongs to, how many looks a flash gets and what the journal says about it are the machine's.
   The alternative, a flasher that waits for the radio, would put the link client in the flasher.
3. **`kHalted` alone is the dead line.** `kRunning`, `kSleeping`, `kLockedUp` and a read that failed
   all send nothing. A locked-up core is a chip that *did* reset and then locked up, and a flash
   does not reset a radio twice to find out why it is quiet.
4. **The watchdog is owed when the radio was running a scanner when the flash began** — read off
   the link client before the flash (text mode, or a `HELLO` that calls itself a scanner), not off
   the kind of the run — **and the debug port started the image**, and the image being flashed is
   not itself a scanner (a scanner feeds the watchdog it inherits from its own loop). A flash that
   was not over a scanner waits for nothing.
5. **The line's record lives with the pins**, in the retained record that already holds the reset
   spacing. It grows that record by five bytes, so the first boot of the release that carries this
   does not read the old one back (`interfaces/retained.h`: a record of another size is not the same
   record) — the spacing is then the full second, as after any power-up, and the line reads
   *untried* until the next flash. No retained slot was added: RTC SLOW memory has twelve bytes
   free (task p15-06).
6. **`Nrf52::systemReset()` has callers again**, which is what p18-01's review expected of this
   task: `Flasher::finish()` on a module with no reset pin, and `Flasher::startAgain()` on a core
   the pulse left halted. It is unchanged.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port; nothing of this was on a board. The lines are in `docs/bench.md` where the
rig's order puts them — §2 with the reset line itself, §11 with the ladder:

1. **§2: what the chip says about its reset pin.** `rak swd id` on the unit's own radio prints
   `UICR.PSELRESET: 0x…, 0x…` and either *the pin reset is enabled* with the pin, or *NO pin reset*.
   **This is the first reading that says which of the two candidates the unit at Bellevue is**
   (`tasks/phase-18/FOLLOWUPS.md`, *What the line is on that unit*): `PSELRESET` not set on the
   module, or the wire on the adapter. Write it down.
2. **§2: `rak reset` with the RAK's console open.** The radio restarts and `rak status` says
   `booted: 2 (reset pin)` on a unit whose line works. A module whose UICR says *enabled* and whose
   radio does not restart is the wire, and a meter on nRESET during the pulse is what settles it.
3. **§11: cured by the pulse, or came back by itself.** Holding nRESET to ground and pulling the
   jumper during rung 2's wait is a radio the pulse cannot restart: the incident should read *cured
   by itself (rung 2, a reset pulse restarted nothing)*. Making it quiet some other way should read
   *cured by rung 2, a reset pulse*.

And the one the task names: **the first flash on the unit after the release**. Its journal says what
UICR holds, which of the two started the image, and what the look found — `rak update status` and
the check-in's `journal` section both carry it. Thomas reads that from meshmaster.dk.

**What is still only a simulation here**: that a core the pulse did not reset is still *halted* when
the port is opened again a boot window later. It is what the chip does by construction — nothing
starts a halted core but a reset — and the Product Specification's reset table is the authority, but
no silicon has answered that `DHCSR` read on a unit yet.
