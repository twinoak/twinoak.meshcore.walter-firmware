# p14-05 — A trial the last wake cannot sink

On 15 November 2026 v0.1.6 was applied to the unit, booted on trial, and five minutes later rolled
itself back: *the backend was not reached inside the trial window*. **It had never asked.** The
wake that took the apply at 08:41:07 UTC ended in a failed call; a failed call leaves the agent's
library a hold; the hold is saved across the restart and put back whole at the next boot, because
the time away is 0 for every boot that is not a timer wake; and the agent's wake sends nothing
while a hold is owed. So the trial's one heartbeat was never posted, and a good image — the one
carrying pile 13's diagnostics — went on the bad list. The rolled-back v0.1.4 owed the same hold
and owed the server the apply's result, and it was the timer wake eleven minutes later that told
meshmaster.dk what had happened.

Four changes, all of them one sentence each:

- **a trial boot owes no wait**, and neither does the boot of the old image while it still owes a
  rollback's result;
- **a heartbeat that fails is tried again** while the window lasts, one heartbeat and nothing
  else, paced by the library's own wait;
- **not reached and not asked are told apart**, in the journal and in what the server is sent;
- and the figures are read where a person looks: `self status`, `agent state`, the apply's result.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**The bench did not configure the trial's third condition, so no agent test had ever run it.**
`WakeWalter::begin()` (`host_test/core/cycle/wake_bench.h`) did everything `main/main.cpp` does at
boot except the one line `Console::begin()` does for the trial: hand `server.url` to the
`UplinkCheck`. Without it `AgentUplinkCheck::check()` answers *no server is configured*, condition
3 is **not applicable**, and every trial on the wake bench confirmed on its first two conditions —
including `test_firmware_jobs.cpp`'s *the Walter's own apply … and the result from the next boot's
wake*, which looked exactly as it should and proved nothing about the uplink. The line is in the
bench now, with the comment that says why; no existing test changed behaviour because of it. It is
a finding about the bench, not about the firmware, and it is the reason this task's first run of
the new cases passed when it should not have.

**`tools/heap_at_start.py` could not replay this build, and the fault was in the recorded
programme.** The model asserted (`assert not block.free`) at operation 75. The recorded programme
ends `free 63 / free 60 / free 59 / alloc 20 / free 59 / alloc 20 / free 59`, and those last two
frees are wrong: `--from-record` matches a recorded free to an allocation **by address**, scanning
`live` forwards, so when the allocator hands the same block back for the next `alloc 20` the free
is credited to the *first* operation that ever used that address. It replayed only because all
three of those allocations landed on the same block; 24 bytes of .bss moved one of them and the
replay fell over. The scan is backwards now — the most recent live allocation at that address,
which is what a free of a pointer means — and the programme's last two lines are `free 74` and
`free 76`. Nothing about the recording changed: the operations are the same operations. This is
latent in the tool and would have bitten whichever task next moved .bss by the wrong amount.

**One route of the owed result is unchanged and is not this task's.** A trial that confirms in
**step 9** rather than inside step 8's exchange still posts its apply's result at the *next* wake:
`postOwedResult()` runs after an answered heartbeat, and at that moment the trial has not ended,
so there is nothing to report yet. That is the behaviour before this task and it is unchanged — it
costs one wake on a *successful* update, where the server learns of the confirmation a quarter of
an hour later. The task's third deliverable is the **rollback**, where the result was held back by
the wait and is not any more, and that is what the acceptance criterion tests.

## Decisions taken without asking

**Where the line is drawn, and what the library is told.** In `AgentBackend::loadState()`, and
nowhere else: the wait is dropped from the saved state *before* it is handed over, and the time
away is handed over **as measured**. The library is told nothing untrue — it is told there was no
wait, which from this boot's point of view is the decision this firmware has taken — and
everything else of the state (the backoff, the run of failures, the halt on a 401, the counters,
the accepted interval, the budget, what was kept across the sleep) is restored exactly as it was
saved. A test holds that: `backoffMs`, `consecutiveFailures` and the library's own `httpFailures`
come back equal to what the failing wake saved.

**Two boots lay the wait aside and no others.** A boot that is on trial, and the boot of the old
image that comes back owing the server a rollback's result. Both follow a *restart*, which is a
boot that was away seconds and can prove none of them; both have exactly one thing to say that an
older image's failed call must not answer for them. Every other boot owes the wait as before.

**`SelfUpdate::bootIsTrial()`, because of main's boot order.** `main/main.cpp` restores the
agent's state (`backend.begin()`, line 682) *before* `console.begin()` (line 715) reaches
`SelfUpdate::begin()`, so `onTrial()` is still false when the decision has to be made. The new
const reader answers from what `begin()` itself reads — the bootloader's *pending-verify* and the
journal's last self-update entry — and `begin()` and it share one private function, so the two
cannot come apart. For the same reason `FirmwareJobs::owedResultDue()` makes the finding
`SelfUpdate::begin()` is about to make when the old image comes back after a trial the bootloader
cut short: a self-update entry newer than the owed apply, for a slot that is **not** this one.

**`interfaces::Beat`: one heartbeat on the seam, and the wake cycle asks for it.** The retry had to
be driven from `runTrial()` in the wake cycle and not from `AgentUplinkCheck::check()`, because
`Console::tick()` steps the trial on **every turn of the main loop**: a check that made a call of
its own would have put the modem on the air from the console's idle loop, outside any wake and
with no uplink guaranteed. That is the thing the old comment in `agent_uplink_check.h` forbade, and
it still holds. What changed is only that the heartbeat is no longer the wake's *one*: the trial's
loop asks the seam, the seam runs the library's own wake again, and a library inside its own wait
answers `kWaiting` and sends nothing — so *when* the modem speaks is still the library's. The
trial asks every `kTrialUplinkRetryMs`, which is the same clock it reads the condition's answer on.

**One heartbeat and nothing else, said in the library's own units.** `beat()` runs
`AgentLoop::wake()` with a transaction cap of **one** — below the price of the cheapest job, so no
claim is made — and a body cap of **one byte**, which stops every result and every observation
batch and stops neither the heartbeat nor the config call, whose bodies the library does not count
(`agent/AgentLoop.h`, `Budget`). The work of the wake is the next wake's; the trial is about to end
either way.

**`UplinkVerdict::kNotTried`, and what `uplink_tries` counts.** The agent's check is a *reading* of
what the wake's heartbeat came to, not an attempt of its own, and a reading that found nothing new
used to answer *the server was not reached* — so a trial that never sent a heartbeat counted ten
failures and read like a unit that cannot attach. A fourth verdict says *nothing was sent*: the
trial leaves the condition where it was, counts no attempt, and is not paced by it (a free look
must not hold the condition shut for half a minute after the heartbeat it was waiting for was
answered). `SelfStatus::uplink_tries` therefore means **attempts that went out**, which is what it
always meant for the TLS implementation, where every `check()` is an attempt.

**A journal kind of its own for the figures.** The *rolled back* entry's 48 bytes are full, and
`journal.h` already has the precedent: `kind::kTrialEvidence` exists for exactly this reason.
`kind::kTrialUplink` (0x0009, 12 bytes) is written by the image that **rejects itself**, right
after its rollback entry, and read back under the same rule as the evidence — the last trial's
when no self-update entry is newer than it. Only for a rollback by `kNoUplink`: no entry means the
rollback was for another reason.

**Where the bytes came back from.** The first build of this task had 24 bytes more .bss than main
and the tightest big allocation — the 20 kB kept-scratch — fell from 116 bytes to spare to 84. Four
changes put it back to main's address exactly, and each one is commented where it is:
`AgentBackendCounters`' two new counters are **16-bit** and share one four-byte slot (a trial is
five minutes and cannot ask more than a few hundred times); `AgentBackend::laid_aside_` is one
byte **beside the two bools**, where the padding is already there, and the figure is read off
`owed_before_ms_` rather than kept twice (a wait is laid aside whole); `SelfStatus::uplink_last` is
one byte **beside the three conditions**, where the padding is already there; and the wake cycle
keeps **no counter of its own** — the seam's `trial_beats` is the one, and the tests read it there.
An unused `beats_` in `AgentUplinkCheck` went with them.

**The apply's result now carries a `reason` for a rollback with no core dump.** Until this it was
written only when there was a `kTrialEvidence` entry to go with it, so a trial that rejected itself
sent the server an outcome and a journal and no sentence. The sentence is built clause by clause
now — the rollback's reason, then what ended the trial (p11-03) and how hard the backend was asked
(this task) — and goes whenever either half has something to say.

**`MeshMasterScript::failNextOn(route, status, count)`** in the test support: a trial whose first
heartbeat fails and whose second is answered is **one wake**, so the scripted server cannot be told
to stop failing in between. It is the route-scoped twin of `failResults(status, count)`, which was
already there.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port (phase 14, *Not on the board*), so everything below is Thomas's with the
rig. The same lines are in `docs/bench.md` §20, in the order the rig is brought up.

1. **A trial that owes a wait, on silicon.** With this release on the unit: make a call fail
   (`set server.url` to a host that refuses, one wake, then set it back), so `agent state` shows a
   hold owed; then offer and apply the *next* release from meshmaster.dk. Expected at the console
   through the apply's restart: the boot line of the trial, then `agent state` reading
   `… s owed before the sleep, laid aside: this image is on trial`, a heartbeat on the wire in the
   trial's first wake, and `self-update: on trial -> confirmed`. **This is the one that matters**:
   it is the fault of 15 November, reproduced and then not reproduced.
2. **A trial whose first heartbeat fails.** Harder to script on a mast and worth one try: pull the
   antenna or take the SIM's coverage away for the first half-minute of a trial (a Faraday bag over
   the LTE antenna), then give it back. Expected: `self status` during the trial reading
   `uplink failed, trying again (1 sent, the last: the server was not reached)`, then a second
   heartbeat and a confirmation; `agent state`'s last line counting the trial's own heartbeat and
   the asks its wait held off.
3. **A trial that is rolled back for want of an uplink.** With the SIM's coverage away for the
   whole five minutes. Expected: a rollback at the window's end, and — after the restart into the
   old image — `self status` saying `N heartbeat(s) tried in 300 s; the last: the server was not
   reached`, and the apply's result on meshmaster.dk carrying the same sentence. **Then check the
   bad list**: the image is on it, which is S-6 doing its job and is why this walk is done with a
   release that is not wanted.
4. **The owed result after a rollback.** From the same walk: the old image's **first** wake after
   the rollback is the one that posts the result (`agent state` saying
   `laid aside: a rollback's result is owed`), not the timer wake after it. Read the result's
   timestamp on meshmaster.dk against the unit's `journal`.
5. **The heap, on the board.** `heap` at the console on the first boot of this release, against
   `tools/heap_at_start.py`'s model: the model says the main task is created with 11 512 bytes to
   spare and the tightest big allocation has 116. The model is a model; the board is the answer.
