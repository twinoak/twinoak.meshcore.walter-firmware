# p14-06 — A wake a person asked for

On 13 November 2026 Thomas pressed *Wake it up* on meshmaster.dk (job 28399). The unit's radio
answered `ok` at 18:09:02 UTC and raised §8's attention, reason 4. The board woke on the line —
and nothing came from it until its own timer woke it a quarter of an hour later.

What the code said, which nobody had confirmed at a console: the wake before it had ended in a
failed call; a failed call leaves the agent's library a wait; the wait is saved across the sleep
and put back **less the time the board was away**; and the time away was **0 for every wake but
the timer's**, because the only measure of a sleep this board had was the seconds its own sleep
timer had been asked for. So the wait came back whole and the wake sent nothing. This unit has
had a failed call at nearly every wake, so nearly every wake a person asked for found a wait.

Task p14-05 took the wait away from a boot on trial. This takes the root, in two sentences:

- **the radio never sleeps and counts its own seconds**, so its `uptime_s` written down before a
  sleep and read again at the boot is the time away — for every kind of wake, not only the
  timer's;
- **and a wake a person asked for owes no wait at all**, whether the event woke the board or step
  4 found reason 4 still pending in `STATUS`.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

**The criterion's "reason 2" is reason 5 in the tests, because reason 2 moves the band.** §8's
attention reason 2 is *battery low*, `detail` the millivolts, and the supervisor records what the
event carried. A test that raised reason 2 with a plausible `detail` put the interval policy into
a band that does not allow the modem, so step 6 never came up and the wake answered `kNoUplink`
instead of `kWait` — which is correct behaviour and not the case the criterion is about. The
tests therefore use **reason 5** (*the observation ring is nearly full*), which is §8's most
innocuous reason and reaches this board by exactly the same path. `host_test/agent/test_what_is_kept.cpp`'s
three p13-02 cases were moved the same way, and for a second reason: they had been written with
**reason 4**, which after this task lays the wait aside and sends — so they would have been
testing the new behaviour and not the old.

**A `HELLO` at the boot, which was not in the task's words.** Deliverable 2 says "a boot whose
`HELLO` names the same `boot_id`", and `boot_id` comes from `HELLO` and nothing else (§5.1); the
`uptime_s` comes from `PING` (§5.2). So the measurement is two frames, in `DutyCycle::begin()`.
They are sent **only** for a wake that is not the timer's **and** only when there is a reading to
measure against — so a timer wake, which is nearly every wake this unit has, costs nothing, and
a first boot or a power cut costs nothing. The `HELLO` is not a cost in itself either: step 2 of
the wake sends one a moment later and finds the session already up.

**The task says "the `uptime_s` step 4 reads", and step 4 is too late.** `main/main.cpp` restores
the agent's state at `backend.begin()` (line 691); step 4 of the wake runs a long time after
that, and a wait put back whole at the restore is a wake that has already decided to send
nothing. Deliverable 2's own last sentence — "it is known before `restoreState` asks" — is the
one that had to win, so the reading is taken in `DutyCycle::begin()` (line 538), which is where
the wake is worked out and a hundred and fifty lines earlier. Step 4 still reads the same
`uptime_s` into `known_.radio_uptime_s`; it is simply not what the figure is made of.

**The comment in `wake_cycle.cpp` says what is true now and the behaviour did not change.**
Deliverable 4 asks for the comment that "calls a line wake inside a wait expected" to say what is
true. It now says that it is not expected any more — the radio measures that sleep too, and a
wake a person asked for owes no wait — and that what is left (a line wake for another of §8's
reasons that still owes a wait) is **in the wake record**, with its seconds and with what measured
its time away. It is still not news for the journal: an entry for it would cost one per failed
call on a unit whose line is busy and say what the record already says. The task asked for the
comment and not for the entry.

## Decisions taken without asking

**500 ppm, and where the figure comes from.** The radio's `uptime_s` is `millis() / 1000`
(`src/roles/repeater/link/BoardLink.cpp`, `uptimeSeconds()`, read in the sibling clone at
`fb2b988`), and on the RAK4631's nRF52840 that is the Arduino core's tick, which the RTC
peripheral drives from LFCLK. Nordic's nRF52840 Product Specification, *Clock control*, calls the
LFRC a "32.768 kHz +/-500 ppm RC oscillator" and says the crystal is what a design fits "when
better than +/- 500 ppm accuracy is required" — so **500 ppm bounds either source**, and this end
does not have to know which one the module has. The subtraction is **rounded up**, so no span is
ever believed whole. The constant is one line in `core/cycle/wake_reason.h` with that paragraph
beside it.

**A whole second off the span, before the error.** `uptime_s` is whole seconds at both ends, so
each end may be out by up to one and the span by up to one in all. One second comes off before
the 500 ppm, and a span of a second or less is believed to be **no time at all** — which is
*measured* (`awayFrom()` is `the radio`) and is not the same answer as *nothing could measure it*.

**Where the line for reason 4 is drawn: in the exchange, not in `loadState()`.** Task p14-05 drew
its two lines in `loadState()`, where the boot's own facts are known. Reason 4 reaches this board
two ways and only one of them is known at the boot — the event that woke it, **or** a reason still
pending in step 4's `STATUS`, which a *timer* wake finds. Both are in `known.wake.attention` by
the time the exchange runs, so one line in `AgentBackend::run()` answers both; two places would
have been two behaviours. The lay-aside is the same operation p14-05's is, done through the
library's own seam: read `state()`, drop the hold, hand it straight back with no time away, which
re-bases every duration against the clock it finds now — and that clock is now. Nothing else of
the state moves, and a hold that is already 0 (a trial boot, which p14-05 has already dealt with)
is left alone so that the reason a person reads is the first one that applied.

**No bound of this board's own on how often.** §8 rate-limits attention at the radio: at most one
new attention per reason per hour. A second limit here would only make a person press the button
twice, and the thing being spent is one heartbeat.

**`forgetRadioReading()` is called before the `strcmp` in `saveState()`, not after it.** A state
that is what the store already holds is not written again (NVS wear), but it is still a state
taken *now*, and the wait it carries is counted from now. A reading taken before that moment
would measure too much. So the reading is voided as soon as `state()` has been read, whatever
happens to the bytes afterwards.

**The reading is cleared in `Sleep::sleepFor` before the new one is taken.** So a radio that says
nothing at the sleep leaves **none**, rather than leaving whatever was there — which matters for
a build with no agent at all, and for a `sleep now` typed at a console where no `saveState()` ran.
A failed or stale `PING` (the nonce is checked) writes nothing.

**The record is 128 bytes, which is the whole slot.** `kRetainedRecordMax` is 128 and the
duty-cycle record is now exactly that: the `boot_id` and the `uptime_s` need four bytes each, the
"is there a reading" flag took the record's last spare byte, and no packing makes it smaller (a
struct with a `std::uint32_t` member rounds to a multiple of four either way). **The next thing
that has to cross a sleep needs a slot of its own**, and the header, `docs/cycle.md` and a host
test all say so. The cost of the growth is the one p10-01 and p13-02 each paid: a record of
another size in the same slot is not the same record, so the first boot of the release that
carries this reads none — one wake at the `unknown` band's interval, with the wake log and the
band's hysteresis starting again.

**Where the bytes came back from.** The eight bytes of record are eight bytes of `.bss`, and eight
bytes of `.bss` move `_heap_start` and with it the heap the 20 kB kept scratch is carved out of
(`tools/heap_at_start.py`; task p11-01 is why that matters). The first build of this task had the
tightest big allocation at **972** bytes to spare against main's **988**. Three changes, each
commented where it is:

1. **`WakeReason`'s four one-byte members share one word** — the band, the new "was it measured"
   flag, `begun_` and `survived_` — which was padding before, so the flag costs nothing;
2. **`WakeInfo::spurious_in_window` and `faults_in_a_row` are one byte**, which is what the record
   they are read out of keeps them as (capped at 255 there), and they sit with the bools and the
   two enums: a 32-bit field claimed a precision nothing kept. Eight bytes;
3. **`AgentBackend` keeps no third figure** for what a wake laid aside. It is the whole of what the
   store held for a trial or an owed result — the wait is dropped before the library sees it — and
   what this boot still owed for a wake a person asked for, which is `owed_before_ms_` less
   `away_ms_`. Both are arithmetic on two figures already there. Four bytes.

That is twelve back against eight spent, and the result is main's `_heap_start` and main's
tightest figure exactly.

**Two bits of the wake entry's `did` byte, written without `kDidSaid`.** Deliverable 4 asks for
the time away's source in the list of ten "if the entry has room". The entry is ten bytes and
full, but the `did` byte had four free bits, so two of them say *the timer* and *the radio*. They
are **not** written with `kDidSaid`: that bit means "this wake recorded what it did", and p10-02's
contract is that a wake nobody wrote down reads `???` for the three letters. A separate setter
(`awayThisWake`) keeps them apart, so nothing about the letters changed. Both bits clear reads as
*nothing measured it*, which is also what an entry written before this task says — and is the
honest answer for one.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port (phase 14, *Not on the board*), so everything below is Thomas's with the
rig. The same lines are in `docs/bench.md` §3, under *An attention wakes the Walter*, where §8's
own unticked item is — because the box above them, *has an attention ever woken a sleeping Walter
at all*, has to be ticked before any of this can be.

1. **A wake a person asked for, inside a wait.** The one that matters, and it is 13 November
   reproduced and then not reproduced. With this release on the unit: `set server.url` to a host
   that refuses, one `agent wake` (it must fail), `set server.url` back — `agent state` must now
   show a hold of thirty seconds or more. Then `sleep 900`, and press *Wake it up* on the unit's
   page while the board is down. Expected at the console: `agent state` reading
   `… s owed before the sleep, N s away, by the radio's uptime` and, under it,
   `N s of a wait was laid aside: a person asked for this Walter`; a heartbeat **in that same
   wake** (`wake`'s newest entry `-S-` or `MS-`, not `---`); and a check-in on meshmaster.dk dated
   then. Before this release the same walk gives *no sleep this end could measure*, `---`, and
   nothing until the timer wake a quarter of an hour later.
2. **The same walk with an attention that is not reason 4** — battery low, or the observation ring
   — where the wait must be **spent by the sleep** rather than laid aside: `agent state` reading
   `N s away, by the radio's uptime` with N near the sleep's own length. Only a sleep shorter than
   the wait should leave `wake` saying `a wait still owed, N s (the time away: the radio)`.
3. **The radio restarted while the board slept.** With a wait owed, `sleep 900`, and pull the
   RAK's power for a moment while the board is down. At the next wake `agent state` must read
   *nothing could measure how long this board was away* and the wait must be owed whole. **That is
   the honest answer and not a fault**: it is the one case left where a wait outlives a sleep for
   want of a measure, and the follow-up about an epoch beside the saved state is the cure.
4. **The first boot of this release loses the wake log, once.** The record grew by eight bytes, so
   that boot reads none: `wake` says *the record did not survive*, the list is that wake alone,
   the band is `unknown` and the next interval is 60 min rather than 15. The wake after it is
   normal. Anything else — a record that does not come back on the *second* wake — is a finding.
5. **The heap, on the board.** `heap` at the console on the first boot of this release, against
   `tools/heap_at_start.py`: the model says the main task is created with 12 376 bytes to spare
   and the tightest big allocation has 988. The model is a model; the board is the answer.
6. **What the radio's `uptime_s` really drifts by.** 500 ppm is a document's bound on the
   nRF52840's low-frequency clock, not a measurement of this radio. A day's `sleep 3600` walk
   with the unit's `journal` read against a wall clock would say what the real figure is; until
   then the bound errs the safe way, and 500 ppm of a quarter of an hour is half a second.
