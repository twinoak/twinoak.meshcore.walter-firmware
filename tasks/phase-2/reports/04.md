# p2-04 — The Walter's own update

The Walter can now replace its own firmware and **comes back if the new one is no good** - where
"no good" is not "it crashed" but "it cannot do its job". `components/core/update/self_update.h`
is the policy over a new interface, `interfaces/own_update.h` (the two application slots and
ESP-IDF's rollback states; `platform/own_update.cpp` is the thin ESP-IDF side): a release of this
firmware's `ota` file goes into the other slot **through the same six checks a radio image passes**
- the USB transfer of task p2-02 gained a `TransferTarget` seam, and the other slot is its second
target - and is refused at the door when its hash is on the bad list, when it does not fit the
slot, when its `load_address` is not a slot's offset, or when it speaks another link major than the
radio without saying it tolerates the radio's (S-7). `self switch` journals *switching to <version>,
from <version>* with whether the radio answered `HELLO`, sets the boot pointer, sends `SLEEP_NOTICE`
and restarts. The new image then has **one boot** - the bootloader marks its slot pending-verify and
aborts it at the next reset unless it has confirmed itself - to meet three conditions inside the
trial window: its main loop with the pins safe, `HELLO` with the radio (unless the radio was
journalled as known down before the switch), and one exchange over the uplink (an interface; until
phase 3 the firmware's implementation says *no server is configured* and the condition does not
apply). All three: *confirmed*. Otherwise the hash goes on the bad list, the journal says *rolled
back: <the first missing condition>*, and the image rejects itself into the previous one. The image
that boots after **any** restart reads the journal and closes a story the restart cut short, with
its own reason: *never reached its main loop*, *restarted during its trial*, or *the restart came
before the boot pointer: nothing was tried*. The console has `self status`, `self receive`,
`self switch [radio-down]`, `self confirm` and `self reject`; `walterctl.py push --as self` is the
laptop's end; `docs/update.md` has the Walter's half.

The proof is on the host, against a fake of the two slots that carries the bootloader's own rules
read from `bootloader_utility.c` (pending → aborted at a reboot, new → pending, the first boot's
entry for `ota_0`): the good path end to end through a simulated restart; the trial failing on each
condition alone, on the window with two missing, on a reset during it, on an image that never
reached its main loop, and by hand - each rolled back with its own journal line, the hash on the
bad list, and the same image refused if offered again; a radio known dead before the switch not
condemning the image and a radio alive before and silent after condemning it; every precondition;
every refusal at the door including the link-major rule in both directions; the journal's story
after each outcome; and the power going at eight points of the other slot's write with the running
image untouched each time. **A factory image's first boot runs no trial and rejects nothing**, and
the rule that makes that so is stated and tested.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The bench section is `docs/bench.md` §9, not §8.** The task says "8. Updating the Walter";
  §8 is *Updating the radio*, written by task p2-03 after the task files were.
- **`components/hal/` is `components/interfaces/`** (task p0-01's deviation): the interface is
  `interfaces/own_update.h`.
- **A factory image boots `valid`, not `undefined`, and `docs/bench.md` §1 is corrected.** Task
  p0-01 read that a USB-flashed slot's state is `UNDEFINED` and no rollback applies - true of the
  state esptool leaves, but the bootloader's first boot with blank OTA data writes `ota_0` an entry
  of its own in the state `VALID` (`bootloader_utility.c`, `set_actual_ota_seq`: "Set actual
  ota_seq=1 in otadata[0]"), so the console will say `ota state valid` from the first boot on.
  The premise holds - no trial is ever run on it - for a better reason: only `NEW` becomes
  `PENDING_VERIFY`. The fake models the real rule; §1's second item and `docs/console.md`'s boot
  lines now say `valid`, and the bench confirms it.
- **The uplink condition is a seam, not a handshake, in this build.** The task: "in the firmware
  one that a build constant sets to *reach the configured server's TLS port and complete a
  handshake*". There is no modem driver and no server setting until phase 3 (tasks 01–03), so a
  handshake cannot be written here. What exists: `UplinkCheck`, the constant `kUplinkCondition =
  kTlsHandshake` that names what the condition is when a server exists, the server host and port
  constants (empty: not configured), and `UnconfiguredUplink`, which answers *not configured* and
  makes condition 3 not applicable - and *failed*, never *reached*, if a host is named it cannot
  reach. Phase 3's HTTPS client implements the handshake behind the interface and replaces one
  line of `main/`. `docs/update.md` says so.
- **The battery is a precondition of `self switch`, not of `self receive`.** The task lists the
  battery among the preconditions with the reason "an interrupted write is harmless, but a
  brown-out during the first boot is a wasted attempt"; the write is `self receive` and the first
  boot follows `self switch`, so the check sits where the reason points. It also lets the console
  host, whose radio (and so battery) is absent, take `walterctl --as self`.
- **The "known dead radio" is recorded at the switch, and a person can say it.** The task: "the
  radio is in a state the supervisor already knew about before the update (journalled)". The
  supervisor is task p2-05's; here the switch itself asks `HELLO` and journals the answer in the
  *switching* entry (`kFlagRadioUp`), and a caller - `SwitchRequest::radio_known_down`, the
  console's `self switch radio-down` - may say the radio is known down, which is journalled too
  (`kFlagRadioKnownDown`) and makes condition 2 not applicable. A silent radio with nobody saying
  why refuses the switch rather than letting a trial run that must fail.
- **A scanner counts as a radio that answered.** Condition 2 is met by a `HELLO` answer or by task
  p1-02's text-mode detection (NoiseScope answers no frame): the radio is there, in a state the
  unit knows.
- **The `TransferTarget` refactor of `core/image/receive.{h,cpp}`** (task p2-02's files): the
  receiver's protocol is unchanged, the store's behaviour is `StoreTarget`, the constructor and
  `begin(which, slot)` are what they were, and every receive test passes untouched. The target's
  methods answer with a sentence or `nullptr`, so the receiver never looks inside a `StoreError`.
- **The Walter's `ota` image is exempt from `chooseImage()`'s manifest-link check.** Task p2-01's
  rule "the manifest's own `link.major` must be one this build speaks" was written for radio images
  (S-7: a radio of a new major is not for this unit yet); for the Walter's own image it would have
  refused the very image that brings the new major. The check now applies to `use != kOta`, and the
  self-update's own rule takes over for `kOta`.
- **One consequence for the specification.** `FIRMWARE-MANIFEST.md` §2 describes `requires` as
  "what must already be there: `walter` (minimum Walter version), `link_major`". The reading above
  depends on `requires.link_major` meaning *the radio's current major* for a Walter image; the
  release tooling that writes a Walter manifest during a major change should write the old major
  there. A sentence in §2 would make it a rule rather than a reading; not a gap in the fields.
- **The journal payload keeps sixteen bytes of the hash and `X.Y.Z` of each version.** 48 bytes
  do not hold a 32-byte hash and two 32-byte versions; the full hash for the bad list is recomputed
  from the slot's bytes and held to the sixteen, and the versions are cut at the `+` (the slot's own
  application description keeps the whole string, which `self status` prints beside it).
- **`self switch` rejects a switch when the platform refuses the boot pointer**, journalling
  *rolled back: the platform refused* so the story does not end on *switching*; nothing goes on the
  bad list. Tested with the slots' flash failing at that one operation.
- **CI rounds:** see *CI* below.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The interface's shape** (`interfaces/own_update.h`): slots by index 0/1 with address, size,
  state and version; a write that is begin/write/end/abort, with `endWrite()` carrying
  `esp_ota_end`'s own image validation; `readSlot()` so that checks 4 and 5 run over the bytes as
  they lie in the slot; `confirm()` and `rejectAndRestart()` as the two verdicts. Nothing of
  ESP-IDF's names leaks into core.
- **The trial's conditions are tried in order** - the loop, then `HELLO` every two seconds, then
  the uplink once the radio is settled and every thirty seconds after a failure - and the window
  passing names the **first** missing one. The uplink is not asked while the radio is open, so a
  dead radio does not also cost a modem attach.
- **The bad list is the store's** (`Store::markBad` / `isBad`, task p2-02's index record): one
  list of hashes that failed, radio candidates and Walter images alike; a Walter image is refused
  there at `self receive`, before the other slot is erased. The hash is put on it **before** the
  rollback entry and the restart, as task p2-03 does for the radio.
- **The old image writes the rollback entry when the new one could not.** `begin()` tells three
  cases apart by the last entry and the other slot's state: *on trial* → *restarted during its
  trial*; *switching* with the other slot aborted or invalid → *never reached its main loop*;
  *switching* with the other slot neither → *the restart came before the boot pointer*, nothing on
  the bad list, the received image still there to switch to. The hash for the bad list is
  recomputed from the other slot's bytes and held to the entry's sixteen; a mismatch leaves it off
  and the entry says so (`kFlagNotOnBadList`).
- **A pending-verify image with no switching entry runs a trial all the same** (somebody set the
  boot pointer by other means): `HELLO` is asked of the radio, nothing is known for the bad list,
  and the entry carries `kFlagUnjournalled`.
- **The platform's own refusals are honoured, not papered over.** `esp_ota_begin` refuses while
  the running image is pending (so does `canReceive()`, with a sentence); a `rejectAndRestart()`
  the platform refuses (no bootable other slot) leaves the image running, journalled as rolled
  back with *the platform refused*, said on the console.
- **`self switch` is final on a board**: it restarts and does not return. On the host the fake
  restart counts and the bench rebuilds the Walter, so a test sees both sides of the reset.
- **The console host's slots are 3 MB**, the real size, so that a real release's `ota` file can be
  pushed at it; the tests' fake slots are 64 KB, so that a 70 000-byte fixture is "too large" and
  the suite stays fast (the self tests add about 10 s to the 132 s of `core_tests`).
- **The fixture versions are `0.4.x+w<n>`** so that `X.Y.Z` fits the journal's twelve bytes and
  the manifests' `requires.walter` is absent (the store bench's unit is 0.3.0; a Walter image is
  not held to a minimum Walter version by these tests).
- **Twelve new tokens in the console property test** lowered the seeded reset and kill counts; the
  floors moved to 20 and 70.

## Not verified here (and why)

No board was on a desk, so nothing below has run on ESP-IDF's real OTA data. `docs/bench.md` has a
new **§9, Updating the Walter**, in the order to walk it; it needs a test-key build of this
firmware on the board and two more test-key builds as releases (the dry run of
`tools/verify.ps1 -TestKey -Version 0.0.1+bench1` in `out/release/` is one). The short version:

1. **The factory image's first boot runs no trial**: `self status` says `valid (confirmed)`,
   nothing journalled, the same after three resets. (And `ota state valid` in the boot lines -
   the correction to §1.)
2. **`walterctl.py push --as self`, `self switch`**: the restart's reason in the boot lines,
   `running from ota_1 … pending-verify`, the trial's announcement, *confirmed* within seconds (the
   radio answers; no server is configured). **Write down how long the push of a ~400 KB image
   takes**, and how long the trial.
3. **A version built to fail its trial** (or the radio unplugged after the switch) **rolls back**:
   the window, the rollback line, the restart, the old image's `reboots` reason, the other slot
   `invalid`, `image bad` with the hash, the same push refused at the door.
4. **Reset the Walter during a trial**: the old image comes up, the other slot `aborted`, and the
   old image writes *restarted during its trial* with the hash on the bad list - the bootloader's
   rule on silicon.
5. **Pull power while `self receive` is writing**: the running image untouched, the push repeated.
6. **`self confirm` / `self reject`** during a trial; **S-7 on the board**: `rak update` refused
   during a trial, `self switch` refused during a radio update.
7. **The numbers**, when phase 3 has an attach to measure the window against.

What the host tests cannot stand in for: that ESP-IDF's otadata behaves as `bootloader_utility.c`
and `esp_ota_ops.c` were read to say (pending → aborted at a reset; `esp_ota_begin` erasing only
the other slot; `esp_ota_end` validating the image format - which the fixture images lack and the
fake does not check); the real time a `self receive` of a 400 KB image takes over USB Serial/JTAG
in 240-byte frames; whether a repeater that took `SLEEP_NOTICE` still answers `HELLO` to the new
image after a Walter restart of a few seconds; and the two restarts' reasons as `esp_reset_reason`
reports them (`ESP_RST_SW` is expected for both).
