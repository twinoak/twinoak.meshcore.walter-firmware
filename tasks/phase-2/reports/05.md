# p2-05 — The supervisor

When the radio goes quiet the Walter now does something about it — the least thing that might work,
then the next, each after a wait and each a limited number of times a day — and when nothing works
it stops and says so. `components/core/supervisor/supervisor.h` holds **`Supervisor`**, one step per
call over the link client, `RadioPins`, the flasher's read-only `verify`, the radio-update machine,
the journal, retained storage, a clock, a time of day and the battery. Before it acts it works out
**what the silence means** (`spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §9): the scanner is running (reported, left
alone), this Walter has only just powered up (its own glitch reset the radio — `ARCHITECTURE` §2
rule 5 — and it is still booting), an update is in progress (not ours, S-7), or the radio really is
not answering. Only the last is an incident, and an incident is a **ladder**: ask again after a
pause → a reset pulse → a power cycle through the kill line → look at the chip with `verify()`,
changing nothing, and put the golden image back through the update machine's rollback path only if
it differs → **stop**, journalled as *unresponsive after <what was tried>*, kept for the next
check-in, and nothing more until a person, the server, or three quiet days. A radio that answers at
any rung ends the incident there.

What a bug would do here is power-cycle a radio every minute and drain the battery the two share,
so the expensive rungs are **counted, capped, journalled and refusable**: resets, power cycles and
flashes per day, kept in retained storage *and* in the journal (every entry carries the counts after
it), and **a cap is forgiven only when a whole day can be shown to have passed** — a restart keeps
them, a deep sleep that put the monotonic clock back to zero re-anchors the window and keeps them, a
power cut reads them back out of flash. A cap reached is a refusal that is itself reported. A radio
restarting itself (three `EV_BOOT`s in ten minutes, or an uptime that never gets past two minutes)
takes the ladder from rung 3; below their thresholds the battery refuses the rail and the reflash,
judged on `POWER_GET`'s **last good** reading, because the gauge is on the radio's I2C and a dead
radio is a dead gauge.

The console has `rak watch` (which only ever reads — looking at the supervisor cannot reset a
radio), `rak watch reset-caps` and `rak ladder`; `docs/supervisor.md` has the whole of it, including
what a person should do for each reason it stops. **Nothing drives it from the main loop:** when the
supervisor runs is phase 3's wake cycle, which this task leaves out of scope.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The bench section is `docs/bench.md` §10, not §9.** The task says "9. The supervisor"; §9 is
  *Updating the Walter*, written by task p2-04 after the task files were (p2-03 and p2-04 each
  recorded the same shift).
- **One line of task p2-03's `stepCheck()` changed: an unreadable battery does not refuse a run
  whose caller has classified the link as down.** The gauge is the INA3221 on the *radio's* I2C
  (`spec/SYSTEM.md` §2, §5.8's `POWER_GET`), so the moment the supervisor most needs a reading -
  rung 4, putting the golden image back on a radio that will not answer - is the moment it cannot
  take one, and the old condition made that flash impossible: the one flash that exists to unbrick
  a radio would have been refused for want of a gauge behind the dead radio. What protects the
  battery there instead is the supervisor's own daily cap and its threshold on the **last good**
  reading. Task p2-03's subcase that showed the link check letting a classified-down link through
  now does so with a reading that is *too low* (the next check), and a new subcase shows an
  unreadable one let through for `kRoleRepeater`. Nothing else of the machine moved.
- **A radio that was answering frames and is now running the scanner costs one reset to find out.**
  `spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §9 decides text or frames **per session** and says the decision holds
  "until the next reset of the RAK"; task p1-02's client implements exactly that, and in a frame
  session it reads no text at all. So the task's "the scanner is running (text lines: not a fault -
  report it, leave it)" is immediate when the session is new - a wake, or after any reset - and
  after a session of frames it is rung 2 that reveals it, and the climb then ends there. Both are
  tested. The alternative would be for the client to listen for text inside a frame session, which
  §9 forbids.
- **The day is a rolling 24 hours from the first counted action, not a calendar day.** A Walter has
  no time of day until phase 3's modem reads one from the network (`interfaces/wall_clock.h`), and a
  cap that only works once the modem does is not a cap. Where a time *is* known the window is dated
  and the date governs, which is what makes a day a day across a sleep.
- **`kIncidentsKept` is 3.** A retained record is at most `kRetainedRecordMax` (128 bytes; RTC
  memory is 8 KB and shared), and three incidents with their count are 124. The journal keeps the
  whole history; the three are what survive a sleep without a flash read.
- **A battery too low for rung 3 ends the climb instead of falling through to rung 4.** Rung 4 needs
  *more* battery than rung 3 (`kFlashMinMv` > `kPowerCycleMinMv`), so there is nothing above a
  refused rail that the battery would allow.
- **`rak ladder` asks before it climbs.** The task says "run the ladder now"; a console command that
  power-cycles a working repeater because somebody typed it is not what the ladder is for, so the
  command works out what the silence means exactly as a wake would, and says `the radio is healthy:
  nothing to climb` when there is no silence. It does lift a stop, because a person is the other way
  out of rung 5 - and that lift is journalled.
- **`Cause::kByHand` was dropped before it was used.** Who asked for a climb is a flag on its
  journal entries (`kFlagByHand`), not a cause: the ladder is climbed for what the radio is doing.
- **`step()` drains the link client's event queue.** Attentions are recorded (§8) and anything else
  - an `EV_MESH_REPLY`, which is phase 4's - is counted and dropped. `rak events` on the console and
  phase 4's mesh client must take theirs before the wake's supervisor step, which is phase 3's to
  order; nothing in this build drives the supervisor from the main loop, so the console's `rak
  events` behaves exactly as it did.
- **`kWalterPowerUpGraceMs` is measured to the moment the asking began**, not to now. Asking a radio
  that does not answer costs §3's three attempts twice over, which is seconds of the clock, and a
  silence seen while the radio could still have been booting is not evidence of anything. The
  constant is 20 s, the same the update machine gives a freshly flashed radio to say `HELLO`.
- **The boot-loop evidence is cleared when an incident ends.** The restarts that were the evidence
  have been acted on, so a radio that keeps looping is a *new* incident each time - and spends the
  caps, which is the protection - rather than one incident that never ends. The restart the ladder's
  own reset and kill caused is forgotten with them.
- **Rung 4 reads the bootloader's settings page as part of "what is on the chip".**
  `Flasher::verify()` answers both "the bytes are the image's" and "the settings page holds a
  record the bootloader accepts for it" (`settings_valid`); bytes that are right behind a record
  that would not start them
  is a radio that sits in its bootloader for ever, so that counts as *differs* and the golden image
  goes back - which rewrites the settings page.
- **Two of the fakes gained knobs**, both small and both used by more than one test: the fake pins
  count completed reset pulses and kill cycles and call back as each ends (the property test runs a
  simulated month with the transition log off and still has to count what reached the radio), and
  the scripted radio can report an uptime of its own and forget what it was about to say.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **What survives what.** The counts and their window, a stop in force with the rungs it followed,
  and the radio's restarts go in one retained record; the three incidents in a second. Every journal
  entry of `kind::kSupervisor` carries the counts after it, so `begin()` can rebuild them from flash
  when a power cut has taken RTC memory - and **a cap is forgiven only when a whole day can be
  shown** to have passed. The three ways the showing fails (a restart, a clock back at zero after a
  deep sleep, a power cut) all keep the counts and re-anchor the window, which is the side to err
  on; `rak watch reset-caps` is the way out and is journalled.
- **The waits and the caps' numbers**: 30, 60 and 120 seconds after rungs 2, 3 and 4 (they grow
  because what each rung asks of the radio grows); 6 resets, 3 power cycles and 1 flash a day; three
  quiet days after a stop; 3 boots in 10 minutes or two uptimes under 2 minutes for a loop; 3 500 mV
  for the rail and 3 700 mV for a flash. Every one is a named constant with a sentence and is marked
  *to be set on the bench* in `docs/supervisor.md`; not one has met a radio.
- **`StopReason` is an enum of nine, and `docs/supervisor.md` says what a person should do for each
  one.** "It stopped" is not a report; "the chip holds the golden image, so this is not a firmware
  fault" tells somebody which van to get in.
- **The incident keeps a per-rung outcome**, not just a bitmask of what was tried: *done*, *cured*,
  *the chip holds the golden image*, *refused by the cap*, *refused by the battery*, *could not be
  done*, *skipped*. That is what "each rung tried and its outcome" reads as on a console and will
  read as in a check-in.
- **One step per call, with `climbing()`**, exactly as task p2-03's machine: `rak ladder` drives it
  in a loop with a tenth of a second between steps that only waited, and phase 3's wake cycle will
  call `step()` once a wake. The waits are measured against the clock and not counted in steps, so a
  caller that steps coarsely (the property test's simulated month) proves the same thing.
- **An attention and its repeats are one record.** §8 has the radio repeat an attention every second
  until a request arrives, up to ten times; those are one attention, and §8's own rate limit (at
  most one per reason per hour) makes a window of eleven seconds safe to de-duplicate in.
- **`LastGoodBattery` is a decorator in `core/update/`, not something the supervisor owns**, and
  `main` hands it to both machines: the update machine's own battery precondition then reads the same
  last good value the supervisor's rungs do, which is the only way the two can agree.
- **The bench's shape.** The FakeRetained lives in the *world*, because RTC memory is not a Walter's
  (it survives a reset and a deep sleep and not a power cut), and the world reacts to the pins
  through the fake pins' new callbacks. The radio on the bench still runs whatever the chip's flash
  holds, so rung 4 needs no knob at all: wipe the application region and the radio is silent for a
  reason no reset and no power cycle can cure and a reflash can. `scanner_on_chip` keeps a module
  NoiseScope across a boot, because what a radio runs is what its chip holds.
- **The property test counts its own simulated time.** A wake from deep sleep puts the monotonic
  clock back to zero, so a month cannot be measured by reading the clock; what was on it before each
  sleep is accumulated. (Getting this wrong was what made the first run of the test take minutes
  instead of seconds: the clock kept being rewound and the month never ended.)

## Not verified here (and why)

No board was on a desk, so no radio has ever gone quiet on this code: every silence in these tests
is a knob. `docs/bench.md` has a new **§10, The supervisor**, after §9 *Updating the Walter*, in the
order to walk it; it needs §3, §5 and §7 walked first and **a jumper or a flying lead** to hold the
RAK's nRESET to ground, which is how a radio is made to go quiet on a desk. The short version:

1. **Hold nRESET to ground and run `rak ladder`.** Watch the four rungs and the stop; **write down
   how long the RAK takes to answer after the reset and after the power cycle** - those are
   `kAfterResetMs` and `kAfterPowerCycleMs`'s bench numbers - and put a meter on the 3.3 V rail
   during rung 3.
2. **Pull the jumper during the climb** and watch it stop at the rung it is on, with nothing above
   it used.
3. **Flash the RAK over USB with something else** and watch rung 4 find that the chip differs, put
   the golden image back, prove it, and leave the node's identity and name untouched; then watch a
   second such incident the same day refused by the flash cap.
4. **Power-cycle the Walter** and watch it *not* call the glitch an incident (§2 rule 5), and
   **write down how long the RAK really takes to answer after it**: that is
   `kWalterPowerUpGraceMs`'s number.
5. **`rak role scanner`, then `rak ladder`**: left alone, no pin moved.
6. **The caps across a reset, a power cut and `reset-caps`**, and a stop that stays stopped with the
   boot line announcing it.
7. **A boot loop**, if a repeater build that restarts itself can be arranged; otherwise noted as not
   walked.
8. **The numbers**: the three waits, `kKillOffMs` against the rail's measured fall and rise, the
   caps against what a mast can afford of its battery in a day, and the two battery thresholds
   against the real pack under two MCUs awake.

What the host tests cannot stand in for: how long a real RAK takes from a reset pulse and from a
rail that has been and gone to its first `PING` answer (both windows are guesses); whether the
power-up glitch really does reset a RAK through the bulkhead filters and for how long it is then
dark; what a kill of five seconds does to a module that was mid-write to its own file system; and
whether a repeater that is *hung* rather than dead answers a `PING` - the whole ladder rests on
§5.2's claim that a `PING` proves the main loop is turning, and only a hung repeater can show it.
