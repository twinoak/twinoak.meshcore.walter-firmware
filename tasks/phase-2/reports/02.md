# p2-02 — The image store

The Walter now keeps the radio's firmware in its own flash. `components/core/image/store.h` lays the
`rakfw` partition out for **two** radio slots from the start — three images each, *golden*,
*candidate* and *scanner* — and makes each of them either wholly there or not there at all: a
record is made valid by **one final small write**, its magic word, after the bytes have been read
back out of flash and hashed, and is destroyed by **one small write** before anything of its area is
overwritten. A *candidate becomes golden* in seven flash operations whatever the image's size,
because what moves is a pointer in a two-copy index record and not a megabyte of flash, and the old
golden image is kept until the new one is in place. A transfer that an interruption cut short
continues from its last 64 KB progress record. A hash that failed a health check goes on a bounded
bad list in the same index record and is refused at the door, before a sector is erased.

**Nothing enters without task p2-01's verdict, and in two steps.** `ImageVerdict` gained a seam:
`judgeManifest()` is checks 1, 2, 3 and the half of 6 that needs no file, and yields an
**`ExpectedFile`** — what a signed manifest promises about the one file wanted; `judgeFile()` is
checks 4, 5 and the rest over the bytes, and yields the **`AcceptedImage`**. Neither has a public
constructor, and they are the only arguments the store's two write doors take, so a laptop cannot
make the Walter erase a sector without a signed manifest naming that file for that slot, and bytes
in an area are not an image until the six checks have passed **over the bytes as they lie in the
Walter's own flash** — never over what a sender said it sent.

Because there is no server yet, `image receive <golden|candidate|scanner> [@slot]` hands the
console's port to a framed binary transfer that reuses **the box link's own framing** (COBS,
CRC-16, a sequence number, one `0x00` ending a frame) with six type numbers of its own and no new
framing code, and `tools/walterctl.py push --port COM7 --as candidate <release-dir>` is the other
end. The laptop is not trusted. And because a bench tool that has never run is a bench tool that
does not work, `host_test/console_host/` is the firmware's own console and store over a simulated
flash on stdin and stdout, and `tools/test_walterctl.py` pushes the specification's fixture release
into it through a pipe as a ctest.

`tools/verify.ps1` is green: **396 host test cases, 169 103 assertions** (42 new cases under ASan
and UBSan) plus a second ctest of 13 checks for `walterctl`, the firmware built with the store
linked in — **398 352 bytes, 12.7 % of its slot** — and the dry-run release signed and verified.
One session.

- [x] **Both power-loss loops cover every flash operation; the counts.** `sim::SimFlash`
  (`host_test/sim/sim_flash.h`) is a flash region behind `interfaces::FlashRegion` that only clears
  bits on write, erases whole sectors, counts the erases of each one, and can lose the power at the
  Nth write or erase — leaving that one **half done** (half the bytes of a write, half the first
  sector of an erase) and failing everything after it until `powerOn()`. Each loop counts the
  operations of the clean run first and then runs the whole thing once per operation with the power
  cut at that one, then restarts the store (`begin()` again over the same flash) and looks.
  `host_test/core/image/test_store_power_loss.cpp` printed:

  ```
  a store of 20480 bytes over an image of 20480 is 14 flash operations; the power went at each in
    turn: 0 left the old image whole, 14 left the slot empty, 0 left a record over the wrong bytes
  a store of 150000 bytes over an image of 8192 is 82 flash operations; the power went at each in
    turn: 0 left the old image whole, 82 left the slot empty, 0 left a record over the wrong bytes
  a promotion is 7 flash operations; the power went at each in turn: 3 left the old golden image,
    4 had already moved to the new one, 0 left none
  ```

  The 14 operations of the first are: the one small write that destroys the old record, the erase
  of the header sector, five erase-and-write pairs for the image's five sectors, and the record's
  body and its magic word. The second is the same shape over 150 000 bytes — 37 sectors, so 74 of
  the 82 — and **its two 64 KB progress records are among the operations the power goes at**, which
  is why it is there. *Nothing kept the old image*, and that is by design rather than by accident:
  operation 1 **is** the invalidating write, so from the first operation onwards the slot reads
  empty, which is what `store.h`'s table says and what "the slot holds the old valid record or
  none" allows. After every cut: the slot is empty or holds the old image whole (`verify()` passes
  over it), never a record that validates over bytes that are not its own; and the same store runs
  again to completion — resuming from a progress record where there is one, starting over where
  there is not — and `verify()` passes over the new image.

  For the promotion the invariant is stronger and is checked at every one of the seven: **the
  golden image is present and verifies, and is either the old one or the new one**. Where the cut
  fell before the index moved, the candidate is still there and the promotion runs again; where it
  fell after, the candidate slot is already empty, because the area that held the old golden image
  is not something to try. `begin()` finishes a promotion caught between its index write and the
  invalidation it implies (the index carries a `stale` marker for exactly that window, and a role
  pointing at a stale area reads as empty until it is cleared), and the loop runs `reboot()` before
  it looks, so that repair is what is being tested. A third loop does the same over a bad-list
  write (3 operations): afterwards the list is the one before it or the one after it, never torn,
  and the hash that was already on it is still there.

**What the store costs in RAM** (`sizeof`, measured on the host compiler): `Store` 4 464 bytes (a
sector buffer and the index), `Receiver` 9 312 (the manifest buffer, the frame receiver, one
frame), `ImageVerdict` 29 872 — 43 648 bytes of static storage, none of it on the main task's
3 584-byte stack. The firmware's `.bss` is 109 016 bytes of the 341 KB of DRAM; the image grew
17 776 bytes (380 576 → 398 352).

**Wear.** Every store erases the sectors its image covers and its area's header sector, once each;
nothing is levelled within an area because every store rewrites the same sectors of it from the
beginning. A promotion makes the two areas a candidate and a golden image take turns, so each sees
half. Measured over 999 stored images of 20 480 bytes, each promoted (`test_store.cpp`):

```
999 images of 20480 bytes, each promoted: area 0 500 erases per sector, area 1 499,
  area 2 (the scanner's) 0; the two index sectors 999 and 999
```

So **one erase per sector per stored image, halved to 0.5 by the promotion**, and two index-sector
erases per promotion shared evenly between the two copies. At a NOR flash's 100 000 erase cycles
that is 200 000 radio updates before the first sector of an area is at its limit — at one update a
week, some four thousand years; the scanner's area and the 334 unused sectors are untouched. The
`rakfw` partition will wear out somewhere else first.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **`components/hal/` is `components/interfaces/`.** The task asks for the flash-region interface in
  `components/hal/`; ESP-IDF owns a component of that name and a project component with the same
  name breaks mbedtls (task p0-01's deviation, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §1). The interface is
  `components/interfaces/include/interfaces/flash_region.h`, included as `"interfaces/..."`, as
  every other one is.

- **The bench section is `docs/bench.md` §7, not §6.** The task says to add "6. Images"; §6 is
  *The slot*, which task p1-06 wrote. Images come after flashing in the order the rig is brought
  up, and renumbering another task's section would break the reports that cite it, so the new
  section is §7.

- **`partitions.csv`'s comment said "each record at most 1 MB … so 6 MB"; an area is 200 KB less
  than that.** 1 MB an image is 6 MB exactly, which leaves nothing for a header sector or an index:
  the arithmetic in the acceptance checklist is what fits. An area holds 815 104 bytes, which is the
  whole application region of a RAK4630 (`0x26000`–`0xED000`) rather than the whole flash of an
  nRF52840 — and an image larger than its board's region is refused by check 6 long before the
  store sees it, so no image that *can* be accepted fails to fit. The table itself did not change,
  and the 1 MB a radio image is bounded by is still the constraint it was written to; what changed
  is the comment above it in `partitions.csv` and the two lines of `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §11 that
  said "2 radios × 3 images × 1 MB", which now say what the store lays out and that 1 368 064 bytes
  are left if a board ever needs more.

- **"Writing only with task 01's acceptance token" became two doors, and the reason is a
  chicken-and-egg.** The token is the verdict over a *file*, and the file's checks (4 and 5) cannot
  run before the bytes are somewhere the Walter can read — which, for an 800 KB image on a board
  with 341 KB of RAM, means its own flash. So: `beginWrite()` takes an **`ExpectedFile`** (what an
  accepted manifest promises: the slot, the use, the size, the SHA-256, the load address),
  `commit()` takes the **`AcceptedImage`**, and only `commit()` writes a record. Both types are
  constructible by `ImageVerdict` alone. The invariant that matters is kept and is stronger than
  the task's wording: *no bytes are written without a manifest signed by a key this build trusts
  naming that file for that slot*, and *nothing becomes an image — nothing can be read, flashed or
  promoted — until all six checks have passed over the bytes as they lie in flash*. A compile-time
  property, as asked: `test_store.cpp` has `static_assert`s that neither type is
  default-constructible, that both are copyable, and that the two doors' signatures are exactly
  `StoreError (Which, Slot, const ExpectedFile&)` and `StoreError (const AcceptedImage&)` — there
  is no other overload to reach for. A host test also reads every source under `components/` and
  `main/` and fails if `interfaces::FlashRegion` is named outside the interface, the store and the
  platform's partition: the store is the only thing in this firmware that writes the radio's
  images.

- **`core/image/verdict.h` and `verdict.cpp` changed** (task p2-01's files): `judge()` is now
  `judgeManifest()` followed by `judgeFile()` and behaves exactly as it did — every one of p2-01's
  verdict tests passes unchanged — and `AcceptedImage` gained `commit()`, the manifest's 40
  hexadecimal characters, because the store's record says which source an image was built from.

- **`StoreError::kTooLarge` cannot be reached through a real token, and is there anyway.** An image
  larger than an area is larger than any board profile's application region, so check 6 refuses it
  first. The store checks it again because the check costs nothing and because the layout's
  arithmetic is the thing being relied on.

- **The store holds `swd` files and refuses everything else** (`kWrongUse`). The Walter's own `ota`
  image goes to an application partition, which is task p2-04's; a store that took one would have
  to pretend it was for a radio slot.

- **The band a transfer will accept is a guess, said out loud.** §5 check 3 wants the slot's role,
  board *and* band. The board comes from the build's profile. The band is a property of the radio
  module and this firmware cannot read it, so: what the slot's golden or candidate image says, and
  `868` (`core::image::kDefaultBand`) when the slot is empty — and `image receive` prints the three
  words it will match before any byte moves, so a 433 unit's first provisioning fails visibly at
  check 3 rather than silently. Phase 3's settings are where the band belongs.

- **The role a transfer will accept follows the image slot**: `scanner` for the scanner image,
  `repeater` for golden and candidate. S-11's `bridge` role would need a setting that does not
  exist yet; `image list` prints what is actually stored, from the descriptor, so a unit carrying
  something else says so.

- **`image erase` leaves the image's bytes.** It destroys the record and erases the header sector —
  so the slot reads empty and a half-finished transfer cannot be resumed into the next image — and
  leaves the sectors above it, which the next store erases as it reaches them. Erasing 199 sectors
  to make a console command tidier is 199 erase cycles nobody asked for.

- **The record keeps the descriptor rebuilt from its fields, not the image's 96 bytes copied.**
  `build(token.descriptor())` is byte-for-byte the image's descriptor for every descriptor §4
  allows (the twelve reserved bytes are zero and `flags` is carried), and the record is read back
  with §4's own parser, so a record whose copy does not parse is not a record. An image whose
  reserved bytes were *not* zero would be stored with them zeroed; §4 says they are zero and the
  release tooling writes them so.

- **The index is written twice per promotion.** The first write moves the pointers and marks the
  area the golden image left as stale; the second clears the marker after that area's record has
  been destroyed. Three flash operations each (an erase and two writes), seven for the whole
  promotion. One write would have left a "candidate" that is the old golden image, which is not
  dangerous but is not true either.

- **`Store::endWrite()` is separate from `commit()`.** The last piece of a file whose length is not
  a multiple of the sector size sits in the store's buffer until something says the file is over;
  the transfer's `kEnd` frame is that something, and the bytes must be in flash before the verdict
  can read them back. `commit()` then refuses anything but a complete write.

- **`Console::tick()` is new**, called by `main`'s loop every time the USB read times out: it is
  what ends a transfer whose other end has gone away (20 seconds of silence) so that a Walter whose
  cable was pulled mid-push is a console again rather than mute.

- **The console takes the store and the receiver as pointers.** `platform::FlashPartition::begin()`
  fails when the partition table has no `rakfw`, and `main` then hands over nothing rather than a
  store over flash that is not there; the five `image` commands answer *there is no image store in
  this build* in one line. A test covers that build too.

- **CI rounds: none** (below).

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The layout's numbers.** An area is the largest application region of any board profile this
  build knows, rounded up to a sector, plus one header sector; the index is two copies of one
  sector, because a sector is the erase unit and A/B is what "the newer valid one wins" needs. In
  the header sector: 512 bytes for the record (236 used) and the rest in 64-byte progress slots
  (56 of them). A progress record goes down every **64 KB** — sixteen sectors, so a resumed write
  always starts on a sector boundary and never re-enters a sector it had begun — which bounds what
  a power cut costs a transfer at 64 KB and needs 13 of the 56 slots for the largest image; a
  `static_assert` holds the three numbers together. The bad list is **eight** hashes: a unit that
  has refused eight different images is not going to be saved by remembering a ninth, and the
  oldest goes.

- **Three magic words — `TWR1`, `TWP1`, `TWI1` — and a format version in every record**, with the
  version, the number of slots and the number of roles checked on read, so that a firmware whose
  layout changed does not read the old bytes as the new ones (`interfaces/retained.h`'s rule, in
  flash this time). Every record's body is written as whole 4-byte words from offset 4 and its
  magic word last, in one 4-byte write; the CRC-32 is `descriptor.h`'s, over the body only.

- **The index's role-to-area map is a permutation and is checked as one on read**: three areas, each
  named once. A promotion swaps *golden* and *candidate*; the scanner's area never moves, which a
  test shows.

- **A record is keyed to its slot, not its role.** A promotion moves a pointer, so a golden image's
  record usually says it was stored as a candidate; that is kept as information (`stored_as`) and
  never checked. The slot *is* checked: a record found in another slot's area is not read.

- **`StoredImage` re-hashes the bytes it hands out, as they are read in order.** A reader that goes
  through the image from start to end — the flasher's write pass, `image verify` — can ask
  afterwards whether what it read was the record's SHA-256, and a single flipped bit says no; a
  reader that jumps about is told it cannot know (`sequential()` false), rather than being lied to.
  A test flips one bit in a stored image and shows both answers.

- **A transfer's three files, in that order, and its own six type numbers.** The framing, the
  sequence rules and the "a response is the request with bit 7 set" convention are the link's
  (`spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §2 and §3) and the code is `core/link/codec/`'s, unchanged; the numbers
  are the transfer's own, because they are not link messages. A response carries
  `status | next_seq | committed | text`; `committed` in the answer to the image's header frame is
  the resume point, and in the answer to a chunk it is progress and nothing to resume from (the
  bytes between two progress records are in flash, but nothing has written down that they are).
  Every frame the Walter sends is preceded by one `0x00`, so the console's own prompt and echo
  before it are an empty frame — which §2 says a receiver ignores — rather than the start of the
  real one.

- **8 KB is the largest manifest this firmware reads over the wire**, and a sender is held to the
  size it announced as the bytes arrive, not after the buffer is full. The specification's own
  manifests are 1.0 to 1.6 KB; one filled to the parser's bounds (eight images of six files) is
  about 7 KB.

- **20 seconds of silence ends a transfer.** Long enough for a laptop to read a megabyte off a slow
  disk between chunks, short enough that an unplugged cable does not leave the port mute.

- **`walterctl` is strictly lock-step**: one chunk, one acknowledgement, the same chunk again if
  none comes (four attempts, five seconds each). The Walter tolerates more than that — a duplicate
  is acknowledged again and written once, a gap is answered with the number wanted — and the tests
  drive those paths directly; being simple at the laptop's end means that whatever the Walter asks
  for, resending is the right answer. It also checks the release's own file against the manifest
  before it sends anything, because there the message can name the file.

- **A re-sent image header frame does not reopen the write.** The first answer can be lost; the
  store would refuse a second write, so the receiver answers again with how many bytes are in
  flash and lets the chunks start over.

- **`host_test/console_host/` is a test target of the host project, not a tool.** It is `core/`'s
  own console with a simulated flash under it; nothing of it is compiled into the firmware, and the
  four stand-ins it needs (pins that only remember, a real clock, retained storage in memory, a
  UART that will not open) are in that one file, twenty lines each.

- **`pyserial` is pinned in `tools/requirements-verify.txt`** even though nothing in CI opens a
  port: the version a bench installs belongs in the one place every other pin is.

## Not verified here (and why)

No board was on a desk, so nothing below has been on real flash. `docs/bench.md` has a new **§7,
Images**, with eight items in the order they should be walked; it needs a **test-key build**
(`tools/verify.ps1 -TestKey`) and a repeater release signed with the specification's test key,
because until the release key exists (`spec/SYSTEM.md` §10 item 1) a normal build accepts nothing.
The short version:

1. **The store says what it is.** `image list` on a fresh unit prints the layout — `2 slots x 3
   images of 815104 bytes (199 sectors)`, `4923392 bytes of a 6291456-byte partition, 1368064
   left` — and three `empty` lines per slot; the boot lines have gained `image store: rakfw at
   0x630000 (6144 KB), …`. If either disagrees, the partition table and the firmware disagree.
2. **`walterctl.py push --as golden` on a test-key build**, then `image list` and `image verify
   golden`. **Write down the milliseconds `image verify` reports**: that is the first measurement
   of reading a few hundred KB back out of the Walter's flash and hashing it on the ESP32-S3, and
   the radio's update (task p2-03) pays it before every flash.
3. **A second `--as golden` is refused** (*it is replaced by a promotion, not by a load*) and the
   golden image still verifies; `--as candidate` succeeds.
4. **A normal build refuses the same push at check 1**, and `trust` says why — the state every unit
   is in today.
5. **Pull the Walter's power mid-push**, two or three times at different points: afterwards the
   slot is empty, or empty with a half-finished transfer, or the old image whole — and the same
   push run again says *the Walter already has N of M bytes; sending the rest* and finishes.
6. **Kill `walterctl` mid-push**: twenty seconds later the port says *the other end went quiet:
   nothing stored* and is a console again.
7. **`image erase candidate`, and `image erase golden` refused**; `image bad` empty.
8. **`image receive candidate @1`** refused by the console, with nothing on the port.

What the host tests cannot stand in for: the real flash's erase and write times (a 4 KB erase is
tens of milliseconds, and a push of a few hundred KB will be seconds of them), the real throughput
of a lock-step 240-byte transfer over USB Serial/JTAG, and whether `esp_partition_write` over an
unaligned length behaves as the documentation says. Flashing a stored image onto the radio is task
p2-03's and brings its own bench lines.
