# p2-03 — The radio's update

The Walter can now replace the radio's firmware and cannot leave the unit worse off than it found
it. `components/core/update/` holds the **journal** — a ring of fixed 64-byte entries in the
`journal` partition, each written as a body and then its magic word, so that a torn entry never
validates and the one before it governs — and **`RadioUpdate`**, a state machine over the image
store, the flasher, the link client, the journal, a clock and the battery, driven one `step()` at a
time: *check* (every precondition a named refusal) → *flashing-candidate* → *booting* → *proving*
(the health check, all five parts, held against the descriptor the store kept of the image that was
flashed) → *committing* (the store's promotion) → *done*; any failure puts the candidate's hash on
the bad list **first**, then flashes the golden image back, boots and proves it by the same check →
*restored*, or *stuck* when it does not come back either. **Every state is journalled before its
action starts**, and `resume()` at boot takes a run up where the journal says — mid-flash the flash
again, booting or proving the proof over from the start, committing the promotion or the evidence
of it, rolling back the rollback. The same machine with another source and no promotion is
`rak role scanner` and `rak role repeater`. The console has `rak update`, `rak update
--first-install`, `rak update status` (the journal's story in words) and `rak role`;
`docs/update.md` has the whole of it, and what state a unit is in after a power cut at each step.

The proof is the Walter dying after every one of the journal's entries of the good path and of a
rollback, and the power going during every write of every entry, on a bench where the store's
flash, the journal's flash, the simulated nRF52840 and a radio that boots whatever the chip holds
outlive the Walter: **every resumed run reached the same end as the clean one**. Beside that, each
failure of the health check in turn ends *restored* with the candidate on the bad list and a second
`rak update` refused at *check*; a golden image that does not come back ends *stuck*, once; each
precondition is refused with the chip untouched; a first install works and, failing, says *no image
to return to*; the scanner round trip leaves golden and candidate as they were.

`tools/verify.ps1` is green: **433 host test cases, 184 753 assertions** (37 new cases under ASan and
UBSan; task p2-02 left 396 and 169 103), the firmware built with the journal and the machine linked
in — **415 456 bytes, 13.2 % of its slot** — and the dry-run release signed and verified. **Done in
two sessions**: the first ran out of usage credits after the code and most of the tests were written
(the reviewer committed its tree as *work in progress*); this one found the bench answering a
`HELLO` with the firmware the chip no longer held, fixed that and three smaller things, wrote the
docs and this report.

- **The bench section is `docs/bench.md` §8, not §7.** The task says "7. Updating the radio"; §7
  is *Images*, written by task p2-02 after the task files were.

- **Three rolling-back states, not one.** The task's diagram has *rolling-back → restored*; here
  *rolling-back* (the bad list, then the golden image's flash) is followed by *rolling-back
  (booting the golden image)* and *rolling-back (proving the golden image)*, each journalled, so
  that a reset inside a rollback resumes at the right place — a flash or a proof — exactly as the
  forward path does. The words on the console say which.

- **The battery comes over the link.** The Walter has no gauge; the INA3221 is on the radio's I2C

- **The band is held against the golden image's.** Task p2-02's deviation stands: the band is a
  property of the module this firmware cannot read, so "for this slot" means the golden image's

- **"The link is down in a way the supervisor has already classified" is a flag the caller

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The journal's shape.** Fixed 64-byte entries so that the reader never trusts a length it found
  in flash — a torn entry is skipped by stepping over its slot. The body (56 bytes with its CRC) is
  one write and the magic word a second, so the three ways a cut can land (half the body, no magic,
  half the magic) all leave a slot that does not validate; "any bit cleared" makes a slot *used*,
  and the next append goes past it. A ring of the partition's sixteen 4 KB sectors (1024 entries);
  the next sector is erased when the current one fills, so the oldest goes first. `protect(seq)` is
  how "it wraps without losing the entries of the update in progress" is kept: an append that would
  erase the protected entry's sector is refused (`kFull`) rather than done, and `enter()` protects
  the run's first entry and lifts it at a terminal state. A run is at most 48 entries
  (`kEntriesPerRunMax`); a sector holds 64. The CRC is `descriptor.h`'s CRC-32, the same one the
  store uses. Entries carry a *kind* (`kRadioUpdate`, `kSelfUpdate`, `kSupervisor`) so that tasks
  p2-04 and p2-05 write into the same ring; `lastOf(kind)` finds the newest of one.

- **The radio-update entry's payload** (48 bytes): the state, the kind of run, the slot, the
  reason, the proving step, flags (*link known down*, *resumed*), the image's SHA-256, and a
  32-bit detail (the flasher's step on a flash failure, the proving's elapsed ms on a pass). The
  hash is what `resume()` and `stepCommitting()` reason from, and what `markBad()` is given on a
  resumed rollback whose candidate is gone.

- **What the Walter's own update will write** (task p2-04's): byte 0 of a `kSelfUpdate` entry's
  payload is its state, and `self_update::kSwitching` (1) is what this machine reads as
  "unconfirmed" and refuses on (S-7). `kConfirmed` and `kRolledBack` are named beside it. Task
  p2-04 keeps these three values or changes them in both places.

- **`resume()` re-journals the state it resumes at**, with the *resumed* flag, before acting — so
  the story shows the restart, and so a second reset finds a valid entry whatever the first
  resume's action did to flash. Booting and proving both resume at *booting* (the radio was reset
  into the image; what this Walter believed about it is from before its own restart, so
  `radioWasReset()` is called and `HELLO` asked again) and the whole proving period is run again.
  A candidate gone from the store on a resume (flash rot, or a store that moved on) rolls back
  with *the stored image does not verify*; a golden image gone on a rollback resume is *stuck*
  with *no image to return to*.

- **`stepCommitting()` recognises a promotion that already happened**: the golden image's hash is
  the run's. A reset between the store's seven flash operations and the *done* entry is the one
  window where the journal lags the store, and this is how it is closed without a second index
  write. A first install's promotion is the same call: the golden area is empty and `promote()`
  moves the pointer all the same.

- **Where the machine refuses to act without its journal.** `enter()` journals first; when the
  append fails the state does not change, `reason()` is *the journal could not be written*, every
  `step()` after that only reports it, and `start()` returns false. A later `resume()` finds the
  last entry that was written. The console prints `update: stopped in <state>: …`.

- **A restart during proving is caught two ways**: the client's `RestartWatcher` (an `EV_BOOT` or a
  changed `boot_id` seen inside any call) sets a flag that the next `step()` acts on — the callback
  runs inside a client call and must not talk on the link — and every `PING` compares the session's
  `boot_id` with the one proving began with.

- **The `HELLO` refused with `ERR_UNSUPPORTED` during booting is a failure, not a silence** (§5.1:
  the radio does not speak this major); it answered, so it booted, and it goes on the bad list.

- **`rak update` runs the machine to its end inside the command**, the console busy for the flash
  and the proving period, each transition printed as its journal entry lands, the flasher's
  progress lines between. That is what a person at the bench wants to watch; the wake cycle (phase
  3) will call `step()` from its own loop instead, which is why the machine is shaped as it is. A
  run `resume()` took up at boot is driven by `Console::tick()` one step a turn of the main loop,
  with the same lines, and `rak update` meanwhile says one is already running.

- **The story is the last run's entries, oldest first**, gathered backwards from the newest to the
  run's *check* entry (one without the *resumed* flag), into sixteen static slots — the main task's
  stack is 3 584 bytes and a run is at most a dozen entries, counting resumes.

- **A second `Flasher` for the machine**, beside the console's: 12 KB of page buffers each, both
  static, because the console's is a member it holds for `rak flash-test` and the machine must not
  reach into the console. The cost is 12 KB of `.bss` that a later tidy could share.

- **The bench's world and its knobs.** `update_bench.h`: the radio on the bench runs *whatever the
  chip's flash holds* — when the flasher resets the chip, the bench reads the descriptor out of the
  application region and, if the stock bootloader would start it, the scripted radio comes up with
  that version, board and role (or as NoiseScope for a scanner image) after a boot delay; with no
  startable application it is deaf, as a radio in its bootloader is. So "`HELLO` with the old
  version" is not a radio told to lie but a knob that makes the flashed firmware report another
  version, and a half-flashed chip is silent because the bootloader's record says *bank 0 holds
  nothing*. The Walter's death is `die()`: its two flashes powered off, the debug target silent,
  the UART refusing to open. One-page fixture images (`update-golden`, `update-candidate`) keep
  the loops inside the host suite's budget: 153 s for the whole suite, up from 121.

- **The scripted radio's per-type knobs are cleared at every boot** of the simulated radio: a new
  firmware has no memory of the old one's quirks, and the golden image must answer `POWER_GET`
  when the candidate did not.

- **The console property test's ten new tokens** are refused in one line on that rig (it has no
  update machine) and so touch no pin; the seeded reset and kill counts fell to 30 and 98 with the
  bigger bag, and the thresholds moved to 25 and 90.

## Not verified here (and why)

No board was on a desk and no repeater release exists yet, so no radio has been updated by this
code. `docs/bench.md` has a new **§8, Updating the radio**, after §7 *Images*, in the order to walk
it; it needs a test-key build, two repeater releases signed with the test key — a good one and one
**broken on purpose** (a build whose link never comes up) — and §5 and §7 walked first. The short
version:

1. **The good path, watched.** The golden image from §7 running, a newer release pushed as the
   candidate: `rak update`, the transitions as `docs/update.md` lists them, then `image list` shows
   it golden, `rak hello` its version, the RAK's own `get name` unchanged. **Write down how long the
   radio took from the reset to `-> proving`**: that is the boot window's bench number.
2. **A broken candidate rolls back and is never tried again**: it fails proving, the golden image
   comes back and is proved, `rak hello` shows the old version, `image bad` lists the broken one,
   `rak update` again is refused at *check*, `rak update status` tells the story.
3. **Pull the Walter's power during the flash, during proving, during the rollback**: after power
   returns the boot lines announce the resume, the run finishes by itself, and `rak update status`
   shows the resumed entry. During proving the whole period is run again.
4. **`rak role scanner`, `rak text`, `rak role repeater`**: the same `rak id` as before.
5. **`rak update --first-install`** on a module whose application was wiped.
6. **The numbers** — the boot window against a real repeater's boot, the proving period against
   what a wake cycle can afford and what a self-restarting build would need to be caught, the
   battery threshold against the real pack.

What the host tests cannot stand in for: how long a real repeater takes from `SYSRESETREQ` to its
first `HELLO` answer (the window is a guess at twenty seconds); whether a MeshCore repeater takes
`TIME_SET` the way §5.4 says on a clock it restored from flash; the real duration of a 300-odd KB
flash through the wire, which the console sits through; and `platform::FlashPartition` over the
`journal` partition on real NOR flash — the two-write entry relies on `esp_partition_write` of 56
and then 4 bytes behaving as the simulated flash does (a write only clears bits; a torn write
