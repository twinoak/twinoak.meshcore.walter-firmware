# p1-05 — The flasher

Give it an image and the Walter puts it into the radio's application region and into nothing
else: `components/core/flasher/` — the **board profile** (the region, the SoftDevice's end, the
bootloader's settings page, every number with the file and line it came from), the **bootloader's
settings page** as the two bootloaders a RAK4630 may carry actually read it (both read at a named
commit; the task's starting description of it turned out to hold for one of them only), and the
**flasher**: identify, refuse a protected chip, check the SoftDevice and the image before anything
is written, scan every page, halt a *running* core, tell the bootloader that bank 0 holds nothing,
erase the vector table page first, write the pages (skipped, in place, or erased — only the words
that are not blank, each page read back and retried once), write the vector table last, read the
whole image back and hold its SHA-256 against the one given, tell the bootloader the application is
valid, reset through the debug port, release the pins and tell `RadioPins` the radio was reset.
`verify()` reads and compares, changing nothing; `rak flash-test` is on the console. A streaming
SHA-256 (`core/image/crypto/`) came with it, held to the FIPS vectors.

The proof is **the power cut at every flash operation** of a two-page image, six times over —
into a blank region and over another image, from a settings page that is erased, that a UF2
install left (bank 0 valid, CRC check off: every RAK in the field) and that a DFU with a recorded
CRC left: **208 interruptions, 460 `flash()` runs**, and after each one the chip is in a legal
state, no page outside the image's pages and the settings page was touched, `flash()` again
finishes with no more erases than the pages that still differ need, and **neither bootloader's
`bootloader_app_is_valid()` — both implemented in the simulator from their sources — would start a
partly written application** at any point (OTAFIX starts the new image after none of the 208
interruptions; the newer stock bootloader starts it only once it is complete and verified,
through its erased-page fallback during the final record's rewrite, which is what the design
says it does).

**35 new test cases** of the host suite's **279 test cases, 154 595 assertions**, under
AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer; **`tools/verify.ps1` green in 124 s** with the
firmware rebuilt (host tests 112 s — the loop is 100 of them; the host build is now optimised and
the fake pins no longer spend a doctest assertion per clock edge, without which the suite took 277
s). One session.

  **The two differ in the third row, and the flasher does not need to tell them apart**: it
  writes states both accept the same way. Before the first erase of an application image it
  writes `bank_0 = BANK_INVALID_APP, bank_0_crc = 0, bank_1 = BANK_INVALID_APP, sizes 0` — the
  record the bootloader's own DFU writes before it erases bank 0 (`dfu_single_bank.c:167-172`,
  `bootloader.c:356-363`) — which row four refuses in both. After the verified read-back it writes
  `bank_0 = BANK_VALID_APP, bank_0_crc = 0, bank_0_size = the image's size, bank_1 =
  BANK_INVALID_APP` — the record **every UF2 install** (`src/usb/msc_uf2.c:202-241`: the status
  zeroed, `DFU_UPDATE_APP_COMPLETE`, so `app_crc` 0) **and every serial or OTA DFU**
  (`dfu_single_bank.c:236-248` with `m_image_crc`, which `dfu_init()` sets to 0 at `:281` and
  nothing in either bootloader ever computes) leaves behind through
  `bootloader_dfu_update_process()` (`bootloader.c:280-288`) — which row two accepts in both, by
  the CRC-check-off clause, as soon as the first two words are not both blank. So the bootloader
  starts the application for the same reason it starts every application a RAK has ever had
  installed: bank 0 is flagged valid and the CRC check is off. A page with a real CRC (nrfutil's
  settings generator, Nordic's own bootloaders) is accepted as it is when its CRC-16 and size are
  the image's (`Flasher::settingsAccept`, the codec's CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE, which `crc16.c:46-60`
  is) and replaced otherwise. **Erasing the page instead would have been wrong**: OTAFIX and every
  stock release before 0.11.0 refuse an erased page for ever, and the RAK would sit in DFU mode
  after every flash. All of it is in `core/flasher/bootloader_settings.h`'s header comment, and
  the simulator's two rule sets are the same lines.

- [x] **The report gives the expected duration of a full flash at 100 kHz (transfers per word,
  words per image, erase time), since a caller has to budget for it.** Measured on the simulated
  clock, which advances by the wire's own half-periods (`test_flasher.cpp`, "the time a flash
  takes"): **8 dense pages (8 192 words) into a blank chip at 125 kHz: 32 996 ms simulated,
  76 293 SWD transfers — 9 transfers per image word, 4.03 ms per image word.** Where they go: each
  word is **written once** (one `DRW` transfer; `TAR` rewritten every 256 words) and **read three
  times** — the scan, the verify after the write, the final read-back — at two transfers a word
  (`DRW`, then `RDBUFF`: the posted read is not pipelined), so 7 transfers of data and about 2
  more of `TAR`, `READY` polls and the two settings records. A transfer is 54 clocks: **432 µs at
  125 kHz, 540 µs at 100 kHz**. An erase is 85 ms typical and ~40 `READY` polls. Budget for a
  **400 KB image (102 400 words, 100 pages)**:

  | Clock | Per word | Words | Erases (100 × 85 ms) | Total |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 125 kHz (the default; the slowest the chip is specified for) | 9 × 432 µs = 3.9 ms | 398 s | 8.5 s | **≈ 6.8 min** |
  | 100 kHz (the task's figure) | 9 × 540 µs = 4.9 ms | 498 s | 8.5 s | **≈ 8.5 min** |
  | 300 kHz (the predicted ceiling of the filtered lines) | 9 × 180 µs = 1.6 ms | 166 s | 8.5 s | ≈ 2.9 min |

  A repeated or resumed flash is cheaper only in writes and erases: the three read passes stay
  (the scan is what makes "the same image again" cost zero erases and zero writes). The two levers
  that halve it, both outside this task: a pipelined block read in `Dp` (task 04 noted it), which
  takes a read from two transfers to one; and dropping the scan for a region known to be blank
  (phase 2 knows, from its journal). The progress callback fires once per page per pass — 300
  times for that image — so a lease can be extended or a watchdog fed every few seconds.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The application region ends at `0xED000`, not at the task's "more conservative" `0xD4000`.**
  The task asks for "the start of the file system, the more conservative of the linker scripts in
  use". The `0xD4000` script is the companion radio's (`*_extrafs.ld`: a second file system
  below the first, for contacts); the repeater links with `nrf52840_s140_v6.ld` and ends at
  `0xED000`, where the file system holding the node's identity begins — and **the repeater
  firmware's release tooling writes `app_region = 0x26000..0xED000` into every manifest, from the
  script the link actually used** (its `docs/release.md`, "The application region"; its phase 0
  report 02). With the task's number, `BoardProfile::accepts()` — which the task also asks for:
  "a manifest's `app_region` must lie inside it, and is refused if it does not" — would refuse
  every repeater release in phase 2. The spec is on the side of `0xED000` too: `app_region` is
  "the flash a `swd` file may occupy on this board", read from the linker script
  (`FIRMWARE-MANIFEST.md` §2). The profile cites both scripts and says why; the reviewer may want
  to settle the sentence in the task, or make the two repositories agree on `0xD4000` if the
  repeater is ever to carry an extra file system.
- **Step 3 halts a running core; it does not reset-and-halt.** The task says "Reset-and-halt the
  core"; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §5 says "Halt the core", and §4 and task 04's report say why: on
  a build code Fxx module a reset closes the access port until the firmware has reopened it, and
  a core halted at its reset vector never does. MeshCore runs no nRF52 watchdog (no `NRF_WDT` in
  the repository; its `HAS_EXTERNAL_WATCHDOG` is a GPIO-kicked external part) that a halted core
  would trip, and an in-flight flash operation of the application's own is waited out after the
  halt (`waitFlashReady`). Nordic's Product Specification ("Access port protection", read for this
  task) lists the resets that reset `APPROTECT.DISABLE` — pin, power, brownout, watchdog, wake
  from System OFF — and a soft reset is not among them, so the final `SYSRESETREQ` should leave
  the port as it was; the simulator keeps task 04's stricter model, and a `kProtected` from that
  final reset is reported as *the port closed afterwards*, not as a failure.
- **The task's description of the settings page is the stock bootloader's since 2026-05-15 and
  not OTAFIX's.** "With the settings page erased it treats the application as flashed by a
  debugger and checks the vector table instead" — true of stock since commit `67105f8` (release
  0.11.0); OTAFIX, and every stock release before, return `false` on an erased page. The task
  foresaw this ("confirm both ... before relying on either"); the flasher relies on neither,
  writing the two states both accept (above). Also: the bootloader refuses an application whose
  **first two words** are both blank, not "whose first word is blank" — the vector table page
  erased satisfies it either way.
- **"CRC recorded" is not what a DFU leaves on these bootloaders.** Both record `bank_0_crc = 0`
  for every UF2, serial and OTA install (`m_image_crc` is never computed). The scenario still
  exists (other tools) and is one of the loop's three starting states.
- **The host build is optimised (`RelWithDebInfo`), and the fake pins' range check is no longer
  a doctest assertion.** Task 01's `verify.sh` compiled the host tests at `-O0`, and task 03's fake
  pins ran a `REQUIRE` per pin operation — a billion assertions and 277 s for this suite. Both
  changed; the sanitizers are untouched; the suite is 112 s, 100 of them the loop. CI runs the
  same script, so there is no gap between here and there.
- **The scan pass.** The task's "the same image again (zero erases, zero writes)" and its step 6
  (the settings page before step 4) cannot both hold unless the flasher knows, before it touches
  the settings page, that nothing differs: hence a read of every page before anything is written.
  It costs one of the three read passes above and is also what makes a resume cheap.
- **CI rounds: see the end of this report** (filled in after the pull request's run).

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The SoftDevice is checked against the profile before anything is written.** The chip's
  SoftDevice information structure (`0x3004` magic, `0x3008` size) must say the SoftDevice ends
  where the profile's application begins — the bootloader derives the application's start from
  the same word (`dfu_types.h:77`). A chip with S140 7.x (`0x27000`) or with no SoftDevice is
  refused at `kSoftDevice` with the address; task 04's bench worry ("`ffffffff` at `0x26000`
  means the region table is wrong") is now the firmware's own check.
- **The final record carries CRC 0, not a computed CRC-16.** It is what every UF2 and DFU
  install leaves (fleet-proven); a real CRC would turn any run-time write inside the image region
  by some future firmware into a refusal at the next boot; and the flasher's own read-back and
  SHA-256 are stronger than a CRC-16 the bootloader would recompute at every boot. A record with a
  CRC that already matches the image is left alone.
- **Word 0 of a record is written after words 1–6**, in two `writeWords` calls, so that a power
  loss in the middle leaves `bank_0 = 0xFFFF` (an erased page to both bootloaders: the old whole
  application on stock, the bootloader on OTAFIX) and never a bank code with a torn body behind
  it.
- **Only words that are not `0xFFFFFFFF` are written, in runs**, and a page is written in place
  only when every differing word is blank: a word is thus written once per erase (the simulator
  counts anything beyond nWRITE; every bench's destructor requires zero). A word half-written by a
  power loss is not blank and gets its erase.
- **The region handed to the nRF52 layer is the image's own pages**, not the profile's region:
  the guard underneath refuses anything else before the flash controller is touched. The
  settings page is reached only by `settingsWrite`, whose region is that one page.
- **On a failure after the halt the flasher asks for a system reset on its way out** (unless the
  target is silent, the lines are lost or the chip is protected, where it would be pointless),
  so a half-flashed radio runs its bootloader rather than sitting halted; the result says
  `reset_issued`. The failure's step and address are kept; the reset's own failure is reported
  only when nothing else went wrong.
- **A session waits for `NVMC.READY` before its first flash read** (bounded by an erase's time):
  a flash read stalls the bus while the controller is busy, and a resume right after an
  interruption, or a halt in the middle of the application's own erase, found it so. `READY` is a
  register and does not stall.
- **`flashTest` touches neither the settings page nor anything but its two pages**, requires them
  blank, and reports the pattern's SHA-256 through the same digest path as an application image;
  it halts and resets the radio like a flash (the bench item says the RAK restarts).
- **The reset is noted in `RadioPins` after the lines are released**, and a reset pulse then
  waits the full second; the double-reset detection is armed only after a pin reset
  (`main.c:308-313`), but `RESETREAS` accumulates until the application clears it, so the spacing
  is kept whatever the radio last did.
- **`Image` carries an `ImageSource`** (bytes by offset) rather than a pointer, because phase 2
  hands the bytes over from a flash partition a page at a time; `MemoryImage` wraps an array.
  SHA-256 lives in `core/image/crypto/`, phase 2's home for the signature code.
- **The `Flasher` is a member of `Console`**, not a local: its three page buffers are 12 KB and
  the main task's stack is 3 584 bytes. The constructor signature of `Console` is unchanged.
- **The loop's image is two sparse pages, and the third run is made every fifth interruption.**
  Each interruption costs two to three flashes through the bit-level wire (~0.2 s each,
  optimised); the kinds of operation — a record, the first page's erase, in-place writes, erases
  over another image, the final record — are all there, and the count is in the table.
- **The simulator's SoftDevice information structure is installed by the benches**, not by the
  chip's constructor, so that task 04's tests (which expect a blank chip) are unchanged.

## New libraries (with the one-line justification)

None. SHA-256 is written here (FIPS 180-4, 120 lines) rather than vendored, because `core/`
cannot include ESP-IDF's mbedtls and the host tests need the same bytes; phase 2 may keep it or
hold it against whatever it brings for Ed25519. `components/core/flasher/` includes `<cstdint>`,
`<cstddef>` and this repository's headers; the tests use `<vector>` and `<string>`, in
`host_test/` only.

## Not verified here (and why)

No board was on a desk. The simulator proves the documents were followed; only the chip proves
the documents were read right, and only the bench shows what no document says — above all that
the bootloader a RAK actually ships with behaves as the two sources read here. `docs/bench.md` has
a new **§5, Flashing** — the most important bench session of the project — in the order to walk
it:

1. **`rak swd id` first; note the variant; stop if *protected*.**
2. **Note the RAK's identity and one setting from its USB console** (`get public.key`, `get
   name`): they live above the region's end and must be what they were after everything below.
3. **`rak swd mem 0x00003004 3` prints `51b1e5db 00026000 ....00b6`** — the SoftDevice's magic,
   its size (the profile's application start) and S140 6.1.1's firmware id; and `rak swd mem
   0x000ff000 3`: the settings record as the RAK arrived (expect `00000001 ffff00ff 00000000`, a
   UF2 install). Write both down.
4. **`rak flash-test`: passes; the RAK still boots what it had.** The expected lines are in the
   bench item; **write the time down** — the first measurement of a page write and an erase
   through the real wire. `not blank` means the application reaches `0xEB000`: note the address,
   do not force it.
5. **Flash a repeater image; the RAK restarts into it, not into its UF2 bootloader**; `rak hello`
   shows the version; identity and name unchanged; `rak swd mem 0x000ff000 3` shows `00000001
   ffff00ff <size>`. A UF2 drive instead means a bootloader that is not one of the two read here:
   note its version (`INFO_UF2.TXT`).
6. **Pull the Walter's power half-way through a flash; the RAK stays in its bootloader** (stock:
   a UF2 drive; OTAFIX: BLE DFU advertised, no drive); `rak swd mem 0x000ff000 1` reads `000000ff`;
   **flash again: it completes and boots**, with fewer erases than the first time. Twice: during
   the pages, and in the last seconds.
7. **Double-tap the RAK's reset afterwards: the UF2 bootloader still works, and a UF2 of the stock
   firmware still installs.**
8. **Time a full flash; find the fastest SWD speed that still verifies** — against the budget
   above; the whole-flash ceiling may be lower than §4's `connect` ceiling.
9. **A build code Fxx module after an interrupted flash**: with the application gone, does `rak
   swd id` still say *open*? If not, such a module cannot be re-flashed by the Walter after an
   interruption without a power cycle and a bootloader that opens the port — note it, loudly.

Also only a board can show: the real cost of three read passes at 125 kHz (the budget assumes
the wire's 54 clocks a transfer and nothing else); whether a running MeshCore repeater halts
cleanly under `halt()` with the SoftDevice's radio timers in flight (the final reset makes it
moot for the application, not for the halt itself); and whether the bench core's `0x3000`
structure says `0x18` in its first byte as the simulator assumes (nothing depends on it).
