# p1-03 — The SWD wire

The Walter speaks Serial Wire Debug: `components/core/swd/wire` is the protocol at the level of
the two lines — line reset, the JTAG-to-SWD switch, requests, acknowledges, data and parity, the
turnarounds, the idle cycles — over the `SwdLines` that `RadioPins::swdAcquire()` hands out and
the clock's microsecond delay; `components/core/swd/dp` is the debug port over it — `connect()`,
the DP registers, AP access with `SELECT` cached and the posted read hidden, the power-up handshake
both ways, `ABORT` — with every answer a result that says which of eight things happened and no
loop without a constant bound. Every number in both files carries its section of Arm's *ADIv5.0 to
ADIv5.2* (IHI 0031G, ID022122), read from the document itself for this task (`out/docs/`,
git-ignored), or of the nRF52840 Product Specification.

The other half is **`host_test/sim/swd_target`**: a target at the wire level that stands between
`RadioPins` and the fake pins as an `interfaces::Pins` of its own, sees our rising edges, samples
and drives SWDIO on them as a target does, implements the DP, and fails the running test with a
sentence when the host breaks the protocol. Everything above was written against it and it caught
real mistakes on the way (below). **40 new test cases** (and the three deliberate protocol errors,
quoted below) of the host suite's **182 test cases, 239 569 assertions**, under AddressSanitizer
and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer; `tools/verify.ps1` is **green in 40 s** with the firmware
rebuilt. `rak swd connect` is on the console.

Two things differ from the task as written and are under *Deviations*; the one that matters on
the bench is that **the default clock is 125 kHz, not 100 kHz**, because the nRF52840 Product
Specification gives SWDCLK a minimum of 0.125 MHz.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The default clock is 125 kHz (a half-period of 4 µs), not 100 kHz (5 µs).** The task, the
  architecture document and the hardware repository's `BENCH-BREADBOARD.md` §4 all say to start
  at 100 kHz; the nRF52840 Product Specification ("Debug and trace > Electrical specification >
  Control access port") specifies f_SWDCLK from 0.125 MHz to 8 MHz. The runbook's rule — when a
  document and a habit disagree, the document wins — puts the default at the slowest clock the
  chip is specified for. Through the filters a half-period of 4 µs is still four time
  constants (98 % settled), and the bench's ceiling experiment is unchanged. The constant is one
  place (`kDefaultHalfPeriodUs`); `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4 and `docs/bench.md` §4 say so.
- **The lower bound on the half-period is 1 µs (500 kHz)**, the line's time constant, rather than
  something nearer the predicted 300 kHz ceiling: the bench item that *finds* the ceiling needs
  room above it, and at one time constant the line reaches 63 % of the swing before the sampling
  edge — the point below which no setting can work. "Without meaning to" is served by the
  constant's comment and by there being no console setting yet (phase 3's settings task adds one).
- **`readAp` and `writeAp` take the access port as a parameter** (`readAp(ap, addr)`), where the
  task writes `readAp(addr)`: task 04 reads AP 0 (the memory access port) and AP 1 (the control
  access port), and a method that names the port is how "the access port last selected is
  remembered and rewritten only when it changes" becomes testable (it is: the writes are counted
  on both sides). The address is the register's byte offset in the port, `0x00`–`0xFC`, bits
  [7:4] the bank, bits [3:2] on the wire.
- **The nRF52840's DPIDR is not in the Product Specification.** Noted in the table above; the
  constant stays, decoded and annotated, and the bench confirms it. If the first board prints
  another value the console says so in words (*not the nRF52840's 0x2ba01477*) rather than
  refusing: refusing is task 04's, on `FICR`.
- **The simulator insists on 50 clocks *after* the switch sequence, counted on their own.** The
  first `connect()` sent 49-clock line resets and the simulator accepted them, because the
  switch sequence `0xE79E` ends in three ones and 49 + 3 ≥ 50 — a real SW-DP would accept them
  too. B5.2.2 step 3 says to "send at least 50 SWCLKTCK cycles with SWDIOTMS HIGH" after the
  sequence, so the simulator now zeroes its count when it sees the sequence and the task's
  shortened-by-one test means what it says. Stricter than a chip; that is the right side.
- **The simulator holds a host to three things the task does not list**, each from the document
  and each switchable: a request before any line reset (B4.3.3: "a line reset is required when
  first connecting to the target"; `requires_line_reset`); a request other than a `DPIDR` read
  in the reset state (B4.3.3: UNPREDICTABLE — this target chooses silence and says why); and the
  clock stopped under eight idle cycles after a transfer (B4.1.1). And one thing a host must
  *expect*: an AP access without `CDBGPWRUPREQ` and `CDBGPWRUPACK` answers FAULT with
  `STICKYERR` (B2.3.2: "any AP transfer might generate a fault response"; `ap_needs_powerup`).
  Where the document says IMPLEMENTATION DEFINED or UNPREDICTABLE, the simulator takes the
  strict reading and names the section in the sentence.
- **`rak swd connect` asks for the power-up and then withdraws it.** The task says "print DPIDR
  and the power-up state"; built as `powerUp()` (both requests, both acknowledges awaited),
  printed, then `powerDown()` and the acknowledges awaited low — because the Product
  Specification ("Debug and trace > Debug Interface mode") says the chip is in debug interface
  mode "as long as the debugger is requesting power via CxxxPWRUPREQ", and a repeater on a mast
  must not be left there by a console command. B2.3.2 also wants a host to wait for the
  acknowledge to drop before requesting again. `docs/bench.md` §4 asks for the radio's current
  before and after.
- **CI rounds: none** — *(filled in after the pull request's run)*.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The sample point: the end of the low half, just before the rising edge we are about to
  produce.** The task asks for the reasoning in a comment; it is at `Wire::cycle` in `wire.cpp`.
  In short: the target changes SWDIO at the rising edge *it* sees, about 0.7 τ after we drive the
  clock through 470 Ω and 2.2 nF, then has to pull its own 2.2 nF to the new level; reading at the
  end of the low half gives that bit a whole period less the clock's delay (about 7 µs, seven time
  constants, at 125 kHz; 2.6 τ at the predicted 300 kHz ceiling) instead of the half period a
  mid-cycle sample would, for nothing in return — the bit cannot change before the next rising
  edge, which does not exist until we make it. SWDIO's turnaround is the tight spot
  (`BENCH-BREADBOARD.md` §4) because the target starts from the park bit's high. The same
  sequence is what OpenOCD's and CMSIS-DAP's bit-bang drivers do, which is reassurance and not
  a source: the document fixes the target's edges, the host's are ours.
- **`connect()` writes `SELECT` to zero after the `DPIDR` read.** A line reset does not reset
  `SELECT` (B4.3.3: "Other SW-DP registers are reset only by a powerup reset"), and CTRL/STAT is
  at `0x4` only while `DPBANKSEL` is 0, so one write makes the next CTRL/STAT read well-defined
  and the cache known. One transfer; every test counts it.
- **Eight idle cycles after every transfer, and SWDIO released after them.** B4.1.1's third
  option, taken always, because a console command stops the clock when it ends. Released
  because ARCHITECTURE §2 says SWDIO is "input unless a transfer is driving it", and B4.3.2's
  note prefers the line not held low for long.
- **After an acknowledge that is none of the three, the wire backs off 34 clocks with SWDIO
  released and sends no idle cycles** (B4.2.5, Table B4-6: "back off because of possible data
  phase"). What comes next is a line reset or nothing. `connect()` treats a WAIT or FAULT to
  its `DPIDR` read as a protocol error (B4.2.3, B4.2.4: that read never gets either).
- **The WAIT bound is ten retries with the eight idle cycles between, and nothing writes
  `ABORT.DAPABORT`.** B2.2.1: "use this function only in extreme cases"; the flasher (task 05)
  owns what to do with a stalled access port. `clearErrors()` writes the four clear bits and
  nothing else.
- **A parity error on read data is returned, not retried.** `RESEND` exists in the constants
  (B2.2.8) and nothing uses it yet: the caller sees `kParityError` and decides; `connect()` is
  the one caller that retries, by repeating the whole reset sequence once.
- **The simulator's DPIDR read in the protocol-error state is not honoured** (B4.2.5 leaves it
  IMPLEMENTATION DEFINED): only a line reset brings it back, which is what the host does.
- **"Not there at all" is modelled as no pull-up and 2.2 nF of memory:** the line reads as
  whatever the host last drove, which after a request is the park bit's high — so an absent
  radio and a silent one both give "no target", and `docs/bench.md` §4 says what a floating
  input that happens to read low would print instead. A line stuck low is a protocol error.
- **The test-only hook is a method on `Dp`** (`setLineResetClocksForTest`), called by one test
  and by nothing in `components/`.

## Not verified here (and why)

No board was on a desk, so what the simulator proved bit for bit is what the chip must now
confirm — above all that Arm's document was read correctly, which is the one thing a simulator
written from it cannot show. `docs/bench.md` has a new **§4, SWD**, with these four, in the order
the rig is brought up:

1. **`rak swd connect` prints `DPIDR 0x2ba01477`** — `revision 2, part 0xba, DPv1, designer 0x23b
   (Arm) - the nRF52840's`, the power-up acknowledged, the request withdrawn, the pins released
   with `0 WAIT retries, 0 faults, 0 parity errors` — first with short wires, then through 470 Ω
   and 2.2 nF on each line, then through the real bulkheads; the RAK's own console still
   answering throughout. This is also the Product Specification's missing number.
2. **The sample point and the ceiling, with a scope on SWDIO against SWCLK:** the RAK's edges
   following SWCLK's rising edges and settled before the next one; then the half-period lowered
   (`kDefaultHalfPeriodUs`, no console setting yet) until `connect` fails, and the ceiling and
   its first symptom noted (predicted near 300 kHz, the turnaround into the acknowledge first).
3. **With the RAK unpowered: `no target`, and nothing is back-fed** — the rail at 0 V throughout
   the two attempts; and the same with the RAK powered but the lines disconnected.
4. **The radio's current after `rak swd connect`** is what it was before: the power-up request
   withdrawn is what ends debug interface mode, and the meter decides whether that is enough.

Also only a board can show: the real cost of a GPIO write on the ESP32-S3 inside each clock
(the bench's scope reads the actual frequency), `esp_rom_delay_us`'s accuracy at 4 µs, and
whether the 100 kΩ pull-up and 2.2 nF make the "line is confused" refusal appear where the
report predicts "no target".
