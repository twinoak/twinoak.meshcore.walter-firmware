# p1-02 — The link client

`spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` from the Walter's end, as calls instead of bytes. Under
`components/core/link/client/`: the **port** (the UART and its two pins, taken and given back as
one thing), the **client** (one request at a time with §3's retries, the typed calls of §5–§7, the
session §5.1 brings, the events of §6.3 and §8, and §9's "which firmware is over there"), and the
protocol's numbers **in words** for the console. Beside them: `interfaces/byte_stream.h` and
`interfaces/wall_clock.h`, `platform/uart.cpp` (UART1, 115200 8N1, GPIO7/8), eleven new console
commands from `rak hello` to `rak text`, and `host_test/sim/scripted_radio` — a radio on the other
end of a fake UART, driven by the specification's fixtures, which phase 1's and phase 2's later
tasks reuse.

It is held to the fixtures and to the scripted radio by **43 new test cases** (and 13 more in the
console, the parser, the pin map and the no-allocation grep) of the host suite's **142 test cases
and 120 904 assertions**, under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer.
`tools/verify.ps1` is green in 20 s warm.

Nothing in the task was left out; the mesh client's twelve calls are there and nothing calls them,
as the task says. Fifteen things differ from how the task or the specification describes them and
are under *Deviations*; the two largest are that the byte-stream interface has **six** operations
rather than the four the task names — `open()` and `close()`, because a UART that may own its pins
only inside a window has to be told when to take them — and that `codec/wire.h` gained §4's
capability column as a function.

- [x] **Nothing in the client blocks longer than one response timeout, and the report says how the
  firmware's main task schedules it.**

  tick at `CONFIG_FREERTOS_HZ=100` — reading the port between them. So the longest the client is
  *away* is a tick, and the longest a call takes is three attempts of a second when the radio says
  nothing at all, which is what "three attempts" means. A test measures both: the fake clock's
  `on_delay` hook sees no delay longer than `kPollMs` for a whole link-down call, and the call's
  elapsed time is between `3 × 1000` and `3 × 1010` ms.

  waits. Phase 3's wake cycle becomes the second caller; the client holds no task and starts none.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **`components/hal/` is `components/interfaces/`** — task p0-01's deviation, inherited: ESP-IDF
  owns a component named `hal`. Every "hal" in the task file is this directory; the include prefix
  is `interfaces/`.
- **The byte-stream interface has six operations, not the four the task names.** The task asks for
  "read what is there, write, flush, 'has the line been idle'"; `open()` and `close()` are there
  too, because this stream's pads belong to `RadioPins` and the UART may own them only between
  `uartEnable()` and `uartRelease()`. Somebody has to tell the peripheral when to take them and
  when to let go, and on the ESP32-S3 it is not optional: `gpio_config()` takes the pad back to
  the GPIO matrix, so the UART must be routed to it again afterwards, and routed away before it is
  released (`platform/uart.cpp` uses `esp_rom_gpio_connect_out_signal(tx, SIG_GPIO_OUT_IDX, …)`).
  `LinkPort` is the only caller of either, which is also what makes the fourth criterion
  structural.
- **§2's idle `0x00` stays the codec's, and the stream's `idleFor()` answers a different
  question.** The prefix is `FrameWriter`'s, measured from the last frame *it built* (task p1-01);
  the stream's `idleFor()` is the line's own idleness, measured at the port, and the client uses it
  for §9's probe where no frame is built. The caveat p1-01 left open — that "built" is not "sent" —
  **is closed**: `LinkPort::send()` flushes the frame off the port before the client starts
  waiting, bounded by 200 ms (246 bytes at 115200 baud is 21 ms), so the two moments differ by the
  time of one `uart_wait_tx_done`.
- **`codec/wire.h` gained §4's capability column as a function** (`capabilityOf`), which is a file
  task p1-01 wrote. It is a cell of the specification's own table and belongs beside the rest of
  them rather than in a second table in the client; a test reads all 27 types' capabilities off §4
  and holds them against it.
- **`core/board/pin_map.h` gained `uartTxGpio()`/`uartRxGpio()`.** A UART drives its own TX, so the
  peripheral is the one thing outside `core/board/` that drives one of the seven pins — it cannot
  be helped. The numbers reach `platform/uart.cpp` through two `constexpr` functions, so the names
  the `tools/verify.sh` grep guards (`kWalterTx`, `kWalterRx`) stay inside `core/board/` and the
  table stays the one source; a test holds the two functions against the map and against §1's
  GPIO7/GPIO8.
- **A decoded answer's views point into the client's own buffer, not the receiver's.** The codec's
  rule (p1-01) is "valid until the next frame is read"; the client's is "valid until the next call
  on this client", which is stronger and necessary: the chunk a response arrives in may hold an
  event behind it, and feeding that event would overwrite the response. The client therefore copies
  the payload out of the receiver the moment it recognises it.
- **A queued event is the payload's bytes, not a decoded struct.** For the same reason, and because
  it keeps `codec/messages.h` the only place a payload becomes fields. An `EV_MESH_REPLY` can carry
  230 bytes of reply, so the queue is 4 × 243 bytes ≈ 1 KB of the client's 4 KB.
- **`EV_BOOT` brings the session rather than invalidating it.** §5.1 says a changed `boot_id` means
  every piece of per-boot state is gone, and the event's payload *is* the `HELLO` body — the role,
  the slot, the capabilities and the three versions, fresh from the radio. So the client takes them
  from it and marks the session up, instead of waiting for a `HELLO` to ask the same question. What
  it does drop is the per-boot state: the event counter starts again, and the clock is pending.
- **A second `EV_BOOT` with a `boot_id` already seen is not a second restart.** §8 repeats events
  for a Walter that may have been asleep, and telling the supervisor twice would make one boot look
  like a boot loop (ARCHITECTURE §8). News once per `boot_id`.
- **§9's evidence is scoped to the session.** "Lines beginning with `{` were seen" is counted from
  the last `radioWasReset()`: a radio that was running the scanner, was reset, and now says nothing
  at all is a *silent* radio, and calling it a scanner would send the supervisor down the wrong
  rung of its ladder. A test holds it.
- **`rak time set <epoch>` never sends *force*.** §5.4 reserves *force* for "a person's
  instruction", and the task's grammar has no word for it; a backwards step is refused by the radio
  (`ERR_RANGE`) and the console explains why in one line. Phase 3's task 03 owns the console's
  grammar and can add a word for it if the bench wants one.
- **The `rak` refusals were reworded**, and two of task p0-03's tests with them: eleven
  subcommands do not fit a 96-byte error line, so `rak` alone is now *rak needs a subcommand (try
  'help')* and an unknown one *unknown rak subcommand (try 'help'): dance*. `help` lists them all.
- **The console's property test allows one driven pin.** Task p0-03's version asserted that after
  every line *nothing* is driven; the link client keeps the UART open so that the radio's events are
  heard, so the invariant is now "nothing is driven except TX while the UART is enabled", with the
  fake's rules unchanged underneath. Its seeded counts fell with the bigger bag of tokens (47
  resets and 151 kills in 10 482 lines), and the thresholds moved with them.
- **`SEND_TRACE`'s case exercises the refusal path**, because the fixtures' only response for that
  type is §6.2's `ERR_RANGE` for three-byte hashes — which is a true answer to the request case
  they pair it with. And **`PATH_SET` has no response case at all**, so the scripted radio
  synthesises §6.1's "status only"; the test asserts that this is the one type where it had to.
- **The scripted radio is not a second implementation of the protocol.** It parses what the Walter
  sent with the Walter's own codec and answers with the *fixtures'* bytes. What is under test is
  the client's behaviour; that the codec agrees with the specification is p1-01's test, and that
  the two firmwares agree is the bench's (*Not verified here*).
- **CI rounds:** see *CI* below.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`kPollMs` is 10 ms**, one tick at `CONFIG_FREERTOS_HZ=100`: the client reads the port, then
  waits a tick, through the 1 000 ms of §10. Smaller would spin; larger would be a task away from
  its console for longer than it needs to be.
- **The event queue is four, and a fifth event is dropped and counted** rather than pushing the
  oldest out. §3 says an event is never the only way to learn something — every event's content can
  be fetched with a request — so an overflow costs a request, and a Walter that slept through ten
  attentions wants the first (which carries the reason) rather than the tenth (which carries
  `repeat = 9`).
- **The scanner's lines: three of up to 320 bytes.** A `stat` line of NoiseScope's own
  documentation is about 300 bytes and fits; a `sweep` line is about 2.5 kB and is offered and kept
  truncated, with a counter. Forwarding them verbatim is the uplink's business and is not in this
  task; the subscriber seam (`TextWatcher`) is where that will hang.
- **`HELLO`'s three strings are copied into 48 bytes each**, truncated and counted if longer.
  §5.1's own examples are 13, 14 and 25 bytes. A view would not do: the session outlives the frame
  it came from.
- **`poll()` never opens the port.** Taking a pin is not a side effect of listening — the rail may
  be off, and whether to power the radio is the supervisor's decision. `main/` opens the port at
  start-up (and says so on the console) so that an `EV_BOOT` or an attention from a radio nobody
  has spoken to is heard at all; a call opens it by itself when it has to.
- **`radioWasReset()` does not make the clock pending.** §5.4 hangs `TIME_SET` on an `EV_BOOT` or
  a `HELLO` with an unseen `boot_id`, and a reset brings one of those within a second. Setting it
  here would put a `TIME_SET` in front of the first `HELLO` of the new session.
- **A `TIME_SET` the radio refuses is not sent again at every call.** It answered `ERR_RANGE`
  because the time was earlier than its own clock (§5.4); the same time would get the same answer,
  and the Walter has not learned a new one. The pending flag clears and the refusal is counted.
- **`seq` is the last one plus one** (§3 asks only for "different from the previous request's"),
  which is also what makes a `(type, seq)` repeat on the wire mean exactly "a retry" to the
  radio's remembered response.
- **A sensitive request's buffers are wiped when the exchange ends** — the encoded payload and the
  frame built from it, whole, through a `volatile` pointer so the compiler keeps the stores. §6
  says a password is in RAM for the length of the send; the length of the send is the exchange,
  because §3's retry has to put the same bytes on the wire. `SEND_LOGIN` always, `SEND_CLI` when
  the line is flagged sensitive. No console command sends either, so nothing can print one.
- **The console prints the number and the word** (`role 1 (repeater)`, `attention 2 (battery
  low)`). §5.1 says a minor version adds values to exactly these fields and that a receiver passes
  on what it does not know: the number is the answer and the word is the gloss, so a radio one
  minor ahead is still readable.
- **`rak ping`'s nonce is this board's clock**, low 32 bits: §5.2 wants a number that differs every
  time, and the round trip is printed from the same clock.
- **The scripted radio patches the fixture's `HELLO` body** — role, capabilities, `boot_id`, and
  (for a test that wants them) the firmware and board strings — at §5.1's own offsets, cited in
  the code. It also echoes `PING`'s nonce, because the fixture's response carries the fixture
  request's nonce and a radio answering *this* request echoes this one.
- **The simulator lives in `host_test/sim/`** and is built into the test binary by a new glob in
  `host_test/CMakeLists.txt`; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §1 says what is in there now and what phase
  1's task 03 adds.
- **The no-allocation test now covers the client too** (`components/core/link/client/`, its own
  allow-list of includes). `core/` has no heap; a client whose buffers are members is one a reader
  can size, and this is the grep that keeps it that way. The client's own state is about 4 KB: two
  246-byte frame buffers in the receiver, 240 + 247 + 240 for the request, the frame and the
  response, 1 KB of event queue, 1 KB of scanner lines and the session.

## New libraries (with the one-line justification)

None. The client uses `<cstddef>` and `<cstdint>` and nothing else; `platform/uart.cpp` uses
ESP-IDF's own UART driver (`esp_driver_uart`, already in the image) and no new managed component.

## Not verified here (and why)

The link's two ends have never met: both firmwares have only passed the same fixtures. Everything
below is in `docs/bench.md` §3, *The link*, in the order the rig is brought up
(`BENCH-BREADBOARD.md` §7), and needs a RAK4631 wired through both bulkhead filters.

1. **`rak hello`, `rak ping`, `rak status`** against the repeater firmware: the version, role, slot
   and capabilities it answers with, held against what its own console says.
2. **`rak cli get radio`**, a command the mesh refuses and this link does not, and `rak cli reboot`
   — the silence that is the expected outcome (§5.7).
3. **`rak power` and `rak env`**: the three channels' signs and magnitudes against a meter.
4. **A reset of the RAK by its button**: `rak events` showing the boot with its new `boot_id`, and
   the clock set straight afterwards — which on a Walter with no modem means `rak time set <epoch>`
   from a person (`interfaces::WallClock` answers "no time" until phase 3).
5. **NoiseScope on the RAK** (§9): `rak hello` taking three seconds and then saying *scanner
   (text)*, `rak text` showing its `info` and `stat` lines, `rak ping` refused without a frame, and
   a reset putting it back.
6. **An hour of `rak ping` once a second through both filters**: no retries counted. A retry here
   would mean 470 Ω and 2.2 nF are taking the edges off 115200 baud, and the baud rate is then the
   thing to change (§1's electricals are frozen).
7. **An attention** (§8): the event arriving, its repeats, and the two zero bytes before it not
   being mistaken for a frame. Waking from deep sleep on them is phase 3's.

And **`platform/uart.cpp` as a whole**: no session can run it. The host tests run the client
against a fake stream, so what the bench is confirming there is the baud rate and framing, that
`uart_set_pin()` takes the pads `RadioPins` has enabled, that routing the transmitter away in
`close()` leaves TX at the level `RadioPins` last wrote (and no glitch on the line), and that
`uart_wait_tx_done()` returns inside its 200 ms. Reading it is the test, as `platform/` is meant
to be (ARCHITECTURE §1).
