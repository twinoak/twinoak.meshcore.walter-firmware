# p8-01 — The flasher lets the idle task run

One session. `tools/verify.ps1` **green in 492 s** (spec 0 s, spec-tools 1 s, deps 28 s,
host-tests 427 s, release-tests 1 s, build 31 s, partitions 0 s, release 2 s); `core_tests`
**1011 test cases, 716,983 assertions, 0 failed**.

The fault is one sentence long: nothing on the SWD path blocked, so the idle task of the core
never ran and the task watchdog ended every attempt. The fix is one function,
`core/swd/idle_pause.h`'s `letIdleTaskRun()` — `clock.delayMs(10)`, one FreeRTOS tick at
`CONFIG_FREERTOS_HZ = 100`, which `EspClock` turns into `vTaskDelay(2)` — called between one SWD
transfer's last bit and the next one's first, in front of each of the two long things this
firmware does over the wire.

## Acceptance checklist

- [x] **The pause is in every SWD loop longer than a second, and never inside a transfer (each one
  cited).** Two call sites in the flasher and two in the provision sequence cover every such loop;
  the audit of *all* of them, with each one's bound, is *The loops, all of them* below. Never
  inside a transfer: `Wire::transfer` ends every transfer with `kIdleAfterTransfer` = 8 idle
  cycles and releases the line, which is exactly the point ADIv5 (IHI 0031G) B4.1.1 allows the
  host to stop the clock at; f_SWDCLK's minimum of 0.125 MHz (nRF52840 Product Specification,
  *Debug and trace > Electrical specification > Control access port*, the same citation
  `core/swd/wire.h` picks the 4 µs default half-period from) binds the clock while a transfer runs
  and says nothing about an idle line. Held by a test as well as by reading: the fake pins check
  the pin rules on every transition and `FlasherBench`'s destructor asserts
  `pins.violations.empty()` over the whole 417 KB flash, and the simulated target — which answers
  at the level of the two lines and faults on a protocol error — carries the flash through to a
  verified image.
- [x] **The 2 s test fails before the change (measured, in the report) and passes after; it covers
  an image, the hash pass and the base.** `host_test/core/flasher/test_flasher_watchdog.cpp`, two

- [x] **The watchdog's configuration is unchanged.** `git diff` over `sdkconfig.defaults` is

## What it measured

Both runs are the same test binary, the same test, the same fixtures; the only difference is that
in the *before* run `letIdleTaskRun()` was temporarily `{ (void)clock; }` — the code as it was.
Output in `out/experiments/p8-01-before.txt` and `p8-01-after.txt` (git-ignored).

| | longest stretch with no blocking delay | | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| | **before** | **after** | budget |
| a full repeater image (417,284 bytes, 102 pages, 125 kHz) | **408,281 ms** | **1,005 ms** | 2,000 ms |
| the hash pass over it (`Flasher::verify`) | **90,448 ms** | **894 ms** | 2,000 ms |
| `rak provision`'s base, write and verify (48 KB of flash bytes) | **16,211 ms** | **222 ms** | 2,000 ms |

Before: two test cases failed, three assertions. 408,281 ms is **six minutes and forty-eight
seconds** of simulated time with the idle task never running — 13.6 times the watchdog's 30 s, and
the measured shape of what the board did on 6 November. The hash pass alone is 90 s, three times
the watchdog: a flash that had somehow survived would have been reset during the read-back. The
405 blocking pauses the *before* base run still shows are the sequence's own (`softReset`'s two
10 ms waits, `eraseAll`'s 10 ms polls, `kBootloaderSettleMs`) and the store's; none of them falls
inside the write or the verify, which is why those two are one 16.2 s stretch.

After: the longest stretch is one page read (2048 transfers, DRW then RDBUFF for every word) at
125 kHz, which is what it should be — there is nothing shorter to divide a flash into without
pausing inside a transfer.

**The cost, with the arithmetic.** Five pauses a page, not one:

| pass over the pages | pauses a page | what each bounds |
|---|---:|---|
| `scan` | 1 | the page read, ~0.9 s |
| `programPages` → `programPage` | 3 | the page read; the erase + the page's words, ~0.46 s; the verify read |
| `readBack` (the hash pass) | 1 | the page read, ~0.9 s |

102 pages × 5, plus one for the first page's read before its erase, is **511** — the number the
test prints. At one tick that is 5,110 ms of the flash's 413,391 ms, **1.2 %**; on the board
`delayMs(10)` is `vTaskDelay(1 + 1)` and so 10–20 ms, giving **5.1 to 10.2 s on a flash of about
seven minutes, 1.2 to 2.5 %** — or **50 to 100 ms a page**, five times the task's estimate of
10–20 ms, because that estimate assumed one pause a page. One a page leaves a stretch of 3,107 ms
(measured, at the first attempt at this task), and a page cannot be cut finer than its three
segments.

For the base: 106 pauses on a 48 KB base (53 runs of up to 256 words written, 53 compared),
+1,060 ms on 21,871 ms, **4.8 %** — about **10 ms a kilobyte each way**. Scaled to a RAK's real
~185 KB base that is ~408 runs, ~4.1 s of pause (8.2 s at 20 ms) on a write-and-verify of about
62 s. Which is the finding hiding inside this one: **`rak provision` through this firmware would
have been reset by the watchdog too.** 16,211 ms for 48 KB is 62 s for 185 KB, and the whole
write-and-verify was one stretch. The task's goal section says the base "ran on the bench tool,
never through this firmware"; now there is a number for what would have happened.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

1. **The base's page loop is not in `provision_door.cpp`.** The task (and the phase-6 item) put
   the pause "between pages of the base's write in `provision_door.cpp`". There is no such loop
   there: `ProvisionDoor::writeFlash` is one guarded `Nrf52::writeWords` call, and the loop that
   walks the base is `Provision::writeBase` → `flushRun` and `Provision::verifyBase` →
   `compareRun` in `core/provision/provision.cpp`, in runs of `kRunWords` = 256 words. The pauses
   are there. The door's own loop, `writeUicrWords`, is bounded by the same 256 words — a
   hundred milliseconds or so — and needs none. No behaviour differs from what the task asked
   for; only the file does.
2. **One pause a page was not enough, so there are five.** Recorded under *The cost* above rather
   than here because it changes no interface, but it is a departure from the task's stated figure
   and from "between pages" as the only granularity.
3. **The base in the test is 48 KB, not 185 KB.** `base-resume` is the largest base fixture
   (`host_test/fixtures/gen_manifest_fixtures.py`), and task p5-03's own timing test uses it the
   same way, with its report extrapolating to "a real ~180 KB base" from the per-word figures. The
   assertion this task adds is about the longest *stretch*, which is one run and is independent of
   how many runs there are, so a 48 KB base proves the invariant; the totals are extrapolated
   above and the real figures are the bench's (§4a). A 185 KB fixture would be ~540 KB of Intel
   HEX — inside the 640 KB a base area holds — and is the obvious thing to add if a later task
   wants the totals measured in simulation instead.
4. **The task file says "the supervisor ends the run as *stuck* after twelve".** It does not: the
   supervisor has no part in *stuck* (no occurrence of it in `core/supervisor/`), `RadioUpdate`
   does, and not because of the twelve. The twelve is `faults_in_a_row`, which only lengthens the
   next sleep. See *The resume, read once more*.
5. **The phase-6 item is ticked by this task, though that file says "Only the reviewer ticks an
   item".** The task's acceptance criterion and `tasks/phase-8/README.md` ("Each task here takes
   one of them and ticks it") both say to, so it is ticked, with the PR named. The reviewer may
   prefer it untouched; the body of the tick says exactly what is and is not done.

## Decisions taken without asking

1. **`delayMs` of a named constant, not a new `yieldBriefly()` on the interface.** The task
   offered both and asked for the smaller change. `delayMs` is smaller — no interface, no second
   implementation in `platform/`, nothing for the four other `interfaces::Clock` implementations
   in `host_test/` to grow — and it is also the *more honest* one, because it lets the fake clock
   decide whether a delay blocks by the same rule the board uses (`pdMS_TO_TICKS(ms) == 0`), which
   makes every existing `delayMs(1)` in the firmware visible to the new measurement rather than
   only the calls this task added. `delayMs`'s promise is kept: 10 ms is never shortened, and
   `kIdlePauseMs` is a constant with the tick rate cited beside it.
2. **The pause goes in front of `readPage()` and in front of `programPage()`'s write segment,
   rather than at the top of each of the three page loops.** Two call sites instead of six, and
   the bound is a property of the two primitives rather than of each caller remembering — so
   `flashTest()`'s two loops and any future loop over `readPage` are covered by construction.
3. **`kIdlePauseMs = 10`, not more.** One tick is what the idle task needs to be scheduled; the
   task asked for "at least one tick" and anything larger is cost without a reason.
4. **The poll loops were left alone.** `Nrf52::waitReady` and `pollDhcsr` wait `delayMs(1)`,
   which on the board is a busy wait, and a page erase therefore spins for its 85 ms. Each is
   bounded well under a second (300 ms, 50 ms, 100 ms, 10 ms) and each sits inside a segment a
   pause already bounds, so none of them can starve the watchdog. Making them block would mean
   restructuring `for (poll = 0; poll <= timeout_ms; ++poll)` into elapsed-time arithmetic and
   would multiply every one of those timeouts by ten — a change to four timeout semantics for no
   gain in the thing this task is about. It would save ~8.7 s of pure spin per image (85 ms × 102
   erases) and make an erase cost 9 SWD reads instead of ~62; worth a later look, not worth it
   here.
5. **The test image carries a real §4 descriptor** at 0x400, built with `core::image::build` and
   checked with `find()` (exactly one), so what the flasher is handed has the shape of the file
   that failed and not only its length — the task asked for "a valid descriptor" and this is the
   cheapest honest reading of it.
6. **`FakeClock::advance()` ends a stretch rather than lengthening it**, and
   `resetBusyStretch()` exists for a test to start measuring after its own setting-up. `advance()`
   is time passing outside the code under test (a bench setting the scene, a simulated sleep), not
   CPU the firmware spent spinning; counting it would have made every bench that advances by hours
   report a stretch of hours.

## The loops, all of them

Every loop in `components/` around a memory-AP read or write, and every poll, with what bounds it
at 125 kHz. A page read is ~0.9 s (2048 transfers), a word written ~0.44 ms (one transfer), a
transfer ~0.37 ms (46 clocks at 8 µs).

| Loop | Bound | Pause |
|---|---|---|
| `Flasher::scan` page loop | `plan->pages` ≤ `kMaxPages` = 256 | `readPage` |
| `Flasher::programPages` page loop | the same | `readPage` ×2, the write segment |
| `Flasher::programPage` retry | 2 attempts | the write segment, each attempt |
| `Flasher::writeRuns` run loop | ≤ 1024 words in one page, ~0.45 s | bounded by the page |
| `Flasher::readBack` (the hash pass) | `plan->pages` | `readPage` |
| `Flasher::flashTest` blank loop, erase-again loop | `kTestPages` = 2 | `readPage` |
| `Flasher::settingsAccept` CRC loop | the image's bytes, no SWD at all | — |
| `Provision::writeBase` → `flushRun` | a run of ≤ 256 words written, ~95 ms | `flushRun` |
| `Provision::verifyBase` → `compareRun` | the same, read back: ~190 ms | `compareRun` |
| `ProvisionDoor::writeUicrWords` word loop | ≤ 256 words, ~110 ms | the caller's |
| `ProvisionDoor::eraseAll` poll loop | `delayMs(kChipErasePollMs)` = 10 ms | **already blocking** |
| `ProvisionDoor::softReset` | `delayMs(10)` twice | **already blocking** |
| `MemAp::readBlock` / `writeBlock` word loop | the largest caller asks 1024 words, ~0.9 s | the caller's |
| `Nrf52::waitReady` poll | `kErasePageTimeoutMs` 300 ms, `kWriteTimeoutMs` 50 ms | inside a bounded segment |
| `Nrf52::pollDhcsr` poll | `kHaltTimeoutMs` 10 ms, `kResetTimeoutMs` 100 ms | inside a bounded segment |
| `Dp::transfer` WAIT retry | `kWaitRetries` 10, ~5 ms | — |
| `Dp::connect` attempts, `Wire::lineReset`, `backOff` | `kConnectAttempts`, 50 and 33 clocks | — |
| `Dp::powerUp` poll | `kPowerUpPolls` 100 × `delayMs(1)`, ≤ 100 ms | — |
| `Nrf52::identify` | 9 words in three blocks | — |
| `console.cpp` `rak swd mem` | `kMaxMemWords` | — |

## The resume, read once more

What the journal and the supervisor did on 6 November, against `docs/update.md`:

- **The resume at every boot — designed, and correct.** `docs/update.md`, *After a power cut*: a
  last entry of **flashing-candidate** means "the flash again — the flasher's scan skips the pages
  already right and writes the rest; then the reset", and `WakeCycle::stepResume` plus
  `RadioUpdate::resume()` did exactly that. It is also why one fault became nineteen boots: the
  resume cannot tell a step that is slow from one that resets the Walter every time. That is not a
  fault in the resume; it is a gap, and it is written up as a follow-up rather than fixed here.
- **The twelve watchdog wakes — designed, in that nothing counts them against the update.**
  `faults_in_a_row` is kept by `core/cycle/wake_reason.cpp` and read by one place only,
  `core/power/interval_policy.cpp`. No ceiling, no refusal; the console line Thomas saw is a
  warning and nothing more. Whether it should be more is the second follow-up.
- **The lengthened sleep — designed.** `interval_policy.cpp`: "A run of crashes or watchdogs
  lengthens it, and never past the longest interval there is", doubling with `faults_in_a_row` and
  capped at `kMaxFaultDoublings`. The right answer to an unexplained reset loop, and the reason a
  unit in that state is reachable less often rather than more — a cost, not a bug.
- **`stuck` at the end — designed as a state, misleading as a message.** `RadioUpdate::fail` with
  `Kind::kFirstInstall` enters `State::kStuck`, which is what `docs/update.md`'s *stuck* row and
  the `rak update: STUCK: …` console line describe, and the whole run was a `--first-install` with
  no golden image to return to. But the *twelve watchdog resets did not cause it*: a watchdog reset
  leaves the journal at *flashing-candidate*, which resumes. The stuck came from
  `stepFlashing` → `fail(Reason::kFlashFailed, …)`, and `fail()` then replaces that reason with
  `kNoImageToReturnTo` for the printed note — so the person is told the situation and not the
  cause. The flasher's step survives in the entry's `detail` and `rak update status` shows it. Why
  that flash failed is not answerable from the five lines in the task; Thomas's full log, or
  `rak update status` on the unit, has the step. **Follow-up, not a fix** (both are in
  `tasks/phase-8/FOLLOWUPS.md`, tagged `[reviewer]`).

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens the unit's ports this pile (`tasks/phase-8/README.md`), and a watchdog is a
property of a running Walter: the host suite can only show that no stretch of simulated time runs
without a blocking delay in it. The lines are in `docs/bench.md`, with the **release** build of
v0.1.1 or later:

- **§9, a whole image flashed with no watchdog reset, timed.** `rak update --first-install` (or
  `rak update`) of a full repeater image: the flasher's page lines run through the scan, the pages
  and the read-back with no reboot in the middle, and the run reaches *done*. Write down the
  wall-clock time and the image's size; the prediction is 413 s for 417,284 bytes at 125 kHz, of
  which ~5–10 s is this task's pauses. A `boot N: watchdog reset` with `resuming at
  flashing-candidate` after it is this fault back.
- **§9, `boot` afterwards says `power-on` or `software`, never `watchdog`** — right after the
  flash, and again after the proving period. One `watchdog` there, even with the update *done*,
  means some stretch still monopolises the CPU for 30 s.
- **§4a, the same for `rak provision` on a spare radio**, when there is one: the seven step lines
  with no reboot between them, `boot` clean afterwards, and the write and verify timed again
  beside the base's size (a few per cent slower than p5-03's prediction and no more). There is
  nothing to see on a radio that is already provisioned, and the unit's own radio is not
  disposable.
- **The real per-page cost on silicon.** Everything above is the simulated clock at 125 kHz. The
  pause is 10 ms of `vTaskDelay(2)` whatever the wire does, so the *percentage* is whatever the
  board's real flash time turns out to be — §5's "time a full flash" item is where that number
  comes from.
