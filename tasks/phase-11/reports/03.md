# p11-03 — A rollback says what killed it

On 9 November the unit said *the image never reached its main loop (the bootloader gave up on it)*,
and that was all meshmaster.dk could show. The reason had been on the board the whole time: the old
image booted with `esp_reset_reason()` = panic, and the `coredump` partition held
`assert failed: esp_startup_start_app app_startup.c:86 (res == pdTRUE)`. Reading it took a person
at the bench with `esptool read_flash 0xFF0000 0x10000`. Now the unit reports it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **the reader** | `components/core/health/coredump.h` — the flash header, the CRC-32 over every byte, the ELF, four kinds of note and `pcTaskName` in the TCBs. Host-tested **over the real dump**: the panic line, the tasks `ipc0` and `ipc1`, the ELF SHA-256 `5c1cb8f37` |
| **the finding** | the boot that finds a rolled-back trial records its own reset reason — *that* is the reset that ended the trial — and, when the dump's checksum holds **and its application ELF SHA-256 is the trial image's own**, its panic line and the task that crashed. A journal entry of its own, `kind::kTrialEvidence` |
| **where it goes** | `self status` and the rollback's boot line; the apply's result (`reason` and `lastTrial`); `twinoak.fw.state`'s `walter.lastTrial`; and `coredump` on the console, which also names which slot's image wrote it |
| **nothing erases it** | `coredump erase confirm` is the only thing in this firmware that erases a dump — the one of 9 November was readable only because nothing had |
| **what it cost** | **24 bytes** of static DRAM (`_heap_start` 0x3FCD24C8 → 0x3FCD24E0), 8 056 bytes of flash. *The tightest* is 332 bytes, was 356. R22 passes with 11 720 bytes to spare |

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The journal's copy of the panic line is cut to 31 characters, and the whole line is read back
  out of the dump.** The task asks for the panic line in *the journal's rolled-back entry*, and
  that entry's 48 bytes are already full (state, slot, reason, flags, size, 16 bytes of hash, two
  12-byte versions). So the evidence is a journal entry of its own — `kind::kTrialEvidence`,
  48 bytes like every other, written immediately after the *rolled back* entry — and even 48 bytes
  do not hold the line of 9 November: it is 69 characters, and 53 of them are needed before
  `app_startup.c:86` is reached. The entry therefore carries the reset reason (1), the dump's state
  (1), flags (1), the crashed task's name (13) and **31 characters of the panic line** with
  `kEvidencePanicCut` set; `SelfUpdate::lastTrial()` reads the whole line back out of the dump
  whenever the dump is still there and is still that image's, and falls back to the cut copy when
  it is not. A test holds both halves, and `docs/update.md` says so in the table.
- **No `kTrialEvidence` entry for `kSwitchNotTaken`.** The task's deliverable names "the rows of
  `docs/update.md`'s table that end in *rolled back*", which includes *the restart came before the
  other slot was made the next to boot*. Nothing of that image was started there, so this boot's
  reset reason is about something else entirely, and a dump carrying that build's ELF hash would be
  from an earlier life of it. Recording either would be the one thing this task is against, so that
  row records nothing; `self status` says only what it said before.
- **`interfaces::OwnSlot` grew two fields.** `has_elf_sha256` and `elf_sha256[32]`, filled by
  `platform/own_update.cpp` from `esp_app_desc_t.app_elf_sha256` (with a `static_assert` that the
  field is still 32 bytes). Attribution needs them: the dump names its image by that hash and only
  the slot's own application description can answer. `host_test/fakes/fake_own_update.h` gained
  `setElfSha()` for the same reason — the fixture images have no application description at all.
- **meshmaster.dk needs a change to show it, and that goes to its FOLLOWUPS** (the task says this
  repository does not change it):
  - the apply's result now carries `reason` (a sentence) and `lastTrial`
    (`reset`, `dump`, `attributed`, `panic`, `panicCut`, `task`, `dumpHeld`) **after** the keys of
    `FirmwareApplyResult.json`, so every reader that knew the old shape still reads it. The job
    page should show `reason` where it shows the outcome today, and `lastTrial.panic` beside it;
  - `twinoak.fw.state` now carries `walter.lastTrial`, the same object. The unit page should show
    the last trial's reset reason and panic line;
  - the three new journal lines of an apply (`reset`, `panic`, `task`) need no change: the server
    stores the journal verbatim and renders step/note pairs.
- **`core/health/coredump.h` names `interfaces::FlashRegion`**, so it is the fourth file allowed to
  by `host_test/core/image/test_store.cpp`'s rail (after the store, the journal and the agent's
  region store). It reads the `coredump` partition and erases it on one typed command; no image's
  bytes are anywhere near it.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The reader is ours, not ESP-IDF's.** The task allows `esp_core_dump_get_panic_reason()` on the
  board with a reason given. It is not used: it answers only the panic string, and the three other
  things a rollback needs — whose image wrote the dump, whether the checksum holds, which task
  crashed — are not in that API at all (`esp_core_dump_get_summary()` has the task name but needs
  2 KB of `esp_core_dump_summary_t` and still cannot be host-tested). Reading the partition in
  `core/` gets all four, runs under the sanitizers over the real dump, and keeps rail R12.
- **`pcTaskName` at offset 52 is checked, not trusted.** It is `pxTopOfStack` (4) +
  `xStateListItem` (20) + `xEventListItem` (20) + `uxPriority` (4) + `pxStack` (4) in
  `$IDF_PATH/components/freertos/FreeRTOS-Kernel/tasks.c`'s `tskTaskControlBlock`, with
  `portUSING_MPU_WRAPPERS` 0 on Xtensa — read out of the image, cited in the header, and confirmed
  by the fixture. Because it is a layout and not an API, a name that is not printable ASCII
  terminated inside `configMAX_TASK_NAME_LEN` is reported as *unknown* rather than as whatever
  those bytes were, and a `crashed_task_tcb` that matches no kept task leaves `crashed` at -1.
- **Nothing is read out of a dump whose checksum does not hold.** Not even the panic line, which is
  tempting because a half-written dump often still has one: a dump the power cut mid-write is
  exactly the thing that would otherwise be reported as a panic that never happened. The header is
  printed (it tells a dump cut short from an empty partition) and nothing else.
- **A format other than ELF-with-CRC32 is read as nothing.** A binary dump (`COREDUMP_VERSION_BIN`)
  or an ELF one summed with SHA-256 is a real dump and neither is what this build writes; reading
  one with this reader would be reading it wrongly.
- **The evidence is reported while it is the newest word in the story.** `lastTrial()` refuses when
  a `kSelfUpdate` entry is newer than the evidence entry — a *received* or *switching* since then
  means the story has moved on. That is what lets the entry be tied to its rollback without
  spending any of its 48 bytes on an identifier.
- **`CoreDump` has no static storage.** The first version of it kept its 128-byte flash window, its
  segment table and the TCB addresses as members — about 370 bytes of `.bss` — and `tools/verify.sh`
  **refused the build**: those 370 bytes moved the 20 480-byte kept-image scratch out of the DIRAM
  heap and into the 32 kB one, leaving the main task's stack 216 bytes of room instead of 11 788.
  That is v0.1.3's fault exactly, caught by task p11-02's rail eleven days after it cost a unit its
  image. All of it now lives on `read()`'s stack (`struct CoreDump::Reading`), and one `CoreDump` is
  a reference to a region. The whole task costs 24 bytes of static DRAM.
- **`coredump erase confirm` asks for `kConfirmWord`**, the same word `erase confirm`,
  `rak flash-test confirm` and `rak kill 20 confirm` ask for.

## Not verified here (and why)

No session opens a port (`tasks/phase-11/README.md`). Everything below is on the bench, and the
same lines are in `docs/bench.md` §10 in the order the rig is brought up.

- **The `coredump:` boot line.** `coredump: at 0xff0000 (64 KB); 'coredump' says whether there is
  one and what it says`, third among the partition lines. A board whose table had no `coredump`
  would print nothing and `coredump` would say so in one line; `tools/check_partitions.py` makes
  that impossible, but it has never been seen.
- **`coredump` over a dump a real panic handler wrote.** The host tests read the dump of
  9 November, which a real panic handler did write — but to this reader's `interfaces::FlashRegion`
  over a simulated flash, not to `esp_partition_read` over the real one. The first thing to walk is
  §10's new *A version that cannot start at all*: a test-key build with `assert(false)` at the top
  of `app_main`, pushed `--as self`, switched to, and the old image's own account of it. **Bring
  back the boot lines, `self status` and `coredump` whole.**
- **`esp_app_desc_t.app_elf_sha256` as the real platform reads it.** `platform/own_update.cpp` now
  copies it into `interfaces::OwnSlot`; the `static_assert` holds its size and the build passes, but
  no board has yet shown that the nine characters in a dump are a prefix of the thirty-two bytes in
  the slot. `coredump`'s line *that is the other slot's image: slot 1* is the proof, and it is in
  the bench step above.
- **`coredump erase confirm` on real flash.** `esp_partition_erase_range` over all sixteen sectors.
  Walk it after the step above, and then check that **nothing else** erases it: reset the board a
  few times, let a wake run, and `coredump` must still have the dump.
- **The journal's cut copy after an erase.** `self status` must still have the reset reason and a
  31-character panic line once the dump is gone. The host test holds it; the board has not.
- **The apply's result and `twinoak.fw.state` as meshmaster.dk renders them.** The bodies are held
  to their shapes here and the scripted server accepts them; what the job page and the unit page
  *show* is meshmaster.dk's, and the FOLLOWUPS items above are what it needs. Over LTE this is
  task 04's offer-and-apply of 0.1.4.
